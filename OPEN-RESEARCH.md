## 1. Romans 14 — Paul calls the vegetarian "weak in faith"

**Why it matters.** Dr. Tabor's reading of Romans 14:1-2 is the single best unused asset for the diet claim: Paul defines the one who "eats only vegetables" as *weak in faith*, and Hegesippus (in Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23) says James the brother of Jesus ate no flesh and drank no wine. If Paul's "weak" is aimed at the Jerusalem leadership, the verse is evidence *for* the Nazarene diet, from a hostile witness. `questions/nazarene-sect-sacrifice-and-diet.md` already flags Romans 14:2 as "the best unused asset for the flesh claim" and nothing in `christianity/` develops it.

**Source material.** `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/66-did-paul-think-james-the-brother-of-jesus-was-weak-in-the-faith.md` (Tabor's own framing, ten minutes). Tabor, *Paul and Jesus* (2012) — cite the chapter and page; do not quote the captions.

**Done looks like.** `christianity/romans-14-weak-in-faith-james.md`: the text of Romans 14:1-6 and 14:21 (WEB or KJV, since the repo holds no Bible yet), the Hegesippus passage with its edition named, the argument stated narrowly (Paul's category, not Paul's intent, is what the text proves), and the honest counter-reading (Romans 14 may address Roman house-churches, not Jerusalem). Add a row to `TOPIC-INDEX.md` under cluster 8.

## 2. Paul's four misappropriated Hebrew texts

**Why it matters.** Tabor's most structured argument that Paul built a new religion rather than reinterpreting an old one: Habakkuk 2:4, Genesis 15:6, Genesis 12:7 ("seed," singular), and Deuteronomy 25:4 (the ox), each read in a way its context forbids. The repo's Paul critique rests mostly on Deuteronomy 18 and Galatians 2; this is a different, text-by-text line, and it is the one a seminary-trained reader will respect most.

**Source material.** `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/02-how-paul-created-christianity--his-misappropriation-of-four-hebrew-bible-texts.md`, `34-lost-in-translation-was-abrahams-faith-counted-as-righteousness.md`, `38-lost-in-translation-how-paul-misapplies-his-favorite-bible-verse.md`. The Qumran reading of Habakkuk 2:4 is in 1QpHab (Pesher Habakkuk), column 8 — check `christianity/dead-sea-scrolls/` for what we hold.

**Done looks like.** `christianity/pauls-four-texts.md`: for each text, the Hebrew-Bible context in two sentences, Paul's use with the verse cited, and the gap between them. Attribute the argument to Tabor by book and page. Link from `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` under "Go Deeper".

## 3. John the Baptist: locusts or honey cakes?

**Why it matters.** The Baptist's diet is the first thing raised against a vegetarian lineage ("he ate locusts"). The Ebionite gospel (in Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.13) reads *egkris* (cake) where Mark 1:6 reads *akris* (locust), one letter apart in Greek, and the Slavonic Josephus describes a Baptist who ate no flesh. The repo mentions locusts in four research files and prepares none of this.

**Source material.** `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/66-...md` (brief mention), Epiphanius *Panarion* 30.13.4-5 (Williams translation; not public domain, so excerpt and attribute), Mark 1:6 and Matthew 3:4.

**Done looks like.** A section on `questions/sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md` under "Questions People Raise": the objection as a critic states it, the textual variant stated exactly, and the honest limit (the Ebionite reading is a sectarian text; the point is that the tradition is *contested*, not settled either way).

## 4. Mark as a Pauline gospel

**Why it matters.** If the earliest gospel already carries Paul's theology (ransom saying, Mark 10:45; "all foods clean," Mark 7:19), then "red text" is not automatically pre-Pauline, and our own method card has to account for it. Better we say this first than a critic.

**Source material.** `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/30-is-marks-gospel-mainly-a-mouthpiece-for-pauls-ideas.md`, `45-how-pauls-ideas-shape-the-gospels-pt-1.md`, `50-does-mark-see-paul-in-his-mirror-when-it-comes-to-salvation.md`. Scholarship to cite by name: Joel Marcus, "Mark — Interpreter of Paul" (*New Testament Studies* 46, 2000).

**Done looks like.** `christianity/mark-and-paul.md`, plus a paragraph on `questions/method-and-criteria.md` saying how the criterion handles Pauline material inside the Synoptics.
