- **What to add or change:** three corrections, applied in all four places.
  1. **Wording.** Replace the quotation "I came to abolish the sacrifices. And until you cease from sacrificing, the wrath of God will not cease from you" with the held text: "I have come to do away with the sacrifices, and unless you cease from sacrificing, the wrath will not cease from you." ### P8: The arithmetic of the wilderness: a second route to Jeremiah 7:22 and Amos 5:25, from the Torah's own numbers

- **File and section:** `christianity/prophetic-tradition-against-sacrifice.md`, `## Scholarly Context: Why Two Voices in One Bible?` — a new subsection `### The Arithmetic of the Wilderness` after `### The Documentary Hypothesis`. - **What to add or change:**

  > The source-critical argument above says the sacrificial legislation is later than the prophets. There is a second argument that needs no source criticism and uses only the Torah's own figures. The Torah counts "six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty" men of fighting age (Numbers 1:46), confirms the figure by the half-shekel census (Exodus 38:26), adds "twenty and two thousand" Levites (Numbers 3:39), and says about six hundred thousand men left Egypt "beside children" (Exodus 12:37): two to three million people on any reading. It places the tabernacle "in the midst of the camp" (Numbers 2:17), requires every ox, lamb, or goat killed in or out of the camp to be brought "unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation" on pain of being cut off (Leviticus 17:3-5), requires the sin-offering bullock to be carried "forth without the camp unto a clean place" and burnt there (Leviticus 4:11-12), requires a lamb and a bird from every mother after every birth (Leviticus 12:6; two birds for the poor, 12:8), puts the latrines outside the camp (Deuteronomy 23:12-13), and restricts the altar to Aaron and his sons, "and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death" (Numbers 3:10; 18:7).
