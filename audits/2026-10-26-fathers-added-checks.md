**Three things about these editions that affect every check below**

1. **Section numbers.** McGiffert's Eusebius and the ANF volumes number sections their own way. Where the sheet's number points one section away from where the held text puts the words, that is recorded as a numbering difference, not an error in the sheet: the sheet's numbers match the Loeb / modern numbering in each case checked.
2. **Translation.** The sheet quotes modern translations (it links New Advent, which reprints these same ANF/NPNF translations, but its wording often follows other versions). Where the words differ only because the translator differs, the verdict says so.
3. **Not in these editions.** Jerome's commentaries (Matthew, Isaiah, Micah, Ezekiel, Ephesians) are not translated in NPNF and are still not held. NPNF gives Jerome's Letters 20 and 120 as summaries only. Origen's *Homilies on Jeremiah*, *On First Principles* and the Latin *Commentary on Matthew* are not held.   1. *Stromata* 5.14.96: the sheet groups it with Clement quoting the Gospel according to the Hebrews "by name". In the held text the 5.14 form ("He, who seeks, will not stop till he find…") names no source; only the 2.9 form is credited to "the Gospel to the Hebrews".
  2. Eusebius 4.22.8 (McGiffert 4.22.7): the sheet reads three sources ("from the Gospel according to the Hebrews, from the Syriac, and from the Hebrew language"); McGiffert translates one ("from the Syriac Gospel according to the Hebrews he quotes some passages in the Hebrew tongue") and his footnote 1242 explains that the Greek is ambiguous.

  4. Jerome, *Lives* 3: the sheet says Jerome copied the Hebrew Matthew from the Nazarenes of Beroea. Richardson's translation says he had "the volume described to me" by them. The Latin verb is usually rendered "to copy" in other translations, so this is a translation question to settle against the Latin, not an error found.

  The two that cannot be checked are Jerome's *Letter* 20.5 and *Letter* 120.8: NPNF prints only summaries of both letters. The sheet already marks 20.5 as a paraphrase.
- **(c) Sabbath draft (row 6d).** All five passages are now held and quoted below. The draft's descriptions agree with them. ## (a) AUDIT-LIST A8: Irenaeus 1.26.2 and Eusebius 3.27

**Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.2** (Roberts/Rambaut, ANF 1), `irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md:986`:

> 2. Those who are called Ebionites agree that the world was made by God; but their opinions with respect to the Lord are similar to those of Cerinthus and Carpocrates. They use the Gospel according to Matthew only, and repudiate the Apostle Paul, maintaining that he was an apostate from the law. As to the prophetical writings, they endeavour to expound them in a somewhat singular manner: they practise circumcision, persevere in the observance of those customs which are enjoined by the law, and are so Judaic in their style of life, that they even adore Jerusalem as if it were the house of God.

**Eusebius, *Church History* 3.27** (McGiffert, NPNF 2.1), `eusebius-church-history-full-text.md:2806-2818`, chapter heading "The Heresy of the Ebionites":

> 1. The evil demon, however, being unable to tear certain others from their allegiance to the Christ of God, yet found them susceptible in a different direction, and so brought them over to his own purposes. The ancients quite properly called these men Ebionites, because they held poor and mean opinions concerning Christ. [825]
>
> 2. For they considered him a plain and common man, who was justified only because of his superior virtue, and who was the fruit of the intercourse of a man with Mary. In their opinion the observance of the ceremonial law was altogether necessary, on the ground that they could not be saved by faith in Christ alone and by a corresponding life. [826]
>
> 3. There were others, however, besides them, that were of the same name, [827] but avoided the strange and absurd beliefs of the former, and did not deny that the Lord was born of a virgin and of the Holy Spirit. But nevertheless, inasmuch as they also refused to acknowledge that he pre-existed, [828] being God, Word, and Wisdom, they turned aside into the impiety of the former, especially when they, like them, endeavored to observe strictly the bodily worship of the law. [829]
>
> 4. These men, moreover, thought that it was necessary to reject all the epistles of the apostle, whom they called an apostate from the law; [830] and they used only the so-called Gospel according to the Hebrews [831] and made small account of the rest.
>
> 5. The Sabbath and the rest of the discipline of the Jews they observed just like them, but at the same time, like us, they celebrated the Lord's days as a memorial of the resurrection of the Saviour. [832]
>
> 6. Wherefore, in consequence of such a course they received the name of Ebionites, which signified the poverty of their understanding. For this is the name by which a poor man is called among the Hebrews. [833]

**What this means for A8.**
- "Apostate from the law" is in both texts, about the Ebionites. In Eusebius 3.27.4 the subject ("these men") follows on from 3.27.3, the second group of Ebionites who accepted the virgin birth.
- "False prophet" is not in either text in connection with Paul (grep of both files).
- Neither text names the Nazarenes.
- The two files A8 lists (`christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md:86` and `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md:43`) credit the phrase to Epiphanius and to "the Ebionites and Nazarenes". The held texts support "Ebionites, per Irenaeus 1.26.2 and Eusebius 3.27.4". A8's "What would confirm it" column (check Irenaeus 1.26.2 and Eusebius 3.27) is now done; the fix itself is for a person to agree, per the audit-list rule.

## (c) PROCESSING-ROADMAP row 6d: the Sabbath draft's five passages

Each is quoted exactly from the held file. Footnote markers are the translators'.

**1. Ignatius, *Magnesians* 9** (Roberts/Donaldson, ANF 1), `ignatius-epistles-full-text.md:642-646`. Heading: "Chapter IX.--Let us live with Christ." ANF prints both recensions; the shorter comes first.

Shorter recension (`:644`):

> If, therefore, those who were brought up in the ancient order of things [680] have come to the possession of a new [681] hope, no longer observing the Sabbath, but living in the observance [682] of the Lord's Day, on which also our life has sprung up again by Him and by His death--whom some deny, by which mystery we have obtained faith, [683] and therefore endure, that we may be found the disciples of Jesus Christ, our only Master--how shall we be able to live apart from Him, whose disciples the prophets themselves in the Spirit did wait for Him as their Teacher? And therefore He whom they rightly waited for, being come, raised them from the dead. [684]

ANF's notes: [680] "Literally, "in old things."" [681] "Or, "newness of."" [682] "Or, "according to.""

Longer recension (`:646`), for reference; this is where "keep the Sabbath after a spiritual manner" and "the queen and chief of all the days" come from:

> … Let us therefore no longer keep the Sabbath after the Jewish manner, and rejoice in days of idleness; for "he that does not work, let him not eat." [688] For say the [holy] oracles, "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat thy bread." [689] But let every one of you keep the Sabbath after a spiritual manner, rejoicing in meditation on the law, not in relaxation of the body, admiring the workmanship of God, and not eating things prepared the day before, nor using lukewarm drinks, and walking within a prescribed space, nor finding delight in dancing and plaudits which have no sense in them. [690] And after the observance of the Sabbath, let every friend of Christ keep the Lord's Day as a festival, the resurrection-day, the queen and chief of all the days [of the week]. …

The draft's description ("contrasts those who had lived in the old ways and now no longer keep the Sabbath but live according to the Lord's day") matches the shorter text. ANF's note 682 ("according to") fits the draft's "live according to". ANF's text and notes do not mention the "Lord's life" manuscript reading the draft cites; that variant still needs a Greek edition (Lightfoot) to check.

**2. Epistle of Barnabas 15** (Roberts/Donaldson, ANF 1), `epistle-of-barnabas-full-text.md:526-528`. Heading: "Chapter XV.--The false and the true Sabbath." The chapter's closing sentences:

> Further, He says to them, "Your new moons and your Sabbaths I cannot endure." [1669] Ye perceive how He speaks: Your present Sabbaths are not acceptable to Me, but that is which I have made, [namely this,] when, giving rest to all things, I shall make a beginning of the eighth day, that is, a beginning of another world. Wherefore, also, we keep the eighth day with joyfulness, the day also on which Jesus rose again from the dead. [1670] And [1671] when He had manifested Himself, He ascended into the heavens.

Earlier in the chapter, on the six days: "This implieth that the Lord will finish all things in six thousand years, for a day is [1658] with Him a thousand years." The draft's description (eschatological Sabbath; the eighth day kept with joy, the day Jesus rose) matches.

**3. Justin, *First Apology* 67** (ANF 1), `justin-martyr-first-apology-full-text.md:610-612`. Heading: "Chapter LXVII.--Weekly worship of the Christians."

> And on the day called Sunday, [1913] all who live in cities or in the country gather together to one place, and the memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets are read, as long as time permits; then, when the reader has ceased, the president verbally instructs, and exhorts to the imitation of these good things. Then we all rise together and pray, and, as we before said, when our prayer is ended, bread and wine and water are brought, and the president in like manner offers prayers and thanksgivings, according to his ability, [1914] and the people assent, saying Amen; and there is a distribution to each, and a participation of that over which thanks have been given, [1915] and to those who are absent a portion is sent by the deacons. … But Sunday is the day on which we all hold our common assembly, because it is the first day on which God, having wrought a change in the darkness and matter, made the world; and Jesus Christ our Saviour on the same day rose from the dead. For He was crucified on the day before that of Saturn (Saturday); and on the day after that of Saturn, which is the day of the Sun, having appeared to His apostles and disciples, He taught them these things, which we have submitted to you also for your consideration.

The draft's description (gathering on the day of the Sun; apostles and prophets read; prayer and Eucharist; creation and resurrection as the reasons) matches. Nothing in the chapter calls the day a day of rest, as the draft says.

The draft also cites *Dialogue* 47 (`justin-martyr-dialogue-with-trypho-full-text.md:678`, heading "Justin communicates with Christians who observe the law. Not a few Catholics do otherwise."). Justin's answer begins: "In my opinion, Trypho, such an one will be saved, if he does not strive in every way to persuade other men, --I mean those Gentiles who have been circumcised from error by Christ, to observe the same things as himself". That supports the draft's summary.

**5. Council of Laodicea, canon 29** (Percival, NPNF 2.14), `council-of-laodicea-canons-full-text.md:816-818`:

> Christians must not judaize by resting on the Sabbath, but must work on that day, rather honouring the Lord's Day; and, if they can, resting then as Christians. But if any shall be found to be judaizers, let them be anathema from Christ.

Percival's notes that follow (not the canon): the Ancient Epitome, "A Christian shall not stop work on the Sabbath, but on the Lord's Day"; and Balsamon, who says the canon adds "if they can" so that working on Sunday "through need or any other necessity … was not reckoned against him". The draft's paraphrase (line 133) matches the canon.

The draft also cites canons 16 and 49 (line 133):
- Canon 16 (`:346`): "The Gospels are to be read on the Sabbath [i.e. Saturday], with the other Scriptures." (The bracket is Percival's.)
- Canon 49 (`:1122`): "During Lent the Bread must not be offered except on the Sabbath Day and on the Lord's Day only."

Both support the draft's statement that the same council still treats the Sabbath as a liturgical day. Percival dates the synod "between the years 343 and 381" (following Hefele); the draft's "usually dated c. 363-364 (estimates run from the 340s to the 380s)" is consistent with that.
