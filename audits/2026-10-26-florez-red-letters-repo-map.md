## 0. In one paragraph

The sheet and the repo agree on most of the textual-criticism ground the repo already uses: Mark 16, the Comma, the pericope adulterae, Matthew 26:28's added clause, Mark 7:19 as Mark's aside, Matthew 24:36, Didache 9-10, John 6, Thomas 14. The sheet goes much further in Part A (35 scribal entries; the repo uses about five) and on the christological variants (Luke 3:22, Mark 1:1, John 1:18, Luke 2:33), which the repo does not treat at all. Its Part B puts several red-text verses the repo leans on into "composed by the evangelist" (Matthew 28:19-20, Luke 24:44-49, Luke 22:35-38) or CONTESTED (Matthew 5:17-19, Mark 10:45). The main disagreement is Part F. The sheet adopts Edwards's one-book view of the Hebrew Gospel, so it files the Ebionite quotations in Panarion 30 (the sacrifice saying, the Passover saying, the honey-cake) under "the Hebrew Gospel." The repo's CITATION-NOTES and FIX-LIST deliberately stopped doing that. The sheet's own wording of those passages agrees with the held Panarion text, so it is also independent support for AUDIT-LIST A1 and A2.

**1. Trinity card, the Matthew 28:19 answer.** `questions/trinity-deity-of-jesus.md`, heading "The Trinity is in Scripture even if the word isn't — Matthew 28:19 and 1 John 5:7" (111-114), and §5 (48). The card answers 28:19 only with "names three, not homoousios." ## 3. CONFLICTS (audit candidates; nothing changed)

Each item quotes both sides. "Held" means a Tier 1/2 text in this repo that bears on it.

**C1. One Hebrew Gospel or three texts (Edwards's thesis).**
- Sheet F1 (515): "The fathers call it by three names: Matthew in Hebrew, the Gospel according to the Hebrews, and the gospel of the Ebionites... This sheet follows James R. Edwards... [who] argues that the three names refer to one book." F6 (938) tiers the thesis CONTESTED and names Andrew Gregory's reverse view; F3, F7 and F8 then use "the Hebrew Gospel" for the Panarion 30 quotations without a tier, e.g. F8(1) (980): "The Hebrew Gospel came first, and the fathers quote it about 75 times."
- Repo `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` "The Three Related Texts" (60): "Scholarship identifies three overlapping but distinct texts in the Hebrew Matthew tradition"; (86): "Whether they represent one text, two texts, or three distinct documents remains debated."
- Repo `christianity/biblical-case-for-vegetarian-jesus.md` (74): "The source is the **Gospel of the Ebionites**, not 'the Hebrew Gospel of Matthew.' The two are often conflated in popular treatments, but Epiphanius (*Panarion* 30) and Jerome describe distinct documents."
- Held: Pan. 30,3,7c (Wescoe): "And they call it the Gospel according to the Hebrews, as it is true to say, since Matthew alone in the New Testament set forth and proclaimed the gospel in Hebrew and in Hebrew letters"; 30,13,2a: "named 'according to Matthew' — not whole and entirely complete, but falsified and mutilated (and they call it Hebrew)." These support the sheet on what Epiphanius says the Ebionites called their book, and bear on biblical-case:74's "Epiphanius... describe[s] distinct documents." They do not settle Edwards's one-book thesis.
- To confirm: read Pan. 30,3,7 and 30,13,2 in full; decide how the repo labels Panarion 30 quotations (current practice is "Gospel of the Ebionites," per `VERIFIED-SOURCES.md`, Not-held table).

**C2. The Passover saying (Panarion 30.22.4).**
- Sheet F3.3 (592-596): "'Have I desired with desire to eat flesh with you this Passover?' ... There it is a question expecting the answer no, with 'flesh' as its object: Yeshua declines the Passover meat... This is the standing Edwards case." F7 (973): "The doubled Hebrew idiom shows they are one saying, and the Hebrew Gospel's form carries the Semitic grammar."
- Repo `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` (72): "It's from the **Gospel of the Ebionites** via Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.22.4 — who presents it as *their alteration*. The Greek is a wordplay inversion of Luke 22:15, marking it secondary... Evidence of what the Ebionites believed, not of what Jesus said." Same position in `biblical-case-for-vegetarian-jesus.md` (74-78) and `questions/sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md` (71).
- Held: Pan. 30,22,4 (Wescoe) is a statement, not a question: "I have not longed with longing to eat meat this Passover with you"; 30,22,5b: "instead of saying 'With longing I have longed', they added the adverb 'not'"; 30,22,5c: "by writing in 'meat', have led themselves astray." The sheet does not mention Epiphanius's alteration charge. On wording, the sheet's "flesh" (not "lamb") agrees with the held text and with AUDIT-LIST A2.
- Note: the doubled idiom is also in Luke 22:15 (held KJV, "With desire I have desired"), so it is common to both forms.

**C3. "I came to abolish sacrifices" (Panarion 30.16.5): wording and label.**
- Sheet F3.22 (791): "'I came to abolish sacrifices,' and 'if ye cease not from sacrificing, the wrath will not cease from you'" (no "of God"). Labelled Hebrew Gospel; "ESTABLISHED as text" (797).
- Repo `hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` (5) and (32-34): "the wrath of God will not cease from you" ... "quoting the Ebionite Gospel (Hebrew Matthew tradition)"; `prophetic-tradition-against-sacrifice.md` (88): the same wording, under "The Ebionite/Nazarene version of Matthew."
- Held: Pan. 30,16,5a: "I have come to do away with the sacrifices, and unless you cease from sacrificing, the wrath will not cease from you." This is AUDIT-LIST A1 (Evidence in hand); the sheet's wording is independent support. The label question is FIX-LIST "Retractions that didn't finish" (91) and C1.
