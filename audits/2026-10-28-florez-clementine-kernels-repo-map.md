## 0. In one paragraph

The repo already leans on the Clementines in four cards: the Epistle of Peter's "the man who is my enemy" and Homilies 17.19 on the Paul card, Recognitions 1.37 and Homilies 3.45 and 12.6 on the sacrifice card, Recognitions 1.36 on the Torah card, and the false-pericopes doctrine (Homilies 2.38, 3.48-51) on the method card. What the repo lacks is the thing this sheet supplies: an account of *which layer* of these fourth-century books is old, and how old. Our cards say "3rd century, from earlier Jewish-Christian material" or "3rd-4th century"; one research file says the books themselves are "c. 2nd century" and cites Eisenman for a first-century kernel. The sheet puts the books in the fourth century, the lost base novel in about 220-250, and the two kernels (the Peter letters and the Recognitions 1.27-71 history) in about 150-200, and marks which of those steps are disputed. Its biggest gift to the cards is a passage none of them uses: Recognitions 1.66-71, where James argues seven days on the Temple steps, "that enemy" throws him down, and the next chapter gives the enemy a commission "from Caiaphas... to Damascus". That is the strongest held text for the Paul card's section 5, and it is in a file we hold. Almost every content claim checks out against ANF; the few that don't are wording ("hostile man", "fortified place") taken from other translations, and one wrong section number.

## 2. ADDITIONS (what the sheet has that the repo lacks)

1. **The layer model and its dates.** Books (4th century) / Basic Writing, the *Circuits of Peter* (c. 220-250) / kernels (c. 150-200), with a "settled and disputed" table and a "calibrated statement" (line 97). No repo file states this. It gives the cards a defensible answer to "the Clementines are fourth-century fiction": the anti-Paul material is older than the novel, and Recognitions 1.27-71 is "a source dated c. 150-200 embedded in a fourth-century text". Modern dating is Not held (C257); state it as scholarship.
2. **Recognitions 1.66-71 as the James-and-Paul scene.** James on the Temple steps for seven days, the people and the high priest ready for baptism, "that enemy" throwing him "headlong from the top of the steps", and "a commission from Caiaphas... to Damascus" (C245-C247). No card quotes it. The Paul card's §5 currently rests on the Epistle of Peter and Homilies 17.19, both of which leave the opponent's identity to inference; 1.71 makes it nearly explicit, and the ANF editor himself says so (note 597).
3. **Epiphanius's *Ascents of James* (Panarion 30.16.7).** James "declaring against the temple and the sacrifices, and against the fire upon the altar" (C251). Not on the sacrifice card, which uses Panarion 18 (Nasaraeans; AUDIT-LIST A3) and 30.16.5 in research files. 30.16.7 is about the Ebionites and is in a held text.
4. **"One limit on 'early'"** (line 99): the Ascents source already knows Acts, so its value is as "the earliest surviving counter-history to Acts from the James side", not as pre-New Testament evidence. That is exactly the narrow claim CITATION-NOTES' standing rule asks for.
5. **The Kerygmata cautions** (lines 85, 95-97): the Peter material is real but a weaker case for one separate document; Strecker admits no outside evidence. Useful against the stronger claims in `christianity/clementine-literature-nazarene-writings.md` (C1 below).
6. **The external dating anchors**: Bardesanes in Recognitions 9.17, 9.19-29 (held, C249) and Origen's quotation of the Recognitions about 231 (reported in ANF's introduction; the Origen text is not held, C256).
7. **The Hegesippus parallel**, with the honest note that current scholarship sees a shared tradition rather than copying (line 69). Our held texts show both the parallels and the differences (C254).

## 5. Top additions for the cards (at most 5)

1. **`questions/pauls-legitimacy.md`, §5 "The Jewish-Christian tradition remembers him as the opponent"** (after line 43), and a new `sources` entry in `site/data/cards/pauls-legitimacy.json`.
   Quote, from `christianity/Incoming/clementine-recognitions-full-text.md` (Thomas Smith, ANF 8), Recognitions 1.70-71:
   > "that enemy attacked James, and threw him headlong from the top of the steps; and supposing him to be dead, he cared not to inflict further violence upon him."
   > "that enemy had received a commission from Caiaphas, the chief priest, that he should arrest all who believed in Jesus, and should go to Damascus with his letters"
   *Why:* it is the one held passage where the Jewish-Christian tradition all but names Paul (Caiaphas and Damascus, cf. Acts 9:1-2, 22:5), and the ANF editor concedes "an evident attempt to cast a slur upon the apostle Paul". Say "the text never names him" and give the sheet's date range for the source (c. 150-200, scholarship, contested). Claims C246, C247.

2. **`questions/pauls-legitimacy.md`, objection "The Clementines are 3rd-4th century anti-Pauline fiction"** (81-82).
   Add one sentence from the sheet's calibrated statement (line 97, reference level): the books are fourth-century, but Recognitions 1.27-71 is widely treated as an embedded source of about 150-200, and Epiphanius (c. 375) saw an Ebionite book called the Ascents of James. Quote from `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe), Panarion 30.16.7:
   > "And they put forward certain Ascents and instructions, supposedly, in the Ascents of James, as though he were declaring against the temple and the sacrifices, and against the fire upon the altar"
   *Why:* it answers "fiction" with an independent fourth-century witness that a James book against the sacrifices circulated on its own, without overclaiming; the identity of that book with Recognitions 1 is "probable, not certain" (sheet line 97). Claim C251.

3. **`questions/sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md`, the "3rd century, from earlier Jewish-Christian material" bullet** (94), and `site/data/cards/sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.json` (alongside the Recognitions 1.37 source).

Pair it with Homilies 11.35 (C232), where "credentials" is only a conjectural reading. Claim C233.

## 6. What the sheet does to the repo's Clementine story

- **It moves the argument from "the Clementines are early" to "parts of them are older than the rest, and here is which parts".** That is a narrower claim than `clementine-literature-nazarene-writings.md` makes, and a stronger one in a room: it concedes the fourth-century date the critic will raise, and still keeps a second-century James-side counter-history to Acts.
- **It points the Paul card at the right passage.** The card currently argues from Homilies 17.19, where Simon stands in for Paul by inference. Recognitions 1.70-71 gives Caiaphas and Damascus. Use both, with ANF's wording.
- **What stays reference-level:** every date (Strecker, Jones, Van Voorst), the identity of Recognitions 1.27-71 with Epiphanius's Ascents, Lightfoot on Hegesippus, Origen's *Philocalia*, and the Syriac version. What is now checkable: every chapter the sheet cites in the Homilies, Recognitions, Panarion 30.15-16 and Eusebius 2.23 (C230-C255).
