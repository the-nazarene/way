## 0. In one paragraph

The repo's one file on this subject, `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md`, and the sheet agree that every father who speaks to it says Matthew wrote first in Hebrew, and that Epiphanius preserves the Ebionites' anti-sacrifice and Passover sayings. They part on three things. The file says "three overlapping but distinct texts"; the sheet argues, after Edwards, for one book under several names, which is AUDIT-LIST A20 and is still contested (Gregory, whom the sheet itself calls "the standard current edition", argues for two). The file quotes the sacrifice saying with "of God" and the Passover saying with "lamb"; the sheet quotes both without, as the held Panarion has them (A1, A2). And the file treats the Hebrew original as the text "the Greek version removed"; the sheet says plainly that Greek Matthew is not a translation of it (Part J, 348). Beyond that, the sheet is mostly new ground for the repo: a names table, about twenty sayings with their fathers and loci, the Zion-margin readings, Justin's fire in the Jordan, the Tiberias treasury, and Edwards's case that Luke used the Hebrew Gospel. Most of its witness statements can be checked against held texts and match. The ones that can't are Jerome's commentaries, which carry half the sayings and the sentence the sheet calls its most important (*Commentary on Matthew* 12.13). One reading needs care before it reaches a card: the sheet's Passover argument against what the held Epiphanius says (citation check, point 1).

## 3. CONFLICTS (audit candidates; nothing changed)

Each item quotes both sides. "Held" means a Tier 1/2 text in this repo that bears on it.

**C1. One book or three (already AUDIT-LIST A20), with the sheet's new support and the held commentary against it.**
- Repo `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` (60): "Scholarship identifies three overlapping but distinct texts in the Hebrew Matthew tradition"; (80) the Gospel of the Hebrews is "a separate text, possibly independent of the Matthew tradition"; (86) "Whether they represent one text, two texts, or three distinct documents remains debated."
- Sheet C2 (117): "no father, friend or enemy, ever says there were two or three different Gospels in Hebrew." C4 (125): Jerome says "the Gospel which the Nazarenes and the Ebionites use."
- Held: Panarion 30.3.7c (the Ebionites "call it the Gospel according to the Hebrews"), 30.13.2a ("named 'according to Matthew'... and they call it Hebrew"); Jerome, *Against the Pelagians* 3.2 ("the Gospel according to the Apostles, or, as is generally maintained, the Gospel according to Matthew"). Against the sheet's "every witness": Panarion 46.1.9 says some call Tatian's Diatessaron "the Gospel according to the Hebrews" (C50). McGiffert's note 831 (held commentary, 1890) reads Epiphanius and Eusebius as distinguishing two books.
- To confirm: as A20 says, Edwards and Gregory (not held), and Jerome *Comm. Matt.* 12.13 (not held). The file's "debated" is accurate; its heading "The Three Related Texts" states one side.

**C2. Jerome, in our file, says more than the held Jerome.**
- Repo `hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` (21): "first of all the evangelists composed a Gospel of Christ in Judea in the Hebrew language and characters... He further states he personally saw a copy 'in the library at Caesarea which the martyr Pamphilus so diligently collected.'" (82): "Jerome (*De Viris Illustribus* 2) reports it was used by 'the Nazarenes' and that he translated it." (66): "Jerome reports consulting this text at the library in Caesarea and translating portions."
- Sheet A7 (79): "composed a Gospel of Christ, at first published in Judea in Hebrew... preserved until the present day in the library at Caesarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered."
- Held: Richardson, *Lives* 3: "composed a gospel of Christ at first published in Judea in Hebrew... The Hebrew itself has been preserved until the present day in the library at Cæsarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered." *Lives* 2 says of the Gospel according to the Hebrews "which I have recently translated into Greek and Latin and which also Origen often makes use of"; it doesn't mention the Nazarenes. The sheet's wording is the held wording; the file's is not.
- To confirm: whether the file's row 21 wording comes from the *Commentary on Matthew* preface (not held). Line 82's "Nazarenes" belongs to *Lives* 3 or *Against the Pelagians* 3.2.

**C3. The Passover saying: Epiphanius's alteration, or the harder reading?**
- Repo `questions/sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md` (71): "Epiphanius introduces it as the Ebionites' *alteration* of the text... The Greek is a negated wordplay on Luke 22:15... and that inversion marks it as secondary to Luke, not independent of it. So it is not evidence of what Jesus said." Site card: "It is Epiphanius quoting the Ebionites' alteration of Luke 22:15 (Panarion 30.22.4)." `CITATION-NOTES.md`: "Evidence of what the Ebionites believed, not of what Jesus said."
- Sheet D7 (183): "When two forms of a saying exist and one is harder for later believers to accept, textual critics generally hold that the harder one is likelier to be original"; Part H (302): "the harder reading points to Luke softening the Hebrew Gospel, not the reverse."
- Held: Panarion 30.22.4-5 (Wescoe): "I have not longed with longing to eat meat this Passover with you"; "instead of saying 'With longing I have longed', they added the adverb 'not'"; "it is clearly shown that he both kept the Passover and ate meat". Luke 22:15 has the same doubled idiom (KJV "With desire I have desired"; W-H επιθυμια επεθυμησα), so the idiom doesn't decide which form came first. Luke 22:16 W-H "ου μη φαγω αυτο"; KJV "I will not any more eat thereof" (C177, C185).
- The card's position matches what the held Epiphanius says. The sheet's is an argument against Epiphanius, made by Edwards (not held). Two smaller conflicts inside this one: the card (71) prints the saying as a question, which Wescoe doesn't; the file (50) and OBJECTIONS:21 print "Passover lamb" (A2).
- To confirm: Holl's Greek at 30.22.4 (the *mē* as question or negation), and whether the card should present the *lectio difficilior* case as the other side. The card's framing should stand until then.

**4. The community at the "forwarding address" kept a Hebrew Matthew.**
- *Card.* `questions/essene-nazarene-origins.md` §5 "The line of succession runs through Jesus's own family, and it has a forwarding address" (44-57), after the Panarion 29.7 quotation.

**5. The appearance to James.**
- *Card.* `questions/resurrection.md` §3 "The earliest account is a creed older than Paul's letter" (25-31), which quotes 1 Corinthians 15:5 and 15:8 and skips 15:7.
- *Quote.* `christianity/Incoming/kjv/46-1-corinthians.md` 15:7: "After that, he was seen of James; then of all the apostles." `christianity/Incoming/jerome-lives-of-illustrious-men-full-text.md` ch. 2, from the Gospel according to the Hebrews: "but the Lord, after he had given his grave clothes to the servant of the priest, appeared to James (for James had sworn that he would not eat bread from that hour in which he drank the cup of the Lord until he should see him rising again from among those that sleep)".
- *Why.* The creed lists an appearance to James and narrates none; the Hebrew Gospel is the only text that narrates it. It also ties the resurrection card to the James succession on the origins card. Date it as Jerome's quotation (c. 392) of a lost gospel, and say James's presence at the Last Supper ("he drank the cup of the Lord") is this gospel's, not the canonical account.

**6. A second witness for Thomas 2.**
- *Card.* `questions/the-gospel-of-thomas.md` §4 "The use that's actually bulletproof: multiple attestation" (38-46).
- *Quote.* `christianity/Incoming/clement-of-alexandria-stromata-full-text.md` 2.9: "So also in the Gospel to the Hebrews it is written, 'He that wonders shall reign, and he that has reigned shall rest.'" When he becomes troubled, he will be astonished, and he will rule over the All."
- *Why.* §4's examples pair Thomas with a Synoptic. This one pairs Thomas with a second non-canonical gospel, quoted by name about 200. It shows the saying circulating outside Thomas. Keep C57's caveat: the Coptic has no "rest" (that step is in the Greek P.Oxy 654, not held), and the 5.14 form in Clement names no source (C37).

- **It is more careful with the Panarion than our own file.** Its D3, D6 and D7 quote the held wording (all three voices, no "of God", "flesh" not "lamb"). Porting its quotations would close A1 and A2 in `hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md`.
- **It argues one side of A20 and says so.** The sheet names Gregory and Kok as the other side and calls the three-book view "the majority view in modern handbooks" (332). The repo file currently states the three-book view as a heading. Neither should be a card's premise until A20 is decided.
- **Half its sayings rest on Jerome's commentaries, which we don't hold.** Adding Jerome's *Commentary on Matthew* (the modern English translations we know of are in copyright, so this may have to be the Latin, or short excerpts) is the single addition that would let the repo check Part C's central sentence and five of the sayings. Origen's *Commentary on John* II §6, Clement *Stromata* 2.9, Jerome *Lives* 2-3 and *Against the Pelagians* 3.2, and Panarion 29-30 are already held, and they carry the strongest Top additions above.
- **The Passover argument is the one place the sheet and the held text pull in different directions** (C3). The card's current framing agrees with the held Epiphanius; the sheet's case is Edwards's, not held. Present both, in the held words.
