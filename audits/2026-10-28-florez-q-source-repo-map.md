## 0. In one paragraph

The repo uses Q constantly and never defends it. The Torah card and the method card build on Q 16:17 ("in Q, upstream of both evangelists"); the Thomas card says Thomas "retired" the genre objection to Q; the John card calls Q material part of "the red-text spine"; the Two Ways and atonement cards measure Matthew 11:30 against "the shared Q block". The only Q file, `christianity/Incoming/q-source-reconstruction.md`, is a Tier 3 triangulation with KJV parallels, and its note states the Two Source Hypothesis as fact ("they must share a common written source"). This sheet is the missing defense. It gives the evidence (verbatim Greek, variable agreement, independence, order, doublets, alternating primitivity, Thomas, Papias), says plainly that Q is a hypothesis held by a majority and not a consensus, treats the Farrer theory as a serious minority view, and then makes the move that protects every card: the argument rests on the sayings, which are in Luke and Matthew whether or not Q existed. The parts we can check against held texts mostly match, and the headline Greek count is exact (60 of 63 words in the Baptist's sermon). Four textual details need narrowing, the most important being that "sent to Israel only" is Matthew's own material, not Q. The sheet is more careful than our own Q file in three places.

## 2. ADDITIONS (what the sheet has that the repo lacks)

1. **The case for Q itself (Part B).** No repo file explains why scholars posit Q. The sheet's six lines of evidence, each with texts a reader can open, are new, and the Greek parts check out against W-H (C259-C261, C264-C267).
2. **The Farrer fallback (Part D, last answer; E3).** "The sayings do not vanish... What is lost is the claim that they predate Mark; everything else stays." This is the one sentence every card that says "in Q" needs, and no card has it.
3. **The objections list (Part D)** with honest concessions: the minor agreements "should be conceded as a real difficulty" (Mark 14:65 against Matthew 26:68 and Luke 22:64, confirmed in W-H, C267); "Q has no passion because it was a supplement" answered without claiming Q's compilers denied the crucifixion.
4. **What Q lacks (A8, C2)**, stated as an absence anyone can check and kept apart from inferences about why. Our reconstruction's 52 units bear it out. The one passage a critic will raise is Matthew 12:40 (Jonah's "three days and three nights"), which is Matthew's own addition; Luke 11:30 has no such line (KJV).
5. **James and Q (C5):** six parallels in the Epistle of James, all present in the held KJV (C274), with the right caution (substance, not wording; Tier 3 for dependence). Two Gospel sides are not double tradition (Matthew 5:34-37; Luke 6:24), so the list needs that note (C273).
6. **Who holds Q (B9)**, for the "liberal invention" objection: Fitzmyer, Tuckett, Kloppenborg, evangelical introductions. Not held; reference level.
7. **The doublets and alternating primitivity (B5-B6)**, with held examples: Luke 8:18 / 19:26 (C272), Matthew 7:11 / Luke 11:13 (C266), the Beatitudes (C265).

## 5. Top additions for the cards (at most 5)

1. **`questions/method-and-criteria.md`, §2 "The criterion costs us things"** (after line 25), and the note on the Matthew 5:18 source in `site/data/cards/method-and-criteria.json`.
   One sentence, no quotation: *Q is a hypothesis held by most New Testament scholars since the 1860s, not a recovered book; and the argument doesn't depend on it, because Luke 16:17 is in the canon either way, and on the minority Farrer view the saying is simply Matthew's, copied by Luke (Christian Florez, `extended-library/polemic-answers-q-source-florez.md`, Part D).*
   *Why:* the card's "attested independently... upstream of either editor" assumes the Two Source Hypothesis; this keeps the point and closes the gap a well-read critic will use (C4).

2. **`questions/jesus-and-the-torah.md`, §2 "The ranking criterion, stated out loud, with both halves kept"** (after the Matthew 23:23 quotation, line 22), and a `for` source in `site/data/cards/jesus-and-the-torah.json`.
   Quote, from `christianity/Incoming/kjv/42-luke.md` (KJV), Luke 11:42:
   > "for ye tithe mint and rue and all manner of herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone."
   *Why:* the card tells readers to "quote the last clause every time", and the last clause is double tradition (Q 11:42), in Luke word for word. That makes it more than a Matthew-only Torah saying, which is the objection A21 anticipates. Claim C269.

3. **`questions/the-gospel-of-thomas.md`, §3, "The Q overlap" bullet** (line 34).
   Quote, from `christianity/Incoming/greek-nt-westcott-hort/03-luke.md` (Westcott-Hort 1881), Luke 16:13:
   > "ουδεις οικετης δυναται δυσιν κυριοις δουλευειν η γαρ τον ενα μισησει και τον ετερον αγαπησει η ενος ανθεξεται και του ετερου καταφρονησει ου δυνασθε θεω δουλευειν και μαμωνα"
   with one line: Matthew 6:24 has the same 27 Greek words in the same order, without οικετης ("servant"); in the Baptist's sermon 60 of Matthew's 63 words stand in Luke in the same order.
   *Why:* the bullet leans on Q to make Thomas's genre point but never says why Q is thought to exist. A one-verse demonstration in held Greek is the shortest answer to "Q is imaginary". Claims C263, C259.

4. **`questions/two-ways-kingdom-within.md`, objection "Multiple attestation when it suits you, single-source Matthew when it doesn't"** (58-69), as a new bullet; the "narrow gate" is named in the card's Position (3) with no citation.
   Quote, from `christianity/Incoming/kjv/42-luke.md` (KJV), Luke 13:24:
   > "Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able."
   *Why:* the narrow gate is the card's headline image of the Two Ways, and it isn't single-source Matthew (7:13-14): Luke has it too, which puts it in the double tradition (Q 13:24 in our reconstruction). It answers the card's own objection with a held verse. (The sheet lists Q 13:24-29 in A7.)

5. **`questions/blood-atonement-and-salvation.md`, objection "You're reducing the cross to nothing"** (152-153), as one added sentence.
   Quote, from `christianity/Incoming/kjv/42-luke.md` (KJV), Luke 14:27:
   > "And whosoever doth not bear his cross, and come after me, cannot be my disciple."
   with the sheet's point (A8, C2), kept to what the text shows: the sayings Matthew and Luke share beyond Mark contain no sentence calling his death a sacrifice, a ransom or a payment; their one cross saying is about the follower's cross. Add the sheet's own caution, that this does not mean the compilers denied the crucifixion.
   *Why:* it gives the card's relocation of the cross ("what empire and priesthood do to a man") an early-source anchor that costs nothing to check, and Thomas 55 carries the same saying (the Thomas card already concedes it). Claim C281.

## 6. OPEN-RESEARCH and what is still needed

- **A held Q edition.** We hold no IQP text. Harnack's *The Sayings of Jesus* (1908) is public domain and is already named as a source of our reconstruction; adding it would let the reconstruction's wording be checked against a Q text, not only against Matthew and Luke.
- **A synopsis count.** The sheet's "ninety percent of Mark in Matthew, half in Luke" and the "ten to one hundred percent" variable agreement could be computed from held W-H with a verse-level synopsis. Our samples (C259-C264) already span 100% (Matthew 6:24) to about 43% (the Lord's Prayer).
- **Streeter (1924)** is public domain and is the sheet's main classic source; worth adding if the case for Q is going on a card.

## 7. What the sheet does to the repo's use of Q

- **It gives the repo's most-used reconstruction a defense, and a fallback.** Several cards say "in Q" as if it settled independence. The sheet's own position is narrower and stronger: Q is the majority hypothesis, and the sayings stand without it.
- **It is more careful than our Q file** on three points (C1-C3): Thomas's share, the "must share a source" sentence, and "consensus".
- **It needs four textual narrowings before card use:** ask-seek-knock is verbatim only at the start (C262); Israel-only is Matthew (C270); Matthew 16:27 is not an "ashamed" doublet (C271); Luke 6:24 is not double tradition (C273). And a KJV reader won't see the short Luke prayer (C264).
