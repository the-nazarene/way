## Summary

**Counts.** 68 new `claims/` entries (C99-C166): **40 Matches, 3 Matches (other translation), 1 Matches (other section number), 10 Reads differently, 14 Not held.** The existing entries C1, C6, C46 and C77 now also list this sheet under **Raised by**. About 45 more verse checks (KJV wording, routine W-H readings) are in the tables below and not repeated in `claims/`.

**The church-father quotations are accurate.** Every block quotation in B6b (Irenaeus 3.21.1, 5.1.3; Justin *Dialogue* 48, 49) is word for word in the held ANF text, and Irenaeus 1.26.2, Eusebius 3.27, Panarion 29.7.6, Justin 56 and 61, and the four Ignatius "our God" passages all say what the sheet says.

**Points where our held texts read differently:**
1. **The baptism voice in Origen and Justin (B3, Full comment, Short comment).** Origen, *Commentary on John* Book I (Menzies §32), quotes "Thou art My Son, this day have I begotten Thee" as Psalm 2:7, about the eternal begetting, with no reference to the baptism or to Luke (C119). Justin (*Dialogue* 88, 103) gives the voice in the Psalm 2:7 form, as the sheet says, but names no gospel: in 88 he calls the words David's (C109). So "Justin, Clement and Origen" quoting it "as the text of Luke" holds for Justin's wording only; Origen is not a witness in the held books, and Clement's *Paedagogus* is not held.
2. **Irenaeus 3.21.2 (B6b).** "His answer is not the Hebrew. It is the legend" of the seventy elders. The legend is 3.21.2-3, but 3.21.4-10 go on to argue from Isaiah 7, Daniel's stone, Jechoniah and Adam (C101). And in 5.1.3, "natural" in "the old leaven of [the natural] birth" is the translators' supplied word, so "the words are his" holds for "old leaven", not for "natural" (C103).
3. **Genesis 24:16 (B6b).** The verse calls Rebekah "damsel" and "virgin" (*na'arah*, *betulah*). *Almah* is 24:43 ("the maiden"). The argument works as 24:16 with 24:43 (C139).
4. **Isaiah 48:16 (C15).** The verse doesn't open with "and now": it opens "Come ye near unto Me", still in God's voice, and "and now" is its last clause. JPS 1917 reads like the KJV and marks no change of speaker (C147).
5. **John 19:37 (C34).** John's Greek has no "him": οψονται εις ον εξεκεντησαν, "on [the one] whom they pierced" (C132). "It is John who says 'him'" is the English.
6. **Matthew 19:17 (A2, Full comment).** "Watch Matthew change it... laying the two verses side by side" works only in the critical text. The KJV prints Matthew 19:17 in Mark's words (C152).
7. **Micah 5:2 (C35).** "Rests on one English word in the KJV tradition": Brenton's Septuagint also reads "from eternity" (C145). The Hebrew-idiom argument stands; the KJV framing goes too far.
8. **Hebrews and Origen (C7).** Origen's "who wrote the epistle, in truth, God knows" is right (6.25.14), but in the same passage he gives the thoughts to Paul and commends churches that hold it is Paul's (C117). "It is not by Paul" is not Origen's view.
9. **Panarion 30.16.4 (B6).** The archangel is 30.16.4; Adam, the patriarchs and putting on Jesus are 30.3.5-6, and Epiphanius says their accounts vary (C124).
10. Smaller: Titus 2:13's Greek is given in the Textus Receptus order "Jesus Christ"; W-H has "Christ Jesus" (C137). Eusebius 3.27 doesn't name Joseph (C116).

**Not held, and worth checking before use:** Tertullian's "there was a time when the Son was not" is usually cited from *Against Hermogenes* 3, not *Against Praxeas* (C158). "Proposed by Hosius" is an inference from indirect sources (C163). "After Nicaea he quotes it with the formula" is Conybeare's claim about works we don't hold (C156). Habakkuk 1:12 "thou diest not" is a reconstruction, and every held witness reads "we shall not die" (C143). The *adoni*/*Adonai* distinction at Psalm 110:1 rests on Masoretic vowel points (C141). Detail in section 5.

**Internal consistency (section 6):** C18 lists four "probable" verses for Brown, C31 and C41 say five; C40's "the ASV writes 'worshipped' when the object is Yeshua and 'bowed' elsewhere" contradicts A9 (Matthew 18:26, Revelation 3:9); the Zechariah 12:10 entry quotes JPS 1917 and then calls the 1985 wording "the JPS"; D3 includes Luke 22:43-44 as running "both ways" while the short comment says every move goes one direction.

**Held texts the sheet could use** (section 7): Origen, *Commentary on John* II §2, makes the sheet's John 1:1 article argument (C121); Epiphanius, *Panarion* 69.72.8, concedes *homoousion* "were not written" (C125); Panarion 30.16.1b supports the water-only Eucharist (C102). One that cuts the other way: in all five Ignatius passages the fourth-century longer recension **lowers** the wording ("our God" becomes "our Lord... our Saviour"; "blood of God" becomes "blood of Christ") (C110-C114).

**Our own files:** `questions/trinity-deity-of-jesus.md:25` says Matthew **and Luke** soften Mark 10:18; Luke 18:19 has Mark's words (C135). Section 9 has this and three more.

## 6. Internal consistency inside the sheet

1. **Brown's "probable" list.** C18 (355): "a few more probably do (Titus 2.13, 2 Peter 1.1, 1 John 5.20, Romans 9.5 if punctuated one way)": four. C31 (629): "among five 'probable' cases". C41 (855): "among five 'probable' cases". The usual citation of Brown's fifth is John 1:18 (not held; check the article). Also C18 says Brown went through every verse "and arrived at three verses"; C38 (781) quotes Brown accepting "church of God" at Acts 20:28 while doubting it calls Jesus God, which fits.
2. **"The ASV writes 'worshipped' when the object is Yeshua and 'bowed' elsewhere" (C40, 823).** A9 (129) quotes the servant who "fell down and worshipped" the king (Matthew 18:26), and C40 itself (827) quotes "worship before thy feet" (Revelation 3:9). Both are "worshipped/worship" with a non-divine object (KJV 40-matthew.md:677, 66-revelation.md:81 read the same). The first sentence of C40's paragraph contradicts its own examples.
3. **"The JPS" at Zechariah 12:10.** C34 (695) correctly gives the 1917 JPS ("look unto Me because they have thrust him through") and the 1985 JPS ("lament to Me about those who are slain"). The comment version (881) says "the JPS reads it 'lament to Me about those who are slain'", and the box (707) "the JPS reads 'to me, concerning those slain'". The held 1917 JPS says neither. Name the 1985 *Tanakh* when quoting it.
4. **"Every one of those moves goes the same direction" (Short comment, 1003; D3 heading "The scribes kept upgrading the text").** D3's own last bullet (937) says Luke 22:43-44 was "likely added to counter the view that Yeshua was not truly human, which shows the fight ran both ways". And C1 (251) says of John 1:18 "Either a scribe raised Son to God or lowered God to Son", while D3 (931) lists it under "upgrading". The short comment's four examples do run one way; the D3 heading and the "every one" sentence should be scoped to them. (The Ignatius recensions, section 1e, are a held example of a later editor lowering the wording.)
5. **The article rule (C1, 249).** "A word with the article names a specific being. A word without it describes a quality." Thomas's confession, which C4 calls the strongest verse, has the article: ο θεος μου (W-H 04-john.md:926). The nominative-for-vocative use explains it, but someone will apply C1's own rule to 20:28.
6. **"The earliest church fathers who quote the verse... all knew it as 'today I have begotten thee'" (B3, 165; Full comment 995 "every church father before 250").** The held Origen (C119) is not such a witness, and the held Justin (C109) names no gospel. The patristic case the box rightly calls "your strongest card" is narrower in the texts we hold: Justin for the wording, the Ebionite gospel combining both voices (C46).
7. **1 Clement vs the Didache (B7).** 1 Clement is "the only Christian writing outside the New Testament that is securely first century"; the next line dates the Didache "late first century". "Securely" carries it, but say so, since our Didache header dates it c. 50-120.
8. **B6b's "two earliest church writers who argue for the virgin birth".** Ignatius (held) affirms it around 110 (C111, C113). The sentence is about arguing against deniers, so it can stand with that qualifier.
