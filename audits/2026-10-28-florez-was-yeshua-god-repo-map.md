## 0. In one paragraph

The Trinity card and the sheet agree on almost every point the card already makes: the one-way subordination texts (Mark 10:18, Mark 13:32, John 14:28, 17:3, 20:17), John 10:34's "ye are gods," the human-nature reply as a fifth-century framework read backwards, Mark 2:10's delegated authority, John 5:18-19, *proskuneō*, the John 20:28 answers, Mark 14:62 with Daniel 7, the Comma, Matthew 28:19, and the 325/381/451 timeline. The sheet is much bigger than the card. It works through about forty proof texts the card never touches (John 1:1, Philippians 2, Hebrews 1, Titus 2:13, Romans 9:5, 1 Timothy 3:16, Acts 20:28, Jude 5, the Hebrew-Bible texts), adds a "what the first believers said" layer (Acts 2-3, Romans 1:3-4) and the Ebionites' Christology from hostile fathers, and states the opposing scholarship (Hurtado, Bauckham, Wallace, Harris). It is also franker than the card about the evidence against it. It concedes that Ignatius called Jesus "our God" around 110, that John 20:28, John 17:5, Hebrews 1:10-12 and Paul's use of Yahweh texts are strong, that P4, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus read "well pleased" at Luke 3:22, and that you must "not say Nicaea invented the deity of Christ." Most of the patristic quotations can now be checked against texts we hold, and they match. There is one real conflict with the card: the card says Matthew *and Luke* soften Mark 10:18, but the sheet says Luke keeps it, and so do our held KJV and Westcott-Hort. The main difference in framing is that the sheet argues Jesus was an exalted man, not God, while the card argues he was divine and that divinity is shared.

**9. The Hebrew-Bible proof texts, all checkable against JPS 1917 and Brenton.**
- *What.* The card meets no Hebrew-Bible proof text. The sheet gives short answers to about ten, and our two new Old Testament texts confirm the key readings:
  - Isaiah 9:6: JPS 1917 transliterates the name as a sentence, "Pele-joez-el-gibbor-Abi-ad-sar-shalom," and Brenton's LXX reads "the Messenger of great counsel."
  - Zechariah 12:10: JPS 1917 reads "they shall look unto Me because they have thrust him through; and they shall mourn for him," and Brenton reads "mocked."
  - Micah 5:2: JPS 1917 reads "from ancient days."
  - Psalm 110:1: JPS 1917 reads "unto my lord."
  - Isaiah 48:16: the speaker shifts at "and now."
  - Genesis 19:24 is idiom (1 Kings 8:1, Hosea 1:7).
  - Isaiah 43:11: the Hebrew Bible's human "saviours."
