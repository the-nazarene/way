# Audit Worksheet (open draft)

**This is a working draft, open for anyone's input.** It takes each item on `AUDIT-LIST.md` and puts the evidence side by side, so a person can check it in a couple of minutes instead of an hour. Nothing here has been applied to the research files.

**Status:** started 2026-10-20 by Rex's session. A1-A15 are worked as of 2026-10-21 (A1 on 2026-10-20 as the model; A2-A15 on 2026-10-21, every quotation re-grepped and line numbers taken that day). Nothing has been applied; each item still needs a person's check (Processing Roadmap, Next Up 17).

## How to add your input

Anyone in the group can weigh in. Pull the repo, find the item, and add a line under **Input**:

```
- **Your name, date:** what you checked, what you think, and any source we should look at.
```

Disagreements are welcome. That's the point of the draft. If you'd rather not edit the file, send your thoughts to Rex or Matt and they'll add them with your name.

When an item has been checked by at least one person, who says which sources they read, it can move to `FIX-LIST.md` and be applied, or be closed as "not a problem."

## What each item shows

1. **Our current wording:** exactly what the repo says now, with file and line.
2. **The held text:** the passage from the source we hold, with its translator named.
3. **The original language,** where it matters (Greek, Hebrew), or the KJV.
4. **Proposed wording:** the replacement, following the repo's citation rules.
5. **What's still open:** anything a person needs to decide or look up.
6. **Input:** comments from the group.

**2. The held text:** Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30,16,5a, translated by Craig Wescoe (`christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md`):

> "I have come to do away with the sacrifices, and unless you cease from sacrificing, the wrath will not cease from you."

Epiphanius follows it by calling these sayings "the crafty devices among them" (30,16,5b). It comes from the gospel the Ebionites used, which he calls "not whole and entirely complete, but falsified and mutilated (and they call it Hebrew)" (30,13,2).

**3. The Greek** (Holl's edition, via First1KGreek): ἡ ὀργή, "the wrath," with no "of God." The verb for "do away with" is καταλῦσαι, the same verb as Matthew 5:17, "I am not come to destroy."

Label the source as the **Gospel of the Ebionites (via Epiphanius)**, not the Hebrew Gospel of Matthew. In the Hebrew Matthew file, drop "and the Clementine writings": the saying isn't in the Clementines. (Recognitions 1.39 has the related idea of baptism replacing sacrifice, which probably explains the mix-up.)

**5. What's still open:**
- Where did "of God" come from? It's probably M. R. James's 1924 translation (*The Apocryphal New Testament*) or Frank Williams's. If we want to keep that wording, we'd need to hold that translation and name it.
- Is it fair to keep calling it the Hebrew Gospel of Matthew anywhere, given Epiphanius says the Ebionites' version is altered? Probably no, but that's a call for the group.

So the wrong group is named in four of the seven listed places (`biblical-case:154`, `good-shepherd:93`, `questions/sacrifice-culture:37`, `lineage:84`), plus `moses-scroll…:183` under A4. The other three name the Nasaraeans correctly and only need the held wording. The audit's "group label wrong in 7 places" overstates it a little; its own §2.3 lists six (it counts the two A4 files).

**2. The held text:** Epiphanius, *Panarion* 18,1,1-4, translated by Craig Wescoe (`christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md`):

> "Next I shall undertake to describe the sect that comes after the Hemerobaptists, called that of the Nasaraeans; they are Jews by race, originating from Gileaditis and Bashanitis and the lands beyond the Jordan…" (18,1,1)

> "hence, being Jews, they keep all the observances of the Jews, yet they would not offer sacrifice or partake of anything that has life; among them it was unlawful to eat meat or to offer sacrifice." (18,1,4a) "For they used to say that these books are fabricated and that none of these things was done by the fathers." (18,1,4b)

Epiphanius keeps the groups apart. Of the Christian Nazoraeans (Sect 29) he says: "Nor did they call themselves Nasaraeans; for the sect of the Nasaraeans existed before Christ and did not know Christ —" (29,6,1). Of Ebion he says he has "the thinking of the Ossaeans, the Nazoraeans and the Nasaraeans" (30,1,3), three separate things. The summary of Sect 18 says the same of diet: "Nasaraeans, a name interpreted as "rebels"; they forbid all eating of flesh and take no part at all in living things" (Anaceph. I 19,1).

For the **Ebionites** the diet sentence is 30,15,3: "…since Ebion himself and the Ebionites abstain from these things entirely" [animate creatures, meats, "every other food prepared from flesh"]. For the **Nazoraeans**, Sect 29 has nothing on diet. All 86 numbered sentences of Sect 29 were grepped for meat, flesh, sacrifice, animate, food and eat. The only food text is 29,8,6, where Epiphanius quotes the Acts 15 decree *against* them ("to abstain from blood, and from what is strangled…").

Both English versions are faithful to it. The repo's bracket "[mandating sacrifice]" is an insertion: the Greek says only "these books" (ταῦτα τὰ βιβλία), which in context (18,1,3) is the Pentateuch. The names differ in Greek too: Νασαραῖοι (Sect 18), Ναζωραῖοι (Sect 29).

For they used to say that these books are fabricated and that none of these things was done by the fathers." — Epiphanius, *Panarion* 18,1,4 (trans. Wescoe), **on the Nasaraeans**, a Jewish sect of the Transjordan he dates before Christ (29,6,1)

Where a file is about the **Ebionites** (`biblical-case:154`, `lineage:84`), use 30,15,3 instead: "Ebion himself and the Ebionites abstain from these things entirely" (trans. Wescoe), with the note that the Peter half of that sentence is what Epiphanius calls "telling lies against Peter." Where a file is about the **Nazoraeans**, say that Epiphanius reports their law-keeping (29,7,5) and their Hebrew Matthew (29,9,4) and says nothing about their diet. In `good-shepherd:91`, change "The Nazarene sect — the pre-existing Jewish movement Jesus emerged from" to "the Nasaraeans, a pre-Christian Transjordan sect Epiphanius describes (*Panarion* 18)."
