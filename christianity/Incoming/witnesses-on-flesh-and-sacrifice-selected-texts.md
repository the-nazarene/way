# Witnesses on flesh and sacrifice: selected texts

**Attribution:** Hegesippus (via Eusebius), Eusebius, Clement of Alexandria, the Council of Ancyra, the Apostolic Canons, Philo (on the Essenes and on the Therapeutae), Josephus, Porphyry, Jerome; and Schweitzer on Bahrdt
**Translation:** Public-domain English editions, named on each excerpt (NPNF, ANF, Ferrar, Yonge, Whiston, Taylor, Montgomery)
**Original Language:** Greek and Latin (Schweitzer: German)
**Estimated Date:** before 50 CE (Philo) to 393 CE (Jerome); Schweitzer 1906 on Bahrdt 1784-92
**Source:** Each excerpt was downloaded from the URL given under it and sliced from the page text between two anchor phrases, never retyped. Retrieved 2026-10-10.
**Contributed by:** Rex, with Claude, 2026-10-10, for brief D in `questions/OBJECTIONS.md` and the sacrifice card
**Note:** Excerpts, not full texts. The quotation blocks are the sources; everything under **Our note** is ours. Spelling, punctuation and OCR slips are left as the online editions have them.

---

These are the texts behind two questions that come up together: did the Essenes, and did the movement after Jesus, reject sacrifice and meat? And did the idea of a vegetarian or Essene Jesus begin with the Theosophical Society? Read the notes as well as the quotations. Two of these texts are routinely quoted for more than they say.

## 1. Hegesippus on James, quoted by Eusebius

**Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23.4-7, quoting Hegesippus, *Memoirs* book 5 (c. 170 CE)**  
Arthur Cushman McGiffert, NPNF series 2 vol. 1 (1890). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm>

> 4. James, the brother of the Lord, succeeded to the government of the Church in conjunction with the apostles. He has been called the Just by all from the time of our Saviour to the present day; for there were many that bore the name of James. 5. He was holy from his mother's womb; and he drank no wine nor strong drink, nor did he eat flesh. No razor came upon his head; he did not anoint himself with oil, and he did not use the bath. 6. He alone was permitted to enter into the holy place; for he wore not woolen but linen garments. And he was in the habit of entering alone into the temple, and was frequently found upon his knees begging forgiveness for the people, so that his knees became hard like those of a camel, in consequence of his constantly bending them in his worship of God, and asking forgiveness for the people.

**Our note.** The earliest witness to the diet of anyone in Jesus's own family. Hegesippus, not Eusebius, is the source; Eusebius is quoting him. The phrase to cite is "nor did he eat flesh" (not "meat").

## 2. Eusebius on the disciples

**Eusebius, *Demonstratio Evangelica* 3.5 (early 4th century)**  
W. J. Ferrar (SPCK, 1920). Public domain in the US.  
<https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/eusebius_de_05_book3.htm>

> consider the character of the disciples of Jesus. From the men as they stand, surely any sensible person would be inclined to consider them worthy of all confidence; they were admittedly poor men without eloquence, they fell in love with holy and philosophic instruction, they embraced and persevered in a strenuous and a laborious life, with fasting and abstinence from wine and meat, and much bodily restriction besides, with prayers and intercessions to God, (c) and, last but not least, excessive purity, and devotion botli of body and soul.

**Our note.** "botli" is the online edition's OCR of "both", left as found. Eusebius says *the disciples of Jesus*, not "the twelve apostles", and the phrase is "abstinence from wine and meat" inside a longer sentence about their way of life. Quote it whole.

## 3. Clement of Alexandria on Matthew

**Clement of Alexandria, *Paedagogus* (The Instructor) 2.1 (c. 200 CE)**  
William Wilson, ANF vol. 2 (1885). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02092.htm>

> Accordingly, the apostle Matthew partook of seeds, and nuts, and vegetables, without flesh. And John, who carried temperance to the extreme, ate locusts and wild honey. Peter abstained from swine; but a trance fell on him, as is written in the Acts of the Apostles, and he saw heaven opened, and a vessel let down on the earth by the four corners, and all the four-looted beasts and creeping things of the earth and the fowls of heaven in it; and there came a voice to him, Rise, and slay, and eat. And Peter said, Not so, Lord, for I have never eaten what is common or unclean. And the voice came again to him the second time, What God has cleansed, call not common. Acts 10:10-15 The use of them is accordingly indifferent to us. For not what enters into the mouth defiles the man, Matthew 15:11 but the vain opinion respecting uncleanness.

**Our note.** Read the whole excerpt aloud or none of it. Clement reports the tradition about Matthew, then says in the same breath that the use of food is "indifferent to us". He is a witness to the tradition, not an advocate of it.

## 4. The Council of Ancyra, Canon 14

**Council of Ancyra (314 CE), Canon 14, with the translator's note on the text**  
Henry Percival, NPNF series 2 vol. 14 (1900). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3802.htm>

> Canon 14 It is decreed that among the clergy, presbyters and deacons who abstain from flesh shall taste of it, and afterwards, if they shall so please, may abstain. But if they disdain it, and will not even eat herbs served with flesh, but disobey the canon, let them be removed from their order. There is a serious dispute about the reading of the Greek text. I have followed Routh, who, relying on three mss. the Collectio of John of Antioch and the Latin versions, reads εἰ δὲ βδελύσσοιντο instead of the εἰ δὲ βούλοιντο of the ordinary text, which as Bp. Beveridge had pointed out before has no meaning unless a μὴ be introduced. Zonaras points out that the canon chiefly refers to the Love feasts. I cannot agree with Hefele in his translation of the last clause. He makes the reference to this present canon, I think it is clearly to the 53 (52) of the so-called Canons of the Apostles, τῷ κανόνι the well-known Canon.

**Our note.** A council legislating against clergy who abstain from flesh, a generation after Constantine. Councils legislate against what is happening. Percival's note matters: the Greek reading is disputed, and he ties the canon to Apostolic Canon 53 (below).

## 5. The Apostolic Canons 51 and 53

***Canons of the Holy Apostles* 51 and 53 (compiled in Syria, late 4th century)**  
Henry Percival, NPNF series 2 vol. 14 (1900). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3820.htm>

> Canon 51 If any bishop, presbyter, or deacon, or any one of the sacerdotal list, abstains from marriage, or flesh, or wine, not by way of religious restraint, but as abhorring them, forgetting that God made all things very good, and that he made man male and female, and blaspheming the work of creation, let him be corrected, or else be deposed, and cast out of the Church. In like manner a layman. [Canon 52 omitted] Canon 53 If any bishop, presbyter, or deacon, does not on festival days partake of flesh and wine, from an abhorrence of them, and not out of religious restraint, let him be deposed, as being seared in his own conscience, and being the cause of offense to many.

**Our note.** The church distinguishing abstinence "by way of religious restraint" (permitted) from abstinence "as abhorring" flesh (punished). That distinction only needs drawing if Christians were abstaining for the second reason.

## 6. Philo on the Essenes

**Philo of Alexandria, *Every Good Man Is Free* (Quod Omnis Probus Liber Sit) 75 (before 50 CE)**  
C. D. Yonge, *The Works of Philo* (1854-55). Public domain.  
<http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/yonge/book33.html>

> (75) Moreover Palestine and Syria too are not barren of exemplary wisdom and virtue, which countries no slight portion of that most populous nation of the Jews inhabits. There is a portion of those people called Essenes, in number something more than four thousand in my opinion, who derive their name from their piety, though not according to any accurate form of the Grecian dialect, because they are above all men devoted to the service of God, not sacrificing living animals, but studying rather to preserve their own minds in a state of holiness and purity.

**Our note.** The earliest outside witness to the Essenes, writing in Jesus's lifetime, and the cleanest support for the sacrifice half of the resolution. It says nothing about their diet.

## 7. Josephus on the Essenes and the Temple

**Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.1.5 (c. 94 CE)**  
William Whiston (1737), Project Gutenberg. Public domain.  
<https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2848/pg2848.txt>

> The doctrine of the Essens is this: That all things are best ascribed to God. They teach the immortality of souls, and esteem that the rewards of righteousness are to be earnestly striven for; and when they send what they have dedicated to God into the temple, they do not offer sacrifices [Whiston's note 3] because they have more pure lustrations of their own; on which account they are excluded from the common court of the temple, but offer their sacrifices themselves; yet is their course of life better than that of other men; and they entirely addict themselves to husbandry.

**Our note.** Do not quote this for "the Essenes rejected sacrifice". The same sentence says they "offer their sacrifices themselves", and the "not" is itself in question: a few late Greek manuscripts omit it (B. J. Roberts, "The Qumran Scrolls and the Essenes", *New Testament Studies* 3.1, 1956, p. 63 n. 2, citing Naber's edition of 1888-93). Either way it shows a break with the Temple's sacrifices, not a rejection of sacrifice. It says nothing about meat.

## 8. Josephus on the Essene meal

**Josephus, *Jewish War* 2.8.5 (c. 75 CE)**  
William Whiston (1737), Project Gutenberg. Public domain.  
<https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2850/pg2850.txt>

> the baker lays them loaves in order; the cook also brings a single plate of one sort of food, and sets it before every one of them; but a priest says grace before meat; and it is unlawful for any one to taste of the food before grace be said. The same priest, when he hath dined, says grace again after meat; and when they begin, and when they end, they praise God, as he that bestows their food upon them;

**Our note.** Josephus's fullest description of the Essenes describes the meal and does not say what was on the plate. In Whiston's English "meat" means food ("grace before meat"). Nothing in Josephus says the Essenes abstained from flesh or wine.

## 9. Porphyry on the Essenes

**Porphyry, *On Abstinence from Animal Food* 4.11-12 (c. 270 CE)**  
Thomas Taylor (1823). Public domain.  
<https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/porphyry_abstinence_04_book4.htm>

> At that period, therefore, there were three kinds of philosophers among them. And of one kind, |122 indeed, the Pharisees were the leaders, but of another, the Sadducees, and of the third, which appears to have been the most venerable, the Essenes. The mode of life, therefore, of these third was as follows, as Josephus frequently testifies in many of his writings. For in the second book of his Judaic History, which he has completed in seven books, and in the eighteenth of his Antiquities, which consists of twenty books, and likewise in the second of the two books which he wrote against the Greeks, he speaks of these Essenes, [...] the baker places the bread in order, and the cook distributes to each of them one vessel containing one kind of eatables. Prior, however, to their taking the food which is pure and sacred, a priest prays,

**Our note.** Porphyry includes the Essenes in a book arguing for abstinence from animal food, and says he is following Josephus. **He never says the Essenes abstained from animal food.** The sentence circulating as his, "the Essenes, who are a sect of the Jews, abstain from all animal food" (read aloud in Jesus Way episode 026 at 1:29:10, and quoted in several files in this repo), is not in Book 4 in Taylor's translation. Books 1-3 were checked too (2026-10-10). The nearest thing is Book 2.26, quoting Theophrastus: the Jews sacrifice animals but "do not feast on the flesh of the sacrificed animals", burning them whole. That is about the Jews and their sacrifices, it names no Essenes, and Theophrastus wrote in the fourth century BCE, before the Essenes existed; only Taylor's 1823 footnote connects it to them.

## 10. Jerome on the Essenes

**Jerome, *Against Jovinianus* 2.14 (393 CE)**  
W. H. Fremantle, G. Lewis and W. G. Martley, NPNF series 2 vol. 6 (1893). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/30092.htm>

> 14. Josephus in the second book of the history of the Jewish captivity, and in the eighteenth book of the Antiquities, and the two treatises against Apion, describes three sects of the Jews, the Pharisees, Sadducees, and Essenes. On the last of these he bestows wondrous praise because they practised perpetual abstinence from wives, wine, and flesh, and made a second nature of their daily fast.

**Our note.** The only ancient text found that says outright the Essenes abstained from flesh, and it rests on nothing. Jerome credits Josephus; the Josephus passages above do not say it. The editors of this very translation note it at the passage (NPNF 2.6, note 4769): "Josephus nowhere says that the Essenes abstained from flesh and wine, or fasted daily... Jerome here, as above, borrows from Porphyry." His next sentence (Neanthes and Asclepiades on Pygmalion) is the passage that follows the Essenes in Porphyry, and Porphyry doesn't say it either. The same note adds "Philo commends them for so doing": Philo's Essene passage (excerpt 6) does not, and the meatless, wineless table in Philo belongs to the Therapeutae (excerpt 12). Cite Jerome as Jerome, with all of that, or not at all.

## 11. Bahrdt's Essene Jesus, as Schweitzer describes it

**Albert Schweitzer, *The Quest of the Historical Jesus*, ch. 4, on K. F. Bahrdt's *Ausführung des Plans und Zwecks Jesu* (1784-92)**  
W. Montgomery (1910). Public domain.  
<http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/schweitzer/chapter4.html>

> Jesus came under the notice of the Order immediately after His birth. As a child He was watched over at every step by the Brethren. At the feasts at Jerusalem, Alexandrian Jews, secret members of the Essene Order, put themselves into communication with Him, explained to Him the falsity of the priests, inspired Him with a horror of the bloody sacrifices of the Temple, and made him acquainted with Socrates and Plato.

**Our note.** Not evidence about Jesus. Bahrdt's book is a novelistic "life", and Schweitzer treats it as one. It is evidence about the *claim*: an Essene, anti-sacrifice Jesus was in print by the 1780s, about ninety years before the Theosophical Society (founded 1875).

## 12. Philo on the Therapeutae

**Philo of Alexandria, *On the Contemplative Life* 73 (before 50 CE)**  
C. D. Yonge, *The Works of Philo* (1854-55). Public domain.  
<http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/yonge/book34.html>

> And in those days wine is not introduced, but only the clearest water; cold water for the generality, and hot water for those old men who are accustomed to a luxurious life. And the table, too, bears nothing which has blood, but there is placed upon it bread for food and salt for seasoning, to which also hyssop is sometimes added as an extra sauce for the sake of those who are delicate in their eating,

**Our note.** This is Philo's flesh-free, wine-free table, and it belongs to the Therapeutae, a contemplative community near Alexandria, not to the Essenes. Don't move it across.

## 13. Eusebius: the Therapeutae were the first Christians

**Eusebius, *Church History* 2.17.22-24 (early 4th century)**  
Arthur Cushman McGiffert, NPNF series 2 vol. 1 (1890). Public domain.  
<https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm>

> on the days referred to they sleep on the ground on beds of straw, and to use his own words, taste no wine at all, nor any flesh, but water is their only drink, and the reish with their bread is salt and hyssop. [...] But that Philo, when he wrote these things, had in view the first heralds of the Gospel and the customs handed down from the beginning by the apostles, is clear to every one.

**Our note.** Eusebius is wrong about who the Therapeutae were, and modern scholars do not accept the identification. The point for brief D is narrower and solid: in the fourth century the church's own historian already tied the earliest Christians to a community that took "no wine at all, nor any flesh".
