# Claims: the Bible in Greek and in the Hebrew-based and Greek-based English translations

Held texts, one verse per line:
- Greek New Testament: `christianity/Incoming/greek-nt-westcott-hort/` (Westcott and Hort, 1881). Unaccented lower-case Greek, as the file has it.
- Hebrew Bible in English: `christianity/Incoming/jps-1917/` (Jewish Publication Society, 1917). Hebrew verse numbering; the KJV number is given where it differs.
- Septuagint in English: `christianity/Incoming/septuagint-brenton/` (Brenton, 1844/1870). Septuagint numbering. English only: we hold no Septuagint Greek.

We hold no Hebrew text. Claims about a Hebrew word can be tested only as far as the JPS English shows it. KJV wording is in `bible-kjv.md`.

### C132 · John 19:37 (Westcott-Hort)
**Claim:** Quoting Zechariah 12:10, John "had the word 'me' available and wrote 'him'"; "it is John who says 'him'".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/greek-nt-westcott-hort/04-john.md` (Westcott-Hort 1881)
**Text says:**
> και παλιν ετερα γραφη λεγει οψονται εις ον εξεκεντησαν
**Note:** John's Greek has no pronoun: "they shall look on [the one] whom they pierced" (εις ον). The "him" is supplied by English translations (KJV "on him whom they pierced"). So John drops Zechariah's "me" but does not write "him"; the sheet's point (John does not keep "to me") holds in that narrower form.

### C139 · Genesis 24:16 (JPS 1917)

**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/jps-1917/01-genesis.md` (JPS 1917)

### C141 · Psalm 110:1 (JPS 1917)
**Claim:** "In Hebrew the first is YHWH and the second is *adoni*, the ordinary word for a human superior, not *Adonai*."
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/jps-1917/27-psalms.md` (JPS 1917)
**Text says:**
> The LORD saith unto my lord: 'Sit thou at My right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.'
**Note:** JPS prints "my lord" in lower case, as the sheet says; the KJV prints "my Lord". ### C143 · Habakkuk 1:12 (JPS 1917)
**Claim:** "O LORD my God, my Holy One, thou diest not"; the received Hebrew reads "we shall not die" because the scribes altered it, one of the eighteen *tiqqune sopherim*.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/jps-1917/22-habakkuk.md` (JPS 1917)

**Note:** The received text reads "We shall not die", as the sheet says.
