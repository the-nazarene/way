# Claims: the Bible (KJV)

Held text: `christianity/Incoming/kjv/` (1769 Oxford text, Project Gutenberg #10), one verse per line. One entry per claim; where a claim needs several books, the entry names the main one and the note gives the others.

The routine checks of Christian Florez's sheet, that each verse it says the KJV adds really reads that way in the KJV (about 45 verses, all matching), are in the table in `audits/2026-10-26-florez-red-letters-citation-check.md` §1 and are not repeated here.

### C76 · Matthew 26:3, 26:57 (Caiaphas)
**Claim:** "Matthew and Mark have none of these names" (Caiaphas among them), and the details "appear in no other Greek gospel".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/40-matthew.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> unto the palace of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas,
> And they that had laid hold on Jesus led him away to Caiaphas the high priest
**Note:** Caiaphas is named in Matthew 26:3 and 26:57, and in John 11:49 and 18:13-14, 24, 28.

### C77 · Acts 2:38, 8:16, 10:48, 19:5 (baptism formulas)
**Claim:** "Every baptism recorded in Acts is in the name of Yeshua."
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/44-acts.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins
> only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.
> And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord.
> When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.
**Note:** Four baptism scenes in Acts state a formula, and all four name Jesus or "the Lord"; none uses the triadic formula. About five more baptisms state no formula at all. So "every baptism that states a formula" holds, and "every baptism" is broader than the text. In the KJV, 10:48 reads "in the name of the Lord", not "Jesus".

**Checked:** 2026-10-26, Claude (`audits/2026-10-26-florez-red-letters-citation-check.md`); person check: not yet

### C78 · "New" in the sayings of Jesus
**Claim:** These (the new covenant at the Last Supper) are "the only places Yeshua is reported saying 'new'".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/41-mark.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> until that day that I drink it new in the kingdom of God.
> they shall speak with new tongues
**Note:** He also says "new" at Matthew 26:29 ("drink it new") and John 13:34 ("A new commandment"). The narrower claim holds: these are the only places he speaks of a new covenant or testament (Matthew 26:28, Mark 14:24, Luke 22:20).

### C79 · Mark 1:2
**Claim:** Mark 1:2 reads "in Isaiah the prophet".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/41-mark.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> As it is written in the prophets, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face
**Note:** "In Isaiah the prophet" is the critical-text reading (the sheet quotes the ASV). The KJV, following the Textus Receptus, reads "in the prophets". Both are accurate for their own text; say which text is quoted.

### C81 · Mark 8:19-21 (the loaves)
**Claim:** "Do you not yet understand about the loaves?" is Mark 8:19-21.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/41-mark.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> When I brake the five loaves among five thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took ye up? They say unto him, Twelve.
> And he said unto them, How is it that ye do not understand?
**Note:** 8:19-20 are the basket counts and 8:21 is "How is it that ye do not understand?" The nearest wording to the claim is 6:52, "they considered not the miracle of the loaves". The feeding accounts in the KJV also hand out fish (Mark 6:41, 6:43, 8:7; John 6:11), which the file doesn't mention.

### C82 · Leviticus 14:19 (the leper's offering)
**Claim:** The leper's offering is "a civil-status statute, not a sin offering" and "not atonement for sin".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/03-leviticus.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> And the priest shall offer the sin offering, and make an atonement for him that is to be cleansed from his uncleanness
**Note:** The text calls it a sin offering, and 14:12-13 and 14:21-22 add a trespass offering. The atonement is "for him that is to be cleansed from his uncleanness", so a narrower wording (atonement for ritual uncleanness, not for moral sin) may still make the cards' point.

### C152 · Matthew 19:17
**Claim:** "Watch Matthew change it. Matthew 19.17, reporting the same scene, reads 'Why askest thou me concerning that which is good? One there is who is good.'... you can see it happen by laying the two verses side by side."
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/kjv/40-matthew.md` (KJV)
**Text says:**
> And he said unto him, Why callest thou me good? there is none good but one, that is, God:
**Note:** In the KJV, Matthew 19:17 has Mark's wording, so a KJV reader laying the verses side by side will see no change. The change is in the critical Greek (Westcott-Hort, C134) and in translations made from it, such as the ASV the sheet quotes. Say "in the earliest Greek text" when using it.
