### C36 · Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 2.9.45
**Claim:** Clement quotes the Gospel according to the Hebrews by name: "He that wonders shall reign, and he that reigns shall rest".
**Result:** Matches, other translation
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clement-of-alexandria-stromata-full-text.md` (Wilson, ANF 2)
**Text says:**
> So also in the Gospel to the Hebrews it is written, "He that wonders shall reign, and he that has reigned shall rest.
**Note:** One word differs ("has reigned"). The named source is confirmed.

### C37 · Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 5.14.96
**Claim:** The longer form, "He that seeks will not rest until he finds", is also Clement quoting the Gospel according to the Hebrews by name.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clement-of-alexandria-stromata-full-text.md` (Wilson, ANF 2)
**Text says:**
> He, who seeks, will not stop till he find; and having found, he will wonder; and wondering, he will reign; and reigning, he will rest.
**Note:** In the held text this form names no source. It follows a passage of Plato, with no mention of the Gospel according to the Hebrews; only the 2.9 form (C36) is credited to it. That the two are one saying is the usual scholarly view; the held text simply doesn't say so. The wording also differs ("will not stop till he find").

### C38 · Origen, Commentary on John 2.12 (Menzies Book II, section 6)
**Claim:** In the Gospel according to the Hebrews Jesus says "My mother, the Holy Spirit, take me by one of my hairs" and carried him to "the great mountain Tabor".
**Result:** Matches, other section number
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/origen-commentary-on-john-full-text.md` (Menzies, ANF 9)
**Text says:**
> If any one should lend credence to the Gospel according to the Hebrews, where the Saviour Himself says, "My mother, the Holy Spirit took me just now by one of my hairs and carried me off to the great mount Tabor,"
**Note:** Menzies prints it in Book II, section 6 (modern editions: chapter 12), and the wording differs by translation ("took me just now", "carried me off", "the great mount Tabor"). Origen frames it as a condition, "If any one should lend credence to the Gospel according to the Hebrews", and goes on to discuss how the Spirit can be Christ's mother.

### C39 · Council of Laodicea, canon 29
**Claim:** The council forbids Christians to rest on the Sabbath ("judaize") and tells them to honour the Lord's day, resting then if they can.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/council-of-laodicea-canons-full-text.md` (Percival, NPNF 2.14)
**Text says:**
> Christians must not judaize by resting on the Sabbath, but must work on that day, rather honouring the Lord's Day; and, if they can, resting then as Christians.
**Note:** Percival dates the synod "between the years 343 and 381" (following Hefele). The draft's "usually dated c. 363-364 (estimates run from the 340s to the 380s)" is consistent with that.

### C119 · Origen, Commentary on John, Book I (Menzies section 32)
**Claim:** The Bezae reading of Luke 3:22, "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee", is quoted as the text of Luke by "Origen (Commentary on John, book 1)"; the baptism voice "in Justin, Clement and Origen says 'today I have begotten thee'".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/origen-commentary-on-john-full-text.md` (Menzies, ANF 9)
**Text says:**
> "Thou art My Son, this day have I begotten Thee," [4608] this is spoken to Him by God, with whom all time is to-day
> [4608] Mark i. 11; Ps. ii. 7; Heb. i. 5.
**Note:** Origen quotes the Psalm 2:7 words and reads "to-day" as God's timeless present, the eternal begetting of the Son. Nothing in the section mentions the baptism, the Jordan or Luke, and Menzies' note points to Mark 1:11, Psalm 2:7 and Hebrews 1:5, not Luke 3:22. This is the only place the words occur in the held books (I, II, VI, X). If Origen quotes Luke 3:22 in this form elsewhere, it is in a work we don't hold.

### C120 · Origen, Commentary on John, Book II (Menzies section 29) and Book VI (section 2)
**Claim:** At John 1:18, Origen is a witness for "only begotten God".
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/origen-commentary-on-john-full-text.md` (Menzies, ANF 9)
**Text says:**
> No one hath seen God at any time; the only-begotten God, who is in the bosom of the Father, He hath declared Him."
> "The only-begotten Son of God who is in the bosom of the Father, He hath declared him."
**Note:** Book II quotes "the only-begotten God"; Book VI quotes "the only-begotten Son of God". So Origen, in Menzies' English, has both. The Greek of these passages is not held.

### C121 · Origen, Commentary on John, Book II (Menzies section 2)
**Claim:** In John 1:1, "God" has the article in the second clause (*ton theon*) and not in the third (*theos*), and John made the distinction on purpose.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/origen-commentary-on-john-full-text.md` (Menzies, ANF 9)
**Text says:**
> He does not write without care in this respect, nor is he unfamiliar with the niceties of the Greek tongue.
> He uses the article, when the name of God refers to the uncreated cause of all things, and omits it when the Logos is named God.
> all beyond the Very God is made God by participation in His divinity, and is not to be called simply God (with the article), but rather God (without article).
**Note:** The sheet does not cite Origen, but he makes its C1 argument about 230 in a held text, and quotes John 17:3 ("the only true God") with it. The Greek wording of the verse itself is in Westcott-Hort (C129).

### C175 · Clement of Alexandria, Paedagogus 2.1 (Matthew without flesh)
**Claim:** Clement says the apostle Matthew ate seeds, nuts and vegetables without flesh (*The Instructor* 2.1.16).
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/witnesses-on-flesh-and-sacrifice-selected-texts.md` (Wilson, ANF 2; Tier 2 excerpt)
**Text says:**
> Accordingly, the apostle Matthew partook of seeds, and nuts, and vegetables, without flesh. And John, who carried temperance to the extreme, ate locusts and wild honey.
**Note:** The excerpt has no paragraph number (2.1.16 is the modern one). The next sentence has John the Baptist eating "locusts and wild honey", and Clement treats food as "indifferent to us" (the excerpt's own note). Read with the sheet's D2, which gives the Ebionite Baptist no locusts.
