### C51 · Didache 7.1 and 7.3
**Claim:** The Didache has the triadic baptism formula.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md` (Richardson)
**Text says:**
> "baptize" in running water, "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."
**Note:** The formula appears twice, in 7.1 and again in 7.3 ("in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit").

### C52 · Didache 9.5
**Claim:** The Didache has both formulas: triadic in 7.1 and "in the name of the Lord" in 9.5.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md` (Richardson)
**Text says:**
> You must not let anyone eat or drink of your Eucharist except those baptized in the Lord's name.
**Note:** The wording matches. 9.5 is a rule about who may share the Eucharist, and it refers to baptism "in the Lord's name". The baptism instructions themselves (chapter 7) give only the triadic form, twice.

### C54 · Didache 8.2
**Claim:** The Didache's Lord's Prayer reads "give us today our needful bread; and forgive us our debt as we forgive those indebted to us; and do not bring us to the test, but rescue us from the evil one", with no doxology.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md` (Richardson)
**Text says:**
> give us today our bread for the morrow; and forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors
> for yours is the power and the glory forever.
**Note:** The wording in our Q file is not that of our held Didache, and our file leaves out the doxology, though its own note says the Didache "preserves a short form of" it. The Q file names no translation for its wording.

### C55 · Didache 9-10
**Claim:** The Didache describes a eucharist with no body or blood words at all.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md` (Richardson)
**Text says:**
> We thank you, our Father, for the holy vine of David
**Note:** Chapters 9-10 give thanks for the cup ("the holy vine of David"), the broken bread "scattered over the hills", and "the life and knowledge"; there is no body, blood or death language. Chapter 14 uses "sacrifice" three times for the gathering's own offering; the claim says nothing about sacrifice, so there is no conflict.

### C56 · Clementine Recognitions 1.39
**Claim:** The Recognitions say baptism was given so that sins would be remitted "instead of sacrifices" (the sheet calls it a thematic parallel, not a quotation).
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clementine-recognitions-full-text.md` (Smith, ANF 8)
**Text says:**
> ### Chapter XXXIX — Baptism Instituted in Place of Sacrifices
> He instituted baptism by water amongst them, in which they might be absolved from all their sins on the invocation of His name
> purified not by the blood of beasts, but by the purification of the Wisdom of God
**Note:** The idea is there, as the sheet says. But the words in quotation marks, "instead of sacrifices", are not in the text of 1.39; the nearest is the chapter heading, which is most likely the ANF editor's. The quotable words are "purified not by the blood of beasts". The sheet's link is labelled "Homilies 3", which does not match the citation (Recognitions Book 1).
