### C115 · Didache 9.2-3 and 10.2
**Claim:** "Yeshua is 'thy servant' (*pais*) in its prayers, 9.2 and 10.2. Not God."
**Result:** Matches, other translation
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md` (Richardson)

### C231 · Epistle of Peter to James 1-3 and the adjuration (the Contestatio, ANF chapters IV-V)
**Claim:** In the frame story Peter sends his preachings to "bishop" James and asks for great care in handing them on, so they will not be falsified by followers of "the hostile man".
**Result:** Matches, other translation
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clementine-homilies-full-text.md` (Thomas Smith, ANF 8)
**Text says:**
> Peter to James, the lord and bishop of the holy Church
> I beg and beseech you not to communicate to any one of the Gentiles the books of my preachings which I sent to you, nor to any one of our own tribe before trial
> to transform my words by certain various interpretations, in order to the dissolution of the law
> but to one who is good and religious, and who wishes to teach, and who is circumcised, and faithful.
**Note:** The sense is all there. "The hostile man" is not ANF's wording (ANF has "the man who is my enemy", C230); it is the rendering the sheet takes from Strecker's quotation. ANF does not print a separate "Contestatio": the oath is chapters IV-V of the Epistle of Peter, headed "An Adjuration Concerning the Receivers of the Book", and it requires the receiver to be circumcised and tested "not less than six years". The editor's note 889 calls the letter "the latest portion of the literature", the opposite of the sheet's reading; that is Riddle's 1886 view, not evidence.

### C242 · Clementine Recognitions 1.37 and 1.39 (the place of safety)
**Claim:** The Temple's fall is the penalty for continuing to sacrifice; those who believe are kept safe "in a fortified place in the country" while war destroys the rest (Recognitions 1.37, 1.39), widely read as a memory of the flight to Pella.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clementine-recognitions-full-text.md` (Thomas Smith, ANF 8)
**Text says:**
> that a people who offer sacrifices are driven away and delivered up into the hands of the enemy
> is baptized in His name, shall be kept unhurt from the destruction of war which impends over the unbelieving nation, and the place itself
**Note:** The penalty and the protection are there. No place of refuge is named or described in Rufinus's Latin as translated: there is no "fortified place in the country" in 1.37 or 1.39 (a grep of the whole file for "fortified" finds nothing relevant). That wording is not from this translation, and its source can't be checked here. Say "kept unhurt from the destruction of war" when quoting our text. The plainest held statement that the Temple falls because sacrifice continued is 1.64: "because ye will not acknowledge that the time for offering victims is now past, therefore the temple shall be destroyed". Eusebius, *Church History* 3.5.3 (held) is the Pella text.

### C249 · Clementine Recognitions 9.17, 9.19-29 (Bardesanes)
**Claim:** The Circuits author used Bardaisan's *Book of the Laws of Countries* in Recognitions 9.19-29; Strecker treats that as the earliest possible date for the Basic Writing.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clementine-recognitions-full-text.md` (Thomas Smith, ANF 8)
**Text says:**
> Ch. 17 and ch. 19-29 are taken in an altered form from the writing ascribed to Bardesanes, De Fato.
> but more recently the opinion has prevailed, that the passage was not originally in the Recognitions, but was inserted in the Recognitions towards the middle of the third century, or even later.
**Note:** The ANF note confirms the chapters (and adds 9.17). ### C250 · Rufinus, Preface to the Recognitions (to Gaudentius)
**Claim:** The later editors visibly tone down the kernels' theology, and Rufinus softened further in Latin.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/clementine-recognitions-full-text.md` (Thomas Smith, ANF 8)
**Text says:**
> There are also in both collections some dissertations concerning the Unbegotten God and the Begotten, and on some other subjects, which, to say nothing more, are beyond our comprehension.
> These, therefore, as being beyond our powers, I have chosen to reserve for others, rather than to produce in an imperfect state.
> It is quite probable that the apparent heresy of some passages, rather than their incomprehensibility, led him to omit them.
**Note:** Narrower than the sheet: what Rufinus says he left out is doctrine about the Unbegotten and the Begotten, not anti-Pauline material. The third line is the ANF editor's note 531. Rufinus also says he left the Epistle of Clement to James out "because it is of later date". At 1.69 the editor thinks a Trinitarian sentence was framed "to accord with the Catholic doctrine" (note 595).
