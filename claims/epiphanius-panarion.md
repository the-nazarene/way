### C46 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.13.7
**Claim:** The Ebionite gospel gives the Psalm 2:7 form at the baptism ("This day have I begotten thee") rather than the majority form; the Spirit enters into him; a great light; John asks to be baptized.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**
> coming down and entering into him.
> You are my beloved Son, in you I am well pleased'; and again: 'Today I have begotten you.'
> And at once a great light shone round the place.
> I beg you, Lord, baptize me yourself.
**Note:** The details (entering, light, John's request) match. But the text has the "well pleased" form twice and the Psalm 2:7 line once: a third voice follows, "This is my beloved Son, on whom I am well pleased". So it combines both readings rather than giving the Psalm 2:7 form instead of the majority one. The sheet's F3.6 quotation leaves out the "in you I am well pleased" clause that sits between the two parts.

### C49 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.22.4
**Claim:** The Hebrew Gospel asks "Have I desired with desire to eat flesh with you this Passover?", a question expecting the answer no, and an earlier form that Luke 22:15 depends on.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**
> With longing I have longed to eat this Passover with you
> I have not longed with longing to eat meat this Passover with you.
> they added the adverb "not".
**Note:** Two things. (1) Wescoe renders a negative statement, not a question. The Greek μή can be read either way, so both are possible translations; say which one is quoted. (2) Epiphanius presents the line as the group's change to Luke's sentence, made by adding "not" and "meat" ("the mu and the eta are additions"). `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` records the same reading: secondary, an inversion of Luke 22:15. The sheet does not mention Epiphanius's statement that it was altered.

### C124 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.16.4 (with 30.3.3-6)
**Claim:** Panarion 30.16.4: the Ebionites held that Christ was not begotten of God the Father but created as one of the archangels, ruling over the angels, who came into Adam and the patriarchs and finally clothed himself in the body of Jesus.
**Result:** Matches, other section number
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**
> But they do not say that he was begotten of God the Father, but that he was created like one of the archangels, and yet is lord both of angels and of all [the things] made by the Almighty
> but that he comes here whenever he chooses, just as he came in Adam too, and showed himself to the patriarchs, putting on the body
> No, but the Spirit, which is the Christ, came into him and put on the one called Jesus.
**Note:** The archangel part is 30.16.4 (first line). Adam, the patriarchs and putting on Jesus are 30.3.5-6a (second and third lines). Epiphanius says their accounts vary ("one in one way and another in another", 30.3.1; "now one thing and now another", 30.3.6b), and 30.16.3 adds that Jesus "was born from a man's seed and was chosen, and so by election was called Son of God". So "a high created being" is one Ebionite view among several he reports.

### C170 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.3.8-9 and 30.6.9 (the treasury at Tiberias)
**Claim:** A convert named Joseph of Tiberias found Hebrew translations of the Gospel of John, the Acts and Matthew kept in a sealed treasury by the Hebrew authorities there.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**

### C173 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.15.1-3 (the Travels of Peter)
**Claim:** The Ebionites also read the *Circuits of Peter*, which they claimed Clement of Rome wrote, "in which Peter abstains from flesh and condemns the sacrifices".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**
> They also make use of some other books — the so-called Travels of Peter, supposedly, written by Clement
> and they say that he abstained from animate creatures and from meats, as they themselves do
> as though he were declaring against the temple and the sacrifices, and against the fire upon the altar
**Note:** Clement's authorship and Peter's abstaining from meat are in 30.15.1-3. Condemning the sacrifices is not: 30.15 says Peter was "baptized every day" and abstained from meat. The anti-sacrifice book Epiphanius names is the *Ascents of James* (30.16.7, third line), where James, not Peter, declares against the temple and the sacrifices.

### C252 · Epiphanius, Panarion 30.16.8-9
**Claim:** The Ascents claimed Paul was a Greek who became a proselyte to marry "the high priest's daughter" and, when rejected, wrote against circumcision, the Sabbath and the Law.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md` (Wescoe)
**Text says:**
> Then they say that he was a Greek, the son of a Greek mother and a Greek father, and that he went up to Jerusalem and stayed there for a time, and that he desired to take the daughter of the priest in marriage and for this reason became a proselyte and was circumcised.
> And when after all he did not get such a girl, he grew angry and wrote against circumcision, the sabbath and the law.
**Note:** One word differs: "the daughter of the priest", not "the high priest". The rest matches. Epiphanius links the story to the Ascents with "so that here too they are not ashamed to accuse Paul" (30.16.8), and calls it "composed out of the knavery and error of their false apostles". This is the passage AUDIT-LIST A8 names as Epiphanius's real charge against Paul.
