### C15 · Eusebius, Church History 4.22.8 (McGiffert 4.22.7)
**Claim:** Hegesippus quoted "from the Gospel according to the Hebrews, from the Syriac, and from the Hebrew language" (three sources), and was himself a believer from among the Hebrews.
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/eusebius-church-history-full-text.md` (McGiffert, NPNF 2.1)
**Text says:**
> And from the Syriac Gospel according to the Hebrews he quotes some passages in the Hebrew tongue
**Note:** McGiffert reads one book, "the Syriac Gospel according to the Hebrews", where the claim reads three sources. His note 1242 gives the Greek, says "the exact meaning of this sentence is very difficult to determine", and says the grammar requires a Syriac gospel "in addition to the Hebrew". So the three-source reading has support in the Greek, but it is not what this translation prints. McGiffert numbers the passage section 7.

### C117 · Eusebius, Church History 6.25.13-14
**Claim:** Hebrews is anonymous: "Origen in the third century said 'who wrote the epistle, in truth God knows' (quoted by Eusebius, Church History 6.25). It is not by Paul, not by an apostle".
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/eusebius-church-history-full-text.md` (McGiffert, NPNF 2.1)
**Text says:**
> But who wrote the epistle, in truth, God knows.
> If I gave my opinion, I should say that the thoughts are those of the apostle, but the diction and phraseology are those of some one who remembered the apostolic teachings
**Note:** The quotation matches (McGiffert 6.25.14). The next sentence of the sheet goes past Origen: in 6.25.13 he gives the thoughts to Paul and says "if any church holds that this epistle is by Paul, let it be commended for this". He doubts Paul wrote it down, not that it is Pauline.
