### C31 · Ignatius, Magnesians 9 (longer recension)
**Claim:** Ignatius says "Let us therefore no longer keep the Sabbath after the Jewish manner", and calls the Lord's day "the queen and chief of all the days".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/ignatius-epistles-full-text.md` (Roberts and Donaldson, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> Let us therefore no longer keep the Sabbath after the Jewish manner, and rejoice in days of idleness
> let every friend of Christ keep the Lord's Day as a festival, the resurrection-day, the queen and chief of all the days
**Note:** The words are there, but only in the longer recension, a fourth-century expansion. They are not in the shorter text that the date "c. 110" belongs to (C30). Quoted under Ignatius's name with an early date, they read differently from the held shorter text.

### C34 · Justin, First Apology 67
**Claim:** The first description of a Sunday service: Christians gather on the day of the Sun, read the apostles and prophets, pray and share the Eucharist, giving creation and the resurrection as the reasons.

### C105 · Justin, Dialogue with Trypho 48
**Claim:** Trypho calls a pre-existent God born a man "not merely paradoxical, but also foolish". Justin answers that the proof that Jesus is the Christ "does not fail, though I be unable to prove that He existed formerly as Son of the Maker of all things, being God, and was born a man by the Virgin", and that "there are some... of our race, who admit that He is Christ, while holding Him to be man of men; with whom I do not agree". The manuscript reads "our race"; some editors propose "your race".
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/justin-martyr-dialogue-with-trypho-full-text.md` (Dods and Reith, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> this [assertion] appears to me to be not merely paradoxical, but also foolish.
> [the proof] that this man [2096] is the Christ of God does not fail, though I be unable to prove that He existed formerly as Son of the Maker of all things, being God, and was born a man by the Virgin.
> of our race, [2097] who admit that He is Christ, while holding Him to be man of men; with whom I do not agree, nor would I,
> [2097] Some read, "of your race," referring to the Ebionites.
**Note:** The quotations are word for word. The concession is conditional ("though I be unable"), and Justin goes on to argue pre-existence and the virgin birth at length from chapter 55 (the chapter 63 heading is "It is proved that this God was incarnate"), so "a claim he might not be able to prove" is the sheet's reading of a debating move. The sentence continues "even though most of those who have [now] the same opinions as myself should say so". ANF note 2097 gives "your race" as a variant and reports the editor Maranus defending "our". Justin uses no word like "heretic" for them here, as the sheet says.

### C109 · Justin, Dialogue with Trypho 88 and 103
**Claim:** The Bezae reading of Luke 3:22, "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee", "is quoted as the text of Luke by Justin Martyr (Dialogue with Trypho 88 and 103, around 155)".
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/justin-martyr-dialogue-with-trypho-full-text.md` (Dods and Reith, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> a voice, which was uttered also by David when he spoke, personating Christ, what the Father would say to Him: Thou art My Son: this day have I begotten Thee;'
> at the time when the voice spake to Him, Thou art my Son: this day have I begotten Thee,' [2344] is recorded in the memoirs of the apostles to have come to Him and tempted Him
**Note:** Both chapters give the baptism voice in the Psalm 2:7 form, as the sheet says. Neither names Luke. In 88 Justin presents the words as David's (Psalm 2:7); in 103 "the memoirs of the apostles" is cited for the temptation that followed, with the voice in a time clause. ANF note 2344 points to Psalm 2:7 and Matthew 3:17. So the held text supports "Justin knew the voice in the Psalm 2:7 form"; "as the text of Luke" is the sheet's inference. (C32 is a different claim from chapter 103.)

### C110 · Ignatius, Ephesians, salutation
**Claim:** Ignatius calls Jesus "our God" without qualification (Ephesians, opening).
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/ignatius-epistles-full-text.md` (Roberts and Donaldson, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> by the will of the Father, and Jesus Christ, our God:
> by the will of God the Father, and of our Lord Jesus Christ our Saviour:
**Note:** The first line is the shorter recension. The second is the fourth-century longer recension, which drops "our God". The same happens in C111 to C114: in every one of these passages the later editor of Ignatius lowered the wording.
