### C99 · Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.26.1-2
**Claim:** The Ebionites "hold the same opinion of Christ as Cerinthus and Carpocrates", whom Irenaeus has just described as teaching that Jesus was born of Joseph and Mary like other men and that the Christ descended on him at his baptism.
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> but their opinions with respect to the Lord are similar to those of Cerinthus and Carpocrates.
> He represented Jesus as having not been born of a virgin, but as being the son of Joseph and Mary according to the ordinary course of human generation
> Moreover, after his baptism, Christ descended upon him in the form of a dove from the Supreme Ruler
**Note:** The first line is 1.26.2; the other two are 1.26.1, on Cerinthus. Carpocrates is 1.25.1 ("Jesus was the son of Joseph, and was just like other men"). ANF prints "similar" with no note. The Latin of 1.26.2 is usually reported to read "not similar" (*non similiter*), which editors correct from Hippolytus; we hold neither, so check an edition of the Latin before resting anything on that one word. The Matthew-only and Paul half of the same sentence is C1.

**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)

But not as some allege, among those now presuming to expound the Scripture, [thus:] "Behold, a young woman shall conceive, and bring forth a son," [3708] as Theodotion the Ephesian has interpreted, and Aquila of Pontus, [3709] both Jewish proselytes. The Ebionites, following these, assert that He was begotten by Joseph;
**Note:** Word for word. The Greek *neanis* and the Hebrew *almah* are the sheet's additions; the ANF English has "young woman" only. ANF note 3709 dates Theodotion's version to 181.

### C101 · Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.21.2-10
**Claim:** "In 3.21.2 his answer is not the Hebrew. It is the legend that seventy elders translated the Septuagint separately and produced word for word identical Greek"; "the defence of the virgin birth, at its first full statement, is a defence of a Greek translation against the Hebrew original."
**Result:** Reads differently
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> sent to Ptolemy seventy of their elders
> For all of them read out the common translation [which they had prepared] in the very same words and the very same names, from beginning to end
> For what great thing or what sign should have been in this, that a young woman conceiving by a man should bring forth,--a thing which happens to all women that produce offspring?
**Note:** 3.21.2-3 is the seventy-elders story, as the sheet says (first two lines). It is not his only answer. 3.21.4-10 go on to argue from Isaiah 7 itself (the "sign", third line, 3.21.6; "Hear, O house of David", 3.21.5), from Daniel's stone "cut out without hands" (3.21.7), from the curse on Jechoniah's line (3.21.9), and from Adam formed out of untilled ground (3.21.10). So the held text supports "his first answer is the Septuagint legend", not "his answer is the legend".

### C102 · Irenaeus, Against Heresies 5.1.3
**Claim:** "Vain also are the Ebionites, who do not receive by faith into their soul the union of God and man, but who remain in the old leaven of [the natural] birth... Therefore do these men reject the commixture of the heavenly wine, and wish it to be water of the world only"; so the Ebionites "celebrated the Lord's meal with water only, no wine".
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> Vain also are the Ebionites, who do not receive by faith into their soul the union of God and man, but who remain in the old leaven of [the natural] birth, and who do not choose to understand that the Holy Ghost came upon Mary
> Therefore do these men reject the commixture of the heavenly wine, [4454] and wish it to be water of the world only
> [4454] In allusion to the mixture of water in the eucharistic cup, as practised in these primitive times. The Ebionites and others used to consecrate the element of water alone.
**Note:** The quotation is word for word. Irenaeus's sentence is figurative (it is about refusing the union of God and man); reading it as a water-only Eucharist is the ANF footnote's. Epiphanius independently says the Ebionites used "for the other part of the mystery, water alone" (*Panarion* 30.16.1b, Wescoe), so the practice is attested in a second held text. The link to James's Nazirite abstinence is the sheet's reading.

### C103 · Irenaeus, Against Heresies 5.1.3 ("old" and "new")
**Claim:** Irenaeus calls the Ebionite belief in a natural birth "the old leaven" and the virgin birth "a new [kind of] generation" that God "showed forth"; "the words are his" (after Charles Vaclavik, citing ANF 1, p. 527).
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> remain in the old leaven of [the natural] birth
> who effected the incarnation of this being, and showed forth a new [kind of] generation
**Note:** Both phrases are there. The square brackets are the translators' supplied words: "natural" and "kind of" are not in the Latin, so "the words are his" holds for "old leaven" and "new generation", not for "natural". Our file has no page numbers, so p. 527 can't be checked here.

### C104 · Irenaeus, Against Heresies 4.20.11
**Claim:** At John 1:18, Irenaeus (in Latin) is a witness for "only begotten God".
**Result:** Matches
**Held text:** `christianity/Incoming/irenaeus-against-heresies-full-text.md` (Roberts and Rambaut, ANF 1)
**Text says:**
> "The only-begotten God, [4097] which is in the bosom of the Father, He hath declared [Him];"
> This text, as quoted a short time ago, indicated the only-begotten Son;
**Note:** The second line is ANF note 4097, quoting the editor Harvey: an earlier quotation of the verse in the same work reads "Son" (he also points to 3.11.5). Harvey judges from the Syriac that "God" is what Irenaeus wrote and that the "Son" quotation was adjusted to the Vulgate. So the held Irenaeus has both readings, and its editor favours "God".
