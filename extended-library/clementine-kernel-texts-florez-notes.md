## What it is

A dating argument in three tiers. The Clementine Homilies and Recognitions are fourth-century books. Under both lies a lost early-third-century novel, the *Circuits of Peter* (c. 220-250). Under that, two older kernels: the *Kerygmata Petrou* (Peter's preachings, the Letter of Peter to James and the *Contestatio*) and the source in Recognitions 1.27-71, conventionally called the *Ascents of James*, both dated about 150-200. Those kernels keep the voice of a Torah-keeping, James-centered, anti-sacrifice, anti-Paul community that the later editors were already softening.

## The argument

Five independent lines, which the sheet says point the same way:
1. **External dating fixes the middle layer.** The novel uses Bardaisan (died 222), and Origen quotes it in the 230s, so it exists by about 230 and anything it absorbed is older.
2. **The kernels break the novel's style and frame.**
3. **The theology is unassimilated:** Torah, baptism replacing sacrifice, James above Peter, hostility to Paul, toned down by later editors.
4. **The community it assumes is second-century:** live opponents (Sadducees, Samaritans, followers of John), the flight and the Temple's fall.
5. **Epiphanius saw the free-standing works** (*Circuits of Peter*, *Ascents of James*) around 375.

## Its own cautions

The sheet's table of what is settled and what is disputed is its best feature, and anything we take from it should keep it:
- The lost base novel: near-consensus. Recognitions 1.27-71 as an older source dated 150-200: broadly accepted.
- That source being the same book as Epiphanius's *Ascents of James*: **disputed** (Jones rejects it; Epiphanius's Greek-proselyte story about Paul isn't in Recognitions 1).
- The Kerygmata as one distinct document: **contested**; Strecker himself admits no evidence for it outside the novel.
- **One limit on "early":** the Ascents source already knows and rewrites Acts, so it is later than Acts. Its value is as the earliest surviving counter-history to Acts from the James side.

## What we checked

29 new entries: 17 Matches, 3 Matches in another translation, 2 Matches under another section number, 4 Read differently, 3 Not held.

**The content claims hold up.** The Letter of Peter's "the man who is my enemy" is word for word in ANF (C230). Recognitions 1.71 does name both Caiaphas and Damascus (C247). The seven days on the steps, James thrown "headlong from the top of the steps" and left for dead, sacrifice as a concession (1.36), baptism replacing sacrifice (1.39, 1.54), and Panarion 30,16,7 on the *Ascents* all match.

### Where the held texts read differently

1. **"A certain hostile man" / "the enemy man" (Rec 1.70).** Smith's ANF has "some one of our enemies" and "that enemy". The text never names Saul; the chapter heading "Tumult Raised by Saul" and note 596 are the editor's. The Letter of Peter has "the man who is my enemy", not "the hostile man" (C246, C230, C231). Logged as other translation.
2. **"A fortified place in the country" (Rec 1.37, 1.39).** Not in our text. 1.39 says believers "shall be kept unhurt from the destruction of war" and names no place (C242).
3. **"Quench the fire of the altars" as Homilies 11.19.** It is Homilies 3.26 (C235).
4. **Homilies 17.19, "a full year".** Peter asks why "our teacher" taught the disciples for a whole year, not whether Jesus taught Simon (C240). The "condemned" echo of Galatians 2:11 holds, with two cautions: the chapter's first "condemned" is an editor's emendation, and the KJV of Galatians 2:11 reads "to be blamed", so the echo only shows in the Greek (C239).
5. **Rec 1.27-71's "own narrator".** In the held text it is Peter's own discourse to Clement, broken at 1.44 (C248). The difference in content from the novel is real; the narrator point isn't.
6. **Panarion 30,16,9** has "the daughter of the priest", not "high priest" (C252).
7. **Hegesippus (Eusebius 2.23)** parallels Recognitions 1.70 but differs: Hegesippus has James thrown from the pinnacle and killed; Recognitions has him thrown from the steps, and he survives (C254).
