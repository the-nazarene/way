## Summary

*Jesus' Words Only* (eighteen chapters, four appendices, about 188,000 words) argues that the Bible supplies its own test for adding a writer to scripture, that the early church never applied it, and that when it is applied only Jesus passes. The test is assembled from Deuteronomy 4:2 (do not add to or diminish the commandments), Deuteronomy 13:1-5 (a prophet whose signs come true is still false if he draws you away from the commandments), Deuteronomy 18:20-22 (a prophet must predict in the Lord's name an unlikely event that comes to pass), and Isaiah 8:20. Jesus passes on both counts: he predicted the fall of the Temple and his own resurrection, and he affirmed every jot and tittle of the Law (Matthew 5:17-19). Paul, on Del Tondo's reading, has no qualifying prophecy and teaches that the Law of Moses is abolished, which makes him the kind of prophet Deuteronomy 13 and Matthew 7:15-23 warn about. The book's constructive proposal, "JWO," is that Jesus' words alone are the inspired part of the New Testament; the epistles of John, Peter, James and Jude stay as authoritative messengers of Jesus' teaching, and Paul's letters are to be read, if at all, the way Jerome read the Apocrypha: for edification, never for doctrine.

The method is forensic. Del Tondo is a lawyer, and the book reads like a brief: a rule is stated, each piece of evidence is tested against it, counter-arguments are quoted at length from Pauline commentators (Gill, Clarke, Barnes, Vincent, Robertson, Luther, Calvin, MacArthur, Stanley, Dillow, Wilkin, Ryrie) and then answered. He works almost entirely from the ASV and KJV, Strong's, Liddell-Scott, Young's Literal Translation and the free Interlinear Scripture Analyzer, with E-Sword commentary modules. His recurring move is to show that the harmonizations offered by Pauline commentators presuppose Paul's validity, which is the question at issue; he calls this the "bootstrap fallacy" and applies it to dispensationalism, covenant theology, Calvin on "inherit," and the translation of John 3:16.

The argument builds in layers. Chapters 1-5 set up the test and apply it to Paul's teaching on the Law. Chapters 6-7 take the one command Jesus repeats three times in Revelation (do not eat meat sacrificed to idols) against the three places Paul permits it, and identify the "teaching of Balaam" in Revelation 2:14 as Paul's. Chapters 8-9 compare salvation doctrine: the Synoptics, John (read with the Greek present tense as "keeps on believing"), and Revelation all require repentance, endurance and works; Paul does not. Chapters 10-13 reconstruct a historical trial of Paul at Ephesus from Revelation 2:2, 2 Timothy 1:15 and 4:16, Acts 19, the Epistle of James (read as a brief for the judges), the Ebionite records in Epiphanius, the Dead Sea Scrolls as Eisenman reads them, Clementine Homily 17, and John's epistles. Chapter 14 adds the prophecy of the Benjamite wolf (Genesis 49:27) and Ezekiel 22. Chapters 15-16 trace how Jesus was marginalized to make room for Paul (Marcion, Luther, Calvin, dispensationalism) and argue that the church of 125-325 and the Orthodox East never held Paul's distinctive doctrines. Chapters 17-18 lay out what preaching from Jesus' words only would look like and conclude that Jesus is the sole teacher and the apostles were messengers and judges, not oracles.

Two things about the book's own position are worth knowing before reading it against this repo. Del Tondo is a conservative evangelical in most respects: he holds that Jesus is the "I AM" who gave the Law at Sinai, that Jesus' death is the once-for-all atonement that fulfilled the Law of sacrifice, that Revelation is apostolic and inspired, that the Millennium is literal, and that the Hebrew Matthew was written before 65 AD. His quarrel is not with any of that; it is with the claim that Paul's letters are inspired. And he is not a vegetarian or a full Torah-observance writer for Gentiles: he reads the kosher laws as health rules rather than moral commands, distinguishes Israel-only commands from sojourner commands the way he says James did in Acts 15, and keeps Sabbath, Passover and the idol-meat rule as binding. People who share this repo have read him selectively (see the Shabbat Studies notes), which is the right way to read any book, and these notes try to say what is actually in it.

## Chapter by Chapter

### Chapter 1 — If a later prophet diminishes a prior prophet, he is a false prophet
The thesis and the test. Deuteronomy 4:2 and 13:1-5 mean a later prophet is judged by the earlier ones; Jesus and Moses therefore test Paul, not the reverse. Jesus' warnings about signs-and-wonders prophets (Matthew 7:15-23, 24:11, 24; Mark 13:22) quote Deuteronomy 13, and the word *anomia* in Matthew 7:23 is read as "negation of the Law." A survey of canon history (Carthage 397, Trent, Luther's 1522 preface, Metzger's "self-authenticating" canon) finds that no council ever stated a biblical criterion for inspiration; the lists were reading lists. One short line carries the chapter: "Books do not authenticate or impose themselves." (ch. 1). The twelve apostles are messengers (*apostoloi*) and judges (binding and loosing, Matthew 16:19; 19:28), not prophets whose every word is scripture; the epistles of John, Peter, James and Jude never claim inspiration, while Paul repeatedly does (1 Corinthians 14:37; 1 Thessalonians 4:8; 2:13). Acts 15 shows Paul as a messenger carrying a question to James; Acts 21:18-26 shows James still uninformed of Paul's actual teaching on the Law and Paul not correcting him. Balaam (Numbers 22-24, 31; Revelation 2:14) is introduced as the model of a true prophet who later apostatized. A historical note argues the Pharisees' fault was negating the Law by tradition, not legalism (Matthew 15, 23:23; Josephus, *Antiquities* 13.10.6).

### Chapter 2 — Does Paul's long acceptance in the New Testament prove God's will?
Against the "sovereignty of God" objection (God would not have let an error stand for two thousand years). Deuteronomy itself was lost for generations (2 Kings 22); 1 Chronicles 29:29 names three prophetic books now lost; 1 Enoch has been in the Ethiopian canon for two millennia and was quoted by Jude, so either Ethiopia has wrongly added or the West has wrongly subtracted; the longer ending of Mark was added centuries later. Tradition is not a criterion (Luther on Galatians; 1 John 4:1). Luther rejected James and Revelation, Calvin rejected 2 Peter, so neither treated long acceptance as proof. The earliest tradition excluded Paul: the Ebionites (dated here to about 65 AD) and Tertullian's *Against Marcion* (207 AD). Jerome's inclusion of the Apocrypha "for edification, not doctrine," later forgotten and treated as inspiration by Trent, is offered as the model of how Paul's letters were joined to the canon and then misunderstood.

### Chapter 3 — Must we apply the Bible's tests for a true prophet to Paul?
The tests in detail. A valid prophecy must be made in the Lord's name, must predict something improbable (Jeremiah 28:8-9 on predictable doom), and must come true; a second-level test rejects anyone who then "seduces you from the way" (Deuteronomy 13:5; Isaiah 8:20), with Norman Geisler quoted in support. The McDowell "authoritative, dynamic, received" test is traced to the Shepherd of Hermas (Mandate 11) and used to explain Hebrews' presence in the canon. Paul's one candidate prophecy, the shipwreck (Acts 27:22-25), is attributed to an angel without God present, is not improbable, and contradicts his own earlier prediction (27:10). Balaam's Star Prophecy (Numbers 24:17) is used to show that true prophecy and belief in Messiah did not save Balaam; a long excursus on the Magi, Daniel 9:25-26 (444 BC plus 483 lunar years as 33 AD), Zoroaster as Daniel's student, and Babylon as the harlot who knows the true God but has the wrong salvation principles.

### Chapter 4 — Did Jesus warn of false prophets who would negate the Law?
The *anomia* argument. Liddell-Scott gives "negation of the law" as a sense of *anomia*; read that way, Matthew 7:15-23 parallels Deuteronomy 13:1-5 clause by clause, whereas "iniquity" would make every human prophet false. Paul says he became *anomos*, "without the Law," in 1 Corinthians 9:20-21. If "iniquity" is insisted on, Paul still qualifies: he curses (Galatians 1:8-9), calls brethren fools (3:1), boasts (2 Corinthians 11), and condemns Peter publicly (Galatians 2:11), each against a command of Jesus or James (Matthew 5:22, 7:1, 18:15; James 3:10, 4:16). Matthew 5:18-19 and 15:1-9 (with Isaiah 29:13 and Nehemiah Gordon on the Karaite reading) show Jesus' view that the Law continues and that teaching men's commandments produces empty worship.

### Chapter 5 — Did Paul negate the Law's further applicability?
The long central chapter. Ephesians 2:15, Colossians 2:14-17 (including the Sabbath), 2 Corinthians 3:6-17 ("ministry of death... done away"), Romans 7:1-6 (*katargeō*, Jews "released"), Romans 14:5, 1 Timothy 4:4 are read with Luther, Gill, Barnes and Vincent agreeing that Paul abolished the whole Law, moral law included. Paul's replacement ethic is expediency ("all things are lawful," 1 Corinthians 6:12, 10:23; Romans 14:22), illustrated by Bob George on fornication. The most serious charge: Galatians 3:19 says the Law was ordained by angels, 4:8-9 calls them "no gods" and "weak and beggarly elements" (read as angels, with Vincent and Jubilees 2), while Romans 13 demands submission to Roman rulers as God's ministers; and the Hagar/Sarah allegory (4:21-31) is "the Great Inversion," making Israel into Ishmael. Jude 4 and 8 ("rail at dignities") are read as aimed at Paul. Jesus says God spoke in the bush (Mark 12:26) and that disbelieving Moses makes belief in him impossible (Luke 16:31; John 5:46). Matthew 5:18's two "untils" and Genesis 3:15 put the Law's expiry at the passing of heaven and earth. Luther's 1525 sermon "How Christians Should Regard Moses" is quoted as the clearest Pauline reading, with Luther's later Antinomian Theses (1537) as his repentance. James's Acts 15 ruling is explained by the Law's own Israel/sojourner distinction (Leviticus 17, 20; Deuteronomy 14:21), so the burden on Gentiles was never large. The pro-Law Pauline verses (Romans 3:31, 7:12; 1 Corinthians 7:19; Acts 24:14) are answered, Acts 24:14 producing a dilemma (either Paul or Luke is lying). Trophimus in the Temple (Acts 21:28-29) against Ezekiel 44:9, with the Temple's destruction in 70 AD as God's enforcement of the still-valid Law. Jeremiah 31:31-34 and Isaiah 42:21 close it.

### Chapter 6 — Paul contradicts Jesus about idol meat
The three-against-three argument. James prohibits idol meat three times (Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25), grounded in Exodus 34:13-16; Jesus prohibits it three times in Revelation (2:6, 14, 20) and calls it a *skandalon*; Paul permits it three times (Romans 14:21; 1 Corinthians 8:4-13; 10:19-29), restricting it only for the sake of a "weaker brother." Robertson's note that the Nicolaitans justified it by "not under law but under grace" and Irenaeus on Nicolas are used to say Jesus was attacking a Pauline teaching. Pastors Barnes and Hill are quoted admitting Paul's permission. Revelation 2:2's "liar" and 1 John 2:4's "liar" are linked. The fruit test (Matthew 7:15-20) is applied.

### Chapter 7 — Why does Jesus mention Balaam in Revelation 2:14?
Balaam as a parable of Paul: converted from enemy to prophet by an angelic encounter on a road, with companions, filled with the Spirit (Numbers 24:2), later teaching Israel to eat idol meat and fornicate (Numbers 31:16; Revelation 2:14); the donkey's "why have you smitten me" and the goads are set beside "why persecutest thou me" and "kick against the goads" (Acts 22:7; 26:14). Fornication in Hebrew (*zanah*) means adultery, so Paul's permission for a deserted wife to remarry (1 Corinthians 7:15) against Matthew 5:32 (in both the Greek and Howard's Hebrew Matthew) is the "fornication" Balaam taught. Galatians 5:19-21 is examined through MacArthur ("practice," occasional sin tolerated) and Dillow (loss of rewards only); 1 Corinthians 5:5 through Dillow, Hunt, Kendall and Gromacki as the strongest eternal-security text; Luther's "fornication and murder a thousand times a day" letter. Irenaeus's defense of Paul from Luke's Acts is answered: a vision on a road does not preclude a later Balaam.

### Chapter 8 — Does Jesus share salvation doctrine with Paul?
Luke 7:47: the Greek *hoti* makes her love a cause of forgiveness, and 7:50 is the only Synoptic passage where faith is said to save, mixed with love. Faith is otherwise absent from Synoptic salvation teaching, which is "works and attitudes" (Matthew 25:31-46; 19:17; 5:20; 16:27; Mark 9:42-48; the Talents; the Sower). Del Tondo proposes that the Synoptics were written after Paul's letters precisely to counter them. John's Gospel uses the present active participle for "believe" (3:16; 5:24; 6:35-47 and so on), which he renders "keeps on believing," with James White's *Drawn by the Father* conceding the continuous sense; Romans 10:9 uses the aorist. The Parable of the Sower (Luke 8:5-15) is worked verb by verb: the second seed "keeps on believing for a while" and falls; the third does not "produce to completion" (*telesphoreō*); only the fourth "keeps on bearing fruit with endurance." Luther's sermon on the Sower is shown injecting faith-alone into a parable that says the opposite.

### Chapter 9 — Is Jesus' salvation doctrine in Revelation a rebuttal to Paul?
Revelation is post-Pauline, apostolic, addressed to Gentile churches, and spoken by Jesus; it never mentions grace except in greetings, never faith as saving, and shows twelve foundation stones with no thirteenth (21:14). A Methodist minister's essay "Why the Book of Revelation Is Heresy" is quoted at length as an unwitting admission that Revelation contradicts Paul. The crown of life for the faithful (2:10 = James 1:12); works judged (2:23; 20:12-13; 22:12); Sardis's "incomplete works" and dying spark (3:1-3) matched to the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1-13, oil as Spirit, virgins as Christians, the NIV's "any oil" corrected) and to James 2:17; Laodicea spewed out (3:15-16). The Sheep and the Goats (Matthew 25:31-46) is laid in a table beside James 2:14-17 as "virtually verbatim copies," both groups calling Jesus Lord; Dillow's tribulation-only reading, Calvin's "inherit means gift," and Wilkin's "scripture cannot contradict itself" are answered; Daniel Fuller is quoted on reading each writer for himself. The seven churches are mapped to the four seeds of the Sower. Charity as central (Daniel 4:27; Isaiah 58; Deuteronomy 15:7-8).

### Chapter 10 — Did Jesus applaud the Ephesians for exposing Paul as a false apostle?
Revelation 2:2 commends Ephesus alone for trying men who said they were apostles and finding them liars; Ephesus is the one church of the seven Paul is known to have visited and the one he wrote to claiming apostleship (Ephesians 1:1). No one but Paul calls Paul an apostle of Jesus (Crossan and Reed: "for Luke, Paul is simply not an apostle"; Witherington on *apostoloi* in Acts 14 as agents of Antioch); the Twelve are twelve for eternity (Matthew 19:28; Revelation 21:14; James not replaced after Acts 12:2); and self-witness is invalid (John 5:31; Deuteronomy 19:15). The trial is located from 2 Timothy 1:15 ("all in Asia turned away from me"), 4:14-17 ("my first defense... all forsook me"), 2 Corinthians 1:8-9, and Acts 19:8-9 (Paul withdraws from the Ephesian synagogue after three months). Tertullian's *Against Marcion* is read as preserving the verdict: Paul is inferior to the Twelve, his apostleship rests on his own word, and he fits Jesus' warning of signs-and-wonders prophets. The church of 125-325 never taught Paul's distinctive doctrines; the Orthodox still keep Saturday Sabbath; Roman Catholicism as we know it begins after 325. Canon then meant a reading list. The charge of the "Jews from Asia" in Acts 21:28 (against the people and the Law) is matched to Jeremiah 31:31-37 and the "everlasting for all generations" ordinances. A note on Bonhoeffer's *Cost of Discipleship* as a modern JWO.

### Chapter 11 — Was James writing his epistle for a trial of Paul?
James 2:2-4 describes seating at a *synagōgē* judicial assembly (R. B. Ward 1969, via Stulac), and James was head bishop of the church (Acts 15:19 "I judge"; Eusebius; Hegesippus; Jerome *On Famous Men* 2; Epiphanius *Panarion* 29.3.4; Clement of Alexandria), so his letter is read as a doctrinal brief for the Ephesus judges. Luther's admissions that James contradicts Paul are quoted. Genesis 15:6 in Hebrew (Victor Hamilton, NICOT) can be read with Abram as the one reckoning, so the verse "had nothing to do with justification by faith"; the Septuagint (dated 247 BC, called badly translated with Nehemiah Gordon) created the ambiguity Paul relied on; 1 Maccabees 2:52 and James 2:21-24 take Abraham's faithfulness in Genesis 22 as the justifying act; Coffman and Whiteside on Abraham's prior righteousness. Deuteronomy 6:25 (obedience "shall be righteousness unto us") and Ezekiel 33:12 give the Hebrew Bible's own formula; Matthew 5:23-24 (*dōron*, *thusiastērion*) and the Mishnah's Day of Atonement rule make reconciliation precede atonement. Paul's quotation of Psalm 32:1-2 omits verse 5. "Paul was wrong. James was right." (ch. 11). Faith in Hebrew is inseparable from faithfulness (Deuteronomy 9:23); Habakkuk 2:4 reads *emunah*, "faithfulness," with H. Ray Dunning quoted on the Septuagint's *pistis*. James 2:19 (demons believe) answers Romans 10:9; James 1:13-15 answers Romans 7:7-13 (the Law arouses sin); James 3:17 ("without hypocrisy, without variance") is applied to 1 Corinthians 9:20-21, Acts 21, Timothy's circumcision against Galatians 5:4, Galatians 2:16 against Romans 2:13, Philippians 2:12-13, and the Cretan paradox of Titus 1:12; James 3-4 on boasting against 2 Corinthians 11.

### Chapter 12 — The Ebionite records on the trial of Paul
Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.16.6-9: the Ebionites said Paul was a Greek of Greek parents who came to Jerusalem, wanted the priest's daughter, was circumcised as a proselyte, and when refused wrote against circumcision, Sabbath and Law. Eusebius's hostility is explained by Constantine, the Roman bishop's lineage from Linus (appointed, on the *Apostolic Constitutions* 7.46, by Paul), and the book burnings under Valens and Theodosius that left no Ebionite text. Eisenman's thesis is adopted: the Dead Sea Scrolls' *ebyonim* are the Ebionites, the *Zaddik* is James, the "Spouter of Lies" is Paul, the Habakkuk Pesher reads 2:4 as faithfulness, and the Damascus Document records a confrontation; Golb's Jerusalem-library theory and the Magen-Peleg "pottery factory" excavation (2004) are cited as freeing the scrolls for a Christian-era date, with Margoliouth (1910) on "Sadducean Christians of Damascus." The Herodian Paul: Romans 16:11, Josephus's Saulus (*Antiquities* 20.9.4), Roman citizenship from birth (Acts 22:28), the Roman name, Manaen at Antioch (Acts 13:1). Clementine Homily 17.19 (Peter against the man who became an apostle "for a single hour" through a vision) is accepted as aimed at Paul, with Roberts, Griffith-Jones and Ehrman; Del Tondo then argues that Acts 22 is the vision Peter attacked and Acts 26 is Paul's revised account, composed to answer the charge, which is how Paul "escaped the mouth of the lion" (2 Timothy 4:17, the lion being Judah). Acts 9:7 against 22:9. 1 John 4:6, Matthew 10:14-15, Galatians 2:11-14 read with Peter in the right; Paul never quotes Jesus (Schweitzer, Campenhausen); 1 Corinthians 11:24 "broken" against Luke's "given." The Letter of Peter to James is boxed.

### Chapter 13 — Did John's epistles identify Paul as a false prophet?
1 John 4:1-6, 2:19 and 2 John 9 give four marks of the false prophet: denying Jesus came in human flesh, not listening to the Twelve, leaving their group, going beyond Christ's teaching. Paul refused the Twelve (Galatians 2:6), left them by a division of mission that Acts 15:7 (Peter as apostle to the Gentiles) makes implausible, and taught docetism: Romans 8:3 ("likeness of sinful flesh") and Philippians 2:7 ("likeness of men"), with Vincent's glosses taken as concessions; Marcion is said to have got his docetism straight from Paul. John's letters concern a teacher near Ephesus (Ivor Davidson). Papias quotes no Paul (Nielsen on Papias as polemicist against rising Paulinism); Polycarp probably did not know the Apostle John (Stuckwisch), so his "glorious Paul" is no counter-evidence. Tertullian's "no man is for himself both claimant and witness" is boxed.

### Chapter 14 — Who is the Benjamite wolf in prophecy?
Genesis 49:27 (Benjamin a ravening wolf: devours in the morning, divides the spoil at evening) beside Matthew 7:15 and Paul's own tribe (Romans 11:1; Philippians 3:5): the murderer of Christians who later divided the church between Gentile and Jewish missions. Hippolytus is quoted applying the verse to Paul; Gill's Targum reading (the Temple in Benjamin's lot) is rejected; Judges 19-21 and King Saul give the tribe's record. Ezekiel 22:26-30 lists the deeds of ravening wolves (violence to the Law, hiding eyes from Sabbaths, no clean/unclean distinction, false visions "thus saith the Lord," vexing the poor, oppressing the sojourner), each matched to Paul: the Law abolished, Sabbath optional, all foods clean, 2 Thessalonians 3:10, Titus 1:12 on Cretans, Galatians 4 making Jews foreigners and 1 Thessalonians 2:14-16 calling them enemies of all men, with Luther's antisemitism via Shirer as the fruit. Luke 21:8 ("the time is at hand... do not go after them") against Romans 13:12 ("the day is at hand").

### Chapter 15 — Does Jesus end up marginalized to make room for Paul?
Marcion's Paul-only canon was defeated by Tertullian's insistence that Paul fit Jesus; "It is Paul who must fit into the words of Christ in the Gospels. It is not the Gospel accounts which must be truncated to fit the words of Paul." (ch. 15). Luther's 1522 preface rates Paul and John above the Synoptics and rejects Revelation; Calvin makes John the key to the other three; Flacius subordinates scripture to the catechism; the Billy Graham Association's tract says Jesus came "that there might be a Gospel to preach"; Walvoord calls the Sermon on the Mount "unimportant truth"; C. S. Lewis makes the epistles more central than the Gospels. Dispensationalism (Larkin, Chafer, Stanford, a Baptist pastor's "What Would Paul Do?" sermon) is diagrammed as circular: its premise that Paul is inspired to define a covenant of grace is its conclusion. John 3:36 (*apeitheō*, "disobey"), John 3:16's subjunctive "should," John 5:28-29, 8:51 and 15:1-6 are offered as John's own anti-Pauline texts, and the lack of a progressive tense in German is suggested as one reason Luther's John 3:16 sounded Pauline.

### Chapter 16 — Long tradition of JWO and minimization of Paul
JWO was the first standard of orthodoxy (Hegesippus's "the Law, the Prophets, and the Lord"; the Ebionite canon dated 64 AD). Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 4.2 (Luke below Matthew and John; Paul's authority derived from the Twelve via Acts 15) and 5.1 (no trace of Paul in the apostle lists; "no man is for himself both claimant and witness"), and *Prescription* 24 (the third-heaven revelations qualify Paul for nothing), followed by 1.23-27 refuting predestination, total depravity and eternal security without naming Paul. The Paulicians as Marcionism's afterlife. Bercot's *Will the Real Heretics Please Stand Up* with Polycarp, 2 Clement, Hermas, Clement of Alexandria, Cyprian, Justin Martyr (*First Apology* 43), Methodius on free will; Irenaeus against idol-meat indifference; Calvin finding only Augustine. The Orthodox: no uniquely Pauline doctrine, baptismal regeneration, theosis, and a Saturday Sabbath (Socrates, *Church History* 5.22; Bingham); Trent's canons 9 and 11; Pelagius as a faith-alone teacher. The "Luther Rediscovery Thesis" is turned against itself with Tertullian's own argument from apostolic churches.

### Chapter 17 — Does it matter if we rely only upon Jesus?
What a JWO evangelist would say: justification by repentance (Luke 18:10-14); heaven maimed or hell whole (Mark 9:42-48); the rich young man and Zacchaeus ("Be a Zaccheus!"); the thief saved by public confession (Luke 12:8; 23:42) against the cowardly believing rulers of John 12:42 and Revelation 21:8; forgiveness revocable (Matthew 6:12; 18:23-35); denying Christ (Luke 12:4-9); fruit required (Matthew 3:10; 7:19; John 5:28-29 with Gathercole; John 15:1-6; the Talents; the Sower; the Sheep and Goats); Luke 13:24 as "use all your strength... they shall not have strength." Paul's message: Romans 10:9 plus 1 Corinthians 12:3, belief plus nothing (Ryrie, Wilkin, Sproul quoted). Even confession with the mouth, where Paul agrees with Jesus, is explained away by Paulinists. Table 10 sets the two systems side by side. Matthew 23:13-23 applied: a church that keeps only tithing. A historical note on Carlstadt, his 1520 *De canonicis scripturis* placing Gospels above epistles, his defense of James and the Law, the Evangelical Brotherhood, and Luther's role in the slaughter of the peasants.

### Chapter 18 — Conclusion: preach and teach from Jesus' words only
Deuteronomy 13:3 makes discerning false prophets the test of loving God. Paul is a false apostle (self-witness only; Tertullian) and the prophesied ravening wolf; he fits Matthew 7:15-23, Luke 21:8, Revelation 2:20, and claimed signs and wonders for himself (Romans 15:19; 2 Corinthians 12:12); Galatians 1:8 puts his gospel above the angels he said gave the Law. Jesus is the sole teacher (Matthew 23:8-10); the Spirit speaks only what it hears from Jesus (John 16:13-14; 14:26), so no new doctrine comes after him, only explanation and prophecy of things to come; John the Baptist decreased; the messenger is not greater than the sender (John 13:16); 2 Thessalonians 2:15 is to be rejected. Binding and loosing is judicial, not legislative, and binding only when joint (Matthew 20:25-27 on *archontes*; the Sanhedrin), so no single apostle, let alone Paul, could bind doctrine. Two masters (Matthew 6:24). The Great Commission plus John 8:51: faith and obedience together "should" save, and endurance to the end "shall." Translation as suppression: John 3:16 ("believes," "shall"), 3:36 ("disbelieve"), Genesis 15:6 (the capitalized "He"), Habakkuk 2:4. Charles Stanley on weeping and gnashing of teeth as a place in heaven. MacArthur's *Hard to Believe* as a Calvinist moving toward JWO; Bonhoeffer; Luther's missed chance to attack indulgences from Jesus' words. The goal: a Bible without a false apostle's letters.

### Appendix A — Greek issues
The present active participle and indicative in John denote continuous action, against the aorist as punctiliar; James White, Dale Moody (John 10:27-28 "keep on hearing... keep on following"), Dana and Mantey, Hewitt. Matthew 10:22 uses the aorist for "endured." The NIV adds "keep on" or "continue to" in seventeen-plus places but not where it would touch faith-alone or eternal security.

### Appendix B — How the canon was formed
The Ebionite canon (Hebrew Matthew only, 64-70 AD), Marcion's (144), the Muratorian Fragment (dated to the fourth century with Hahneman), Origen (240; Hebrew Matthew; 2 Peter disputed), the Hebrew Matthew and Marcan priority, Robinson's early dating, Eusebius (324; doubts about Revelation for political reasons), Laodicea (363; canon 60 omits Revelation and Enoch), Athanasius (367), the Syrian Apostolic Canons (380), Rufinus (380), Carthage (397), Luther (1522), Trent. 2 Peter as the one consistently rejected book, a pseudograph (Calvin via Metzger; Eusebius; Peter Kirby).

### Appendix C — The Easter error
Jesus' "do this in remembrance" was a change to steps four and thirteen of the Passover Seder, not a new rite; Polycarp kept 14 Nisan from the apostles (Eusebius 5.24); Nicaea 325 moved the date on Constantine's anti-Jewish rationale (Theodoret); the English name comes from Eostre (Bede); the Quartodecimans were excommunicated; all non-English Christianity still calls the feast Pascha. Jeroboam's invented month (1 Kings 12:33) and Daniel 7:25 ("change the times and the Law"). Jesus intended his Jewish apostles to keep Passover; Gentiles may.

### Appendix D — The Abrahamic covenant
The covenant is Genesis 17:1-7, conditioned on "walk before me and be blameless," confirmed to Isaac because Abraham obeyed (Genesis 26:4-5), not Genesis 15:5-6 as Paul claims; Deuteronomy 6:25, Ezekiel 33, Daniel 9:4 and John 8:51 share one formula: obedience justifies, repentance restores, atonement applies to the already-reconciled (Matthew 5:23-24).

## Key Arguments

1. **The Deuteronomy test, and only Jesus passes it** (ch. 1, 3). Deuteronomy 4:2, 13:1-5 and 18:20-22 are the Bible's criteria for adding to scripture; no council ever applied them; Jesus' Temple-fall and resurrection prophecies qualify him, and his Law-keeping keeps him valid; Paul has no qualifying prophecy and fails the second-level test.
2. ***Anomia* means negation of the Law, and Paul admits to it** (ch. 4, 18). Matthew 7:23's "workers of *anomia*" paraphrases Deuteronomy 13; Paul's "I became *anomos*" (1 Corinthians 9:21) is a confession in the same vocabulary.
3. **Paul abolished the whole Law, Sabbath included, and attributed it to angels** (ch. 5). Ephesians 2:15, Colossians 2:14-17, 2 Corinthians 3, Romans 7, Galatians 3:19 and 4:8-9, and the Hagar allegory, with Luther as witness to what Paul meant.
4. **Idol meat is the test case** (ch. 6-7). James three times, Jesus three times in Revelation, Paul three times the other way; the "teaching of Balaam" in Revelation 2:14 is Paul's, and Balaam's biography mirrors Paul's.
5. **Jesus' salvation doctrine is faith plus repentance, endurance and works** (ch. 8, 9, 17, App. A). The Synoptic parables (Sower, Talents, Ten Virgins, Sheep and Goats), John read in the present tense, and the seven letters of Revelation all require fruit brought to completion; Romans 10:9 and Ephesians 2:8-9 do not.
6. **Revelation is Jesus' post-Pauline verdict** (ch. 9, 10). Written after Paul, it never mentions his grace doctrine, fixes the apostles at twelve, condemns his idol-meat teaching, and commends Ephesus for trying a false apostle.
7. **There was a trial of Paul at Ephesus, and James's epistle was its brief** (ch. 10, 11). Revelation 2:2, 2 Timothy 1:15 and 4:16, 2 Corinthians 1:8-9, Acts 19:9 and James 2:2-4 are read together; Tertullian preserves the verdict.
8. **Paul misread Genesis 15:6 and Habakkuk 2:4 through the Septuagint** (ch. 11, 18, App. D). The Hebrew allows Abram as the one reckoning; *emunah* is faithfulness; James and 1 Maccabees 2:52 read the verses the Hebrew way; the real Abrahamic covenant (Genesis 17) is conditioned on obedience.
9. **The Ebionites were the Jerusalem church, and their records survive in Epiphanius, the Scrolls and the Clementines** (ch. 12). The priest's-daughter charge, Eisenman's *ebyonim*/*Zaddik*/Spouter identifications, Homily 17.19, and Acts 22 versus 26 as Paul's revised testimony.
10. **John's epistles and Genesis 49:27 name Paul without naming him** (ch. 13, 14). The four marks of 1-2 John, Paul's "likeness" language as docetism, the Benjamite wolf, Ezekiel 22's ravening wolves.
11. **Paulinism is Marcionism, and dispensationalism is its logic** (ch. 15). Luther, Calvin, Lewis, Graham, Walvoord and the dispensationalists all subordinate Jesus' words to Paul's; the harmonization is a bootstrap fallacy.
12. **The church of 125-325 and the Orthodox East never held Paul's distinctive doctrines** (ch. 16). Tertullian, Polycarp, 2 Clement, Justin, Methodius, Irenaeus, Bercot's synthesis; the "Luther Rediscovery Thesis" requires every apostolic church to have apostatized at once.
13. **Jesus is the sole teacher; the apostles were messengers and judges** (ch. 1, 18). Matthew 23:8-10, John 16:13-14, John 13:16; binding and loosing is judicial and joint; no apostle, and certainly no thirteenth, legislates doctrine.

## Claims to Check

Specific factual or historical statements a careful reader should verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation, and some are where the book is strongest.

1. **Jude quotes Enoch "as the words of true prophecy (Jude 17)"** (ch. 2). The Enoch quotation is Jude 14-15; verse 17 is about the apostles' predictions. Minor, but it is in a load-bearing paragraph.
2. **1 Enoch was "part of universal Christianity's canon until 363 A.D." and was "dropped" at Laodicea** (ch. 2, App. B). Tertullian defended Enoch (*De Cultu Feminarum* 1.3), but no surviving canon list before Laodicea includes it, and Origen, Jerome and Augustine treat it as outside the canon. "Widely read and sometimes treated as scripture" survives; "universal canon" does not. The Ethiopian canon point is sound.
3. **Laodicea's canon 60 omitted Revelation and Enoch for political reasons, and later Catholic historians suppressed the canon** (App. B). Canon 60's authenticity is disputed (Del Tondo acknowledges this); its omission of Revelation is a real feature of the Eastern lists of the period (Cyril of Jerusalem, Gregory Nazianzen also omit it). The Constantine/Eusebius motive and the Dionysius Exiguus cover-up are the book's inferences.
4. **Tertullian said Paul was "not inspired," "possibly a false prophet," and that this was the "verdict at Ephesus"** (ch. 2, 10, 15, 16, 18). *Against Marcion* 5.1 does demand proof of Paul's apostleship and says no one is his own witness; 4.2 does place Luke below Matthew and John and derive Paul's authority from the Twelve via Acts 15. But Tertullian immediately claims Paul as "mine," defends him through five books, and calls him the apostle of the heretics only in Marcion's hands (3.5). The repo's own note (`09-ch4d`, Claims to Check 3) says: use the rhetorical challenge, not the conclusion. The link to an Ephesus verdict is Del Tondo's reconstruction.
5. **Tertullian says Barnabas wrote Hebrews** (ch. 1, 3). True (*De Pudicitia* 20), and it is one ancient opinion among several; Origen's "God knows" is the other.
6. **LSJ gives "negation of the law" as a meaning of *anomia*, and the word targets the Law of Moses** (ch. 4). LSJ lists "lawlessness, lawless conduct" and "the negation of law" (as a condition). The step from "negation of law" to "negator of the Mosaic Law" is interpretive; *anomia* also renders generic "sin" in the Septuagint and appears in Matthew 23:28 and 24:12 where "lawlessness" fits. The Deuteronomy 13 parallel is the real argument; the lexicon does not settle it alone.
7. **"All things are lawful" is Paul's own axiom, not a Corinthian slogan he is quoting** (ch. 5, 7). Most commentators and several translations (NIV, NRSV) treat 1 Corinthians 6:12 and 10:23 as quoted slogans Paul qualifies. Del Tondo rejects that reading in a sentence; a reader should know it is the majority reading.
8. **Galatians 4:8's "no gods" and 4:9's "weak and beggarly elements" are the angels of 3:19** (ch. 5, 18). The "elements" (*stoicheia*) are variously read as elemental spirits, pagan deities, or basic principles; 4:8 is most often read of the pagan gods the Galatians formerly served. Vincent is quoted fairly; the identification with the Law-giving angels is one reading.
9. **Paul "cut off Israel" and made the Jews Ishmael** (ch. 5, 10, 14, 18). Galatians 4:21-31 is quoted accurately. Romans 11:1-2 and 11:26 ("God has not cast away his people"; "all Israel shall be saved") are not engaged; the book notes elsewhere that Marcion's Romans lacked chapters 9-11. A fair presentation gives both.
10. **Jude 8's "dignities" are angels, and Jude is aimed at Paul** (ch. 5). The reading of "glories" as angels is standard; the targets of Jude are usually taken as libertine teachers of the late first century. Aiming it at Paul is inference.
11. **Trophimus entered the Temple, and the destruction of 70 AD was God's response to that breach** (ch. 5). Acts 21:29 says the Jews "supposed" Paul had brought him in; the text never says Trophimus entered. The causal link to 70 AD is theology, not history.
12. **Deuteronomy was lost for 300 years with no copy made** (ch. 2). 2 Kings 22 reports a book of the Law found; "no copy" and "300 years" are inference.
13. **The Daniel 9 arithmetic (444 BC + 483 prophetic years = 33 AD) and the Magi trained by Daniel through Zoroaster** (ch. 3). The 360-day-year calculation is Anderson/Hoehner's scheme and is disputed on both the start date and the year length; Zoroaster as Daniel's pupil rests on a 13th-century Syriac chronicle via Hyde (1700) and Faber (1816). Legend, not history.
14. **"Irenaeus… Refutation of All Heresies, 7.24" on Nicolas** (ch. 6 fn.). The *Refutation* is Hippolytus's; Irenaeus's Nicolaitan reference is *Against Heresies* 1.26.3. Also, in App. B, "Irenaeus… wrote about A.D. 125" is a misreading of the Catholic Encyclopedia sentence about Papias; Irenaeus wrote c. 180.
15. **The donkey's "goads" and Acts 26:14** (ch. 7). Numbers 22 has no goads; "kick against the goads" is a Greek proverb (Euripides, *Bacchae* 795; Pindar). The Shabbat Studies 4A notes cover this. The "why persecutest thou me" parallel to the donkey's question is a reading, not a verbal link.
16. ***Porneia* in Matthew 5:32 "can only mean adultery," and Howard's Hebrew Matthew shows the original** (ch. 7). *Zanah* covers prostitution and cultic unfaithfulness as well as adultery; *porneia*'s range in Greek is broader still. Howard's Shem-Tob text is a 14th-century Hebrew Matthew whose priority is a minority position (Howard himself argued for an early substratum; most scholars see a medieval translation preserving some old readings). Use it as a variant, not as the original.
17. **1 Corinthians 5:5 means Paul ordered the man killed, as Tertullian understood** (ch. 7). Most readers take it as excommunication; Tertullian's *Against Marcion* 5.7 treats the "destruction of the flesh" as bodily affliction under discipline. Check the passage before saying Tertullian read it as execution.
18. **Luke 7:50 is the only Synoptic passage where faith is said to save, and the Synoptics "were written specifically to counter the message of Paul"** (ch. 8). Luke 8:12 ("believe and be saved"), 17:19 and 18:42 use the same verb; and no scholar the book cites supports the counter-Paul purpose. The repo's own open question (`OPEN-RESEARCH.md` §4) is whether Mark is Pauline.
19. **The present participle in John 3:16 means "keeps on believing," only Young's Literal translates it rightly, and the Vulgate preserved the sense** (ch. 8, 15, App. A). The continuous force of the substantival participle (*ho pisteuōn*, "the one believing") is debated; Wallace (whom the book cites against) and most grammars treat it as generic rather than durative. James White and Dale Moody are quoted accurately. Latin has no progressive present either. Present it as an argument from aspect, not a translation fact.
20. **The Westminster Confession of 1647 "initially excluded the Book of Revelation"** (ch. 9). False; WCF 1.2 lists Revelation. Calvin wrote no commentary on Revelation (true; also none on 2-3 John, as `FIX-LIST.md` notes).
21. **Ephesus is the only one of the seven churches Paul visited, and 2 Timothy 1:15 and 4:16 record a church trial there** (ch. 10, 12). Acts 19:10 has all Asia hearing Paul; Colossians 4:13-16 implies contact with Laodicea. 2 Timothy 4:16's "first defense" is normally read as a Roman legal hearing, and the letter's setting is Rome; 2 Corinthians 1:8's "affliction in Asia" is unexplained in the text. The book also relies on 2 Timothy, which it elsewhere treats as doubtful.
22. **Genesis 15:6 "had nothing to do with justification by faith" because Abram is the one reckoning** (ch. 11, 18, App. D). Hamilton presents the Abram reading as grammatically possible, not as the meaning; Nehemiah 9:8, Psalm 106:31, 1 Maccabees 2:52, Jubilees 14:6, 4QMMT and James 2:23 all take God as the subject. `09-ch4d` Claims to Check 21 says the same. The Septuagint is dated by tradition to the third century BC; "247 BC" and "very bad translators" are rhetoric.
23. **Habakkuk 2:4 reads "faithfulness," and the Qumran pesher reads it that way** (ch. 11, 12, 18). Accurate, and the repo agrees (Tabor; `OPEN-RESEARCH.md` §2; 1QpHab 8). The Habakkuk Pesher is not held in the repo; cite at reference level.
24. **Eusebius "was quoting Epiphanius"** (ch. 12). Chronologically impossible (Eusebius died c. 339; the *Panarion* is c. 375). Eusebius 3.27 has the Ebionites rejecting Paul; the priest's-daughter story is Epiphanius only.
25. **The Scrolls' *ebyonim* are the Ebionites; James is the Zaddik; Paul the Spouter; Qumran was a pottery factory; Golb is "vindicated"** (ch. 12). Eisenman and Golb are minority positions; paleography and radiocarbon place most sectarian scrolls before the Christian era; Magen and Peleg's pottery-production thesis is contested. The repo's do-not-use list covers the *ebyonim* identification. "Carbon dating only dates the paper" is misleading: it bounds the writing date.
26. **Paul was a Herodian: Romans 16:11, Josephus's Saulus, Roman citizenship, a Roman birth name** (ch. 12). *Syngenēs* means fellow-Jew in Romans 9:3 and 16:7, 21; Josephus's Saulus is active in the 60s, after Paul's conversion, so the chronology in the book is backwards; many Diaspora Jews carried a second Latin or Greek name. Eisenman's thesis; label it.
27. **Acts 26 was invented by Paul at the trial to answer Peter's Homily 17 charge; the "lion" of 2 Timothy 4:17 is Judah** (ch. 12). The Homily 17.19 passage is verbatim in the held text. The rest is reconstruction, and the book cites "2 Timothy 2:17" where it means 4:17.
28. **Paul quotes Jesus only at Acts 20:35 and the Supper words, and 1 Corinthians 11:24's "broken" contradicts Luke** (ch. 12, 18). 1 Corinthians 7:10-11, 9:14 and 1 Thessalonians 4:15 are also sayings attributed to the Lord; "broken" (*klōmenon*) is a later textual variant, and the earliest manuscripts read "which is for you."
29. **Paul was "100% docetic" (Romans 8:3; Philippians 2:7)** (ch. 13). Galatians 4:4, Romans 1:3 and 9:5, and 1 Corinthians 15:21 affirm real flesh, which is why Marcion had to cut them. "Language Marcion could use" is defensible; "Paul denied the flesh" is not (`09-ch4d` Claims to Check 4).
30. **Papias's fragments quote no Paul; Polycarp did not know the Apostle John** (ch. 13). Both rest on real articles (Nielsen 1974; Stuckwisch 1997) and both are minority positions against Irenaeus's testimony.
31. **Hippolytus said Genesis 49:27 "thoroughly fits Paul"** (ch. 14, 18). The fragment is genuine (Hippolytus, *On the Blessings of Jacob*), cited through Bercot's dictionary code "W 5.168"; find the ANF or GCS locus before citing. Hippolytus reads the verse in Paul's favor.
32. **Luke 21:8's "the time is at hand" is "exactly identical Greek" to Romans 13:12** (ch. 14, 18). Same verb (*ēngiken*), different noun (*kairos* / *hēmera*). The same verb is in Jesus' own "the kingdom of God is at hand" (Mark 1:15), so the verb alone cannot mark a false prophet.
33. **1 Thessalonians 2:14-16 is Paul's slur on all Jews, and Luther's antisemitism is its fruit** (ch. 14). The passage is widely argued to be an interpolation (Pearson 1971), which the book does not mention; the Luther quotations via Shirer are accurate; the causal chain to 1933 is interpretation.
34. **Luther "declared" James and Revelation uninspired in 1522; Calvin "said 2 Peter was a forgery"** (ch. 2, 9, 15, App. B). Luther's prefaces disparage both and set them apart with Hebrews and Jude, but keep them in the volume; he softened the Revelation preface in 1530. Calvin's commentary doubts Petrine style and concludes a disciple wrote at Peter's direction, accepting it as canonical. Metzger's "probably not written by the apostle himself" is the most the quotation supports.
35. **Romans 8:1 and 8:39 are "never once cited by any patristic-era father"** (ch. 16, from the e-catena index). Origen's *Commentary on Romans* covers both; Irenaeus and Tertullian cite Romans 8 freely. Check the index's coverage before repeating.
36. **The Orthodox "keep the Saturday Sabbath" and hold "not one iota of anything uniquely Pauline"** (ch. 10, 16). Orthodoxy honors Saturday liturgically (no fasting, Divine Liturgy permitted) but does not keep it as a Mosaic rest day; Orthodoxy venerates Paul and its creed and liturgy quote him. "Reject Augustinian original guilt" and "reject faith alone" are accurate; the rest is overstated.
37. **Pelagius taught salvation by faith alone** (ch. 16). The "per solam fidem" line is in Pelagius's Romans commentary as the Catholic Encyclopedia reports; whether it amounts to the Reformation doctrine is a specialist question.
38. **Carlstadt led a 250,000-strong "Evangelical Brotherhood"; 100,000 were slain "with Luther's blessings"; Luther wrote "The New Judas" against Carlstadt in 1521** (ch. 17). The Peasants' War death toll of roughly 100,000 is standard and Luther's *Against the Robbing and Murdering Hordes* (1525) is the relevant tract (the book cites a 1522 exhortation instead). Carlstadt's 1520 *De canonicis scripturis* and his rejection of Luther on James are real. The "Evangelical Brotherhood" membership figure and the "New Judas" title need a source.
39. **Polycarp kept 14 Nisan from the apostles; Constantine's anti-Jewish rationale at Nicaea; Bede on Eostre; the Seder's fifteen steps "standardized for millennia" before Jesus** (App. C). The first three are accurately sourced (Eusebius 5.24; Theodoret 1.9; Bede, *De Temporum Ratione* 15). The fifteen-step Seder is rabbinic (Mishnah Pesahim 10, post-70) and cannot be shown in that form in Jesus' day.
40. **The Ebionite canon dates to 64-70 AD** (ch. 2, 16, App. B). Rests on Irenaeus 1.26.2 (c. 180) plus the inference that the Ebionites mention no other gospel; the date is the book's own.
41. **The Muratorian Fragment is fourth-century** (App. B). Hahneman's thesis is real and minority; the mainstream date remains c. 170-200.
42. **2 Peter was "expressly rejected by Eusebius as a pseudograph"** (ch. 2, App. B). Eusebius (3.25) lists it among the disputed books (*antilegomena*), not the spurious (*notha*).
43. **Hegesippus's "the Law, the Prophets, and the Lord"** (ch. 16). Eusebius 4.22.3 does report Hegesippus testing the churches by that rule; a usable line, not held in the repo.
44. **"When no Christian would think of buying a Bible any longer that contains the words of a false apostle"** (ch. 18). Not a factual claim, but the book's stated end-state; readers citing the book to say Paul should be read "for edification" (ch. 2, 10, 16) should know the conclusion goes further than that.
