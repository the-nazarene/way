## Summary

This is Thomas Smith's 1886 Ante-Nicene Fathers translation of the *Recognitions of Clement* (all ten books, from Rufinus's Latin), re-worded by a small Hebrew Roots editorial team so that God, Jesus, the apostles, the places and the religious vocabulary carry Hebrew forms. The edition in hand is the "Third Revision" dated 13 February 2010 (ISBN 978-0-557-42521-1, about 276 pages, roughly 135,000 words). Snyder is credited with the concept and the introduction; Ted Dornan did the editing and recorded the audiobook; Chris Plourde was the final editor and wrote the short note on typography.

The volume contains, in order:

1. A title page that subtitles the work "The Recognitions of Clement, The Preaching of Peter & The Ascents of James," with two epigraphs (Acts 15:13-18 and Philippians 4:3, both in the editors' own true-names wording). There is no separate "Ascents of James" or "Preaching of Peter" text inside; the subtitle names what the editors take the *Recognitions* to contain, not added documents.
2. Plourde's editor's note on the typographic convention: the divine name is set in one font when the speaker is reverent and in an italic font when the speaker mocks. This distinction does not survive the PDF-to-text conversion, so it cannot be checked in our copy.
3. Snyder's "Introductory Comments" (about four pages). See "Editor's Notes and Front Matter" below.
4. A table of contents listing every chapter heading of the ten books (headings are Smith's, with names changed).
5. Books I-X of the *Recognitions*, with Smith's chapter numbering and chapter titles retained.
6. Appendix A, "Kefa's Letter to Ya'akov" (the Epistle of Peter to James plus the Contestation/Adjuration), and Appendix B, "Clement's Letter to Ya'akov" (the Epistle of Clement to James). Neither belongs to the *Recognitions* in the manuscripts; both are the prefatory letters to the *Homilies*, and both are in this repo's Smith text at the head of `christianity/Incoming/clementine-homilies-full-text.md`.
7. Appendix C, a glossary of the Hebrew and Aramaic terms used (Elohim, qahal, mebakker, mikvah, zebah, navi, malach, talmidim, and so on), followed by an advertisement for Snyder's Codex Sinaiticus New Testament and "About" pages for the three editors.

The stated aim is to let readers meet the Clementine Peter as a Torah-observant, Hebrew-speaking figure, free of what the introduction calls nineteenth-century church language, and to connect his teaching to the Dead Sea Scrolls and the "sect of the Nazarenes" of Acts 24:5. Snyder frames the book as recovered apostolic literature that was neither canonized nor condemned, and as a counterpart to the canonical Acts told from Peter's side rather than Paul's.

What the edition drops from the ANF volume: Riddle's introductory notice, Rufinus's preface to Gaudentius, and every one of Smith's footnotes (scripture cross-references, manuscript notes and the editor's bracketed comments). What it adds: the two letters as appendices, the glossary, one in-text note at Book III and a handful of numbered footnotes in Book VIII (see below).

## How It Differs From Smith

**The base text is Smith's, sentence for sentence.** Across every passage sampled, the syntax, clause order and argument are Smith's 1886 English. The editors did not go back to Rufinus's Latin. The changes are of four kinds.

**1. Names.** Systematic substitution throughout: Peter becomes Kefa (474 occurrences; "Peter" survives 14 times, mostly in the front matter); Jesus becomes Y'shua; Christ becomes Moshiach; James becomes Ya'akov (with "haZaddik" added in the introduction); Simon Magus becomes Shimon HaMag; Saul becomes Shaul; Barnabas becomes Yosef barNaba; Caiaphas becomes Kayafa; Zacchaeus becomes Zacchai; Moses becomes Moshe; Jerusalem becomes Yahrushalayim; Jericho becomes Yericho; Damascus becomes Dameshek; Egypt becomes Mitsrayim. Rufinus's odd form "Auses" for Joshua (1.38) becomes Yahoshua. In the roll-call of Peter's companions at 2.1, Sophonius, Joseph, Michaeas, Eliesdrus, Phineas, Lazarus, Elisaeus and Nicodemus become Tzephanyah, Yahusef, Micha-El, Eli-Esdrus, Pinchas, El-Azar, El-Isha and Nakdimon. Greek and Roman names (Clement, Niceta, Aquila, Faustinianus, Theophilus) are left alone.

**2. The divine name and titles.** Smith's "God" (910 occurrences) is split between YHWH (521) and Elohim (482); Smith's "Lord" (103) disappears entirely, rendered as YHWH, as Y'shua, or as "Master" (27 in Smith, 70 in Snyder) depending on who the editors think is meant. This is the one substitution that is interpretive rather than mechanical, because Rufinus's *Dominus* does not say which. At 1.43, for example, Smith has "the passion of the Lord, the Church of the Lord ... ordained bishop in it by the Lord"; Snyder resolves the three as Y'shua, YHWH and YHWH. "Holy Spirit" at 10.71 becomes "the Shechinah," glossed in-line as YHWH's presence.

**3. Religious and moral vocabulary.** Church becomes qahal; bishop becomes mebakker (the Qumran overseer's title, a deliberate choice); presbyters become zaqenim; deacons become azariim or attendants; disciples become talmidim; apostles become shlichim; the law becomes Torah (154 "law" in Smith, 47 in Snyder, 120 "Torah"); the True Prophet becomes Navi Emet; Gentiles become Goyim; Jews become Yahudaim; the temple becomes Hekel; sacrifice becomes zebah/zebahim; baptism becomes mikvah and "baptized" becomes "immersed" (Smith's 31 occurrences of "baptiz-" go to zero); Gospel becomes Besorah; angels become malachim; glory becomes kavod; peace becomes shalom; "eternal" becomes "ageless" (67 occurrences) and "wicked" becomes "immoral" (126). "Soul," which Smith uses 130 times, is removed entirely, replaced by "spirit," "inner being" or "mind." A few forms are modernized ("ye" to "you," "which" to "that," "shall" to "will," "recondite" to "complicated," "prerogative" to "exclusive right").

**4. Structure.** Book, chapter and title all follow Smith, so a Smith citation such as 1.39 or 4.18 lands on the same passage in Snyder. Three structural differences were found:

- Book III, chapters 2-11, which Smith omitted as absent from the best manuscripts and untranslatable, are omitted here too (the text runs from chapter I to chapter XII). The introduction's statement that part of Book III was reconstructed from the *Homilies* is not borne out by the text in hand; see Claims to Check 3.
- Book II, chapter XXXI ("Peace and War") has lost its heading and runs on as part of chapter XXX. The content is all present; the number is missing.
- Book X, chapter 72 stops at "paid him no less honor than they did to the apostle." Smith's final sentence, in which Peter ordains a bishop and presbyters and baptizes a great number, is not there. Smith's footnote 886 had already flagged that sentence as a probable scribal addition; the editors removed it silently.

Chapter counts otherwise match Smith exactly (I 74, II 72, III 65 as numbered, IV 37, V 36, VI 15, VII 38, VIII 62, IX 38, X 72).

### Side-by-side samples

**Recognitions 1.36-39 (sacrifice allowed for a time; baptism instituted in its place).** The four chapters are Smith's text with the substitutions above: "Egyptians" becomes "the Mitsrayim," "his soul shall be cut off" becomes "his spirit will be cut off" (1.36); "the true Prophet" becomes "Y'shua" and "foreign nations" becomes "foreign tribes" (1.37); "temple" becomes "Hekel" and "wicked kings" becomes "immoral melekim" (1.38); "baptism" becomes "mikvah" and "baptized" becomes "immersed" (1.39). Two changes alter sense:
- At 1.37 Smith reads that the place where victims were to be offered "is his Wisdom"; Snyder has "in his Hokmah," which breaks the sentence and looks like an editing slip.
- At 1.39 Smith ends the key clause "but by the purification of the Wisdom of God." Snyder adds three words, "which is Y'shua," that are not in Smith. The identification is defensible from 1.37 and from the Clementine theology of the True Prophet, but it is the editors' gloss, not the text. Since this is the single most-cited Recognitions line in the repo, anyone quoting it from Snyder would be quoting an addition.

**Recognitions 1.43-71 (the section scholars call the Ascents of James).** Content matches Smith throughout. The heading for 1.70, "Tumult Raised by Shaul," is Smith's own heading ("Tumult Raised by Saul") with the name changed; in the body of both editions the attacker is only "one of our enemies" and "that enemy," never named. Smith's footnote recording that a manuscript margin identifies him as Saul, and the ANF editor's remark that the suppression of the name is significant, are dropped along with all the other footnotes. Snyder's introduction says Saul is "specifically identified" here as the ringleader; the identification actually rests on the chapter heading and the marginal note, plus the Damascus-letters detail in 1.71. At 1.71 the text is identical apart from names (Yericho, Gamali-El, Kayafa, Dameshek). At 1.43 "a week of years ... from the passion of the Lord" becomes "of Y'shua," and James is "ordained mebakker in it by YHWH." At 1.44 the "twelve apostles" become "twelve shlichim" and "the day of the passover" becomes "the day of the Pesach."

**Recognitions 4.18 (demons suggest thoughts that feel like one's own).** Identical to Smith except that "soul" becomes "inner being" or "spirits," "passionateness" becomes "passions," and "melancholy" becomes "melancholia." The passage the repo cites (the measure of faith that drives the demon from the soul; the suggestions believed "as if they were the perceptions of their own souls") is fully present.

**Recognitions 2.1 (Peter's habit of recalling the words of his Lord).** Identical apart from the Hebraized roll-call and "the words of my Lord" becoming "the words of my Master."

**Recognitions 4.34 (the temptation).** Identical apart from "prince of wickedness" becoming "prince of immorality," "King of peace" becoming "King of shalom," "glory" becoming "kavod," "impious" becoming "disobedient," and "worship of one God" becoming "worship of one YHWH."

**Recognitions 7.6 (Peter's diet).** The sentence the repo cites, "I live on bread alone, with olives, and seldom even with pot-herbs," is word for word Smith's in Snyder.

**Recognitions 10.72 (the ending).** Beyond the dropped final sentence noted above, one substitution changes a fact: Smith's "on the next Lord's day he baptized him" becomes "on the next Shabbat he immersed him." Smith's "Lord's day" means Sunday; "Shabbat" is an editorial correction toward what the editors believe the Nazarenes would have done, not a translation of anything in Smith.

**Plainly:** apart from renderings, the underlying content matches Smith. Anyone who reads Snyder is reading Smith's *Recognitions* in Hebrew Roots dress. The places where the dress changes meaning are few but real: the added "which is Y'shua" at 1.39, "Shabbat" for "Lord's day" at 10.72, "Shechinah" for "Holy Spirit" at 10.71, the dropped last sentence of 10.72, the removal of "soul" everywhere, and the case-by-case resolution of "Lord."

## Editor's Notes and Front Matter

Snyder's introduction makes these points, summarized:

- The *Recognitions* is Clement's journal of his conversion and discipleship under Peter, written on James's commission, and it is the Petrine side of the story the canonical Acts tells from Paul's side. Paul is absent from Peter's speeches, appearing only as the unnamed attacker of James on the temple steps (1.70).
- Peter's letter to James about guarding his preaching from his "enemy" is read as referring to either Simon Magus or Saul of Tarsus.
- Clement was a Flavian, kinsman of the emperors, and Peter's chosen successor; the two intervening "popes" in some lists were administrators for Peter's and Paul's separate Roman assemblies.
- Snyder reports that he found a portion of the *Homilies* in 1989 and judged this Petrine literature as old as Acts and superior to it; he asks why it was never canonized, never read in church, and never condemned either.
- The "strange" doctrines of the Clementines (the Standing One, the Primal Adam, demons and disease, the food ordinances, "apocryphal" commandments) were dismissed by earlier scholars as Gnostic interpolation; the Dead Sea Scrolls show they came from sectarian Judaism (Nazoreans, Ebionites, Rechabites, Elchasaites).
- The delay in publishing the Scrolls is attributed to a mostly Catholic translation team under Cardinal Ratzinger.
- The reader is pointed to Robert Eisenman's *The Dead Sea Scrolls and the First Christians* for background, and to Snyder's own site.
- Snyder thanks Dornan for editing "the previous work of Moore"; no Moore is identified anywhere in the volume.

Dornan's in-text notes (signed TAD):
- At Book III, chapter 1, a note quotes Smith's footnote about the missing chapters 2-11 and asks whether they are really untranslatable or whether an adversary has been allowed to withhold them.
- At Book VIII, chapter 25 ("Plants and Animals"), a note concedes that some of Peter's natural science does not make sense, compares it to Darwinism as a belief taken for fact, and cites Acts 17:30 and Epistle of Barnabas 10 for God's patience with inaccurate ideas.
- Numbered footnotes in Book VIII explain the Greek terms *akatonomaston* and *ogkoi* via Strong's numbers and web links; cite Wikipedia on Deucalion; propose that Isaiah 28:9-10 supports a reading in 8.60; relay an anonymous claim that the Brahmins descend from Abraham; and recount what Indian acquaintances told Dornan in the 1960s about suttee.

The glossary is practical and mostly accurate as a key to the substitutions. A few entries carry the editors' views: "Goy, Goyim" is glossed as all people outside Israel and "dog"; "Zaddikim" is split into Zadokites (righteous Torah-keepers) and Sadducees (lovers of money); "Navi Emet" is called a lost teaching rediscovered; "Y'shua" is given as short for "Yahshua."

The "About" pages: Snyder is a retired United Methodist minister (M.Div., Emory) who has taught "the faith of the sect of the Nazoreans" for fifteen years and runs several Hebrew Roots sites and publications; Dornan came through the Worldwide Church of God and the Hebrew Roots movement and assists Moshe Koniuchowsky's B'nai Yahshua Synagogues; Plourde is a deacon in the Netzari Vero Yahad (Vero Beach, Florida) and ministers in Haiti. David The Nazarene's video 004 is a presentation to a "Vero Essene Yahad," which shares the Vero name; whether it is the same congregation is not established.

When someone brings a Nazarene Acts quotation, find the chapter in Smith (the numbering is the same) and quote that.

So the link holds for video 003. Two cautions. When the NazActs transcripts come in, a spot-check of two or three against Snyder's chapter openings will settle whether he reads it verbatim.

## Claims to Check

1. **Clement of Rome was a Flavian, kinsman of Vespasian, Titus and Domitian** (introduction). This is the identification with Titus Flavius Clemens the consul (executed 95 CE), which Riddle's ANF introduction calls a confusion. Mainstream scholarship treats the two as distinct; the Pseudo-Clementines themselves make Clement a relative of the emperor, which is part of why the literature is pseudonymous.
2. **Paul is "specifically identified" as the ringleader at 1.70** (introduction). In both Smith and Snyder the text says only "one of our enemies"; the name Saul comes from a manuscript marginal note (Smith, footnote 596), from the editorial chapter heading, and from the Damascus-letters detail at 1.71. The inference is widely accepted; "specifically identified" overstates it.
3. **"Part of Volume III we reconstructed from the Homilies"** (introduction). The text in hand runs Book III chapter I to chapter XII with Dornan's note in between; no reconstruction of chapters 2-11 appears. Either a later edition supplies it, or the sentence refers to something else. Worth asking, because David The Nazarene's series has videos for Book 3, chapters 1-7 and 8-11.
4. **1.39, "which is Y'shua."** Not in Smith; not in Rufinus as far as the ANF text shows. An editorial gloss presented as text.
5. **10.72, "the next Shabbat."** Smith has "Lord's day." The change makes a third-century text say what the editors think a first-century Nazarene would have done. Rufinus's Latin is not held here; check it before calling either reading certain.
6. **10.71, "the Shechinah—YHWH's presence."** Smith has "the Holy Spirit." The in-line gloss is the editors'.
7. **10.72's last sentence dropped.** Smith's footnote 886 regards it as a scribal addition; removing it is a judgment call the edition does not announce.
8. **"Soul" removed throughout.** Smith uses "soul" 130 times; the edition replaces it everywhere with "spirit," "inner being" or "mind," which reflects a doctrinal position (no separable soul) rather than anything in Smith's text.
9. **The intervening "popes" (Linus, Cletus) were administrators for Peter's and Paul's separate Roman assemblies** (introduction). A reconstruction; the succession lists (Irenaeus 3.3.3; Eusebius 3.2, 3.4, 3.13-15) do not say this.
10. **Peter spent only his last few years in Rome; Clement was Peter's direct successor** (introduction). Depends on the Epistle of Clement to James, which is itself part of the pseudonymous corpus; Irenaeus places Clement third after Peter.
11. **The Clementine doctrines (Standing One, Primal Adam, food rules) are paralleled in the Dead Sea Scrolls, which date 150 BCE-136 CE** (introduction). The Scrolls' sectarian halakha and dualism have real parallels in the Clementines (Eisenman's thesis), but "the Standing One" is Samaritan and Simonian vocabulary and "Primal Adam" is Jewish-Christian and Elchasaite; neither phrase is in the Scrolls as such.
12. **The Scrolls' publication was delayed by a Catholic team under Cardinal Ratzinger** (introduction). The original team was mostly Catholic, under de Vaux of the École Biblique, and the delay was real (publication opened up in 1991); Ratzinger, as prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith from 1981, had no role in the Scrolls that any history records. This is the Baigent-Leigh *Dead Sea Scrolls Deception* (1991) thesis.
13. **Snyder found a portion of the Homilies "in an old scroll" in 1989** (introduction). The *Homilies* survive in two Greek manuscripts (Paris and Vatican), both long known; "scroll" here is the edition's word for any book and should not be read as a manuscript discovery.
14. **Brahmins descend from Abraham** (Dornan, footnote 8). Offered from an anonymous source the editor could not recall; a folk etymology, not a historical claim.
15. **Mebakker is the right rendering of "bishop"** (glossary and throughout). The Qumran *mebaqqer* (Damascus Document 13-14) does overlap in function with the Christian *episkopos*, and the equation is argued in the scholarship; using it as the translation decides the question in advance.
16. **"Goyim" means "dog"** (glossary). It does not; *goyim* means nations. The gloss is the editors'.
17. **Peter's letter to James concerns "my Torah-observant preaching" rejected for a "Torahless" preaching** (Appendix A, chapter 2). The ANF Homilies text in this repo has "my lawful preaching" and "lawless and trifling preaching." "Torah" narrows a word that could also mean "lawful" in the ordinary sense, though the context (the eternity of the law given by Moses) supports the editors' reading.
18. **The "previous work of Moore"** (introduction). Unidentified. If an earlier modernized edition of Smith exists under that name, it would be the real base text; worth asking Snyder.
