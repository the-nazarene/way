## What it is

A first-timer's guide to the lost Gospel in Hebrew that the fathers tie to Matthew. It does three things: introduces every ancient witness who says the book existed, argues that the "Gospel according to the Hebrews", the Nazarenes' Gospel and the "Gospel of the Ebionites" are one book under several names (Edwards's view), and collects every surviving saying and reading. Each witness is tiered: Tier 1 a mainstream father reporting plainly, Tier 2 a real witness with a known problem, Tier 3 late or disputed.

## The argument in five steps

1. Seven fathers across three centuries say Matthew wrote first, in Hebrew. None says otherwise.
2. The book survived among the Nazarenes and Ebionites. Irenaeus says the Ebionites use only Matthew; Eusebius says they use only the Gospel according to the Hebrews; Epiphanius says the Nazarenes had Matthew complete in Hebrew.
3. No ancient writer speaks of more than one Hebrew Gospel. "Gospel of the Ebionites" and "Gospel of the Nazarenes" are modern labels.
4. The fragments differ the way copies and translations of one book differ.
5. What it says matters: Yeshua comes to end the sacrifices, refuses the Passover meat, appears first to James; and Luke appears to have used it (Edwards).

## Its own cautions

It is careful in the right places, and these should travel with anything we take from it:
- Our Greek Matthew is not a translation of the Hebrew Gospel, and the sheet says so (Part J). Greek Matthew's use of Mark is no objection to an earlier Hebrew Gospel; it only rules out Greek Matthew being that Gospel.
- Shem Tov's medieval Hebrew Matthew proves nothing about the original, and the sheet doesn't lean on it.
- It grants that Jerome probably didn't translate the whole book and may have taken some quotations from Origen.
- It states the one-book view as Edwards's, against the majority three-gospel scheme, and names Kloppenborg's review as the main reply.

### Where the held texts read differently

1. **The Passover saying (D7).** Epiphanius says the Ebionites added "not" and "meat"; in Wescoe it is a statement, "I have not longed with longing to eat meat this Passover with you" (30,22,4), not a question. The same passage says Jesus "both kept the Passover and ate meat." Luke 22:16's "I will not eat it" (*ου μη φαγω*) is in Westcott-Hort; the KJV reads "not any more." "Both versions agree he did not eat" goes past both texts (C49, C177, C185).
2. **The *Travels of Peter* (D8).** Panarion 30.15 has Peter abstaining from meat but says nothing about him condemning the sacrifices; that is the *Ascents of James*, with James speaking (30,16,7) (C173).
3. **The Tiberias treasury (A6).** 30.3.8 names only John. In 30.6.9 Matthew is "Hebrew by origin", not a translation, and the man is Josephus. This suits the sheet's point better than its own wording (C170).
4. **Papias's *logia* used of Mark (Part J).** McGiffert prints "discourses", and his note 962 says the manuscripts are split (C169).
5. **Papias and the Gospel according to the Hebrews.** The woman's story is in McGiffert's 3.39.16, not "the very next sentence", and Eusebius doesn't say Papias used that gospel (McGiffert note 967) (C14).
6. **Pantaenus.** Eusebius 5.10.3 has "Hebrew language"; "letters" is Jerome's word (C16, C22).
7. **Hegesippus.** McGiffert numbers it 4.22.7 and prints one book, "the Syriac Gospel according to the Hebrews" (C15).
8. **Jerome at Beroea.** Richardson's translation has the volume "described to me", not copied. A question for the Latin, not an error found (C20).
9. **"Every witness speaks of one book."** Panarion 46,1,9 applies "according to the Hebrews" to Tatian's Diatessaron (C50).
10. **The prophets sentence.** In NPNF it stands outside Jerome's quotation marks (C25).
11. **Clement 5.14 and Thomas 2.** The 5.14 form names no source, and the Coptic Thomas 2 has no "rest" (C37, C57).
12. **Smaller points.** Jerome puts the "handle me" saying in the letter to Polycarp (C21); Origen's "2.12" is Menzies II §6 (C38); Wescoe has a gap in D5's list of names (C44); the sacrifice saying is 30,16,5a, not 30.16.4 (C48).

**The biggest gap is ours, not the sheet's.** Its lead witness for the one-book view, Jerome's *Commentary on Matthew* 12.13 ("the Gospel which the Nazarenes and the Ebionites use"), isn't held (C84), and neither is Edwards (C202). So the Edwards figures in `questions/MINING-2026-10-debates.md` stay unverified.
