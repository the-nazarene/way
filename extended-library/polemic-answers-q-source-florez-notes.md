## What it is

The answer to "Q is a fantasy no one has ever seen." Three cards already stand on Q (method, Thomas, Two Ways) without defending it; this sheet is the defense.

| Part | What it covers |
|---|---|
| A | What Q is and isn't: the Synoptic Problem, Markan priority, the double tradition (about 235 verses), why Q is cited by Luke's numbers, what it contains and doesn't, its date (40s-60s, before Mark) |
| B | Why scholars find it credible: verbatim Greek agreement, uneven agreement, Matthew and Luke not knowing each other, common order, doublets, alternating primitivity, Thomas as a real sayings gospel, Papias, who holds it, an honest summary |
| C | Why it matters: the earliest reachable layer; no cross, atonement or resurrection; Torah to the last stroke; addressed to Israel; close to James; what is and isn't claimed |
| D-F | Objections and answers (Farrer first), the argument in brief, short versions |

## The argument in four steps

1. Q is a hypothesis, and we say so. No manuscript is claimed.
2. The inference is strong and mainstream: majority view since the 1860s, held across confessions.
3. Q matters for a narrow reason: its content is in the Bible either way, and it has no atoning death in it.
4. The best objection, Farrer (Luke copied Matthew), is a serious minority view. If it is right, the sayings move into Matthew; they don't disappear.

## Its own cautions

The sheet keeps its tiers apart and says so: Tier 1 for what the double tradition contains, Tier 3 for anything about why or about the compilers' beliefs. Its C6 is the line to keep: the claim is not a recovered book, not exact wording, and not Kloppenborg's layers. Its A8 admits the one cross saying (Q 14.27, the follower's cross) and says outright that the compilers knew of the crucifixion; they didn't build their book on it.

## What we checked

26 new entries: 17 Matches, 2 Matches in another translation, 4 Read differently, 3 Not held.

**The headline Greek claim is exact.** John the Baptist's sermon: Matthew 3:7b-10 has 63 words, Luke 3:7b-9 has 64, and 60 are identical and in the same order (C259). The Lord's Prayer counts are right for the critical text, 44 Greek words in Luke to 61 in Matthew (C264); a KJV reader won't see it, because the KJV prints the long form in Luke.

### Where the held texts read differently

1. **Ask, seek, knock.** Word for word only in Matthew 7:7-8 // Luke 11:9-10; across the whole passage it is 52 of 73 words (C262).
2. **"Israelite towns", "to Israel only".** Luke 10 has no such restriction. That command is Matthew 10:5-6, Matthew's own material, not double tradition (C270).
3. **Matthew 16:27** is not an "ashamed" doublet (C271).
4. **"Q 6.24"** (woe to the rich, set beside James 5:1): Luke 6:24 is Luke-only, not double tradition (C273).

**One addition for our own use.** Q is not silent about his death. It sets him among the prophets Jerusalem killed: "the blood of all the prophets... may be required of this generation" (Luke 11:50, KJV) and "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, which killest the prophets" (13:34). A murdered prophet, not a sacrifice. That helps our case, and saying it first stops anyone claiming we hid it.
