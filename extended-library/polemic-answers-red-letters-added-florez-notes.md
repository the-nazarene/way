## What it is

The sheet goes passage by passage through the red letters that scholarship says Jesus did not say, as they stand in a red-letter Bible. It sorts them into two kinds.

- **Part A, words added by scribes.** The oldest manuscripts lack them, and the standard Greek New Testament (NA28 and UBS5) brackets or omits them. Examples are Mark 16:9-20, the woman taken in adultery, the doxology of the Lord's Prayer, and the Johannine Comma. There are 35 entries in all, with a separate section on changes that bear on who Jesus is.
- **Part B, words composed by the Gospel writers.** Every manuscript has these, so the Greek is secure. The argument is literary and historical. Examples are Matthew 28:19, "upon this rock", Mark 7:19's "making all meats clean", and John's discourses. There are 18 entries.

Then come what this does to doctrines (Part C), the standard objections with answers (Part D), and comment-length versions for posting (Part E). Part F covers where the Greek gospels came from. It gives the patristic witnesses to a gospel in Hebrew and about thirty fragments set beside their Greek parallels. It also covers the Gospel of Thomas, Q, and a source map of the four gospels. The sheet closes with a source list and its own list of items still to verify.

The sheet appears to have been written for a YouTube channel. It refers to "this channel" and its apostolic-succession episode, and says which claims are "safe to say on camera". It uses "Yeshua" for Jesus and "Hebrew" rather than "Jewish" for him and the early believers.

### The three tiers

Every entry carries one of three tiers.

| Tier | The sheet's definition |
|---|---|
| **ESTABLISHED** | Bracketed or omitted in the critical Greek text, or near-consensus among critical scholars, with conservative scholars conceding it |
| **CONTESTED** | Qualified scholars are divided; both sides are reported |
| **QUARANTINED** | A popular claim with no scholarly support in the form it circulates; the sheet says why it fails and what the defensible version is. "Do not use a QUARANTINED claim on camera" |

This is close to how our own cards work. `questions/method-and-criteria.md` reads only the seams textual scholarship has already marked, and `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` keeps a do-not-use list.

### The argument in eight steps

This summarizes the sheet's own "Shape of the Argument".

1. Red ink is a publisher's choice, first made in 1899. The manuscripts disagree about which sentences belong at all.
2. Some red-letter passages are missing from the oldest copies, and the standard Greek text marks them.
3. The people who say so are the church's own textual critics: Metzger, who was a Presbyterian minister, and the evangelical scholar Daniel Wallace.
4. Other red letters are secure in the Greek but were composed by the evangelists. Mainstream scholarship says so, not only its skeptical wing.
5. Scribal changes about who Jesus is run one way, toward a higher view of the Son. Ehrman makes this case in *The Orthodox Corruption of Scripture*.
6. Whole doctrines rest on these sentences: baptism for salvation, snake-handling, the Trinitarian formula, the papacy, the blood covenant, the end of the food laws, and the divorce exception.
7. Every standard objection has an answer from the church's own sources.
8. Under all of it, the Greek gospels were assembled from earlier sources. One of them is the Hebrew Gospel, which survives in fragments.

### Part F: the earliest sources

- **F1, terms.** The Hebrew Gospel, Thomas, Q, Markan priority, M and L, the Two-Source hypothesis, and the Critical Edition of Q.
- **F2, the witnesses to the Hebrew Gospel,** in date order. They run from Ignatius and Papias through Hegesippus, Irenaeus, Pantaenus, Clement, Origen, Eusebius, Epiphanius, Didymus and Jerome. Two manuscript witnesses follow: the *to Ioudaikon* margin notes and the Stichometry of Nicephorus.
- **F3, thirty fragments set beside their Greek parallels,** in four groups:
  - (a) found only in Luke, F3.1-F3.11;
  - (b) earlier or shorter forms, F3.12-F3.16;
  - (c) same scene, different detail, F3.17-F3.21;
  - (d) absent from the Greek gospels, F3.22-F3.30.

  The last group includes "I came to abolish sacrifices", "my mother, the Holy Spirit", the honey-cake, the second rich man, and the risen Jesus appearing to James.
- **F4, the Gospel of Thomas.** It covers the dating debate, stated for both camps, and eight logia that named scholars judge earlier than the canonical form. It also covers the links between Thomas and the Hebrew Gospel, Thomas 12 on James, and Thomas 14 on food.
- **F5, Q.** It covers Kloppenborg's layers, what Q contains and lacks, the Farrer-Goodacre case against Q, and Edwards on Q.
- **F6, how the Greek gospels were assembled,** with a source map table.
- **F7, what this does to the red letters,** and **F8, comment-length versions.**

The sheet follows James R. Edwards, *The Hebrew Gospel and the Development of the Synoptic Tradition* (2009). Edwards treats the Gospel according to the Hebrews, Hebrew Matthew and the Ebionites' gospel as one book, and argues it was the source of Luke's special material. The sheet tiers that thesis CONTESTED, and it names Andrew Gregory's argument for the reverse direction.

**Where the held texts match the sheet**

- **Panarion (Wescoe).** Sections 29.9.4, 30.3.7, 30.13.2-8, 30.14.3, 30.14.5 and 30.16.5 match in substance. The sheet's wording of 30.16.5, "the wrath will not cease from you" with no "of God", matches the held text. So does its "flesh", not "lamb", in the Passover saying. Both independently support our own audit items A1 and A2.
- **One of the sheet's own open items is now settled.** Item 8 on its Part F list asks about Panarion 46.1.9. The held text reads: "And the gospel through Four is said to have been made by him, which some call the Gospel according to the Hebrews."
- **Didache (Richardson).** Chapter 7 gives the triadic baptismal formula. Chapter 8 has the two-part doxology, "for yours is the power and the glory forever". Chapter 9 says "baptized in the Lord's name". Chapters 9 and 10 have no body or blood words. All of this matches the sheet. B1's point that the Didache carries both baptismal formulas holds in the held text.
- **Thomas (Lambdin).** Logia 9, 14, 54, 64, 65 and 66, 79, 99, 100, 101 and 104 match the sheet's wording.

**Where the held texts read differently, or support a narrower wording**

These points are for anyone quoting the sheet. They are worth passing back to Christian.

1. **Baptism in Acts.** B1, Part C and E3 say every baptism recorded in Acts is in the name of Yeshua. KJV Acts has about nine baptism scenes. Four state a formula: 2:38 "in the name of Jesus Christ", 8:16 "in the name of the Lord Jesus", 10:48 "in the name of the Lord", and 19:5 "in the name of the Lord Jesus". The other five state none. None is triadic. The held text supports "every baptism in Acts that states a formula names Jesus or the Lord; none uses the three names." Romans 6:3 and Galatians 3:27 say "baptized into Christ", not "in the name of". Clementine Homilies 11.26, which we hold, uses the triadic form. Recognitions 1.73 says "in the name of Jesus".
2. **F3.1, Caiaphas.** The sheet says no other Greek gospel names Caiaphas. Matthew does, at 26:3 and 26:57, and so does John, at 11:49 and 18:13.
3. **Panarion 30.13.7 (A27, F3.6, F7).** The Ebionites' gospel there gives both readings: "You are my beloved Son, in you I am well pleased; and again: 'Today I have begotten you.'" F3.6's quotation leaves out the "well pleased" clause.
4. **Panarion 30.22.4 (F3.3, F7).** Wescoe renders a statement: "I have not longed with longing to eat meat this Passover with you." In 30.22.5 Epiphanius says the group changed Luke's sentence by adding "not" and writing in "meat". The sheet reads the saying as the earlier Hebrew form behind Luke 22:15. `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` takes the opposite view, that the saying is the Ebionites' version and secondary. The doubled idiom "with desire I have desired" is in Luke 22:15 too.
5. **Recognitions 1.39 (F3.22).** The words "instead of sacrifices" are close to the chapter heading in the Ante-Nicene Fathers edition, "Baptism Instituted in Place of Sacrifices". They are not in the text, which reads: "lest haply they might suppose that on the cessation of sacrifice there was no remission of sins for them, He instituted baptism by water." The sheet's link points to Homilies book 3.
6. **Thomas 2 (F3.13, F4).** The Coptic in Lambdin runs seek, find, troubled, astonished, "rule over the All". The step "rest" is in the Greek fragment P.Oxy 654, which we don't hold.
7. **Smaller Thomas wording.** Thomas 12 in Lambdin has "James the righteous", not "the Just"; `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` already records this variant. Thomas 20 has "a great plant", not "a great branch".
8. **A24.** In the KJV, Jesus says "new" outside the covenant verses too: "drink it new", "a new commandment", "new tongues". The held text supports the narrower form: the covenant verses are the only places he speaks of a *new* covenant.
9. **John 1:18, three places in the sheet.** The A-section introduction and Part C follow Ehrman, who holds that "only begotten Son" came first and was raised to "God". A30 reports that the earliest copies, P66 and P75, read "God", and tiers the question CONTESTED. E(4) says the later copies read "Son" where the early Egyptian copies read "God". On the sheet's own description, that is a change from the higher reading to the lower. E(4) also states Mark 1:1 and Luke 3:22 without the CONTESTED tier they carry in A29 and A27.
10. **Critical-text wording.** Some of the sheet's details hold in the critical Greek but not in our KJV. Mark 1:2 reads "in the prophets" in the KJV, not "in Isaiah the prophet". John 1:42 and 21:15 read "Jona" and "Jonas", not "son of John". F3.12 quotes Luke 11:3 in Matthew's wording; Luke reads "day by day".

**Added since (2026-10-26), and checked.** Irenaeus, Eusebius's *Church History*, Ignatius, Justin, Jerome's *Letters*, *Against the Pelagians* and *Lives of Illustrious Men*, Clement's *Stromata* and Origen's *Commentary on John* are now held. The sheet's 31 quotations from them are checked in `audits/2026-10-26-fathers-added-checks.md`. Fifteen match, nine differ only in translation wording, and two can't be checked because the held edition prints only a summary (Jerome, Letters 20 and 120). Four read differently in the held texts: *Stromata* 5.14 names no source for its longer form; McGiffert's *Church History* 4.22.8 reads one book, "the Syriac Gospel according to the Hebrews"; *Church History* 5.10.3 reads "in the Hebrew language", not "in Hebrew letters"; and in Richardson's *Lives* 3 the Nazarenes of Beroea described the volume to Jerome, rather than Jerome copying it. McGiffert's section numbers also run one off in places (Papias's woman is his 3.39.16).

**Still not held, so cited at reference level only.** The manuscripts, NA28 and UBS, Metzger, Hegesippus's own text, Jerome's commentaries on Matthew and Isaiah, Shem-Tob, the Greek Thomas fragments, Josephus *War* 6, the Mishnah, and the Qumran passages the sheet cites (1QS 5:25-6:1, CD 9:2-8). A public-domain Westcott-Hort Greek New Testament is now held too, so the Greek readings the sheet gives (Mark 7:19, John 1:18, Mark 1:1) can be checked; it lacks accents.
