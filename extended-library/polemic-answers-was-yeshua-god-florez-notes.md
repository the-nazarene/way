## What it is

The sheet answers one question: did Yeshua claim to be God, did the people who knew him believe it, and does the New Testament say so? It calls itself "a teaching document, not a cheat sheet," written for someone new to the question who needs to check every verse and explain it to the next person. Like Christian's first sheet, it seems to be written for a YouTube channel (it names the ASV as "the standing translation for this channel"), uses "Yeshua" for Jesus, and quotes the ASV.

### The argument in six steps

This summarizes the sheet's own "Shape of the argument."

1. In the first three gospels Jesus never says he is God. He prays to God, calls the Father the only true God, says the Father is greater, says there are things only the Father knows, and after the resurrection still says "my God."
2. The first preaching calls him a man God approved, a servant God raised, a man God made Lord and Messiah.
3. The Hebrew believers who kept his teaching, the Nazarenes and Ebionites, held him to be the Messiah, a man anointed by God, and the fathers who opposed them say so.
4. The clear statements that he is God sit in the latest gospel and in a few letters that are either pseudonymous or rest on a text scribes changed.
5. The doctrine was argued over for three centuries and settled at a council called by an emperor in 325, with a word (*homoousios*) that is in no verse of scripture.
6. **"Exalted is not the same as God."** The New Testament says God raised him, seated him and gave him the name above every name, and the sheet agrees with all of it. The question is whether he was the God of Israel, the one who did the exalting.

### Its own cautions

The sheet marks where its side overreaches, in boxes headed "How to present this." These are worth using as they stand:

- "Do not say Nicaea invented the deity of Christ. Ignatius said it in 110 and John's prologue is older than that." (D4)
- Matthew 28:19: "Do not say 'the manuscripts lack it,' because they do not." (C12)
- Luke 3:22: the oldest papyrus (P4) reads "well pleased," so argue from the breadth of the early fathers, not the age of the manuscripts. (B3)
- The Nazarenes: Epiphanius says he cannot tell what they held, so say only that neither group is recorded as holding him to be God. (B6)
- "Do not say 'Paul never applies Yahweh verses to Yeshua.' He does." (C25) "Do not say 'the New Testament never prays to Jesus.'" (C39) Hebrews 1:10-12: "Admit this one is strong." (C30)
- The Granville Sharp rule at Titus 2:13 is "within its conditions well supported." (C41)

It also names the strongest opposing scholarship (Hurtado, Bauckham) and answers it rather than leaving it out (D5).

## What we checked against texts we hold

Full detail, with file:line references, is in `audits/2026-10-28-florez-was-yeshua-god-citation-check.md`. Each check is a `claims/` entry; `python scripts/claims.py find <passage>` finds one.

**Counts.** 68 new claims (C99-C166): 44 match (40 plain, 3 in another translation, 1 under another section number), 10 read differently, 14 not held. About 45 more routine verse checks are in the report's tables.

**Where the held texts match.** Every block quotation the sheet gives as a church father's own words is word for word in the held text: Irenaeus 3.21.1 and 5.1.3, Justin's *Dialogue* 48 and 49. Irenaeus 1.26.2, Eusebius 3.27, Panarion 29.7.6, Justin's *Dialogue* 56 and 61, and the Ignatius "our God" passages (Ephesians opening and 18, Romans 3, Smyrnaeans 1) all say what the sheet says. So do the Greek readings it relies on in Westcott-Hort: *horisthentos* at Romans 1:4, *ος* at 1 Timothy 3:16, the article in John 1:1, Matthew 19:17's rewording of Mark 10:18.

**Where the held texts read differently, or support a narrower wording.** For anyone quoting the sheet, and worth passing back to Christian:

1. **Origen and the baptism voice (B3).** Origen's *Commentary on John* Book I quotes "this day have I begotten Thee" as Psalm 2:7, about the Son's eternal begetting, with nothing about the baptism or Luke. Justin (*Dialogue* 88, 103) gives the voice in that form, but names no gospel. So "the church fathers quoted it as the text of Luke" holds for Justin's wording, not for Origen.
2. **Irenaeus 3.21 (B6b).** The seventy-elders legend is his first answer (3.21.2-3); 3.21.4-10 go on to argue from scripture. In 5.1.3, "[the natural]" in "the old leaven of [the natural] birth" is the translators' supplied word.
3. **Genesis 24 (B6b).** Rebekah is *betulah* at 24:16 and *almah* at 24:43. The argument works as 24:16 with 24:43.
4. **Isaiah 48:16 (C15).** The verse opens "Come ye near unto Me," still in God's voice; "and now" is its last clause.
5. **John 19:37 (C34).** The Greek has no "him": "they shall look on [the one] whom they pierced."
6. **Matthew 19:17 (A2).** The side-by-side works only in the critical text. The KJV prints Matthew in Mark's words.
7. **Micah 5:2 (C35).** Brenton's Septuagint also reads "from eternity," so it is not only the KJV tradition.
8. **Hebrews and Origen (C7).** Origen's "who wrote the epistle, in truth, God knows" is right, but in the same passage he gives the thoughts to Paul.
9. **Panarion 30.16.4 (B6).** The archangel is 30.16.4; Adam, the patriarchs and putting on Jesus are 30.3.5-6, where Epiphanius says their accounts vary.
10. **Deuteronomy 32:43 (C29).** Brenton's English of the Vatican text reads "let all the angels of God worship him... and let all the sons of God strengthen themselves in him," the reverse of what the sheet reports for Swete. Open Swete and Rahlfs before repeating C29.
11. **Didache 10.** Our held Richardson text has "Hosanna to the God of David!", which some read as addressed to Jesus. The sheet's "Not God" for the Didache (B7) doesn't mention it.

**Internal points.** C18 lists four "probable" verses for Brown; C31 and C41 say five. The comment versions drop the cautions the body gives (Luke 3:22, Nicaea), and the short comment says every scribal change goes one way while D3 says "the fight ran both ways." A held text adds a counter-case: in every Ignatius passage above, the fourth-century longer recension *lowers* the wording ("our God" becomes "our Lord... our Saviour").

**Not held, and worth checking before use.** Tertullian's "there was a time when the Son was not" is usually cited from *Against Hermogenes* 3, not *Against Praxeas*. "Proposed by Hosius" is an inference from indirect sources. "After Nicaea he quotes it with the formula" is Conybeare's claim about works we don't hold. Habakkuk 1:12 "thou diest not" is a reconstruction (every held text reads "we shall not die"). The *adoni*/*Adonai* point at Psalm 110:1 rests on the Masoretic vowel points, which are medieval.
