## Summary

*Questioning Paul* is a verse-by-verse case against Paul built on Galatians. The author argues that Christianity rests on Paul rather than on Jesus, and that Paul fails the Torah's tests for a prophet (Deuteronomy 13 and 18). He walks through Galatians from 1:1 to 6:18, setting each of Paul's claims and scripture citations against the Torah, the Prophets and the words of Jesus, and scores the letter as largely inaccurate.

The load-bearing moves recur throughout. He reads Paul's four Torah citations in Galatians 3:10-13 (Deuteronomy 27:26, Habakkuk 2:4, Leviticus 18:5, Deuteronomy 21:23) as truncated or reversed. He sets Galatians 2 against Acts 15 to argue Paul misrepresented the Jerusalem council. He reads 2 Peter 3:15-16 as Peter condemning Paul's letters rather than endorsing them, and gathers texts he takes as warnings about Paul (Matthew 5-7 and 24, Revelation 2:2 with Acts 19, Genesis 49:27 on Benjamin the wolf, Acts 26:14 traced to Euripides). The closing chapter reads Habakkuk 2 as a prophecy naming Paul.

His method rests on Hebrew-name renderings, word studies and early manuscripts (P46, Codex Sinaiticus), and on reading the *nomina sacra* (the abbreviated sacred names in early manuscripts) as placeholders for Hebrew names. He holds that circumcision and the Torah remain binding, and that Gnostic and Greek religious ideas entered the faith through Paul.

**Where it meets this repo:** strong agreement on many of the same texts (the Deuteronomy 18 test, Revelation 2:2, 1 Thessalonians 4, Matthew 7 and 24, the Acts 15 decree reversed in 1 Corinthians 8, the Ebionite rejection of Paul). The main differences: the repo deliberately declines a verdict on Paul's character and treats Paul's letters as better evidence than Acts, and the book does the opposite on both; the book makes circumcision a condition of salvation and treats Gnosticism as Paul's poison, both against the repo's Core Thesis. Each chapter below lists its own claims to check.

## The Author's Conventions

A note on the author's conventions, since they run through every chapter: Paul is "Sha'uwl" (which he links to *She'owl*, the grave) or "Paulos" (which he glosses "little and lowly"); Jesus is "Yahowsha'" ("Yahowah saves"), a "diminished corporeal manifestation" of Yahowah; Messiah is "Ma'aseyah" ("work of Yah"); the Torah is "Towrah" ("teaching and guidance," never "law"); the Holy Spirit is the "Set-Apart Spirit" and is presented as feminine, a "Spiritual Mother"; the festivals of Leviticus 23 are "Miqra'ey" or "Invitations to be Called Out and Meet." The *nomina sacra* (abbreviated sacred names in Greek manuscripts) are called "Divine Placeholders" and read as standing for Hebrew names. He gives his own translation first, then compares it with the Nestle-Aland 27th edition with McReynolds interlinear ("NA" or "NAMI"), the KJV, Jerome's Latin Vulgate ("LV"), and the New Living Translation ("NLT"), and reads Greek tense, voice and mood as carrying theological weight.

## Chapter by Chapter

### Chapter 00: The Truth About Paul

**Argument:** A preface addressed to the reader. The author's thesis is that Christianity rests almost entirely on Paul's thirteen letters rather than on anything Jesus said, and that Paul "consistently and deliberately contradicted God" (ch. 00). He describes his own path: he began the study inclined to defend Paul, assuming scribal error and bad translation explained the conflicts; he rewrote the book five times; by the end of Galatians 3–4 he concluded Paul was running a deliberate strategy, and he then found what he counts as seven prophecies in which God and Jesus warn about Paul by name or description. He also announces two readings he treats as settled fact throughout: that Paul "admitted to being demon-possessed" (2 Corinthians 12:7, the "messenger of Satan") and "insane" (2 Corinthians 11:23).

He sets the evidentiary rules the rest of the book follows. The only reliable witnesses are the Torah, Prophets, and Psalms, plus Jesus's own words as recorded in Matthew, John, and Revelation; Mark and Luke are "hearsay portraits." Paul's letters are to be read from the oldest manuscripts, translated with amplified word studies, and compared clause by clause to the passages Paul cites. A recurring frame is that a counterfeit works by blending truth with falsehood, so Paul's accurate statements are expected and do not count in his favor. He compares Paul to Muhammad repeatedly, drawing on his earlier books on Islam.

**Key texts used:** The list of thirteen Pauline letters (he treats all thirteen, including the Pastorals, as Paul's). Psalm 19:7 as the closing statement of the Torah's nature. Pointers forward to 2 Corinthians 12:7 and 11:23. His other books (*Yada Yah*, *An Introduction to God*, *Prophet of Doom*, *Tea With Terrorists*) are cited as the places where supporting proofs live.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** This chapter introduces the vocabulary the whole book uses. God's name is rendered "Yahowah" (he says the pronunciation is provable and that the name occurs 7,000 times). Jesus is "Yahowsha'" ("Yahowah Saves"), with the title "Ma'aseyah" ("Work of Yahowah") replacing "Messiah/Christ." Paul is "Sha'uwl" (Hebrew, which he glosses "question him" and says is written identically to She'owl, the grave) who adopted "Paulos" (Latin, glossed "little and lowly"). "Towrah" is always "teaching and guidance," never "law"; "shamar" is "closely examine and consider," never "keep." Jerusalem is "Yaruwshalaim," glossed "source of guidance regarding reconciliation." James, Peter, and John are "Ya'aqob," "Shim'own," and "Yahowchanan." He calls his translations "amplified": each Hebrew or Greek word is followed by a parenthetical list of possible senses.

**Claims to check:**
1. That Sha'uwl and She'owl are "indistinguishable in Hebrew." The consonants (שאול) are the same; the Masoretic pointing differs (Sha'ul vs. She'ol). Both are usually traced to the root sha'al, "to ask." Whether "question him" is a legitimate gloss of the name Saul (normally "asked for") is a lexical question.
2. That Paulus means "little and lowly." Latin paulus does mean "small"; "lowly" is an extension.
3. That Yahowsha' "is affirmed over 200 times in the Torah and Prophets." The name Yehoshua (Joshua) does occur roughly that often; the pronunciation "Yahowsha'" (and "Yahowah") is the author's reconstruction and is not the scholarly default.
4. That 2 Corinthians 12:7 is an admission of demon possession, and 2 Corinthians 11:23 an admission of insanity. Both are interpretive readings of "a thorn in the flesh, a messenger of Satan" and of a rhetorical "I am talking like a madman"; standard commentaries treat the latter as irony. The book's whole structure leans on these two readings.
5. That Mark and Luke are hearsay while Matthew and John are eyewitness. This is the traditional attribution; mainstream scholarship treats all four as anonymous.

### Chapter 01: Sha'uwl – Question Him

**Argument:** Galatians is the foundational Christian document: without it, he argues, there is no scriptural case against the Torah, the Sabbath, the feasts, or circumcision, and no "two covenants." He gives seven reasons Paul wrote it (Acts's account of Paul's relationship with the Galatians; 2 Peter's reference to a letter to that audience; the Jerusalem meeting as its occasion; its autobiographical content; its place in Papyrus 46, which he dates 85–125 CE; the handwritten closing; and the vocabulary of charis). He then lists, in a long run of "I understand that Christians believe X, but that is not possible," his own positions: no one named "Jesus Christ" existed (the name is a seventeenth-century creation; "Christos" means "drugged"; the placeholders in the manuscripts stand for Hebrew names); "Lord" is the title God gives Satan (Ba'al); the Trinity is Babylonian; God did not die and there was no bodily resurrection (the body was burned that night per Exodus 12:10; the soul went to She'owl on Unleavened Bread; recognition failures on the third day prove a non-corporeal return); most souls simply cease to exist; Christmas and Easter are pagan; the covenant renewal of Jeremiah 31 is still future and is with Israel and Judah; the New Testament text has over 300,000 variants and lacks whole passages (John 8, Mark 16).

The chapter's center is Galatians 3:10–13. He sets the KJV, NLT, and the Nestle-Aland/McReynolds interlinear beside his own rendering of Papyrus 46, then opens each passage Paul cites: Deuteronomy 27:26, Habakkuk 2:4, Leviticus 18:5, Deuteronomy 21:22–23. In each case he argues Paul truncated the verse and reversed its sense, so that the citation in context affirms the Torah's saving role. He treats Paul's own use of nomos for the Hebrew towrah in Galatians 3:10 as settling that Paul is attacking the written Torah, not rabbinic law. Habakkuk 2:4–6, which Paul cites, is then read as a prophecy about Sha'uwl himself (developed in chapter 03). He contrasts Paul's partial quotations with Jesus quoting Deuteronomy 8:3 in full against Satan (Matthew 4:4).

The second half reads Matthew 5–7 as Jesus's advance warning: Matthew 5:17–19 (not one stroke of the Torah passes; whoever loosens the least command will be called "least," elachistos, which he equates with the meaning of Paulos); 5:20; 7:7–12 (the Torah as the Father's good gift); 7:13–14 (the narrow way vs. the broad, popular way, which he links to Habakkuk 2:5); 7:15–20 (false prophets in sheep's clothing; wolves, harpax, linked to Paul's harpazo in 1 Thessalonians 4:17 and to Genesis 49:27, "Benjamin is a ravenous wolf," since Paul claims Benjamin in Romans 11:1; "thorn" linked to the thorn in the flesh and the goad of Acts 26:14); 7:21–23 (he argues ou pas should read "not any," so that calling Jesus "Lord" is itself disqualifying; the excluded are those practicing anomia, Torahlessness); 7:24–27. He then states ground rules: only the Torah, Prophets, and Psalms are Scripture; of the Greek texts only Jesus's words are inspired; Paul quotes Jesus once and gets it wrong. Seven numbered points on the Torah vs. the covenant follow, and the chapter closes with several pages of his own renderings of Isaiah, Deuteronomy 4, 6, 30, 31, Joshua 8 and 24, Psalms 19, 25, 37, 78, and Numbers 15.

**Key texts used:** Galatians 3:10–14 and 2:16–3:25 (a continuous "literal" rendering). Deuteronomy 27:26; Habakkuk 2:1–6; Leviticus 18:1–5; Deuteronomy 21:22–23; Deuteronomy 8:1–6; Matthew 4:4, 5:17–20, 7:7–27, 16:11, 24:30–34; John 5:43; Genesis 49:27; Romans 11:1; 1 Corinthians 9:20–22; 2 Corinthians 12:6–7; Acts 26:14; Deuteronomy 18:15–19 (as a prophecy of Jesus). Translations compared: KJV, NLT, Nestle-Aland 27 with McReynolds interlinear, Vulgate.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** Each verse is given three or four times: interlinear, amplified (every word with glosses and grammatical parsing: stems, tenses, moods), "trimmed," and then the English versions. Nomos is always rendered "Towrah" and derived from nemo, "to allot/nourish," so that the Greek word is said to mean "the allotment which provides an inheritance" rather than "law." Scripture references are given as "Dabarym / Words / Deuteronomy," "Mizmowr / Song / Psalm," "Mattanyah / Yahowah's Gift / Matthew." Lexica cited by name: Strong's, Complete Word Study Dictionary, Dictionary of Biblical Languages, Exegetical Dictionary. He reads tense and voice as theologically decisive (e.g., subjunctive = "mere possibility," so faith enters; middle voice = the subject benefits himself).

**Claims to check:**
1. Papyrus 46 dated 85–125 CE and "the oldest meaningful codex" of the New Testament. The usual date is about 200 CE (a minority argument for a late first-century date exists). He also says P75 is "one hundred years later" than P46, which would place it well after its usual third-century date.
2. That charis "can only be attested in one other place" outside Paul before the fourth century. Later in chapter 02 he himself counts occurrences in John 1, Luke, 1–2 Peter, James, Jude, and Revelation and argues each away on manuscript grounds; the Septuagint's routine use of charis for Hebrew chen is not addressed. He also says there is no first-, second-, or third-century manuscript of Luke, though P75 and P45 contain Luke.
3. That "Jesus" was "initially conceived in the 17th century" after the letter J, and that the name was "never written on any page of any pre-Constantine codex" because Divine Placeholders were used instead. The abbreviations exist (the nomina sacra, see ch. 02); whether they are placeholders for Hebrew names or contractions of the Greek Iesous is the issue. The "J" point concerns English spelling, not the name's existence.
4. That "Christos" means "drugged" and that the manuscript abbreviations were based on "Chrestus." Standard lexica give christos as "anointed," from chrio, "to smear/anoint." See ch. 02 for the Chrestus evidence he cites.
5. That Deuteronomy 27:26 reads "invoking harm upon oneself is whoever is not established by the words of this Towrah." The standard sense is "cursed is he who does not confirm/uphold the words of this law by doing them." The point of disagreement is whether Paul's citation reverses the verse or restates it.
6. That Habakkuk 2:4–6 names Sha'uwl. The name does not appear in 2:4; the identification rests on his reading of ki-she'ol ("like Sheol") in 2:5 as "associated with Sha'uwl" (ch. 03 expands this). The Masoretic text and the Qumran pesher (1QpHab) differ on 2:5 ("wine" vs. "wealth" betrays), and he says his rendering is "based upon the Dead Sea Scrolls"; compare 1QpHab column 8.
7. That Matthew 5:19's elachistos ("least") is a pun on Paulos. The Greek word means "least/smallest"; the link to the Latin name is his inference, not a textual feature.
8. That Acts 26:14 ("hard to kick against the goads") was "plagiarized from the words of the Greek god Dionysus." The proverb appears in Euripides, *Bacchae* 794–795 (spoken by Dionysus), and in Aeschylus and Pindar; it was a common Greek saying.
9. That Jesus "spoke Hebrew and Aramaic, never Greek," that Matthew wrote in Hebrew, and that the Greek Matthew was made "perhaps a century later" in Egypt. Papias (via Eusebius) is the source for a Hebrew/Aramaic Matthew; the dating and location are the author's.
10. That "there isn't even a Hebrew word for obey," and none for "submit." Shama' is routinely rendered "obey" in context by standard lexica; whether that is a mistranslation is the claim.
11. That "Lord" (kyrios, 'adon, Ba'al) is "the title God ascribes to Satan," that God is never properly called Lord, and that Matthew 7:21 should read "not any one saying Lord, Lord" rather than "not everyone who says." The Greek ou pas ho legon is normally read "not everyone who says"; 'adon/'adonai is applied to God thousands of times in the Hebrew Bible.
12. That the physical body of Jesus was incinerated the night of the crucifixion per Exodus 12:10, and that no bodily resurrection occurred. This is doctrinal, not textual; no gospel says this.
13. That there are "over 300,000 known differences" between manuscripts and that no words for church, cross, holy, saint, Christian, Jesus, Christ, Lord, God, Spirit, or Gospel "can be found in any ancient manuscript." The variant count is in the usual range (often given as 400,000); the second claim depends on treating the nomina sacra as non-Greek.
14. That Paul quotes Jesus "only once" (1 Corinthians 11:24–26) and gets it wrong. The repo's own count is about twice (1 Corinthians 7:10–11; 9:14) plus the Supper words.
15. That Yahowah's name is "found 7,000 times." The usual count for YHWH is about 6,800.
16. That Matthew's name is "Mattanyah." The Hebrew behind Matthaios is usually given as Mattityahu (Mattithiah); Mattanyah (Mattaniah) is a different biblical name.

### Chapter 02: Euangelion – Healing and Beneficial Message

**Argument:** A clause-by-clause commentary on Galatians 1:1–24. Galatians 1:1: Paul's choice of a Latin name signals allegiance to Rome; "apostle" is a title the Twelve never gave him; "Jesus Christ" has title and name reversed and lacks the article; "raised from the dead" is wrong because the Son is a "diminished corporeal manifestation" of Yahowah (John 14:28, with E=mc² as illustration) and the body was not raised. He applies the Deuteronomy 13 and 18 tests in summary form. A long excursus on the Divine Placeholders follows: the overlined abbreviations (ΙΗΥ, ΧΡΥ, ΘΥ, ΚΣ, ΠΝΑ, and the stauros abbreviation) that appear in Greek manuscripts are, he argues, deliberate stand-ins for Hebrew names and titles that Greek letters cannot spell; translators who expand them to "Jesus," "Christ," "Lord," "God," "Spirit," and "cross" have substituted words the authors did not write. He argues the Chi-Rho forms derive from Chrestus ("useful, upright") rather than Christos, citing Codex Sinaiticus at Acts 11:26, 26:28, 1 Peter 4:16 ("Chrestianos"), 1 Peter 2:3 (chrestos), Tacitus *Annals* 15.44, and Suetonius *Claudius* 25. He rejects the term "nomina sacra" and the "too sacred to write" explanation, and then concludes that Paul himself probably did not use the placeholders; an Egyptian scribe harmonized his letters with the Gospels and Septuagint.

Galatians 1:2–3: ekklesia is "called out" and the KJV's "church" is a political substitution he attributes to Francis Bacon and King James; charis is the name of the Greek Graces (Charites, Roman Gratia), erotic goddesses in Homer, so "the Gospel of Grace" is pagan and violates Exodus 23:13 and Hosea 2:16–17; the Hebrew chen/chanan would have been the right concept. Galatians 1:4: the "present evil age" (aionos poneros) from which Christ "plucks out" (exaireo, "gouge out") believers is identified, via Colossians 1:24–26 (where he reads Paul as "co-savior" and "co-author" of Scripture), as the Torah and its covenant. An extended reading of Romans 7:1–12 (the widow analogy) is offered as proof that Paul's "old written code" is the Torah, that Paul calls the Torah the source of sin, and that Paul's "lusts" blamed on the Torah suggest, in his view, a hidden sexual life. Galatians 1:5: "amen" is linked to the Egyptian Amen-Ra; doxa is "opinion."

Galatians 1:6–9: Paul's "I marvel" shows he is not a prophet; metatithemai is mutiny; "another gospel" means the competitor was God's own Torah; "gospel" (he argues) does not mean "good news" but "Gott's spell," and euangelion means "healing and beneficial message/messenger," first attested of Augustus at Priene in 9 BCE; the Gospels did not yet exist (Matthew in Hebrew only, Mark from Peter's preaching per Eusebius, Luke unknown, John not referenced), so Paul cannot have meant them. The curse of 1:8–9 is read as Satan cursing through Paul and as the origin of Christian intolerance. Galatians 1:10 (peitho, "persuade God?"), 1:11–12 (gospel "by me," by revelation; John 18:20 "I said nothing in secret" against secret revelations; Romans 16:25 and Ephesians 3:2–9 "mystery" language as pagan), 1:13–14 (he reads the imperfect tenses of portheo and prokopto as "I was and still am destroying the ekklesia" and "I continue to advance in Judaism," so there was no conversion; Acts 22:3 on Gamaliel contradicts "not taught by man"; 1 Thessalonians 2:14–16 as the seed of Christian antisemitism), 1:15–16 (election from the womb vs. freewill; P46 lacks "called me through his grace"). Galatians 1:17–19: the Arabia sojourn is rejected on five grounds (Acts 9 has Paul preaching in Damascus within days; the three-year story was needed to match the Disciples' three years with Jesus; Acts 15 vs. Galatians 2 shows Paul's unreliability; the Torah was given in Arabia, so a contrary message cannot have come from there; the nineteen-year arithmetic from Galatians 1:18 + 2:1 back from a 50 CE council puts the conversion at 31 CE, before the crucifixion). Acts 9:15–16's bastazo ("carry/bear my name") is read as Satan wanting the "burden" of his reputation carried away. "James the Lord's brother" is used against Catholic perpetual virginity and against the KJV's "James" for Ya'aqob. Galatians 1:20 ("I do not lie" marks a liar), 1:21–24 (Philip Comfort and the NLT's "Christians"; "they glorified God in me" as Stockholm syndrome). The chapter includes the author's own biography (Presbyterian elder in youth, entrepreneur, Value America, *Prophet of Doom*, meeting al-Qaeda) as a contrast to Paul's.

**Key texts used:** Galatians 1:1–24 (every clause); Colossians 1:24–26; Romans 7:1–12; Romans 16:25; Ephesians 3:2–9; 1 Thessalonians 2:14–16; 2 Timothy 1:15; 2 Corinthians 11:14, 12:6–7; Acts 9:15–26, 22:1–3; John 14:28, 18:20; Exodus 12:10, 23:13; Hosea 2:16–17; Jeremiah 23:27; Leviticus 24:9–16; Deuteronomy 7:6, 13 and 18 (summarized); Psalms 19, 25, 37, 40, 105, 119:65–72; Proverbs 4:1–2; Deuteronomy 29:29–30:20; Matthew 24:23–28; Genesis 49:27. Manuscripts and secondary sources named: P46, P72, P75, P78, P18, Codex Sinaiticus, Codex Vaticanus, Tacitus, Suetonius, Pliny, Homer (Iliad, Odyssey), Eusebius on Mark, Philip Comfort's *Encountering the Manuscripts*, Wycliffe, the Vulgate, Merriam-Webster and the Encyclopaedia Britannica on "god/gospel."

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** The Divine Placeholder theory is the chapter's signature. He renders every placeholder in his translations with its Greek letters and a note ("ΘΥ – a placeholder ... to convey 'elohym"). "Euangelion" is never "gospel"; "ekklesia" is never "church"; "charis" is rendered "Grace" with a note that it is the goddesses' name. Pairs of near-synonyms are split so one is Godly and one is not: shalowm (reconciliation) vs. eirene (absence of war); chen vs. charis; paralambano vs. harpazo; epiginosko (knowing) vs. dokei (opinion) vs. pistis (faith). The author reads Greek verb tenses as biography (imperfect = still happening at the time of writing).

**Claims to check:**
1. The nomina sacra: that they occur "on every page of every extant codex written within 300 years" without exception; that they stand for Hebrew names and titles rather than contractions of Greek words; that stauros was always abbreviated; and that "Mother" was abbreviated in Sinaiticus. The abbreviations are real and near-universal in early Christian manuscripts (Hurtado, Roberts); the standard explanation is that they are contracted Greek words (Iesous, Christos, kyrios, theos, pneuma, stauros). The Hebrew-placeholder reading is the author's.
2. That the Septuagint used placeholders and that early LXX copies wrote the Tetragrammaton in paleo-Hebrew, with Kappa-Sigma replacing it only from the third century. The paleo-Hebrew Tetragrammaton in some pre-Christian LXX fragments (e.g., 8HevXIIgr, P.Fouad 266) is documented; the dating of the KS transition is debated.
3. The Chrestus evidence: Sinaiticus's first hand does read "Chrestianous/Chrestianos" at Acts 11:26, 26:28, 1 Peter 4:16 (later corrected); whether Vaticanus does is doubtful. Tacitus's manuscript reads "Chrestianos" for the group but "Christus" for the founder; the quotation given in the chapter has "Chrestus" for both. Suetonius does write "Chresto." 1 Peter 2:3 is a citation of Psalm 34:8 LXX ("taste that the Lord is good"), where chrestos is the ordinary adjective "good/kind," which bears on whether it is a title there.
4. That "Christos means drugged," that "Rx" derives from Rho-Chi, and that "Christian" means "those who are drugged." Lexica give christos "anointed"; Rx is usually traced to Latin recipe.
5. That charis is the name of the Graces and that Paul's usage "originated" the Christian sense. Charis as "favor, grace" is standard classical and Septuagint vocabulary (the LXX uses it for chen about 190 times, which the chapter does not mention; it does concede charis is absent from Matthew and Mark, which is correct).
6. That "gospel" does not mean "good news," that "god" never meant "good" in Old English, and that Wycliffe used "euangelie." Old English gōdspel is a calque of euangelium ("good" + "story/message"); Wycliffe's version uses "gospel."
7. That "church" entered the English Bible through Francis Bacon and the KJV. Wycliffe and earlier English use "chirche"; Tyndale notably used "congregation." Bacon's involvement in the KJV is a conjecture without documentary support.
8. That the name "James" was coined to flatter King James. "James" (from Late Latin Iacomus) is attested in English centuries earlier, including in Wycliffe.
9. That the Vulgate contains Jerome's gloss identifying James as "James the Less... a cousin." The Vulgate text of Galatians 1:19 does not; Jerome argued the cousin view in *Against Helvidius*.
10. That Eusebius says Mark wrote from Peter's preaching. Eusebius quotes Papias to this effect (*Church History* 3.39); the chapter attributes it to Eusebius, Origen, Tertullian, and Clement.
11. The Priene inscription (euangelia of Augustus, 9 BCE) is genuine and often cited.
12. Papyrus 46 readings: that P46 omits "and called me through his grace" in Galatians 1:15 (it does); that it reads elthon/apelthon rather than anelthon in 1:17; that it has meno rather than epimeno in 1:18; that it has peri rather than hyper in 1:4. Each is checkable in the Comfort–Barrett transcription.
13. The Acts 9 vs. Galatians 1 chronology (nineteen years back from 50 CE puts the conversion before the crucifixion). This depends on reading Galatians 2:1's "fourteen years" as counted from the three-year visit rather than from the conversion, and on a 50 CE council and a 33 CE crucifixion; most reconstructions take one or both differently.
14. That Jews in Damascus had no authority to arrest or kill, and that there were no high priests outside Jerusalem. Acts 9:2's "letters to the synagogues" is the text in question.
15. That Paul "fell in love with Timothy" and may have been homosexual. No text supports this; it is the author's conjecture and should not be repeated.
16. That "amen" derives from or evokes Amen-Ra, and that a Roman obelisk reads "The Name of our God is Amen." Hebrew 'amen means "firm, true"; the obelisk claim needs a source.
17. That Philip Comfort "coordinated" the NLT New Testament and acknowledges the placeholders in *Encountering the Manuscripts* pp. 224–225 while ignoring them in the NLT. Comfort was a senior NT editor on the NLT; the page citation is checkable.

### Chapter 03: Yaruwshalaym – Source of Salvation

**Argument:** Galatians 2:1–10 is read as Paul's rebuttal of the Jerusalem meeting of Acts 15, written immediately afterward (he dates both to 50 CE). Galatians 2:1–2: the "fourteen years" and "by revelation" are false (Acts 15:2 says he was sent because of a dispute); kata idian does not mean "privately," so the KJV's "privately" was added to keep Galatians 2 and Acts 15 apart; "those who seemed" (dokei) is Paul demeaning the Disciples as men of mere repute; "lest I run in vain" shows a man seeking human approval. He then lays out his version of the Deuteronomy 18:20–22 test as six elements (claims to speak for God; arrogance, zyd; public preaching in God's name; message inconsistent with the Torah; speaking in other gods' names; failure of history and prophecy) and Deuteronomy 13:1–6 (adding to or subtracting from the Torah; signs and wonders; other gods), promising to apply them at the end of the book.

The center of the chapter is Habakkuk. He translates 1:2–13 and 2:1–19 at length as a prophecy written "666 years" before Galatians: 2:1 (God will not change His requirements), 2:2 ("write it in large letters... so that he who reads may run" becomes "so that he might run away"), 2:3 (the vision is "for the mow'ed," the appointed times, which Jesus fulfilled in 33 CE while Sha'uwl was in Jerusalem), 2:4 (the puffed-up man; the righteous live by 'emuwnah, trust, which Paul misquotes twice as "faith"), 2:5 (the man of wine and betrayal; he reads the Hebrew ki-she'ol as naming Sha'uwl, and "gathers all the Gentiles" as Paul's mission), 2:6 (none question him; "woe to him who increases what is not his" with rabah read as "rabbi"), 2:7 (creditors; Paul solicits money), 2:8 (plunder; Rome's destruction of Jerusalem as consequence of Paul's antisemitism), 2:9–11 (the house set on high; Rome; "the stone shall cry out" as Shim'own the Rock summoning Paul), 2:12 (the city built on blood), 2:15–16 (making neighbors drink, looking on their nakedness; he reads this as "looking at their genitals" and the he'arel of 2:16 as Paul's opposition to circumcision; the "shame" as Paulos's "lowly" name), 2:18–19 (the carved image; "awake!" to wood; read as Paul's cross and resurrection preaching).

Galatians 2:3–5: Titus uncircumcised. This triggers the circumcision excursus: Genesis 17:9–14 verse by verse (shamar the covenant; circumcise "every male"; the eighth day; "those bought with money" re-read as "those longing to be included," adoption; the uncircumcised soul "cut off from her people," which he reads as the Spirit's family); circumcision is required of every covenant male, so an uncircumcised man cannot be saved (though circumcision itself saves no one), and baptism is only symbolic; souls that reject the covenant cease to exist; She'owl is for Satan and those who lead others away. Ezekiel 44:5–9 (uncircumcised foreigners in the sanctuary as "the greatest abomination") is applied to Paul. Galatians 2:4's "false brothers... spies" is answered with a long parallel between Paul's religion and Mithraism (rock-born savior, December 25, Phrygian cap, keys, seven heavens, lightning, handshake, exclusion of women, "Pater"). Galatians 2:5: he reports that P46 lacks "in submission" and "of the gospel" and carries a placeholder for God between "the truth" and "might continue." Galatians 2:6: "whatever they were makes no difference"; the KJV's "God accepteth no man's person" is read as edited to protect royal authority. Galatians 2:7–8: Paul divides the world into circumcised and uncircumcised, contradicting Pentecost (Acts 2) where all the Disciples were equipped for the nations; the masculine participle energesas ("the one who worked") cannot be the (feminine Hebrew) Spirit, so the power in Paul is identified via 2 Corinthians 12:7 as Satan. Galatians 2:9: "the right [hand]" was given to Paul alone and "fellowship" to Barnabas; dokei again; stulos vs. stauros; chrestos, eleos, and other words Paul could have used instead of charis; Yahowchanan's mission to the Greek world (Ephesus, John's Gospel, Revelation) refutes a Jews-only limit. Galatians 2:10 ("remember the poor") is read as a joke on Paulos ("lowly").

He then turns to Acts 15:1–7 and concludes with a 19-point list of contradictions between Galatians 2 and Acts 15: the meeting was forced by a dispute, not a revelation; the Torah-advocates were former Pharisees and elders, not spies; the welcome preceded Paul's presentation, so it was no endorsement; Peter's speech claimed the Gentile mission from the beginning (Acts 15:7), so there was no territorial division; and Paul yielded in practice by circumcising Timothy (Acts 16:3).

**Key texts used:** Galatians 2:1–10; Acts 15:1–7; Acts 16:3; Habakkuk 1:2–13, 2:1–19 (with 1QpHab consulted for 2:5–8); Deuteronomy 13:1–6, 18:20–22; Genesis 17:9–14, 1:27, 1:31; Ezekiel 44:5–9; Exodus 20; Revelation 3:12, 10:1; Matthew 24:23–28 (via ch. 02); 1 Corinthians 11:20–21; Romans 1:17; 2 Corinthians 12:6–7; Acts 2:1–12.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** "Dokei" ("those who seemed") is treated as a loaded word for subjective opinion, in contrast to epiginosko (knowledge). Habakkuk is rendered with the same amplification as Galatians, and the prophetic "he" is taken as a single named man. Hebrew grammatical stems (niphal, piel, hophal), the infinitive absolute, and the "paragogic nun" are invoked as carrying theological content (freewill, benefit to the parent, etc.). Circumcision is "the sign/signature of the Covenant." Baptism is "symbolic only." The five "conditions of the Covenant" (leave Babylon; trust Yahowah; walk to Him; observe the covenant; circumcise your sons) and five "benefits" (immortality on Passover, perfection on Unleavened Bread, adoption on FirstFruits, enrichment and empowerment on Seven Sabbaths) appear here and in ch. 04.

**Claims to check:**
1. That kat' idian in Galatians 2:2 does not mean "privately" and that no lexicon gives "by" for kata. Kat' idian is a standard idiom for "privately, in private" (used in the Gospels, e.g., Matthew 17:19); this is the chapter's load-bearing textual claim about the relation of Galatians 2 to Acts 15.
2. That hoi dokountes ("those who seemed/were reputed") is a slight. The phrase is a known idiom for "the men of repute," and commentators divide on how ironic Paul is being.
3. Habakkuk 2:5: that ki-she'ol can be read as "associated with Sha'uwl." The MT reads "he enlarges his appetite like Sheol." Consonants permit the reading; vowels and all ancient versions do not. 1QpHab reads "wealth" (hon) rather than "wine" (yayin) at the start of the verse.
4. Habakkuk 2:2: "so that he who reads it may run" (standard) vs. "so that by reciting it he might run away." The subject of "run" is normally the reader/herald.
5. Habakkuk 2:15–16: "their nakedness" (me'oreyhem) rendered "their genitals," and he'arel ("show yourself uncircumcised") read as Paul's anti-circumcision teaching; 1QpHab reads hera'el ("stagger") here, a known variant. Also 2:6's rabah ("increases") read as "rabbi."
6. The "666 years before Galatians" figure: Habakkuk is usually dated c. 609–605 BCE; 605 + 50 = 655.
7. Genesis 17:14 "cut off from her people": he reads "her" as the Spirit. The feminine suffix agrees with nephesh ("that soul"), which is feminine in Hebrew; standard translations render "his people" because the referent is the person.
8. Genesis 17:12–13 "bought with money" (miqnat kesep) re-pointed as kasap "longing." The MT pointing is kesep; the chapter concedes both readings and prefers the non-standard one.
9. That blood clotting factors peak on the eighth day. A popular claim traced to mid-twentieth-century sources; check against current pediatric data before repeating.
10. Ezekiel 44:7–9 as a prophecy about Paul and as proof that uncircumcised men cannot enter heaven. The passage concerns Temple service; the application is the author's.
11. The Mithraism parallels (December 25 as Mithras's birthday; blood baptism; keys; handshake; seven heavens; Phrygian cap as "freedom from law"). Several are from older (Cumont-era) or popular treatments; e.g., the blood rite (taurobolium) belongs to Cybele's cult, and Natalis Invicti (354 CE calendar) is Sol's, not certainly Mithras's. Treat each as a separate claim.
12. Galatians 2:8: that the masculine participle energesas rules out the Spirit as the agent. Greek participles agree with their (here implied) subject; pneuma is neuter in Greek regardless of ruwach's gender in Hebrew.
13. Galatians 2:9: that "the right [hand]" was given to Paul alone and only "fellowship" to Barnabas. The Greek is dexias edokan emoi kai Barnaba koinonias, normally "gave the right hand of fellowship to me and Barnabas."
14. Galatians 2:5 in P46: that "in submission" (te hypotage) and "of the gospel" are absent and a placeholder for God stands in their place. The known variant at 2:5 is the omission of hois oude ("to whom not even") in some Western witnesses; the P46 reading he describes should be verified in the transcription.
15. That the welcome in Acts 15:4 preceded Paul's presentation and so cannot be the "right hand" of Galatians 2:9. This is a sequencing argument; Acts 15:4 and 15:12 both have Paul and Barnabas reporting.
16. That Peter "quoted Yahowsha'" in Acts 15:7. The speech refers to Cornelius (Acts 10); no quotation of Jesus appears.
17. That the Jerusalem council was in 50 CE and Galatians written immediately after. Dates for both range widely (council 48–51; Galatians 48–57, before or after the council).
18. The return date: he says in 2013 it is "twenty years shy of Yahowah's return," i.e., 2033 (ch. 04 specifies Yom Kippur, October 2, 2033). A dated prediction; note it as such.

### Chapter 04: Anomos – Without an Inheritance

**Argument:** Acts 15 continued, as the "contemporaneous platform" for judging Paul, with Peter as "star witness." Acts 15:8–9: God gave the Spirit to Gentiles "just as to us" and made "no distinction," which he takes as refuting Paul's circumcised/uncircumcised division. Acts 15:10: Peter's "why do you tempt (peirazo) God by putting a yoke on the disciples' neck" is read as Peter accusing Paul (not the Torah-advocates) of tempting God like Satan and of trying to yoke the Disciples; John 21:18 ("another will gird you") is read as Jesus warning Peter about Paul. Deuteronomy 27:26 is defended again. Acts 15:11: Peter's "grace" would have been chen in Hebrew. Acts 15:12: sigao ("fell silent") is derived from sige, "a hiss," so the assembly "hissed" at Paul's signs and wonders. Matthew 24:4–5, 23–26 is applied: the one who says "here is the Christ... in the desert... in the inner rooms" is Paul (Damascus, Arabia); didomi "will give" signs means they were offered as proof, not performed; the Ebionites excluded Paul and Marcion canonized him. Luke 10:18 (Satan falling "like lightning") is set beside Acts 22:6 and 26:13 (light "from heaven... like lightning") and the three differing conversion accounts.

A long tour of 2 Corinthians 10–12 follows: Paul shakes down followers (9:7), demands obedience (10:6), speaks of "another Jesus" and "a different spirit" (11:4), calls himself not inferior to the "super apostles" (11:5), "robbed other churches" (11:8), identifies false apostles who are "masquerading" (11:13, which he turns on Paul), says Satan appears as "an angel of light" (11:14, "exactly like Paul experienced him"), judges by motives and deeds instead of by the Torah (11:15), "I speak as a fool" (11:16–21), the hardship list (11:23–28: paraphroneo "having become insane," deaths "many times," a day in the deep; he offers a diagnosis of psychosis, schizophrenia, and bipolar mania), "the God and Father of the Lord Jesus knows I do not lie" (11:31), the Aretas escape (11:32–33; Aretas controlled Damascus no earlier than 37 CE, so Paul's chronology fails), the third heaven and paradise (12:1–4, with harpazo and the Islamic night journey as parallel), the thorn/messenger of Satan (12:7–8), and "my grace is sufficient... power is perfected in weakness" (12:9–10), where he reads "the Lord" as Satan and astheneia as "sickening perversions," contrasting Isaiah 53:4/Matthew 8:17. 1 Corinthians 9:20–23 ("to the Jews as a Jew... to those without law as without law... all things to all men") is read as a confession of fraud and of being anomos; Matthew 16:26 (kerdaino) and 10:8 are set against it; 1 Corinthians 11:3–13 and Ephesians 5:22–24 are compared to Muhammad on women (with Genesis 3:16's b- rendered "with" rather than "over").

2 Thessalonians 2:7–11: "the mystery of lawlessness (anomias) is already at work" is read as Paul's own (asexual, singular, neuter) spirit, Satan, currently negating the Torah through Paul; "only the one who restrains" is God; the "lawless one" of 2:8 cannot be a noun; since the verbs are present tense, the "Torahless One" is Paul himself, so Paul is "indistinguishable from the Antichrist"; and since Satan cannot be killed, "the Lord Jesus will slay" is also wrong; epiphaneia is pagan vocabulary; and Paul's own "signs and wonders" are here attributed to Satan.

Back to Acts 15:13–29: James's speech. He compares James's Amos 9:11–12 citation in Acts (Codex Sinaiticus), the Septuagint, and the Hebrew: "Edom" (Hebrew) vs. "mankind" (Greek), "possess" vs. "seek," and concludes either James, Luke, or a scribe erred, and that Sinaiticus is unreliable (he suggests a Roman, Constantinian origin and repeats the Tischendorf "rescued from burning" story). Acts 15:19–20: P45 omits "to God" and "sexual immorality"; "strangled" comes from rabbinic kashrut, not the Torah; the list omits the covenant, the Ten Statements, and the feasts. Acts 15:21: "Moses is read in the synagogues every Sabbath" is the chapter's high point: the first followers kept the Sabbath in synagogues reading the Torah, so Sunday worship and "Christians" are later. Acts 15:22–29: Judas and Silas were sent to supervise Paul; the letter's tarasso ("unsettled you") and "we gave no such instruction" are a repudiation; but by compromising, not naming the Torah, and saying "it seemed good to the Holy Spirit," the Disciples erred (the Spirit has no opinions; the four "burdens" misrepresent a Torah that is "not too difficult," Deuteronomy 30:11). He sets out the five covenant conditions and five benefits, reads Exodus 34:12–15 (altars, pillars, Asherah) against Paul's "new covenant" and the Graces, shows 1 Corinthians 8 rejecting the decree on idol-meat, and ends with the Paul–Barnabas split (Acts 15:39–40; Paul "handed over to the Grace of the Lord"), Paul circumcising Timothy (Acts 16:3), and the conclusion that Galatians, Corinthians, Thessalonians, and Romans can be set aside: where Paul agrees with the Torah, use the Torah; where he contradicts it, ignore him.

**Key texts used:** Acts 15:1–41, 16:3, 22:1–6, 26:13–14, 9:3; John 21:18–19; Matthew 7:21–23, 8:17, 10:8, 16:26, 24:4–5, 23–26; Luke 10:18–19, 2:51; 2 Corinthians 9:7, 10:3–18, 11:1–33, 12:1–19; 1 Corinthians 4:14, 8:1–13, 9:20–23, 11:3–13; Ephesians 5:22–24; 2 Thessalonians 2:7–11; 1 Thessalonians 4:17; Romans 2:9, 8:35; Galatians 3:16; Isaiah 53:4; Amos 9:11–15; Deuteronomy 6:6–8, 13:1–6, 27:26, 30:10–11, 31:12, 19; Exodus 20:8–10, 34:12–15; Genesis 3:16, 9:4; Leviticus 3:17, 17:12–14; Proverbs 30:5; Psalm 19:7; Bukhari, Muslim, Tabari, and Ibn Ishaq on Muhammad's first revelation and his "devil"; Papyrus 45, Codex Sinaiticus.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** "Anomos/anomia" is "without an inheritance / Torahless" (from the nemo etymology), not "lawless." "Astheneia" is given an extended gloss ("perversions which have made us ill") built from the Hebrew choly of Isaiah 53:4 and the root chalal ("profane"). The psychiatric vocabulary (psychosis, schizophrenia, bipolar, megalomania, Stockholm syndrome) is applied as diagnosis. Islamic sources are used as a template for recognizing "the same spirit." The Disciples are criticized where their words depart from the Torah, so the author's standard is applied to Acts as well as to Paul. Phrases such as "the Devil's Advocate," "the Lowly and Little," and "the wannabe apostle" are used for Paul throughout.

**Claims to check:**
1. Acts 15:10's "yoke" as Paul's yoke on the Disciples rather than the Torah's requirements on Gentiles (the usual reading, since Peter is answering the Pharisaic believers of 15:5). Also John 21:18 as a prophecy of Paul (traditionally read of Peter's martyrdom).
2. Sigao (Acts 15:12) as "hissing." Sigao means "to be silent"; the "hiss" is drawn from a proposed etymology of sige and is the kind of etymological argument `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` warns against (compare the kolasis entry).
3. That Paul quotes Jesus only once (1 Corinthians 11:24–26). See ch. 01, claim 14.
4. That the Ebionites were "the first to propose a canon." Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 1.26.2) and Eusebius (3.27) say they used Matthew only and rejected Paul; Marcion's is usually called the first canon.
5. The three conversion accounts (Acts 9, 22, 26) as evidence Paul lied, and the Luke 10:18 lightning parallel. The repo's own card declines to use the accounts this way (see overlaps).
6. The Aretas chronology (2 Corinthians 11:32; Galatians 1:18, 2:1). Aretas IV's control of Damascus is usually placed c. 37–40 CE, which is also the reason many scholars date the conversion c. 33–36 and the escape c. 37–39; whether the arithmetic fails depends on how the fourteen years are counted (see ch. 02, claim 13).
7. The "third heaven" as borrowed from or parallel to Islam. Jewish apocalyptic (Testament of Levi, 2 Enoch, rabbinic texts) already has multiple heavens, centuries before Muhammad.
8. Paraphroneo (2 Corinthians 11:23) as a literal admission of insanity; the NASB's parenthetical "(I speak as if insane)" is quoted accurately, but it marks the phrase as rhetorical.
9. The gloss on astheneia/choly as "perversions." Isaiah 53:4's choly is from chalah ("be sick"); chalal ("profane") is a different root.
10. That Paul "told those who joined his assembly not to participate in Passover" (1 Corinthians 11:20–21, cited in ch. 03). The passage criticizes how the Corinthians ate the supper; check the text.
11. 2 Thessalonians 2:7–8: that to mysterion is "the one of mystery," that ho anomos cannot be a substantive ("the lawless one"), and that anaireo cannot mean "slay." Articles agree with their nouns (mysterion is neuter); substantival adjectives are ordinary Greek; anaireo "kill" is its common NT sense.
12. The Amos 9:12 variant (Hebrew "Edom"/"possess" vs. Greek "mankind"/"seek"). This is a well-documented LXX/MT difference; his inference that a scribe added the citation is his own.
13. Codex Sinaiticus as "written in Rome on the order of Constantine" and found "moments before" being burned. Scholars locate its production in Caesarea or Egypt; the burning story is Tischendorf's own account and is disputed by St. Catherine's.
14. P45 readings at Acts 15:19–21 (omission of "to God," "sexual immorality," "in every city"; ekei for echei). P45 is fragmentary here; verify each against the published transcription.
15. That "strangled" (pniktos) has no Torah basis and comes from kashrut, and that the blood prohibition would only "enrich kosher butchers." Genesis 9:4, Leviticus 17:10–15 (including "that which dies of itself or is torn"), and Deuteronomy 12:16, 23 are the relevant texts; the Noahide-law background is the usual explanation.
16. That Acts 15:21 proves the first followers met in synagogues on the Sabbath reading the Torah. The verse says Moses is read every Sabbath in the synagogues; what it implies about the assembly's own practice is an inference.
17. Genesis 3:16's mashal b- as "rule with you" rather than "rule over you." Mashal with b- is the ordinary Hebrew idiom for "rule over" (e.g., Genesis 1:18, 4:7).
18. Exodus 34:13's "Asherah" identified with the Greek Charites/Roman Graces and so with "grace." The text has the plural 'asherim (cult poles); the equation is the author's.
19. That Silas means "woody" (Latin silva). Silas is usually taken as a Semitic or Greek short form of Silvanus; the Latin derivation applies to Silvanus.
20. That "Paul will twice attest that he had not been taught by men" and that this contradicts Acts 22:3. Galatians 1:12 says the gospel was not taught to him by man; whether that excludes rabbinic schooling is interpretive.
21. The dated return: Yom Kippur, October 2, 2033 (Year 6000 by his reckoning).

### Chapter 05: Kataginosko – Convicted and Condemned

Covers Galatians 2:11–15, with long excursions into 2 Peter 3, John 21, Genesis 49, Revelation 2, Acts 19, 1 Timothy 1 and Acts 26.

**Argument:** The chapter opens on the Antioch confrontation (Galatians 2:11–14). The author reads it as a turf dispute: Paul, in his telling, was threatened by Peter's presence among Gentiles, and the two verbs Paul uses against him, *anthistēmi* ("stood against") and *kataginōskō* ("stood condemned"), are among the strongest condemnations available in Greek. He argues that if Peter really were "convicted and condemned" then Peter's own letters could not be trusted, which would remove the one passage Christians cite to make Paul's letters scripture, 2 Peter 3:15–16. So he turns to 2 Peter 3:12–18 and re-translates it as a warning *against* Paul rather than an endorsement: "beloved brother" is either ironic or a later scribal softening; *sophia* means human learning, not inspiration; *makrothymia* is righteous indignation rather than patience; "speaking of these things" becomes "speaking all around them" in circular reasoning; *dysnoētos* means not just "hard to understand" but "detrimental to understanding"; and the phrase usually rendered "the other scriptures" (*tas loipas graphas*) should be "the remaining inferior writings," meaning the rest of Paul's letters. He adds that *graphē* only ever means "writing," so no New Testament passage can confer "Scripture" status on anything. He notes that Eusebius, Jerome and Calvin all doubted 2 Peter's authorship and argues they did so because the letter damages Paul. He then presents Marcion as the reason Paul's letters were canonized at all, and says the oldest manuscript of 2 Peter (Papyrus 72) bears Marcion's influence.

The second movement collects texts he reads as prophecies about Paul. John 21:18–19 (Peter will be girded by "another" and led where he does not wish) is read as foretelling Paul's constraint of Peter rather than Peter's death; he treats verse 19's explanatory gloss as a scribal addition and argues the Greek *thanatos* stands behind Hebrew *deber*, "plague." Genesis 49:27 ("Benjamin is a ravenous wolf") is read as the Benjamite Paul (Romans 11:1), with each Hebrew word applied to Paul's career; Deuteronomy 33:12 is offered as the better Benjamite path. Revelation 2:2 (Ephesus tested "those who say they are apostles and are not") is applied to Paul and Timothy, since Ephesus is the only one of the seven cities where Paul is known to have preached; Acts 19 is then walked through as Paul's own confession of the charge (re-baptizing John's disciples, disputing daily, "setting boundaries" around the disciples, the Sceva episode, the book burning). 1 Timothy 1:1–11 and 5:13 are translated to show Paul calling Torah-teachers thoughtless and claiming the gospel was "entrusted to me." Acts 26:14, "hard for you to kick against the goads," is traced to Euripides' *Bacchae*, and a long comparison of Dionysus with Pauline Christianity follows. The chapter closes on Galatians 2:14–15: *Ioudaizein* is a word Paul "concocted," *hamartōlos* ("sinners of the Gentiles") a slur, and Mark 7:9–13 (Korban) is given as how Jesus actually criticized rabbinic tradition.

**Key texts used:**
- Galatians 2:11–15 (the passage under review), with Galatians 1:1–2:10 recapped in the author's rendering.
- 2 Peter 3:1, 3:12–18 and 1 Peter 1:1 (recipients include Galatia, so 2 Peter is addressed to the same audience as Galatians); Calvin's commentary on 2 Peter 3:15.
- Habakkuk 2:3, 2:5–6 in the author's rendering, where he reads Paul's name into the text.
- Matthew 7:15–25 (false prophets, fruit, "Lord, Lord," the house on the rock), rendered to point at Paul.
- John 21:14–19; Acts 15:10 (Peter's "yoke"); Psalm 23 ("my shepherd").
- Genesis 49:1, 49:10, 49:27; Deuteronomy 33:12; Romans 11:1.
- Revelation 2:2–3; Acts 19:1–19; 1 Corinthians 15:32; 1 Timothy 1:1–11, 5:13; Psalm 51:11–16 ("clean heart").
- Acts 26:14; Euripides, *Bacchae* (he gives line 790); Arthur Dyott Thomson, *Gospel History and Doctrinal Teaching Critically Examined* (1873), quoted at length on "Iesou" as a zodiacal term, with the author saying he cannot vouch for it.
- Thomas Jefferson's letter to William Short on Paul as "first corrupter."
- Mark 7:9–13; Hosea 4:6.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** Treats Greek compound etymology as the primary meaning (*kataginōskō* = "against knowing," *hypostellō* against *apostellō*, *akoloutheō* = "unified with the Way"). Reads nomina sacra (ΚΥ, ΘΥ, ΙΣ) as Hebrew names and titles. Argues 2 Peter was translated from a Hebrew original via Mark, which licenses him to adjust tenses. Introduces the "Marcion edited the text" explanation for any wording that favors Paul (here, *agapētos*). Dates Galatians to 50 CE as Paul's first letter, after the Jerusalem council and before the split with Barnabas. Dates Yahowah's return to Yom Kippur 2033 ("year 6000 Yah") and reads 2 Peter 3:12 as possibly describing a nuclear exchange. The *deber*/"plague of death" motif, drawn from his Habakkuk 2:5 reading, is used as the hinge for John 21 and Genesis 49.

**Claims to check:**
1. **2 Peter 3:16, *tas loipas graphas*.** He says *loipos* means "remaining" not "other," that *allos* would have been used for "other," and that *graphē* never means "Scripture." Check *loipos* usage (e.g., Romans 1:13 "the rest of the Gentiles," Luke 18:9) and *graphē* usage (Matthew 21:42, John 10:35, 2 Timothy 3:16, Romans 1:2) in a standard lexicon. The whole 2 Peter reading rests on this.
2. **Why Eusebius, Jerome and Calvin doubted 2 Peter.** Eusebius lists it among disputed books (*Church History* 3.3.1, 3.25.3) on grounds of reception; Jerome (*On Illustrious Men* 1) cites a style difference from 1 Peter; Calvin's doubts are about style and old age. Verify the Calvin quotation and whether any of the three connects the doubt to Paul, as the author claims.
3. **Papyrus 72 is "the most Free, and thus least reliable" pre-Constantine manuscript and shows "Marcion's fingerprints."** P72 (Bodmer VII–VIII) is 3rd–4th century and usually classed with the Alexandrian text; Marcion's canon excluded the Petrine letters entirely. Check what text-critical literature says about P72's character and whether any scholar links it to Marcion.
4. **Marcion biography and canon.** He says Marcion was born c. 85 to a bishop, fled to Rome in 133 during Bar Kokhba, studied under Cerdo, elevated Paul's "thirteen epistles" plus edited Luke *and Acts*, and "coined the term Old Testament." Standard accounts: arrival in Rome c. 140, break 144, a canon of ten Pauline letters (no Pastorals) plus an edited Luke and *no* Acts; "Old Testament" is usually credited to Melito of Sardis. Also his "300,000 discrepancies, nearly twice as many as words" line: the 300–400k figure is Ehrman's estimate of variants across all manuscripts against a New Testament of about 138,000 words; check how he is using it.
5. **John 21:18–19 as a prophecy of Paul.** The identification of "another" (*allos*) with Paul, and the re-rendering of verse 19 ("by what death he should glorify God") as "what kind of deadly plague he will attribute to God," with *thanatos* mapped onto Hebrew *deber*. Verse 19 is in all manuscripts; Tertullian (*Scorpiace* 15) already read verse 18 as Peter's crucifixion. The claim that a crucified man "cannot be taken where he does not want" is his argument against that reading.
6. **"No one recognized Him" in every post-resurrection account, negating bodily resurrection.** John 20:20 and 20:27 (Thomas and the wounds) and Luke 24:39 cut the other way; the non-recognition motif is real in Luke 24:16 and John 20:14, 21:4 but is not universal. The repo's `questions/resurrection.md` is the place to weigh this.
7. **Genesis 49:27 and the "only Judah and Benjamin were traceable" argument**, including the claim that all genealogical records were stored in the Temple and lost in 70 CE. Josephus (*Against Apion* 1.7) describes priestly records kept in Jerusalem; Luke 2:36 names Anna of Asher; Paul calls himself a Hebrew of Hebrews. The timing argument needs these.
8. **Revelation written in 69 CE; Ephesians written in 62; Paul died 67; Ephesus "the only one of the seven cities where Paul preached."** Mainstream dating of Revelation is c. 95 (Irenaeus places it under Domitian) with a minority at 68–69; Colossians 4:16 ties Paul to Laodicea; Acts 19:10 says "all Asia" heard. Revelation 2:2 plus 2 Timothy 1:15 is also in the repo's own Deuteronomy 18 file (see overlaps).
9. **Acts 19:11, *dynameis ou tas tychousas*.** He renders it "powers, not having obtained in association with God," i.e., Paul disclaiming God's involvement. The idiom *ou tas tychousas* means "not ordinary, extraordinary" (also Acts 28:2) and the sentence's subject is God ("God did extraordinary miracles by the hands of Paul"). This one is checkable from any lexicon.
10. **Acts 19:13–16: "there is no such thing as a Jewish exorcist," Sceva is a Latin name meaning "mind reader," no Jewish high priest lived in Ephesus, and "seven sons" becomes "both" (*amphoteroi*).** Josephus (*Antiquities* 8.2.5, Eleazar before Vespasian), Tobit, the Qumran exorcism psalms (11Q11) and Matthew 12:27 all attest Jewish exorcism; *Scaeva* means "left-handed"; *amphoteroi* for "all" is a known later-Greek usage (compare Acts 23:8), and commentators discuss "chief priest" as a member of a high-priestly family. Not a fabrication by default.
11. **Acts 19:19, *ta perierga* means "meddling," not magic, and the books burned were Torah and gospel scrolls.** *Perierga* is the standard term for magical arts in Hellenistic Greek (LSJ; Ephesus was famous for the "Ephesian letters"). His cross-reference to 1 Timothy 5:13 (busybodies) is a different form and context.
12. **"Tyrannus" denotes "the Lord is a tyrant."** Tyrannos was an ordinary Greek personal name attested in inscriptions. Check before repeating.
13. **Acts 26:14 and Euripides.** The goads proverb is in *Bacchae* 794–795 (he says 790), and also in Aeschylus (*Agamemnon* 1624) and Pindar (*Pythian* 2.94–96): a common saying, not uniquely Dionysian. The repo's shabbat-studies recap already makes this correction (see overlaps). His list of Dionysus parallels (virgin mother Semele, Egyptian flight, spring resurrection "on the Sunday nearest the equinox," the Charites as Dionysus's daughters, Lent as Babylonian) is comparative-religion material of the Hislop/Frazer type; the Semele and weekday-cycle details are checkable and the Charites' parentage differs by source (Hesiod makes them daughters of Zeus and Eurynome).
14. **Galatians 2:14: *Ioudaizein* "isn't a word" and was coined by Paul.** It appears in LXX Esther 8:17, Josephus (*War* 2.454, 2.463) and Plutarch (*Cicero* 7.6). Also his claim that Papyrus 46 omits "and not as the Jews" from 2:14; check the NA28 apparatus.
15. **"There is no Hebrew word for obey" (recurs in chapters 06–07).** *Shama' b-qol* ("hear the voice of") is the ordinary idiom for obey, including in Deuteronomy 28:1–2, which he quotes in chapter 07. A rendering choice presented as a lexical fact.
16. **Habakkuk 2:5 names Paul.** His whole prophetic frame depends on reading *she'ol* ("he enlarges his desire as Sheol") as *Sha'uwl*. Part 1's reader covers chapter 01 where this is introduced; note here only that chapters 05–08 lean on it repeatedly.
17. **Peter's first language was Hebrew and 2 Peter was translated from Hebrew by Mark**, used to justify changing tenses. Most scholarship regards 2 Peter as composed in an ornate Greek. Check before citing his tense choices.
18. **Timothy as Paul's "lover."** Asserted without a text. Recurs in chapter 08 (see there). The repo's cards explicitly avoid character arguments about Paul.

### Chapter 06: Pistis – Trust and Reliance

Covers Galatians 2:15–3:9, with word studies on *nomos* and *pistis*.

**Argument:** Here, he says, the debate proper begins: Galatians 2:16 denies that anyone is justified "by works of the law," and the author takes this as a denial that the Torah saves. His position is that the Torah is the only place the Covenant and the seven festivals are set out; those festivals (Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Weeks) are the mechanism of salvation that Yahowsha' fulfilled; Yahowsha' is the Torah embodied; so faith in Christ "if not by works of the law" is a distinction that severs the Messiah from the only thing that makes his life meaningful. He argues Paul chose *oida* (the weakest verb for knowing) in 2:16 to discourage investigation, and that *pistis* originally meant "trust and reliance based on evidence" but, because Paul supplies no evidence to trust, must be rendered "faith/belief" in his letters, and that Paul's popularity shifted the word's meaning in the language itself.

A long section on *nomos* argues from its root *nemō* ("to allot, to pasture") that the word properly means a nourishing allotment or inheritance, that the Septuagint's use of *nomos* for *torah* (about 200 of 220 occurrences) means *nomos* in Paul is "Torah" not "law," and that Paul's own quotation of Deuteronomy 27:26 at Galatians 3:10 proves he is targeting the written Torah and not rabbinic Oral Law. He surveys Strong's, the Exegetical Dictionary of the New Testament (including Monsengwo Pasinya's view that *nomos* in the LXX means "instruction"), TDNT and the Complete Word Study Dictionary, and gives the pictographic meanings of the four Hebrew letters of *torah*.

Galatians 2:17–21 is then read verse by verse: 2:18's *katalyō* ("what I tore down") is Paul admitting he dismantled the Torah's household (*oikodomeō* linked to Hebrew *beryth*/*beyth*); 2:19 ("through the law I died to the law… crucified with Christ") is Paul claiming to have been killed by the Torah and to be a co-sufferer, paired with Colossians 1:24 ("filling up what is lacking") as Paul casting himself as co-savior; 2:20's "Christ lives in me" is Paul as Christ incarnate, and *paradidōmi* ("gave himself for me") is read as Christ handing over his authority to Paul alone; 2:21 inverts the Torah's logic, and *charis* is tied to the Greek goddesses (the Charites/Gratiae). Matthew 23:1–7 is translated so that "do what they say" becomes "scrutinize what they say and do not do what they do," and Paul, a rabbinic student, is placed among the leaders Jesus warned about. Galatians 3:1–5 (*anoētos*, *baskainō*, spirit by "hearing of faith") is answered with Genesis 1:1–4 on the Spirit as feminine and John 3 on birth from above. Galatians 3:6–9 is answered from Genesis 15:6 (*'aman* = trust established through verification, not faith), Genesis 26:5 (Abraham kept "my Towrah," so the Torah accompanied the Covenant from the start), and the Covenant's five conditions and five benefits; he notes Paul always writes "Abram," the pre-Covenant name. Hebrews 10:22–23 is used as a "laboratory" to show Greek had other words for belief (*katechō*), confession (*homologia*), hope (*elpis*) and persuasion (*peithō*), so *pistis* cannot have meant those things. 2 Peter 3:15–17 is quoted again as the fitting verdict.

**Key texts used:**
- Galatians 2:15–3:9 (verse by verse); Galatians 3:10 cited early as proof of *nomos* = Torah.
- Romans 7:5–11 (Torah as occasion of sin), Colossians 1:24–25 (co-sufferer), Colossians on "fullness of the godhead."
- Matthew 23:1–7; Matthew 7:12; Luke 5:17 and John 8:17 (conceded as places where *nomos* refers to rabbinic law).
- Exodus 20:6 ("mercy to thousands… who keep my commandments").
- Genesis 1:1–4 (the Spirit hovering, feminine); John 3:1–21 (Nicodemus).
- Genesis 15:6; Genesis 26:4–5; Daniel 6:23 (*'aman*); Hebrews 10:22–23.
- Babylonian Talmud Shabbat 116a, Tosefta Shabbat 13:5 on burning the *gilyonim* and books of the *minim*, as evidence a Hebrew Matthew was burned.
- Lexicons: Strong's; EDNT (nomos and pistis entries); TDNT; Analytical Lexicon of the Greek NT; Complete Word Study Dictionary; 1 Peter 3:7 (*aponemō*).
- Papyrus 46 readings: no *de* at 2:16; "God and Christ" rather than "Son of God" at 2:20; placement of "crucified with Christ."

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** Grammatical parsing is read as doctrine: aorist = "a snapshot without any plan or process," subjunctive = "probable but not certain" or "reduced probability," middle voice = "affected by his own action," genitive singular = "exclusively for him." The *nomos* = "nourishing allotment" rendering is applied inside Paul's own sentences, producing deliberately paradoxical translations. "Trust" versus "faith" is set up as a strict opposition: trust follows evidence, faith fills its absence. The five Covenant conditions (leave Babylon, trust Yahowah, walk to Him and become perfect, observe the Covenant, circumcise sons) and five benefits (eternal life, perfection, adoption, enrichment, empowerment) are the author's own schema and appear in every chapter. The claim that scribes added the nomina sacra to Paul's letters to harmonize them with the gospels appears here. Comparisons to Muhammad and the Qur'an begin here and continue through chapter 08.

**Claims to check:**
1. ***Nomos* means "nourishing allotment," not "law."** The derivation from *nemō* is correct (LSJ), but in Koine *nomos* ordinarily means law, custom or statute, and the LXX chose it for *torah* because the Torah functioned as Israel's law. Verify the EDNT and TDNT quotations, especially the Pasinya paragraph and the line that "the concept that nomos means law is religious in origin."
2. **Counts.** *Torah* about 220 times in the Hebrew Bible with about 200 rendered *nomos*; *nomos* 430 times in the LXX; *nomos* 195 (or 197) times in the NT with 122 in Paul, 27 in Luke-Acts, 14 in Hebrews, 32 in Galatians; *nomos* absent from 1–2 Peter; ten times in James; *pistis* and *pisteuō* 243 times each. These are checkable with a concordance and mostly look right (standard figures are *nomos* 194, Galatians 32, Hebrews 14, James 10; *pistis* 243, *pisteuō* 241).
3. ***Pistis* "originally" meant trust and Paul's letters changed it to faith/belief.** Classical and Hellenistic *pistis* already covers trust, pledge, proof and belief; Philo, Josephus and Plutarch use it in religious senses before or alongside Paul. The EDNT claim that the words "were not religious terms" should be verified in context. The thesis that one author's usage shifted the lexicon is not testable.
4. **Genesis 15:6 "mentions nothing related to faith."** *He'emin* (hiphil of *'aman*) is the ordinary verb for believe/trust; the LXX has *episteusen*, which is what Paul quotes. His "trusted through verification" is a rendering, and James 2:23 quotes the same verse (see `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md`). Habakkuk 2:4's *'emunah* = faithfulness is a stronger, separate point and already in the repo's podcast notes.
5. **Genesis 26:5 proves Abraham had the Torah.** The Hebrew is plural, *torotay* ("my instructions"); the rabbinic reading that Abraham kept the whole Torah (b. Yoma 28b) has precedent, but it is an anachronism argument on its face. Note the author uses it to deny Paul's "430 years later" sequence in chapter 07.
6. **Paul "consistently" writes "Abram," the pre-Covenant name.** The Greek *Abraam* is the LXX transliteration of *Abraham* (Hebrew final *-m*; LXX Genesis 17:5 has *Abram* → *Abraam*). This claim appears to be a misreading of the Greek form, and it recurs in chapters 07–08.
7. **"Yahowsha'" would be a girl's name in Greek, and *Iēsous* resembles Zeus.** *Iēsous* is the standard LXX form of Joshua/Yehoshua, used for the book of Joshua. Check before repeating anything built on it.
8. **The nomina sacra in P46 were added by Alexandrian scribes and originally stood for Hebrew names.** Nomina sacra are abbreviations of Greek words (*Kyrios*, *Theos*, *Iēsous*, *Christos*, *Pneuma*) found in nearly all early Christian manuscripts. This assumption underlies the whole book's method; it is worth a one-line note wherever the book is cited.
9. **P46 dating and readings.** "As early as 85 CE, no later than 125": the common dating is c. 200, with Y. K. Kim's late-first-century proposal (1988) widely rejected. The variants he cites at 2:16 (*de*), 2:20 ("God and Christ"; P46 and B do read *theou kai Christou*) and 2:14 are checkable in the NA28 apparatus; the 2:20 one is genuine and worth knowing.
10. **Hebrew Matthew burned by rabbis (Shabbat 116a; Tosefta Shabbat 13:5).** The *gilyonim*/*minim* passage is real; whether *gilyonim* means gospels is debated, and nothing in it says "Hebrew Matthew." The Hebrew-original claim rests on Papias via Eusebius (*Church History* 3.39.16). Compare `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md`.
11. **Colossians 1:24 as Paul claiming to be co-savior.** The common reading is the "messianic woes" (sufferings still to be endured by the church), and Colossians' authorship is disputed. Interpretation, not lexicon.
12. ***Charis* is "the name of the Greek goddesses of charity, licentiousness, and merriment."** *Charis* is a common noun (grace, favor, thanks) across Greek literature and the LXX; the Charites are personifications of it. Etymological association presented as meaning; recurs in chapters 07–08.
13. **Matthew 23:3 "do what they say" retranslated as "scrutinize what they say."** The repo's Torah card handles this verse as an objection (see overlaps). His rendering removes the tension rather than answering it.
14. **Galatians is Paul's first letter, 50 CE, after the Jerusalem council and before the Barnabas split.** Dates range 48–55 and many put 1 Thessalonians first; the North/South Galatia question decides much of this. Flag whenever his chronology is load-bearing (it is, for his Peter/2 Peter sequence in chapter 05).
15. **Hebrews was written by "one of Paul's disciples."** Authorship is unknown. Minor, but he builds his *pistis* "laboratory" on it.

### Chapter 07: Towrah – Prescriptions for Living

Covers Galatians 3:10–29, with a long preface of Proverbs and Psalms on the Torah and a closing scorecard of all 74 verses so far.

**Argument:** After quoting Proverbs 4, 6, 7, 28, 29 and Psalms 1 and 19 in his rendering as "God's perspective on His Torah," he reaches what he calls the verses that prompted the whole project (a New Zealand radio program's claim that the Torah had been replaced by faith). Galatians 3:10 is "the ultimate confession": by translating Deuteronomy 27:26's *torah* as *nomou*, Paul shows that every *nomos* in Galatians means the written Torah, so the "he meant rabbinic law" defense is closed. He then argues that each of Paul's four Torah citations in 3:10–13 (Deuteronomy 27:26, Habakkuk 2:4, Leviticus 18:5, Deuteronomy 21:23) was chosen for word-linkage (curse/Torah/do; Torah/justified; Torah/perform; curse/hanging on wood) and that each, restored to its Hebrew context, says the opposite of what Paul makes it say. Deuteronomy 27:26 in his rendering curses those *not* established by the Torah's words; Habakkuk 2:4 is about trust/reliance (*'emunah*) and sits beside the verse he reads as naming Paul; Leviticus 18:5, parsed as a qal perfect ("observe") followed by a qal imperfect ("do"), shows God asks for complete observation but only ongoing, imperfect doing, so flawless performance was never the standard; Deuteronomy 21:22–23 is a prophecy of Passover (the hanged man's body removed before night, *qalalah* read as "diminished," the body burned per Exodus 12:10), and Paul's "Christ became a curse" makes "the cure for the disease the disease."

Galatians 3:15–18 is read as a tactic to pry the promises to Abraham apart from the Torah that records them: human covenants are revoked constantly; "seed" singular (3:16) is a ploy because *zera'* is collective; the 430 years (3:17) is arithmetically fine (Genesis 15:13's 400 and Exodus 12:40's 430 are both in the Torah) but the inference that the later Torah is irrelevant to the earlier Covenant collapses on Genesis 26:5; *diathēkē* and *klēronomia* carry the sense of a dead father's will, which is where "Old Testament" comes from; *klēronomos* contains *klēros* (lot), so Paul makes heirship a matter of chance. In 3:19–25 he takes "added because of transgressions" (bracketed as missing from an older manuscript), "ordained by angels through a mediator," "the law is not against the promises," *synkleiō* ("shut up," he says: netted like fish), *phroureō* ("kept in custody") and *paidagōgos* ("schoolmaster," which he renders slave-disciplinarian and traces to *pais*/*paiō*, "to strike," and to rabbinic usage) as a cumulative portrait of the Torah as prison and taskmaster. Against it he sets Matthew 5:17–19, arguing *mē nomisēte* forbids a belief that would become commonplace, and that *elachistos* ("least in the kingdom") is the Greek for "Paulos." In 3:26–29 he reads baptism as replacing circumcision, *endyō* ("put on Christ") as "plunge/sink" with a pun on Satan's name, "neither Jew nor Greek, male nor female" as contradicting Jeremiah 31 and Paul's own teaching on women, and 3:29's "Abraham's seed" (plural, all believers) as contradicting 3:16's singular seed. He closes with a scorecard: of Galatians 1:1–3:29 (74 verses), none completely accurate, 58 inaccurate (78%), and 96% inaccurate, incomprehensible or irrelevant; he also describes his translation workflow and admits he began the project hoping to rescue Paul by reading *nomos* as rabbinic law.

**Key texts used:**
- Proverbs 4:1–4, 6:23, 7:1–2, 28:4–5, 28:9–10, 29:18; Psalm 1:1–2, 19:7–9 (his renderings; note Proverbs 4:3 is rendered so that the speaker becomes the "only begotten Son").
- Galatians 3:10–29 verse by verse; Galatians 1:4 recalled as the "old system" parallel to 3:13.
- Deuteronomy 27:9–26 and 28:1–2; Habakkuk 2:1–6, 9–10, 15–16; Leviticus 18:1–5; Deuteronomy 21:22–23; Exodus 12:10.
- Genesis 15:7–18 (the 400 years, the cutting of the covenant), 17:7–9, 26:4–5, 3:15 (seed of the woman); Exodus 12:40 implied.
- Matthew 5:17–19, 7:12; Luke 24:44 anticipated; Hosea 4:6; Jeremiah 31 (reconciliation of Judah and Israel); Yom Kippur "Year 6000."
- Colossians 1:24–25 recalled; Romans 7 recalled.
- Lexicons as in chapter 06; the NLT Galatians committee named (Borchert, Moo, Schreiner, Taylor, Comfort).

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** The "word-linkage" thesis (Paul picks verses for shared vocabulary, not shared meaning) is the chapter's organizing idea and its most transferable one. Hebrew stem/conjugation parsing (qal perfect consecutive vs. qal imperfect) is read as doctrine the same way Greek tenses were in chapter 06. "Curse" is unpacked through *ara*/*airō* (prayer, lift) so that *katara* becomes "not having one's burden lifted." The verse scorecard (Completely Accurate / Irrelevant / Insufficient / Half Truth / Unintelligible / Inaccurate) is the author's own grading. He introduces the NA27 interlinear as a "handrail" quoted before each verse so readers have an independent witness. Chronology: Year 4000 Yah = 33 CE, 6000 = 2033, 7000 = 3033. He reverses the usual story: perhaps Paul created Marcion rather than Marcion creating Paul.

**Claims to check:**
1. **Deuteronomy 27:26 says the opposite of Galatians 3:10.** Hebrew: *'arur asher lo-yaqim et-divrei ha-torah ha-zot la'asot otam* ("cursed is he who does not uphold the words of this torah to do them"). His rendering makes the person the object ("is not established by the words") rather than the subject ("does not uphold the words"); the LXX, which Paul quotes closely, reads it the ordinary way and adds "all." The "diametrically opposed" verdict depends on that subject/object swap. Check the syntax with a Hebrew grammar or an interlinear.
2. **Habakkuk 2:4 *'emunah*.** "The righteous will live by his faithfulness/steadfastness" is a real and well-supported reading (it is Tabor's and the shabbat-studies hosts'); the Qumran pesher (1QpHab col. 8) reads it as faithfulness to the Teacher. Separate this from his reading of 2:5 as naming Paul, and note the 2:5 textual issue (MT "wine," 1QpHab "wealth"). Check `christianity/dead-sea-scrolls/` for what we hold of 1QpHab.
3. **Leviticus 18:5 grammar: perfect = complete observing, imperfect = imperfect doing, so flawless performance was never required.** In instructional Hebrew the *weqatal* (perfect consecutive) carries the same modal/future force as the imperfect; the aspect contrast he builds on does not work that way. Also note Paul quotes the LXX of 18:5 verbatim, the same phrase recurs in Ezekiel 20:11, 13, 21 and Nehemiah 9:29, and the rabbinic gloss "live by them and not die by them" (b. Yoma 85b) is the traditional answer to the flawless-performance reading.
4. **Deuteronomy 21:23 as a Passover prophecy, with *qalalah* = "diminished and slighted as a result of an *owth* (sign)."** *Qelalah* means curse (Genesis 27:12; Deuteronomy 11:26); there is no "sign" in the Hebrew; the verse says bury the same day, not burn. Two genuinely useful observations sit nearby and are checkable: Paul's quotation drops the LXX's "by God," and the Temple Scroll (11QT 64:6–13) applies "hanged on a tree" to crucifixion, which would be a held primary source if the repo has that column.
5. **"Seed" singular (3:16).** *Zera'* is collective and has no distinct plural for offspring; that much is right. But singular-seed readings have Second Temple precedent (4QFlorilegium on 2 Samuel 7:12; Genesis 22:17–18 with singular verb), so "ploy" is a judgment. This is item 2 of `OPEN-RESEARCH.md` (Tabor's Genesis 12:7 argument).
6. **The 430 years.** Genesis 15:13 (400) vs. Exodus 12:40 (430 in Egypt per MT; "in Egypt and Canaan" per LXX and Samaritan). Paul follows the LXX/Samaritan count from Abraham; the author's reconciliation is plausible but should be stated with the textual note.
7. **Matthew 5:19 *elachistos* = "Paulos."** *Elachistos* is the ordinary superlative "least"; the connection to Latin *paulus* ("small") is wordplay. If this line is ever used, 1 Corinthians 15:9, where Paul calls himself *elachistos* of the apostles, is the textual hook he does not mention.
8. ***Paidagōgos* is "a loanword from rabbinic usage," and *pais* derives from *paiō* ("strike").** *Paidagōgos* is classical Greek (Plato, Xenophon); rabbinic Hebrew borrowed it (*pedagog*), not the reverse. LSJ gives *pais* and *paiō* as different roots. The Torah-as-slave-driver reading rests on these.
9. ***Klēronomos* = "heir by chance" because *klēros* means casting lots.** *Klēros* means lot, portion, allotment; *klēronomia* is the LXX's ordinary word for Israel's inheritance of the land. Etymology presented as connotation.
10. **Galatians 3:19 "added because of transgressions" bracketed as absent from "an older manuscript."** He does not name it. Check the NA28 apparatus for the *paradoseōn*/*praxeōn* variants and for what P46 actually reads.
11. **Galatians 3:1: the "cross" is pagan and *stauros* is only an upright pole.** Koine *stauros* covers both stake and cross; the "pole" argument is the familiar one. Also, in 3:22 he renders *graphē* as the Torah ("the writing shut up all under sin") after arguing in chapter 05 that *graphē* never designates Scripture; the two chapters treat the word differently.
12. **Galatians 3:13 "redeemed us from the curse of the law" means redeemed from the Torah itself.** The genitive can mean "the curse the law pronounces"; most readings take it that way. His reading is one option, not the only one.
13. **NLT committee attribution.** He names Gerald Borchert, Douglas Moo and Thomas Schreiner (plus Mark and Daniel Taylor and Philip Comfort) as the Galatians team. Verify against the NLT's published list before repeating.
14. **The scorecard (0 of 74 verses "completely accurate").** Useful to know when the book is cited by others; the categories are the author's grading, not a measurement.
15. **Proverbs 4:3 rendered as the "only begotten Son by way of the Mother's presence."** The Hebrew is Solomon speaking of being his father's son, tender and only (*yachid*) before his mother. Compare any standard translation before using his Proverbs prefaces.

### Chapter 08: Echthros – Despised Adversary

Covers Galatians 4:1–20 (the Hagar/Sarah allegory, 4:21–31, belongs to the next chapter).

**Argument:** Galatians 4:1–7 is read as the "fourth stroke" against the Torah: the heir as a child is "no different from a slave" under a *kyrios* (which he notes is the word translators render "Lord," and which he connects to *ba'al*), under *epitropoi* ("foremen") and *oikonomoi* ("estate managers") until the father's set time. The key word is *stoicheia tou kosmou* (4:3, 4:9), which he argues was a technical term in Greek religion and Stoic cosmology for the four elements and the deified heavenly bodies, so that Paul is equating the Torah with pagan mythology. He quotes the Exegetical Dictionary of the New Testament at length to show that Christian scholars themselves take Paul to be using *stoicheia* of "the Jewish Torah" together with the Galatians' former paganism, and argues the plural forms and surrounding context make the Torah the referent. He adds that "Judaizers" are a Pauline invention and that Jews did not proselytize. 4:4 ("born of a woman, born under the law") could have been accurate if *hypo* were read "by means of," but the context makes it "subject to"; 4:5's *huiothesia* ("adoption") is a word Paul coined; 4:6's "Abba" replaces Hebrew *'ab* with Aramaic, and "of his Son" is absent from P46; 4:7's "heir" again contains *klēros* (lot). Against this he sets Luke 24:44–49: everything written in the Torah, Prophets and Psalms must be fulfilled; *nekros* means separation (from the prodigal son), not a corpse; the "promise of the Father" is the Spirit at Shavuot; the disciples, not Paul, are the witnesses.

Galatians 4:8–10 is read as a trilogy: enslaved to "those which by nature are no gods," returning to "weak and beggarly *stoicheia*," and "observing days, months, seasons and years," which, since P46 reads a participle that joins verse 10 to verse 9, means the Sabbath, the festivals and the Jubilee are being named as the instruments of enslavement. He records that on his first pass he read this as an attack on pagan calendars (Druid, Persian, Cybele, the Temple of Augustus at Ancyra, the Dionysia) and now rejects that reading. Galatians 4:11–20 is treated as Paul's personal appeal: "become as I am" is an imperative to imitate Paul rather than Yahowsha'; *deomai* ("I beseech") is derived from *deō* ("bind") and read as a wish to control; the "infirmity of the flesh" (4:13) is tied to 2 Corinthians 12:7's "messenger of Satan"; P46's "my trial" (4:14) means Paul's own temptation; "received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus" is Paul comparing himself to the Messiah; the plucked-out eyes (4:15) are called sadistic; 4:16's *echthros* ("your enemy") gives the chapter its title; 4:17's unnamed opponents were, he thinks, Torah-teachers from Jerusalem; 4:19's birth pangs make Paul "the mother of his Faith," which he connects to speculation about Paul's sexuality; 4:20's "I am perplexed" shows Paul did not know what to say because he did not know Yahowah. The chapter compares Galatians' tone to the Meccan surahs of the Qur'an and counts Paul's first-person pronouns in 4:11–20.

**Key texts used:**
- Galatians 4:1–20 verse by verse; Galatians 3:7, 3:18–25 recalled as the chain "curse → death → prison → taskmaster → slave."
- Colossians 2:8, 2:20 (*stoicheia* again); Romans 8:23, 9:4 (*huiothesia*); Romans 1:1 ("slave of Christ").
- 2 Corinthians 12:6–7 (thorn, messenger of Satan); 2 Timothy 1:15, 4:16 implied ("die alone").
- Luke 24:44–49; Luke 15:11–32; Isaiah 9:6; Psalm 19:7; Hosea 4:6; Jeremiah 31; Leviticus 23 implied (Shabuwa').
- Habakkuk 2:3–6, 10, 15–16 (his rendering, including "observing genitals" and "uncircumcision"); Daniel 11:37 implied ("regard for the desire of women").
- Acts 14 (Zeus and Hermes at Lystra); the Monumentum Ancyranum / Temple of Augustus and Rome at Ancyra; Res Gestae.
- EDNT on *stoicheion* (quoted at length); Complete Word Study Dictionary ("Paul calls the ceremonial ordinances of the Mosaic Law worldly elements"); Analytical Lexicon on *ginomai*.
- P46 readings at 4:6, 4:10, 4:11, 4:14.

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:** Reads *stoicheia* through its cognates (*stoicheō*, "march in rank"; *systoicheō*, which Paul uses in 4:25) and a list of *sy-* compounds as a character sketch of Paul. Treats the four controlling nouns (*paidagōgos*, *epitropoi*, *oikonomoi*, *stoicheia*) as a single metaphor for the Torah; notes they are plural, which he first says might point to rabbinic law and then rules out. Reads nomina sacra as evidence: ΥΙΣ used of believers at 4:7 is taken as a scribal slip that shows scribes added placeholders to Paul generally. "Son set" is his literal gloss for *huiothesia*. The Antichrist is "the Towrahless One." Pronoun-counting as a measure of self-focus. Aside comparing Paul's "change" to a 2008 campaign slogan. Repeated speculation about Paul's relationship with Timothy. The chapter's final line stamps the date "09-01-2013."

**Claims to check:**
1. ***Stoicheia tou kosmou* is a pagan religious term and refers to the Torah.** The meaning is genuinely disputed: physical elements, elementary principles, or elemental/astral spirits (Delling in TDNT argues for "elementary things"; many commentators for astral powers). The same word means physical elements in 2 Peter 3:10, 12, which the author translated that way in chapter 05, and "first principles" in Hebrews 5:12. Verify the EDNT quotation and whether it says what he says it concedes. The repo's Tabor notes read it as planetary powers (see overlaps).
2. **"All four Greek terms were rendered in the plural."** *Paidagōgos* is singular in 3:24–25; *epitropoi*, *oikonomoi* and *stoicheia* are plural. Small, but he draws an inference from it.
3. **"Judaizers were invented by Paul" and "Jews have seldom if ever proselytized."** *Ioudaizein* predates Paul (LXX Esther 8:17; Josephus *War* 2.454, 2.463; Plutarch); Matthew 23:15, Josephus on Izates of Adiabene (*Antiquities* 20.17–96), Horace (*Satires* 1.4.142–143) and Dio Cassius 57.18.5a attest proselytizing. The Galatians' opponents are unnamed in the text; the author's identification of them as Jerusalem Torah-teachers is as inferential as the "Judaizer" label he rejects.
4. ***Huiothesia* is "a word Paul made up and only he uses."** It is a standard Hellenistic legal term for adoption, attested in inscriptions and papyri from the 2nd century BCE. Likely wrong; check LSJ or Moulton-Milligan.
5. **P46 variants claimed:** omission of "of his Son" at 4:6; participle *paratērountes* at 4:10; aorist *ekopiasa* at 4:11; "my trial" (*mou*) and omission of "nor rejected" at 4:14. The 4:10 participle is a recognized P46 reading and 4:14's *mou* vs. *hymōn* is a known split (the Alexandrian witnesses have "your"); confirm 4:11, 4:6 and the omission at 4:14 in the NA28 apparatus before relying on them. The joined 4:9–10 reading is load-bearing for his "Sabbath and festivals named" claim.
6. **"Abba" demeans Hebrew; the Spirit would say *'ab*.** Mark 14:36 puts "Abba" on Jesus's lips; Romans 8:15 parallels Galatians 4:6. Interpretive, and it cuts against his own Jesus-spoke-Hebrew assumption.
7. **Luke 24:46 *nekros* means "separation," not a corpse, so no bodily resurrection is meant.** *Nekros* is the ordinary word for dead/corpse; the prodigal-son usage (Luke 15:24, 32) is figurative by context. `questions/resurrection.md` is where this belongs; do not import his reading.
8. **Luke 24:47 "in His name" refers to Yahowah, "not My."** The Greek *epi tō onomati autou* has the Christ of verse 46 as its nearest antecedent. Check the pronoun before using it.
9. **The Dionysia "five days each spring," resurrection "on the Sunday closest to the vernal equinox," Lent as "the Babylonian festival."** The City Dionysia fell in Elaphebolion (March/April); Greece had no seven-day week, so "Sunday" is an anachronism; the Lent claim is of the Hislop type. The repo's `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` do-not-use list is the model for how to treat this category.
10. **Temple of Augustus at Ancyra "rebuilt from a temple to Cybele."** The Monumentum Ancyranum (temple of Roma and Augustus, carrying the Res Gestae) is real; an earlier dedication to Men or Cybele is discussed in the archaeology. Minor; check if used.
11. **Habakkuk 2:15–16 as God foretelling Paul's fascination with "genitals" and "uncircumcision."** The Hebrew has "look on their nakedness" and "drink and be uncircumcised" (*he'arel*; 1QpHab reads *hera'el*, "stagger"). The application to Paul is the author's. Also the Daniel 11:37 inference about the Antichrist and the claims about Paul and Timothy: no text supports them, and the repo's `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` states plainly that it will not argue from Paul's character.
12. **"Paul would die alone, without a single supporter."** 2 Timothy 4:16 ("no one stood with me") sits beside 4:11 ("only Luke is with me"). Check before repeating.
13. ***Deomai* ("I beseech") derives from *deō* ("bind") and means a wish to control.** *Deomai* (beg, need) and *deō* (bind) are distinct lexemes in LSJ. Etymological conflation; the "control" reading rests on it.
14. **Deuteronomy 13 and 18 named as the tests Paul fails** (he says both, in passing, near 4:13). This is the repo's own framework in `christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md`; the book never develops it in these chapters.
15. **The Zeus/Hermes episode at Lystra "if true, which I doubt."** Acts 14:11–19. He doubts it without argument; note it as the author's judgment.

### Chapter 09: Douleia — Bondage (Galatians 4:21–31)

**Argument:** The chapter opens not with Paul but with Jeremiah 31:31–34, which the author treats as the hinge of the whole book. He argues that *chadash* should be read "renewed/restored," not "new"; that the renewal is promised to the houses of Israel and Judah only, not to Gentiles or a church; that it has not yet happened (he dates it to Yom Kippur in his "year 6000 Yah," 2 October 2033); and that its content is the Torah written on the heart. On that reading, a "New Testament" is a category error and a second covenant is impossible, so Paul's Hagar/Sarah allegory fails before it starts.

He then takes Galatians 4:21–31 verse by verse. His case: Genesis records one covenant, cut with Abraham and confirmed through Isaac and Jacob; Ishmael was expressly excluded (Gen 17:18–19) and Hagar and her son were sent away free, not enslaved (Gen 21:14), centuries before Sinai; Hagar was never at Sinai; and the Sinai covenant commemorates liberation from slavery (Exod 20:2), so pairing Sinai with bondage inverts the text. He argues Paul misquotes Genesis 21:10 (dropping "and she said to Abraham," and changing the ending from "with my son Isaac" to "with the son of the free woman") and misapplies Isaiah 54:1, which he reads as a prophecy about the Set-Apart Spirit's work after 33 CE rather than about Sarah. The chapter closes with a survey of online Christian commentaries on "the Judaizers," arguing that no such group is attested, that Paul's foe in Galatians is grammatically singular (he identifies him as Peter), and that the Galatian audience was mostly Jewish, not Gentile.

**Key texts used:**
- Jeremiah 31:31–34 (his translation, with a list of ten verb occurrences of *chadash* to argue "renew")
- Genesis 16–21, especially 17:18–19, 21:10, 21:12–20 (Ishmael's exclusion; Hagar sent away); Genesis 22:2 ("your only son")
- Exodus 20:2, 20:14; Deuteronomy 13:1 (his numbering; English 12:32, "do not add or subtract"); Isaiah 40:8; Malachi 3:6
- Matthew 5:17–19 (he renders *elachistos*, "least," as "Paulos," reading the Sermon on the Mount as naming Paul); Matthew 7:11–14; Luke 24:44–49
- Psalm 19 (as the counter-text to Paul on the Torah); Proverbs 4:1–4, 28:9
- Isaiah 53–54 (54:1 as the verse Paul quotes; he reads *be'ulah*, "married woman," as "controlled by Ba'al")
- Galatians 4:3, 4:8–10 (*stoicheion*), Romans 9 (two-covenant theme repeated)
- Translations compared: Nestle-Aland McReynolds interlinear, Vulgate, KJV, ESV, NASB, NLT; P46 readings noted at 4:23, 4:30, 4:31
- Lexicons named: Strong's, Analytical Lexicon, Complete Word Study Dictionary, Exegetical Dictionary of the NT (on *Sina*)

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:**
- Hebrew-name renderings used throughout: Yahowah, Yahowsha', Yitschaq, Ya'aqob, Yaruwshalaim, Towrah, Bare'syth (Genesis), Shemowth (Exodus), Dabarym (Deuteronomy), Yasha'yah (Isaiah), Yirmayahuw (Jeremiah), Mizmowr (Psalms), Mattanyah (Matthew), Yahowchanan (John), Sha'uwl (Paul), Ma'aseyah (for "Messiah," glossed "Implement Doing the Work of Yah")

- Geography: Mount Sinai placed in Arabia east of the Gulf of Aqaba; Shur and Paran located in northwest Arabia
- "The Lord" treated as Ba'al's title and therefore Satan's; "Lord" said to replace the divine name 7,000 times in Bibles
- A seven-point summary of what he calls "Pauline Doctrine" (two covenants, Sinai = Hagar, Torah enslaves, promise bypasses Torah, no link between Jesus and Torah, children by promise alone, belief requires rejecting Torah)
- Aside linking Ishmael to Islam and Genesis 16:12 to modern Muslims

**Claims to check:**
1. *Chadash* "primarily" means renew/restore, with ten verb occurrences listed (1 Sam 11:14; 2 Chr 15:8, 24:4, 24:12; Job 10:7 [sic, 10:17]; Ps 51:12 [Heb.]; 103:5; 104:30; Isa 61:4; Lam 5:21). Check the count and the lexica; note that Jer 31:31 uses the adjective *chadashah*, and that the renew/new debate is a live scholarly one, not settled by the verb list.
2. The noun *beryth* "is never plural" in the Hebrew Bible. Check BDB/HALOT. Note that Paul's plural *diathekai* also appears at Rom 9:4 and Eph 2:12.
3. Mount Sinai in Arabia east of the Gulf of Aqaba (the Jabal al-Lawz hypothesis) is a minority position; the traditional site is in the Sinai Peninsula. His reading of Gal 4:25 ("Sinai in Arabia") depends on it.
4. Hagar and Ishmael were "freed." Genesis 21:14 says Abraham sent her away; whether the text uses manumission language is worth checking before repeating "freed."
5. *Epaggelia* was "primarily a legal term" meaning "summons," and the sense "promise" is "a legacy of Paul's argument." Check LSJ and BDAG for pre-Pauline uses of "promise."
6. Galatians 4:30 alters Genesis 21:10. Compare the LXX of Gen 21:10 (which ends "with my son Isaac") against Paul's "with the son of the free woman"; this one is checkable and the difference is real.
7. Galatians 4:27 quotes Isaiah 54:1 in LXX wording; the author's Hebrew rendering ("children controlled by Ba'al") departs from the standard "married woman" (*be'ulah*). Check the Hebrew.
8. "There is no evidence for Judaizers" and the Galatian audience was "mostly Yahuwdym." Mainstream reading takes the audience as Gentile (Gal 4:8, 5:2, 6:12). Check both.
9. P46 is dated "late first or early second century" and P75 "one hundred years after" it. Mainstream dating puts P46 c. 200 CE (the 85–125 proposal is Y. K. Kim's and is not widely accepted) and P75 c. 175–225.
10. Paul's singular foe in Galatians 5:10 is Peter, and Paul "was called to Jerusalem by the Apostles" to explain his preaching against circumcision. Acts 15:2 says the Antioch assembly sent him. Check.
11. Deuteronomy "13:1" for "do not add or subtract" is the Hebrew versification (English 12:32).
12. *Elachistos* in Matthew 5:19 "means Paulos in Latin." *Paulus* means "small" in Latin; *elachistos* is the Greek superlative "least." The pun is the author's, not the text's.
13. Only Esau and Sha'uwl are "called out by name to demean" in the Hebrew Bible. Check (and note the "Sha'uwl" reading depends on Hab 2:5, treated in ch. 12).
14. The name "Israel" is "based upon Sarah's name." Standard etymology derives it from *sarah* (to strive) + *El*, not from the matriarch's name.
15. YHWH is replaced by "Lord" "7,000 times." The usual count of the Tetragrammaton is about 6,800.
16. The messenger who speaks to Hagar in Genesis 21:17 is suggested to be Satan. This is the author's speculation, offered as such.
17. Chronology: Abraham's covenant in 1968 BCE (= "2000 Yah"), the crucifixion in 33 CE (= "4000 Yah"), the return on 2 October 2033 (= "6000 Yah"). A Ussher-style scheme; treat as his system.

### Chapter 10: Pharmakeia — Poisoned (Galatians 5)

**Argument:** The chapter works through Galatians 5:1–26 and argues that Paul here moves from demeaning the Torah to attacking the covenant's sign, circumcision, and then to a flesh-versus-spirit dualism the author identifies as Gnostic. Against 5:2 ("if you are circumcised, Christ will profit you nothing") he sets Genesis 17:7–14 (circumcision as the everlasting sign; the uncircumcised male "cut off") and Ezekiel 44:5–9 (uncircumcised foreigners excluded from the sanctuary), concluding that Paul and the Torah are irreconcilable on this point and that circumcision is one of five conditions for the covenant. Against 5:3 ("obligated to do the whole law") he sets David, whom God calls righteous despite violations, and Psalm 119:1–16. He reads *Charis* in 5:4 as the name of the Greek goddesses (the Graces), *pistis* as having shifted from "trust" to "belief" because of Paul, and the "one troubling you" of 5:10 as Peter. He calls 5:9 ("a little yeast") the only accurate sentence in Galatians and contrasts it with Matthew 16:1–12 on the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees.

The second half treats 5:11–26. He reads the P46 text of 5:12 as a prayed curse of self-castration and compares it to Qur'an 5:33; reads *aphorme* in 5:13 as "the starting point of a violent attack"; argues 5:14 ("the whole law in one word") contradicts Deuteronomy 6:4–7 and that Romans 13:8–9 omits half the Decalogue, which he then walks through from Exodus 20; and reads 5:16–26 as a transcription of Gnostic cosmology (Demiurge versus the One, flesh versus spirit), analysing each term in the "works of the flesh" and "fruit of the spirit" lists to argue they are either hypocritical given Paul's own conduct, or are things the Torah endorses (zeal, jealousy, festival feasting, choice). The chapter also sets out his doctrine of the soul: most souls simply cease to exist at death, with She'owl reserved for Satan, demons and those who lead others astray.

**Key texts used:**
- Genesis 17:7–14 (amplified, with stems and conjugations); claim that Qumran Genesis matches the MT from 17:12 to the end of the chapter
- Ezekiel 44:5–9 (read as addressed to Gentiles, and so to Paul's audience)
- Psalm 119:1–16; Deuteronomy 6:4–7; Exodus 20:1–17 (full walk-through, verse by verse, each paired with an accusation against Paul)
- Matthew 16:1–12 (sign of Jonah; yeast), with a Passover chronology (Thursday 14 Abib, 33 CE; three days and nights; First-Born Children on Sunday)
- Matthew 7:15–20 (good and bad fruit) as the test he applies to the "fruit of the spirit" list
- Habakkuk 2:5 and 2:15 (previewing ch. 12: "plague of death," "intoxicating," "looking at their genitals")
- Romans 13:8–9; Colossians 2:14; Romans 7; Song of Solomon (on sensuality); 1 Thessalonians (anti-Jewish passage mentioned)
- Qur'an 5:33 (three English versions) as a comparison for Gal 5:12; Hitler's *Mein Kampf* as an analogy for persuasive oratory
- Translations compared throughout: NA/McReynolds, Vulgate, KJV, NASB, NLT; P46 variants at 5:6, 5:10, 5:12, 5:14, 5:24, 5:25

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:**
- Grammatical parsing of nearly every verb (person, number, tense, voice, mood) used as evidence of intent
- "Divine Placeholders" (the *nomina sacra* abbreviations in P46) which he says Paul did not write and Alexandrian scribes added to harmonise Paul with the Gospels; where the placeholder would dignify Paul's meaning he substitutes the "corrupted" Greek name instead
- *Charis* = the Charites/Gratiae; *Christos* linked to "application of drugs" and *Chrestus* (title "used by Peter and three Roman historians") offered as the correct Greek title; *akrobystia* "a word Paul made up"
- "Five conditions" of the covenant (his schema), with circumcision as the fifth; Torah "observance" (examining) distinguished from "obedience"
- Gnosticism described as Platonic (Demiurge, Monad, the One), with a claimed Mithraic lion-headed-serpent image as its oldest depiction and Constantine as a Mithras initiate
- Soul annihilation as the default fate; eternal separation only for leaders who mislead
- Lexical readings of the vice list: *eidololatria* from "likeness," *pharmakeia* "administering drugs," *thumos* from *thuo* "sacrifice," *eritheia* "electioneering," *hairesis* "freedom to choose," *komos* equated with Hebrew *chag*
- *stoichomen* (5:25) read as soldiers marching in line; *kenodoxos* applied to Paul

**Claims to check:**
1. *Enechomai* (5:1) "primarily" means "to bear a grudge against" per "the ten most respected lexicons." BDAG gives "be subject to, be held by"; the grudge sense is Mark 6:19. Check.
2. *Akrobystia* is "a word Paul made up." It is standard LXX vocabulary (e.g., Gen 17:11, 14; Lev 12:3), pre-Pauline. This is checkable and appears to be wrong.
3. *Charis* is "a transliteration of the name of the Greek goddesses." *Charis* is an ordinary noun ("favour, grace") across classical Greek and the LXX; the Charites are named from it, not the other way around.
4. *Christos* "speaks of the application of drugs" and *Chrestus* is the proper title "used by Peter and three Roman historians." *Christos* is from *chrio*, "anoint"; Suetonius has "Chrestus" (Claudius 25); Tacitus has "Christus"; 1 Peter 2:3 has *chrestos* as a wordplay on Ps 34:8. Check all three.
5. Genesis 17:12 to end of chapter is "unchanged" between the Dead Sea Scrolls and the MT. Check which Qumran Genesis manuscripts actually cover Genesis 17.
6. P46 is "a complete copy of Galatians dating to the late first century." P46 has lacunae and is conventionally dated c. 200 CE (see ch. 09, claim 9).
7. The *nomina sacra* were added in Alexandria and Paul did not use them. No autograph survives; the origin of the *nomina sacra* is debated, and they appear in essentially all early Christian manuscripts. Treat as hypothesis.
8. Ezekiel 44:6–9 was "expressly written for Gowym – Gentiles" and addresses Paul's audience. The passage concerns foreigners brought into temple service; check the context before applying it to Gentile believers.
9. The Torah has "only five requirements," all concerning the covenant. This is the author's schema, not a textual list.
10. P46 reads *ara* ("curse") rather than *ophelon* at Galatians 5:12. Check the NA28 apparatus.
11. Passover chronology: 14 Abib 33 CE fell on a Thursday, crucifixion Friday, resurrection Sunday, "three days and three nights." The 30 vs 33 CE dating and the Friday/Thursday question are both contested; check.
12. Matthew was "spoken and recorded in Hebrew" by the eyewitness Matthew. This is the Papias tradition; mainstream scholarship holds Greek composition c. 80–90 CE. (The repo's `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` covers the Hebrew Matthew question.)
13. Paul's flesh/spirit language is Platonic Gnosticism with Demiurge and Monad. Developed Gnostic systems are second-century; whether "Gnosticism" existed in the 50s CE is itself disputed. Flag as anachronism risk.
14. The lion-headed serpent is "the oldest known pictorial depiction of a Gnostic deity," found "in Mithraic literature," and Constantine was "an initiate in the cult of Mithras." The leontocephaline figure is Mithraic, not Gnostic; Constantine's attested association is with Sol Invictus. Check.
15. Galatians 5:14 "one word" is wrong because Paul "used six words." *Logos* can mean "saying/statement"; the count depends on reading it as "word."
16. "The Lord" (*kurios*) is "Satan's title" derived from Ba'al. This is the author's system; *kurios* is the LXX's standard rendering of YHWH.
17. *Komos* is "synonymous with the Hebrew word *chag*." Different languages, different registers; a loose equation.
18. Habakkuk 2:15 refers to "looking at their genitals" (*ma'owr*). The word is a hapax; BDB gives "nakedness (pudenda)." The gloss is defensible; the link to Paul's view of circumcision is interpretive.
19. David is called "righteous" and "a man after His heart" despite violating "much" of the Torah (1 Sam 13:14; Acts 13:22). The citations are real; the inference (therefore Paul's "whole law" claim fails) is the author's.
20. Paul, as "the son of a Pharisee," was "circumcised eight days after he was born" (Phil 3:5 says "circumcised on the eighth day"; Acts 23:6 "son of Pharisees"). Fine, but note the reliance on Acts.

### Chapter 11: Prautes — Appropriate Response (Galatians 6; summary verdict on Galatians)

**Argument:** The chapter reads Galatians 6:1–18 and then delivers a statistical verdict on the whole letter. He reads 6:1 ("if someone is caught in a transgression, you who are spiritual restore him") as instituting surveillance and conformity, with *katartizo* an imperative and *skopeo seauton* a command to self-policing; he retranslates *prautes* from "meekness" to "a measured, considered response" (citing Aristotle) and says that is what the book's title asks of the reader. "The law of Christ" (6:2) becomes a third Torah after Sarah's and Hagar's; bearing one another's burdens is read as usurping the role Isaiah 53 assigns to the Servant. He finds 6:3–5 self-contradictory (no one is anything, yet each should boast in his own work; bear others' burdens, yet each bears his own), reads 6:6 as a command to pay teachers, and 6:7–10 as Gnostic flesh/spirit again plus works on behalf of "the household of the Faith."

A long excursus on 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 argues that Paul included himself among "we who are alive" at the coming of the Lord, so the prophecy failed; that *harpazo* describes a violent seizure, the same root Jesus uses for "ravenous wolves" in Matthew 7:15; and that *apantesis* etymologically means parties going in opposite directions. Back in Galatians, 6:11 (large letters) is read as Paul authenticating his own handwriting in his first letter; 6:12–13 (compelling circumcision to avoid persecution) is said to invert the historical record; 6:14's placeholders are taken as proof scribes inserted them; 6:15 ("new creation") contradicts 5:2; 6:16 ("the Israel of God") is called the seed of replacement theology; 6:17 (*stigmata*) is read as slave-brands, contrary to Leviticus 19:28; and 6:18's "Grace," "Lord" and "Amen" are each read as pagan names. The chapter then tallies the 149 statements of Galatians: 1 accurate (5:9), 8 irrelevant, 3 insufficient, 10 half-true, 15 unintelligible, 112 inaccurate (75%), and reprints the author's full rendering of Galatians 1–6.

**Key texts used:**
- Galatians 6:1–18, with P46 variants noted at 6:3 (*eiper*), 6:4 (*ekastos* omitted), 6:11 (*elikois*), 6:12 (placeholder for the name added), 6:13 (*houte*), 6:14 (extra *me*), 6:16 (*stoicheosin*)
- Isaiah 53:4, 6, 10–12 (the Servant bears burdens; claim that rabbis "were told to avoid reading" Isaiah 53)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 (full amplified translation); Matthew 7:15 (*harpax*)
- Galatians 2:9, 2:11 (*dokei*; Peter "condemned") recalled
- 2 Peter 3:15–16 (*loipos* claimed to mean "henceforth," not "other")
- Leviticus 19:28 (cuttings and marks); Acts 16:3 (Timothy's circumcision, implied)
- Aristotle on *prautes* as the mean between anger and no anger
- NA/McReynolds, Vulgate, KJV, NASB, NLT comparisons for every verse

**Distinctive method or vocabulary:**
- *Prautes* reinterpreted as "appropriate response" and made the book's thematic word
- *Pneumatikoi* analysed as *pneuma* plus a suffix from *tikto* ("to give birth")
- "Amen" read as the name of the Egyptian god Amun; "Grace" as the Charites; "Lord" as Ba'al
- *Stigma* as a slave or cult brand rather than a scar
- The verse-by-verse scoring tally (accurate / irrelevant / insufficient / half-truth / unintelligible / inaccurate) and the 75% figure
- Repeated claim that the KJV New Testament is a translation of the Vulgate, not the Greek, and that Francis Bacon headed the KJV project as an occultist
- Comparisons of Paul's commands to the Qur'an, the NSA, Stalin and the Inquisition
- Assertion, without a cited source, that Timothy was Paul's romantic partner

**Claims to check:**
1. *Pneumatikoi* contains a suffix derived from *tikto* ("bring forth"). *-ikos* is a common Greek adjectival suffix unrelated to *tikto*. Checkable; appears to be wrong.
2. Aristotle placed *prautes* between irascibility and inirascibility. *Nicomachean Ethics* 4.5; the substance is right.
3. "Amen" transliterates the name of the Egyptian god. Hebrew *'amen* ("firm, true") is from the root *'aman*; lexica treat the Amun connection as folk etymology. Check.
4. 1 Thessalonians 4:15–17 includes Paul among "we who are alive," so the prediction failed. This matches mainstream scholarship on Paul's imminent expectation (see repo overlap below). *Apantesis* as "going in opposite directions" is etymologising; in Hellenistic usage it is a formal "meeting" or civic reception. Check BDAG.
5. *Harpazo* means "violently seized by thieves." It means "snatch, seize" and is used of the Spirit taking Philip (Acts 8:39) and of a wolf snatching sheep (John 10:12); the range is wider than "robbery."
6. In 2 Peter 3:16 *loipos* means "henceforth," and translators rendered it "other" to make Paul's letters scripture. The adverb *loipon* can mean "henceforth," but 2 Peter 3:16 has the adjective *tas loipas graphas* ("the other writings"). Checkable; the claim does not hold as stated.
7. P46 reads *elikois* ("as old/tall as") for *pelikois* at 6:11. Check the apparatus (P46 and B are usually cited for *ēlikois*).
8. *Stigmata* in 6:17 means brands or tattoos forbidden by Leviticus 19:28. Mainstream readings take it as scars of beatings or as a metaphorical slave-mark; the Leviticus link is the author's.
9. Timothy was Paul's "lover," and Paul "circumcised his lover." No source is given; Acts 16:3 records the circumcision only. Treat the relationship claim as unsupported assertion.
10. Revelation was written in 69 CE, "less than seven years after" Ephesians. Mainstream dating is c. 95 CE (Domitian); the 68–69 date is a minority view.
11. Galatians is Paul's first letter, written 50–52 CE after the Jerusalem meeting, and the Galatians later "tried to stone him." The dating depends on the North/South Galatia question; many date it before the council (c. 48–49) or in the mid-50s. The stoning is at Lystra in Acts 14:19, before the letter on any dating.
12. The KJV New Testament "was a translation of the Latin, not the Greek." The KJV translators worked from the Textus Receptus Greek with the Vulgate and earlier English versions consulted. Check.

### Chapter 12: Metanoeo — Change Your Perspective (closing argument: Habakkuk, Deuteronomy 13 and 18, the Gospels, Paul's own letters)

**Argument:** The closing chapter steps back from Galatians to argue that Paul was foreseen and condemned by name. Its backbone is a reading of Habakkuk 1–3 as a prophecy about Sha'uwl rather than Babylon: the singular "he" throughout 1:3–4 and 1:11–17 is one man who paralyses the Torah, exchanges the Spirit for a different spirit, and nets Gentiles (the fishhook and dragnet are linked to the Christian *ichthys*); "the Rock" of 1:12 is Peter, appointed to rebuke him; 1:6–10 is read as Babylon's modern incarnation, the United States. Habakkuk 2 is the core: 2:3 points to the "Mow'ed" fulfilled in 33 CE; 2:4 is the verse Paul misquotes in Galatians 3:11 and Romans 1:17; 2:5 contains, on the author's reading, Paul's Hebrew name (the consonants of *she'owl*, "Sheol," are the same as *Sha'uwl*), together with "the plague of death" and the gathering of all the Gentiles; 2:6 (*rabah*) alludes to a rabbi; 2:15–16 to intoxication, circumcision and "little and lowly" (Paulos); 2:17 to "demonic beasts"; 2:18–19 to a caricature image and a wooden pillar commanded to rise from the dead. He computes 666 years from the prophecy (c. 615 BCE) to Galatians (52 CE). He notes several places where he follows the Qumran text over the Masoretic (1:15, 1:16, 1:17, 2:5, 2:6, 2:7, 2:18).

He then applies Deuteronomy 13:1–6 and 18:20–22 as a six-element test (claims to speak for God; arrogance; public preaching in God's name; message contrary to God's instructions; speaks in the name of other gods, i.e. Grace, Lord, Amen; predictions that fail) and finds six strikes. He argues at length that Galatians is genuinely Paul's and his first letter (no plausible forger; P46 order; 2 Peter's reference; Romans repeats the two-covenant climax). He then stacks Gospel and Pauline texts: Luke 10:18 (Satan falling as lightning) beside Acts 22:6 (the Damascus light, same root); Acts 26:14 ("kick against the goads") traced to Euripides' *Bacchae* and so to Dionysus, with a list of Dionysus/Jesus parallels and the Charites as Dionysus's daughters; Matthew 24:4–5, 23–26 (false claimants; "in the wilderness," "in the inner room"); Revelation 2:2–5 (Ephesus tested false apostles) with Acts 19:1–12 read as Paul re-baptising Jesus's own disciples and Acts 19:11 parsed as miracles done "not through God"; 1 Timothy 1:1–11 as Paul's confession of a campaign in Ephesus; John 21:15–19 (Peter will be girded and led where he does not wish) as Paul driving Peter out of Antioch; Matthew 5:17–20 and 7:7–27 (*elachistos* = Paulos; narrow gate vs popular way; wolves, *harpax*; "Lord, Lord"; *anomia*); Genesis 49:27 (Benjamin a ravening wolf) with Romans 11:1; 1 Corinthians 9:20–22 (all things to all men) as self-confessed disguise; John 5:43 (another coming in his own name); 2 Corinthians 10–12 (boasting, "out of my mind," the thorn as a messenger of Satan, the third heaven) as admissions of insanity and demon-possession; Matthew 16:26 on gaining the world.

The chapter closes with a psychological portrait offered as speculation: Paul as an insecure man rejected by his father, reduced to tentmaking, hostile to women, drawn to Timothy, recruited by Satan with the promise of influence; a list of similarly "insecure" figures (Nero, Akiba, Marcion, Constantine, Muhammad, Hitler, Stalin); and a call to *metanoeo*, change one's perspective to the Torah's, ending with Psalm 19:7.

**Key texts used:**

**Claims to check:**
1. Habakkuk 2:5 names "Sha'uwl." The MT reads *ka-she'ol*, "like Sheol" (the KJV has "as hell"), in a stock pairing with "never satisfied" (cf. Prov 27:20, 30:16). The consonants match Saul's name; the vocalisation and the idiom do not. Check 1QpHab (which has this verse and applies it to the Wicked Priest) and the repo's KJV at `christianity/Incoming/kjv/35-habakkuk.md`. This is the load-bearing claim of the chapter.
2. The Qumran text of Habakkuk 2:5 reads "wealth" (*hon*) where the MT reads "wine" (*yayin*). The author renders "intoxicating wine" from the MT here while preferring Qumran readings elsewhere in the chapter. Check 1QpHab col. 8.
3. "The Rock" in Habakkuk 1:12 is Peter. The MT's *tsur* is a vocative addressed to God ("O Rock, you have ordained him for correction"). Check.
4. The DSS read "sword" at 1:17 where the MT has "net"; 8HevXII gr has "bread grew large" at 1:16. Check both against published editions.
5. Habakkuk is dated c. 615 BCE and the "666 years" arithmetic. The date is a reasonable guess for the book; the count is numerology.
6. *Rabah* in 2:6 alludes to "rabbi." The verb means "multiply/increase"; *rabbi* derives from *rab*, "great/master." Etymological stretch.
7. *Qalown* in 2:16 means "little and lowly" and so names Paulos. *Qalon* means "shame, disgrace"; "light/small" is *qal/qalal*, a different root.
8. *Shed* in 2:17 is "demonic." The MT word is *shod* ("devastation"); "demon" is *shed* with different vowels and a different root. The author acknowledges the ambiguity and renders both.
9. "There is no Hebrew word for obey." *Shama'* is routinely rendered "obey" (e.g., Exod 19:5, Deut 11:27). Check.
10. Acts 26:14's "kick against the goads" comes from Euripides' *Bacchae* and so identifies the voice as Dionysus. The proverb is in *Bacchae* 795 but is also in Pindar (*Pythian* 2.94–96) and Aeschylus (*Agamemnon* 1624); it was a common Greek saying.
11. Dionysus was born on 25 December, rose each spring, and the Charites were daughters of Dionysus and Aphrodite. Hesiod makes the Charites daughters of Zeus and Eurynome; the Dionysus-Aphrodite parentage is a minor variant; the 25 December claim is a modern parallel, not an ancient attestation. Check each.
12. Acts 19:11 says Paul's miracles were done "not having obtained in association with the god." The Greek *dunameis te ou tas tuchousas ho theos epoiei dia ton cheiron Paulou* means "God did extraordinary (literally 'not the ordinary') miracles by Paul's hands"; *ou tas tuchousas* is an idiom for "not ordinary." The author's parsing does not hold. Checkable in any lexicon under *tunchano*.
13. The "disciples" of Acts 19:1–7 were Jesus's Disciples whom Paul re-baptised. The text says they had received John's baptism; the standard reading is followers of John. Check.
14. Revelation 2:2 identifies Paul and companions as the false apostles at Ephesus, and Revelation dates to 69 CE. The identification is an inference shared with others (see repo overlap); the date is a minority view.
15. 2 Peter was written by Peter and "specifically" addresses Galatians. 2 Peter 3:15–16 names no letter; critical scholars widely treat 2 Peter as pseudonymous. The repo's `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` already handles this.
16. 1 Corinthians 11:20–21 tells believers "not to participate in Passover" and "not to drink wine." The passage rebukes disorder and drunkenness at the common meal. Check.
17. P46 dates to 85–125 CE and is the "oldest extant codex" of Paul. Dating again (see ch. 09, claim 9); the order of letters in P46 (Romans, Hebrews, 1–2 Corinthians, Ephesians, Galatians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 Thessalonians) is correctly reported.
18. Biographical claims: Paul's father "sent him away," he was reduced to "women's work," "never knew the love of a woman," and loved Timothy romantically. No sources are given for any of these. Treat as the author's speculation, which he labels as such at the start of the section.
19. The 185,000 Assyrians (2 Kgs 19:35) and Sargon's 27,290 Samarian captives (Nimrud Prism) are correctly cited; the claim that Hezekiah "found a copy of the Towrah" conflates Hezekiah with Josiah (2 Kgs 22). Check.
20. Jeremiah "denounces Christmas, Easter, Sunday Worship, and calling Him 'Lord.'" No verses are cited. Jeremiah 10:1–5 is the usual Christmas-tree proof-text; it concerns idol manufacture. Flag as unsupported as stated.
21. Matthew 7:15's *harpax* and 1 Thessalonians 4:17's *harpazo* share a root. True (*harpazo*); the inference that the Sermon on the Mount thereby identifies Paul is the author's.
22. Genesis 49:27 (Benjamin as wolf) plus Romans 11:1 identifies Paul as the wolf of Matthew 7:15. The citations are correct; the identification is inference.
23. The tribe-of-Benjamin claim "can no longer be made" because "all genealogical records were destroyed in 70 CE." Check; genealogical claims persisted (e.g., in rabbinic and Christian sources) after 70.
24. Galatians 1 and 2 Corinthians 12:7 constitute Paul's "admission" of insanity and demon possession. The repo's `christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md` cites the same two verses more cautiously (see overlap below).

Chapter 05 gives the line as 790 (the recap says 795) and builds a Dionysus-Christianity parallel list on top; treat that list the way `CITATION-NOTES.md` treats the Gehenna garbage-dump claim.

**Galatians 4:3, 4:9 *stoicheia*, and 4:4 "born of a woman."** `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/06-…` and `14-…` read *stoicheia tou kosmou* as the planetary/elemental powers of Hellenistic cosmology; chapter 08 agrees it is a Hellenistic religious term and differs on the referent (Torah). `dr-tabor/08-…` reads "born of a woman" as the Hebrew idiom for "human"; chapter 08 reads it as Paul setting Mary up against Hagar for 4:21–31. `dr-tabor/03-…` ("trickster move" on the Hagar/Sarah allegory) is the next chapter's ground and belongs to Part 3.

**Marcion.** `questions/method-and-criteria.md` already owns the Marcion objection ("we're his photographic negative"). Chapter 05's Marcion history is not in the repo and contains errors on canon and dates (chapter 05, item 4); chapter 07's reversal ("perhaps Paul created Marcion") is a line, not an argument.

**Hebrew Matthew.** Chapter 06's Shabbat 116a / Tosefta Shabbat 13:5 material overlaps `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md` (which `CITATION-NOTES.md` lists among files corrected in 2026-10). Check how that file cites Papias before adding the Talmudic passage, and keep the *gilyonim* = gospels identification marked as debated.

**Resurrection.** Chapters 05 and 08 deny bodily resurrection (non-recognition motif; *nekros* = separation; the body "incinerated" per Exodus 12:10). `questions/resurrection.md` is the repo's position; these readings should not be merged into it without that card's criteria.

**Source hierarchy.** The book agrees with `CLAUDE.md` in keeping Paul out of the "what Jesus taught" stack. It differs in placing the Torah above the red text (the reverse of the repo's hierarchy), in reading the gospels as translations of Hebrew originals, and in treating its own eschatological calendar (2033) as settled. Nothing in chapters 05–08 is quotable as a source; the usable material is the set of checkable observations flagged above (the 2:20 P46 variant, the Deuteronomy 27:26 LXX "all," the dropped "by God" in 3:13, the Temple Scroll on "hanged on a tree," the Galatians 3:10 *nomou* point, and the word-linkage pattern).

### Chapters 09-12

The repo already works much of the same ground, usually with a narrower claim. The pattern across all four chapters: where the book and the repo cite the same verse, the repo's phrasing is the one that survives a challenge, and the book supplies additional leads (lexical, manuscript, prophetic) that need independent checking before any of them is used.

**Deuteronomy 18 and the failed-prophecy argument (ch. 11, ch. 12).** `christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md` → `## The Biblical Test (Deuteronomy 18:20-22)` and `## Paul's Prophecies That Did Not Come True` make the same core move: 1 Thessalonians 4:15–17's first-person plural includes Paul, and the event did not happen. The repo adds 1 Cor 7:29–31, 1 Cor 15:51–52 and Rom 13:11–12, and anchors it in Ehrman, Allison and Schweitzer. The repo also already lists Matt 7:15–20, Matt 24:4–5, Matt 24:23–26 (wilderness/inner room), Rev 2:2 with 2 Tim 1:15 and Acts 19, 2 Cor 12:7 ("messenger of Satan") and 2 Cor 11:23 ("out of my mind"), and Deut 13:1–5 in its cross-tradition table. Agreement on the texts is close. The difference is in what is claimed from them: `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` says in its position statement that this "is not a claim about Paul's character" and that "nothing here requires him to be a fraud," and under the prophecy objection says "We are not running Deuteronomy 18:22 as a verdict on the man." Chapters 11–12 run exactly that verdict (six strikes, demon possession, insanity, She'owl). The repo's `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` also records why genetic arguments (the Scofield example) cost credibility with audiences, and `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` explicitly declines to use the three conflicting conversion accounts as evidence Paul lied; the book uses them that way.

**Matthew 5:17–20 and the Torah (ch. 09, ch. 12).** `questions/jesus-and-the-torah.md` → `## Strongest Evidence` §1 builds on the same passage, notes the jot-and-tittle saying is in Q (Luke 16:17), and reads "fulfill" as "fill full," not "terminate." The book agrees that Jesus affirmed the Torah's permanence and adds the *elachistos* = Paulos pun, which the repo does not use and which is the author's own. The repo's one-sentence line, "Marcion abolished the Law. Paul relativized it. Jesus intensified it," states a sharper distinction than the book, which treats Paul and Marcion as the same position. `questions/method-and-criteria.md` handles the "you've reinvented Marcion" objection; the book's own answer to it (ch. 09, the "side with Marcion" option) is worth comparing.

**Circumcision (ch. 10, ch. 11).** This is the largest substantive divergence. The book makes physical circumcision a condition of salvation (Gen 17:14; Ezek 44:9). The repo's Torah card reads Thomas 53 ("the true circumcision in spirit") as relocation, not abolition, and offers Didache 6:2–3's graduated yoke as the earliest practice. On this point the repo's Jesus sits closer to Paul than the book does. `christianity/acts-15-james-four-decrees.md` on what James actually required is the relevant file; the book barely engages James.

**Peter at Antioch and the Jerusalem meeting (ch. 09, ch. 10, ch. 12).** `jesus-site-reference/source/docs/paul-vs-the-twelve.md` → `## Paul vs. Peter — To His Face` covers Galatians 2:11. The book's reading of Gal 5:10's singular "whoever he is" as Peter, and of John 21:18–19 and Habakkuk 1:12 as prophecies of Peter being driven out by Paul, have no counterpart in the repo. The repo's rule on Acts (weight it where it embarrasses Luke's harmonising project) differs from the book, which treats Acts via the interlinear as Paul's own testimony throughout.

**Revelation 2:2 and Ephesus (ch. 12).** `christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md` → `## Historical Witnesses Against Paul` already pairs Rev 2:2 with 2 Tim 1:15 and Acts 19. The book adds 1 Tim 1:3 and the re-baptism of Acts 19:1–7 and a mis-parse of Acts 19:11 (claim 12 above); do not import that parsing.

**2 Peter (ch. 11, ch. 12).** The book assumes Petrine authorship and that 2 Peter 3:15–16 targets Galatians. `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` under the 2 Peter objection notes the authorship is contested and uses only the concession that Paul's letters are "hard to understand."

**Habakkuk (ch. 10, ch. 12).** The repo holds Habakkuk only as KJV text, `christianity/Incoming/kjv/35-habakkuk.md`; it has no analysis file. KJV 2:5 reads "who enlargeth his desire as hell," which is the standard reading the book's "Sha'uwl" claim contests. `christianity/dead-sea-scrolls/00-overview.md` and `christianity/dead-sea-scrolls/Incoming/dead-sea-scrolls-selected-texts.md` mention the Habakkuk pesher (1QpHab); check what they say before following any of the book's Qumran readings, especially claim 2 above (*hon* vs *yayin*).

**Hagar, Ishmael and the Genesis narrative (ch. 09).** No repo analysis of Galatians 4:21–31. The Jubilees text in `christianity/ethiopian-bible/unique-ethiopian-texts.md` (Jubilees 14, 17) carries the Hagar/Ishmael story in a second-century-BCE retelling and could be set beside Genesis 16–21 (KJV, `christianity/Incoming/kjv/`) if anyone takes up the allegory question.

**Gnosticism (ch. 10, ch. 11).** Direct tension with the repo's framing. `gnosticism/` and `questions/the-gospel-of-thomas.md` treat the Gnostic gospels as Christianity's suppressed mystical wing and a witness to Jesus's teaching; the book treats Gnosticism as the poison in Paul and as Platonic Demiurge dualism. Core Thesis #3 (kingdom within, shared divinity, participatory salvation) is closer to the material the book condemns than to the book's own Torah-and-covenant soteriology.

**Hell and the soul (ch. 10, ch. 12).** The book's annihilationism (most souls cease to exist; She'owl is only for those who mislead others) overlaps in outcome with `questions/hell-afterlife.md`'s rejection of eternal conscious torment, though by a different route.

**Hebrew Matthew (ch. 10, ch. 11).** The book's claim that Matthew was composed in Hebrew by an eyewitness touches `christianity/hebrew-gospel-of-matthew.md`; see `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` on the Ebionite Passover saying before linking the two.

**Trust vs faith, *pistis* (ch. 10).** The book's distinction between *pistis* as "trust and reliance" and Paul's "belief" has no repo file; `questions/what-we-actually-offer.md` is the nearest neighbour.

**What the repo should not take from these chapters without checking:** the lexical claims flagged above (*akrobystia*, *Charis*, *Christos*/*Chrestus*, *pneumatikoi*, *loipos*, "Amen," *ou tas tuchousas*), the P46 dating, the "Sha'uwl" reading of Habakkuk 2:5, the Dionysus parallels, the Bacon/KJV claim, the "James" renaming claim, and all biographical statements about Paul's family, sexuality and Timothy. These read as precise and are easy to repeat; several appear to be wrong on inspection. The standing rule in `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` applies: primary text first, and the narrow claim over the absolute one.
