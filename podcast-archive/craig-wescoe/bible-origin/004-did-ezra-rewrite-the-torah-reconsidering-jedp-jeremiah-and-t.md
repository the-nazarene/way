## Summary

The talk asks whether Ezra rewrote the Torah during the Babylonian exile, and what the documentary hypothesis (JEDP) gets wrong about the Torah's formation. The host, who does not name himself in the video, says scholars broadly agree on four sources but often disagree about which material belongs to which. He argues the Priestly (P) material is better understood as Elohist (E) or Yahwist (J), so that three sources (J, E, D) may suffice. He says his method starts from "historical attestations" rather than literary analysis, and he draws on Jeremiah 8:8 and 7:22, Hilkiah's scroll in Kings and Chronicles, Esdras, the Clementine Homilies, and the Didascalia Apostolorum.

## Key Teachings

### Three sources, not four
The host accepts the J, E, D, and P labels as the standard frame but argues that P material is better assigned to E or J. His stated method is to root the classification in "historical attestations" rather than in literary seams. The talk does not name the scholars it critiques or the specific passages reassigned from P.

### Jeremiah 8:8 and 7:22: the "lying pen" and the sacrifice-free Exodus
The host reads Jeremiah 8:8 ("The lying pen of the scribes has produced a deception") as a charge against the "Torah of Yahweh," which the people claim as their wisdom. He reads Jeremiah 7:22 ("I did not command you concerning burnt offerings and sacrifices") as evidence that animal sacrifice was not part of the original Torah. He rejects the idea that Jeremiah is calling the whole Pentateuch invented.

### Hilkiah's scroll is the E Torah, not Deuteronomy
The host says Hilkiah's find in Kings and Chronicles is usually identified with Deuteronomy, but Deuteronomy contains sacrifice commands, so it cannot be the sacrifice-free scroll Jeremiah describes. He identifies Hilkiah as Jeremiah's father, so Jeremiah wrote with knowledge of that scroll. He also cites Exodus's Passover lamb as the first sacrifice command, which he assigns to J.

### Ezra rewrote the Torah: triangulation
The host cites Esdras (later repeated by "early church fathers") that Ezra rewrote the Torah during the Babylonian exile after the original was burned. He says both "champions" and "opponents" of Ezra agree on that event, and he calls this agreement "triangulation." His argument is that Ezra wove the E and J sources together and composed Deuteronomy as a "second law" to match the tradition of two Torahs. The host says he cannot say Ezra's exact motive and calls that inference speculative.

### Elohim vs. Yahweh and anachronisms
Genesis 1 uses Elohim throughout and has a diet of plants for humans and animals. Genesis 2 uses Yahweh and has a different creation sequence. The host calls Genesis 1 E and Genesis 2 J, and he treats the two-creation difference as evidence of separate sources rather than something to harmonize. He argues the J source reflects the Levitical priesthood and cites anachronisms: Noah's clean and unclean animals (taken as a Levitical concept), Noah's altar, and nations (Hittites, Girgashites, Hivites) that he says could not have been known in Noah's time. He claims the name Yahweh first appears in an Egyptian inscription about 3,000 years ago and Elohim in Canaan about 5,000 years ago. The talk gives no sources for these dates.

### Jesus in the temple and the early witnesses
The host reads Jesus' temple action as quoting Jeremiah 7:11 ("a den of perets," which he says means "blood shedders," not "thieves"). He identifies the targets as the Sadducees and the sacrificial system. He cites the Clementine Homilies as showing that "false" material is J material, and attributes to Peter in those texts the teaching that falsifications entered the original Torah. He cites the Didascalia Apostolorum for a "first legislation" and "second legislation," tying the second to the golden calf episode, and for a reading of Passover as a corrupted form of the Feast of Unleavened Bread tied to the barley and wheat harvests.

- **Hilkiah's scroll was Deuteronomy.** The host says scholars often assume this, but Deuteronomy contains sacrifice commands, so the scroll Jeremiah describes must have been different (the E Torah).

- **Genesis 1 and Genesis 2 should be harmonized into one story.** The host rejects harmonization and reads the two accounts as separate sources.
- **Jeremiah 8:8 calls the entire Torah a lie.** The host says Jeremiah is attacking the "Torah of Yahweh," the sacrifice-centered version, not the five books of Moses as such.
