## Summary

The host asks whether the fish fry on the beach in John 21 (verses 9-10) describes a real event. He argues it does not. His case rests on the silence of early writers and on internal comparisons with Mark, Luke, and the rest of John. He says no first-century writer (he lists Paul, James, Peter, Jude, and Clement) mentions John 21. Justin Martyr (mid-second century) quotes 18 of John's first 20 chapters but nothing from 21. Irenaeus (writing in the 180s) quotes all of John's first 20 chapters but shows no knowledge of chapter 21. Tertullian (early third century, *Against Praxeas* 25) calls John 20:31 the "termination" of the Gospel, which the host takes as evidence that the Gospel originally ended there. He says Tertullian is also one of the earliest witnesses to chapter 21, though he does not identify which passages from chapter 21 Tertullian uses.

For the internal argument, the host says John's recruitment of the disciples (John 1:35-43) places them as disciples of John the Baptist at "Bethany beyond the Jordan," not as Galilean fishermen. He contrasts this with Mark 1:16-20, where the sons of Zebedee and the fishermen are recruited by the Sea of Galilee. He says the miraculous catch of fish came into Luke from Mark-related material, and that John 21 moves it to a post-resurrection setting. He reads the threefold "do you love me?" exchange (John 21:15-17) as a reversal of Peter's three denials (Mark 14:66-72), meant to restore Peter's authority. He also reads the rumor that the beloved disciple would not die (John 21:20-23) as a response to Mark 9:1. On his reading, chapter 21 was added at the end of the second century to resolve these conflicts. He ends by calling the fish-fry reading "a resounding no" and by saying that the historical evidence leaves the claim that Jesus ate fish on the beach without support. He also draws a link to the kingdom "on earth as it is in heaven" (Matthew 6:10), which he says chapter 21 opposes. This is part of an argument for dietary veganism, which the host states at the start.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The silence of the early and second-century witnesses
The host's central evidence is absence. He lists first-century writers who do not mention John 21 (Paul, James, Peter, Jude, Clement). He says that silence is "not surprising" for the first century, since many things are not covered there. He calls the silence of second-century writers "a big surprise." Justin Martyr (*Dialogue with Trypho*, *First Apology*, *Second Apology*) quotes 18 of John's first 20 chapters, skipping 13 and 17, and never touches 21. The host says that by chance alone Justin's silence on 21 would be roughly "90 or I guess 10%" likely, and he concedes that this "wouldn't be enough evidence on its own." Irenaeus, in *Against Heresies* (180s), quotes "multiple parts of each chapter" of John 1-20 and says nothing about chapter 21. Note: in his final recap the host says Justin "quotes from nearly every chapter," which is looser than the 18-of-20 figure he gave earlier.

### 2. Tertullian and the "termination" of the Gospel
Tertullian (*Against Praxeas* 25) introduces the quotation "that ye might believe that Jesus Christ is the son of God" as the Gospel's ending. The host identifies this as John 20:31 and not the final verses of the Gospel as printed in modern Bibles (John 21:24-25). He reads 20:31 as a natural conclusion, with its "signs" language and its purpose statement ("that by believing you may have life in his name"). He compares it with John 21:25, "There are many more things that Jesus did," and calls the latter "much less elegant" and "tacked on." He also says Tertullian "quotes a couple of parts from chapter 21," so Tertullian is also the earliest witness to chapter 21. The host does not name the passages from 21 that Tertullian uses, so the claim cannot be checked from the talk. He presents the two positions (Tertullian knowing both versions) as consistent, though they pull in different directions.

### 3. Scholarly consensus as the host reports it
The host cites Helmut Koester (transcribed as "Helmet Coaster") as saying that John 20:30-31 is the original ending and that chapter 21 is "widely recognized as a later addition," even though every extant manuscript contains it. He then says scholars "pretty much unanimously" agree that chapter 21 was added later, and says this is "one of the least contended points among Johannine scholars." He adds that the historical evidence points to "the very end of the second century." The talk offers no other scholar to support the date, and the host does not give his reasons for it beyond the points listed here.

### 4. Three purposes for the appendix: Mark-John discrepancies, Peter's authority, and the rumor of delayed return
The host argues the appendix had three purposes. The first was to correct the Mark-John discrepancies. He says John 1:28 places the recruitment at "Bethany beyond the Jordan," which he estimates at about 125 miles (roughly a week's walk) from the Sea of Galilee. In John 1:35-43, the disciples are followers of John the Baptist and are not described as fishermen or as the sons of Zebedee. John 21:1-2 then introduces Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, "the sons of Zebedee," and "two other disciples" as fishermen, which he says is Mark's picture (Mark 1:16-20) imported into John. He also argues that Luke's miraculous catch (Luke 5, which he cites from Luke 1's prologue) is borrowed from Mark-related material, and that John 21:4-6 moves it to a post-resurrection setting.

### 5. Peter's keys, the three denials, and the three affirmations
The second purpose was to answer the question of whether Peter kept his authority after his denials. The host says Mark's last image of Peter is the threefold denial (Mark 14:66-72) and that Peter disappears from Mark after that. He says that in the second century, Matthew 16:18-19 ("on this rock I will build my church," "the keys of the kingdom") was used against James's leadership in Acts 15. He reports that some people "surmised" that Peter's denials had cost him his position. Against this, the host reads John 21:15-17 as Jesus asking Peter three times whether he loves him, with Peter answering affirmatively each time and receiving the commands "Feed my lambs," "Shepherd my sheep," and "feed my sheep." He treats this as cancelling the three denials and confirming Peter's leadership. He then reads John 21:18-19 as a prophecy of Peter's martyrdom and cites the Acts of Peter (37-38, which he dates to the late second century) for his crucifixion head-downward. He calls this the "Markan Peter," a bumbling disciple, and sets him against the "eloquent, sophisticated" Peter of the Pseudo-Clementine Homilies, who in his account taught veganism.

### 6. The rumor about the beloved disciple and Mark 9:1
The third purpose was to answer the claim that Jesus's prophecy of return had failed. The host reads John 21:20-23, where a rumor "spread among the brothers that this disciple would not die," and where Jesus says "if I want him to remain until I return, what is that to you?" He argues that the rumor only makes sense after all of Jesus's original followers are dead, which pushes the redaction into the second century. He identifies the source of the rumor as Mark 9:1 ("there are some standing here who will not taste death before they see the kingdom of God arrive with power"). He says the appendix recasts that saying as conditional ("if it's my will") to explain why the prophecy seemed to fail. He also says that "the rumor started by Mark" and that followers who believed Mark began to lose hope once no contemporary of Jesus was left alive. He adds that the author of chapter 21 claims to be the beloved disciple (21:24), which he treats as a way of claiming the whole Gospel.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The fish fry on the beach is a historical event in John 21:9-13.** The host argues it is a late second-century invention added to the Gospel, with no early witnesses.
- **The Gospel of John ends at 21:25.** He says Tertullian's "termination" is 20:31, and that chapter 21 is an appendix.
- **Peter lost his authority when he denied Jesus.** He says John 21:15-17 reverses the three denials and reconfirms Peter as shepherd of the flock.
- **Jesus promised that some of his followers would live until his return.** He argues that John 21:22-23 corrects a rumor based on Mark 9:1, and that the saying is conditional.
- **The disciples were Galilean fishermen recruited by the Sea of Galilee.** He says John 1 places their recruitment at Bethany beyond the Jordan, and that the fishermen story comes from Mark.
