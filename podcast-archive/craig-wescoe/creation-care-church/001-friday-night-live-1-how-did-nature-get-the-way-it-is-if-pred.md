## Summary

Craig Wescoe asks how animals came to prey on one another if predation was not part of God's design. He starts from Genesis 1:29-30, which he reads as giving humans and animals only plants for food, and from Genesis 1:31, which he reads as calling that original world "very good." He argues that predation entered through human failure. The talk names two mechanisms: humans neglected their role as caretakers and destroyed the habitats God made for herbivores, and humans corrupted animals by selective breeding for profit, using Jacob and Laban's flocks in Genesis 30:41-43 as the model. The Genesis 6 flood narrative is read as showing that human violence and wickedness spread through the world.

Wescoe's answer rests on scripture, not on zoology or history. He offers no ecological, paleontological, or textual-critical evidence. He draws on Matthew 6:10 and 5:9, Romans 8:19-22, Proverbs 12:10, Revelation 21:4, 2 Chronicles 7:14, Isaiah 11:6-9, and 1 John 4:16 to argue that the remedy is repentance, humility, and love toward animals, and that God will restore the earth to its original peaceful state.

The talk ends with a Q&A in which Wescoe answers questions from viewers in the chat. He says that Satan deceived the first humans in Genesis 3, that the wild animals have been "ignored" rather than deliberately corrupted, that the tree of knowledge was a test of trust, and that a righteous person "regards the life of his animal." He defers the question of animals in heaven to a later talk. He also gives a brief, uncertain account of Proverbs 12:10 and notes that translators render it in several ways.

## Key Teachings

### Predation was not God's original design
Wescoe reads Genesis 1:29-30 as God assigning "every green herb for food" to both humans and "every beast of the earth." He concludes that "all of the creatures that god created in this perfect world... was fruit and vegetation," and that death and killing entered only after sin. He reads Genesis 1:31 ("very good") as endorsing this plant-based world.

### Human stewardship failed, and habitats were destroyed
Using Genesis 1:28 and Genesis 2:15, he argues that humans were given dominion as caretakers, "to implement god's will," not license to do as they pleased. He says humans "destroyed these natural habitats" and that animals then "go into survival mode." This is the first of his two causes.

### Breeding corrupted animal nature
The second cause is selective breeding. He cites Genesis 30:41-43, where Jacob breeds the stronger livestock and the flock grows "exceedingly prosperous," and draws a parallel to modern factory farming, the breeding of chickens for laying, and the breeding of cats for pest control. He calls this breeding "for profit" and says it serves "our own misguided interests."

### Violence from Genesis 3 and Genesis 6
Wescoe ties the fall to Genesis 3:1-5, where the serpent casts doubt on God's word. He identifies the serpent as Satan by pointing to Revelation, though he does not give a verse there. He then reads Genesis 6:5, 6:11, and 6:13 as showing that the pre-Flood world was "filled with violence," and presents this as the outcome of human rejection of God's design.

### Restoration through repentance, humility, and peacemaking
He reads Matthew 6:10 ("your kingdom come... on earth as it is in heaven") as a prayer for the restoration of the peaceable world. He reads Matthew 5:9 ("peacemakers... children of God") and Romans 8:19-22 as saying creation is waiting for humans to become God's children. He presents 2 Chronicles 7:14 as a promise of healing "their land" if people humble themselves, and Revelation 21:4 as the promise of a world with "no more death." He concludes that the remedy is a change in attitude, not only in outward action.

### Love as the nature of God and the mark of a disciple
From 1 John 4:16 and Galatians 5:22 he argues that God's nature is love and that disciples should be known by love, including love toward animals. He reads Isaiah 11:6-9, with the wolf, lamb, and "little child" leading the animals, as God's ideal kingdom, and Matthew 18:3 as a call to become like children.

- **Ignoring animals is morally neutral.** In the Q&A he says that "ignoring animals that's better than like causing evil to them but it's still falling far short" of God's ideal.
- **Waiting passively for Jesus's return is enough.** He answers a viewer's question by saying believers should be "active" stewards now, citing the parable of the faithful steward (no verse given).
