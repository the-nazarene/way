## Summary

Wescoe, hosting a live "Friday Night Live" stream for Creation Care Church, asks whether God will bring back the temple practice of animal sacrifice. The question comes from prophetic passages that seem to describe a future temple, chiefly Ezekiel 43:19 and Isaiah 56:7, and from premillennial readings of the millennial kingdom. He treats the question as one of scriptural reasoning: he reads passages, checks them against each other, and offers his conclusion that sacrifice will not return.

His main evidence is an argument from the finality of Christ's sacrifice, drawn mostly from Hebrews (7:23-24, 10:4, 10:10, 10:14). Wescoe argues that Jesus is a high priest "forever" in the order of Melchizedek, so no Levitical successor is needed; that his single offering "perfected forever" those being sanctified; and that repeating it would show "lack of faith." He adds that the New Testament replaces the temple with the body of believers (1 Corinthians 3:16, Revelation 21:22), that Jesus quotes Isaiah 56:7 while clearing the temple (Mark 11:17), and that Hosea 6:6 and Isaiah 65 and 66 show God never wanted sacrifice and envision a peaceful kingdom. He also suggests that Ezekiel may refer to the second temple period, an interpretation he presents as a point "controversial" among scholars rather than settled.

The talk ends with a long Q&A. Wescoe answers objections about the millennial "memorial" view, Isaiah 66:3, the Temple Institute and red heifers, and Jacob sheep breeding, and he directs listeners to creation care and "the sacrifices that we offer" (praise and doing good). He closes with prayer and invitations to the prayer group and the website.

## Key Teachings

### Ezekiel 43:19 may refer to the past, not the future
Wescoe argues that the first assumption of anyone using Ezekiel 43:19 as evidence for returning sacrifice is that the verse is future. He notes that many scholars date Ezekiel before the second temple, so the passage "could have been referring to" the second temple period and "would have been fulfilled thousands of years ago." He presents this as a controversial point, not a certainty.

### The Levitical priesthood is replaced by Christ's Melchizedek priesthood
He contrasts Ezekiel's reference to "the priests, the Levites, who are of the seed of Zadok" with Jesus, who came from Judah and belonged to "the priestly order of Melchizedek." Citing Hebrews 7:23-24, he says Levitical priests died and had to be succeeded, while Jesus "continues forever, has an unchangeable priesthood." A future Levitical priesthood would therefore "replace Jesus," which he says is incompatible with Hebrews. In Q&A he adds that Abraham tithed to Melchizedek and that the Levites were "in Abraham's loins," which he uses to show the Melchizedek order is "higher."

### Christ's one offering makes further sacrifice unnecessary
Relying on Hebrews 10:4, 10:10, 10:14 and 10:18, Wescoe argues that "the blood of bulls and goats" cannot take away sins, that Christ was both priest and offering, and that because the offering was "once and for all," "it never needed to be fulfilled again." He points to Hebrews 10:18 in Q&A ("where there is remission of these, there's no longer an offering for sin") as the strongest reason sacrifice will not return. He concludes that reviving sacrifice "would only be demonstrating lack of faith."

### The temple is now the people of God, not a building
He argues from Revelation 21:22 ("I saw no temple in it, for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple") and 1 Corinthians 3:16 ("Do you not know that you are the temple of God") that the temple has been transformed into believers, "living stones" and "the body of Christ." Rebuilding a physical temple would mean "moving backwards instead of forward." He also says the Melchizedek/temple themes culminate in "the body of believers" and that Christians advocating a physical rebuilding "need to read some of those verses in Hebrews and Corinthians."

### Hebrews 13 and Hosea 6:6 recast sacrifice as praise, mercy and good works
Hebrews 13:15-16 ("the sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of our lips" and "do not forget to do good and to share") is his example of the sacrifices now offered. He cites Hosea 6:6 ("For I desire mercy, not sacrifice") and says Jesus quotes this verse "a couple of times," so the early church and Jesus saw animal sacrifice as not what God most wanted. He ties this to the "fruit of the spirit" (peace, joy, love, gentleness).

### Kingdom peace rules out bloodshed; the millennium does not restore sacrifice
Against the premillennial "memorial" reading (quoted from a viewer), Wescoe argues that Jesus is the memorial the Lord's Supper points to, and that "do this in remembrance of me" gives no warrant for blood sacrifice as memorial. On Isaiah 65:25 and 11:6-9 (no hurting "in all my holy mountain"), he argues that a kingdom of peaceful animals cannot include slaughter. On Isaiah 66:3 ("He who kills a bull is as if he slays a man") he argues that the kingdom is "a place of peace and joy and love," not "a bloodbath." He draws on John 18:36 (paraphrased, "my kingdom is not of this world"; he does not give the verse). He says the millennial kingdom is probably physical but that the animal and temple logic still fails there.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Ezekiel and Isaiah prophesy a future return of temple animal sacrifice.** Wescoe argues Ezekiel may describe the second temple period, and that Isaiah's kingdom passages (65:25, 66:3) and Mark 11:17 rule out sacrifice in the kingdom.
- **Premillennial scholars (per a viewer's quotation) say animal sacrifice will be a memorial in the millennium.** Wescoe answers that Christ himself gave the memorial (the Lord's Supper), so blood sacrifice would not be a memorial "in remembrance" of him.
- **Jesus was from the Levitical priesthood.** He says Jesus came from Judah and belongs to the Melchizedek order, which he presents as higher and older.
- **The temple will be rebuilt as a physical structure.** He argues the temple has become the body of believers and that a rebuilt structure would be regression.
- **Practices such as Jacob sheep breeding or red heifer work prepare a true return of sacrifice.** Wescoe says such efforts are "misplaced" and responds that the best tool is correcting theology with scripture.
