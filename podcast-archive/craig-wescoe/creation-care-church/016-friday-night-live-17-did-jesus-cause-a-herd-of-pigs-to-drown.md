## Summary

Craig asks whether Jesus caused a herd of pigs to drown in the Gerasene (Gadarene) exorcism, which appears in Matthew 8, Mark 5 and Luke 8. He frames the question as a pastoral one: some readers take the story to show that Jesus did not care about animals, and so that people may eat, exploit and disregard them. He reads the three accounts side by side and notes differences, such as Matthew's two men against one man in Mark and Luke. His central argument is that the demons proposed entering the pigs and carried it out themselves. Jesus only ordered them out of the man. He argues that the key word "permitted" in Luke 8:32 (the Greek *epitrepo*, which the transcript renders "epitrepsin") does not signal approval. He supports this by comparing its use in Matthew 8:21-22 and Matthew 19:8, where he reads it as "not standing in the way" of something the speaker does not really want. Craig also draws a theme of demonic violence from the text and applies it to how believers treat animals.

The talk then moves into a 30-minute question-and-answer session. Craig treats Genesis 9:3 as a possible parallel to the "permission" problem, and he says God very likely does not want humans eating animals, though he admits the question is not "super clear." He also answers questions on Jesus's "agency" and the demons' "appointed time," on salvation and purgatory, and on whether demons are fallen-angel offspring. He ends by announcing that the next session will address why God accepted Abel's offering and not Cain's, and then closes with a prayer. The talk is almost entirely Craig's exposition; the audience questions (Philip, Tim, Ross and others) are mostly brief prompts.

## Key Teachings

### The demons, not Jesus, proposed the move into the pigs
Craig's reading is that the text settles who had the idea. In Matthew 8:31 the demons "begged him" to let them enter the herd, and Jesus's reply in Matthew 8:32 is only "Go." He says people who blame Jesus usually assume the idea was his, and "clearly in the text, that's not the case." The demons devised the plan and carried it out "of their own accord after Jesus cast them out of the man."

### "Permit" (epitrepo) as non-endorsement
Craig's main argument is lexical. He concedes that Luke 8:32 says Jesus "permitted them," and he rejects the reading of that word as a "seal of approval." He compares the word in Matthew 8:21-22 (the man who wants to bury his father first; Jesus says "let the dead bury their own dead") and in Matthew 19:8 (Moses "permitted" divorce "because of the hardness of your hearts"). From these he argues the word implies a request not to stand in the way of something the one granting permission does not want. In his words, the demons were saying, "Hey, Jesus, we beg you, don't stand in our way, even though we know this isn't what you want." He calls "permission" "kind of a mistranslation" here. Craig presents this as his reading of usage in these passages, not as a settled lexical consensus, and he does not cite a lexicon.

### Demons are characterized by violence
Craig highlights the word "violent" or "exceedingly fierce" (Matthew 8:28, which he reads in the Berean Study Bible as "so violent that no one could pass that way") and the violence of the pigs' stampede. He argues that "we see that throughout scripture, demons are associated with violence." He then applies this as a test: if our habits, including how we treat animals or pay others to treat them violently, are ours to question, "whether that's God who's motivating us" or something else.

### Confessing Jesus as Son of God is not enough
The demons call Jesus "Son of God" (Matthew 8:29) and "Son of the Most High God" (Mark 5:7). Craig uses this to challenge the view that believing Jesus is the Son of God is sufficient for salvation. If that were all, believers would be "no better off than the demons." He cites John 14:15 ("If you love me, keep my commandments") and Luke 6:36 (be merciful) to argue that faith must bear works of love and mercy.

### Genesis 9 and whether God "permitted" the eating of animals (Q&A)
Responding to a question about whether the Genesis 9:3 grant of animals as food is the same kind of permission, Craig says "maybe." He argues that Genesis 1:29 gives the plant diet as the original arrangement, that Genesis 8:21 attributes the change to "the imagination of man's heart," and that Genesis 9:2 gives animals "into your hand" as a power to be used under God's will or one's own imagination. He concludes: "it seems pretty clear to me that God doesn't want us to be eating the animals." He also notes that Genesis 9 makes animals "fellow recipients of the covenant promise." He concedes that the Genesis passage is in Hebrew, so the Greek word would not be the same, but says the concept might be similar.

### Jesus's agency and the demons' "appointed time" (Q&A)
Asked whether Jesus "had no agency" over the demons, Craig says Jesus had the power but not the license to act against God's timing. He draws on the demons' statement "Have you come here to torment us before the time?" (Matthew 8:29) and on John's record of Jesus saying he "can do nothing unless my father first gives it to me." He reads the abyss as "some kind of prison" until judgment, though he says the text is "not super clear." He acknowledges that the exact timing is unknown.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Jesus caused the pigs to drown.** Craig: the demons asked to enter the pigs, did so on their own initiative, and drowned them; Jesus only cast them out.
- **Jesus endorsed the move by "permitting" it.** Craig: the word means not standing in the way of something Jesus did not want, so it is not an endorsement.
- **Jesus doesn't care about animals, so we may exploit them.** Craig: the text does not support this; he points to Psalm 145:9, Proverbs 12:10 and the Isaiah 11 kingdom vision.
- **Believing Jesus is the Son of God is enough for salvation.** Craig: the demons believe this, so belief must be joined to obedience and mercy (John 14:15; Luke 6:36).
