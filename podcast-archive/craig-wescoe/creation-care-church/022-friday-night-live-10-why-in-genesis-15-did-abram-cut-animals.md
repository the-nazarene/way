## Summary

The host asks why, in Genesis 15, Abram cut animals in half. He argues that this is often cited as proof that God commanded animal sacrifice and does not care about animals, and he says that reading is wrong. His answer is that the cutting was Abram's idea, a human custom he needed because he lacked faith. He builds the case by walking through the text verse by verse. God promises Abram a son (15:4), and Abram believes and is "accounted" righteous (15:6). When God promises the land (15:7), Abram asks, "how shall I know that I will inherit it" (15:8), and God tells him to bring the animals (15:9-10). The host cites a commentary attributed to "Ephraim" (quoted in Keil and Delitzsch) describing the cutting as a Chaldean covenant custom that God "condescended" to follow. He reads Abram's failures in Genesis 16-18 (Hagar, Ishmael, Sarah's laughter) as further signs of incomplete faith, and reads Genesis 22 as the point where Abram's faith matured, with Hebrews 11:17-19 used to explain it.

The talk ends with the host's answer to the one question he finds hardest, why the birds were not cut in half. He says he does not know, and that the main lesson does not depend on it. The rest of the hour is a live Q&A, where the host discusses the Ram in Genesis 22:13, the meaning of "fear God" (drawing on 1 John 4:16-18), and Isaiah 11:6-9 and Matthew 6:10 as pointers to a kingdom without animal bloodshed. He closes by setting next week's topic: Deuteronomy 12:20 and Numbers 11:18, and why God says to eat meat.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The cutting was Abram's custom, not God's command
The host says God only said "bring me" the animals (Genesis 15:9). Abram chose to cut them, and God "obliges" the custom by passing through the pieces as a smoking oven and burning torch (15:17). The host draws on a commentary attributed to "Ephraim" (quoted in Keil and Delitzsch, *Pentateuch*, p. 214), which calls the cutting a custom of the Chaldeans. He reads it as a sign that a covenant-maker stakes his life on the promise.

### 2. Two promises, two levels of faith
The host separates the promise of a son (believed and "credited as righteousness," 15:6) from the promise of the land (doubted, 15:8). He argues that Abram's request for "additional proof" shows a lack of faith, and that this is why the animals were needed.

### 3. Abram's faith grows through the Genesis narrative
The host reads Genesis 16:2 and 16:15 (Sarah's plan, Hagar, Ishmael) and Genesis 17:17-19 (Abraham and Sarah laughing; God names Isaac as the son of the promise) as failures to trust God's way. He says the test in Genesis 22 is where Abram finally trusts God fully, and he uses Hebrews 11:17-19 to say Abraham reasoned God could raise Isaac from the dead.

### 4. The ram is not the provision; Jesus is the lamb
Asked whether God provided the ram in Genesis 22:13, the host says the text does not say so. He says Abram took the ram himself, and that the "lamb" God would provide (22:8) points to Jesus, "the only begotten son" (John 3:16). He states that the ram "was just abraham" acting on his own, and that "we should apply that knowledge" which Abraham did not have.

### 5. Two takeaways for believers
The host gives two lessons: trust God's promises without demanding extra proof, and do not "take matters into our own hands" or rely on human customs when God's way seems unbelievable. He ties these to 2 Corinthians 5:7 ("we walk by faith not by sight") and Romans 4:23-24, which he says credits righteousness to all who believe in the God who raised Jesus.

### 6. Q&A: the animal-sacrifice origin and the kingdom
Responding to a question on Jeremiah 34:18, the host says God "meets us where we are," and that animal sacrifice arose from worship of other gods. He cites Isaiah 11:6-9 and the Lord's Prayer ("your kingdom come") as pointing to a future without bloodshed, and he calls for "the path of love and path of peace."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That God commanded animal sacrifice in Genesis 15 and so does not care about animals.** The host says God only said to bring the animals, and that the cutting was Abram's custom, which God accommodated because of Abram's lack of faith.
- **That God provided the ram as a substitute for Isaac.** The host says the text does not say this. Abram took the ram himself, and "the lamb" God provides is Jesus.
- **That the ram in Genesis 22 is the fulfillment of the lamb prophecy.** The host says this "completely miss[es] the whole point" and that the lamb is Jesus.
