## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of Creation Care Church's "Friday Night Live" livestream, asks whether God created animals to be eaten as food. He opens with Genesis 1:29-30, where God gives humans and animals the green plants and seed-bearing fruit for food, and argues that no diet of flesh is assigned to either. From that starting point he argues that God's purpose for animals was broader: to be fruitful and multiply, to live peaceably, to be known by name, and to praise God. He supports each purpose with passages from Genesis, Isaiah, Psalms, Revelation and the Pauline letters, and he reads the future kingdom in Isaiah 11 as a window onto God's original design.

Wescoe's method is to read the Bible as a single, internally consistent authority. He treats the creation account and the eschatological passages as describing the same ideal, and he treats the predation of carnivores as a result of the fall and of human corruption of animals and their environment. He says this explanation rests on scripture, not on scientific reconstruction, and he says the question of why carnivores exist cannot be settled by guesswork about the distant past. Much of the second half is live Q&A in which he answers chat questions about naming animals, the gospel's scope for creation, the Book of Jubilees, and the spiritual significance of diet.

The talk ends with a closing prayer and a preview of the next live talk, "Is veganism a form of idolatry?" It is a devotional talk aimed at a church audience, and it does not engage critical scholarship on the texts. Its arguments depend on Wescoe's reading of the text and on the authority he assigns to it.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The original diet of Genesis 1:29-30
Wescoe reads Genesis 1:29-30 as God giving "every herb that yields seed" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed" to humans, and "every green herb for food" to the beasts, birds and creeping things. Because God names no flesh diet for either group, he concludes that animals were not created to be eaten by animals or humans. He treats this as the baseline against which the other purposes are read.

### 2. Fruitfulness, dominion and stewardship (Genesis 1:22)
Genesis 1:22 has God bless the animals and tell them to "be fruitful and multiply." Wescoe says the same command is given to humans, but humans alone receive dominion. He reads dominion as stewardship, with humans as "managers" and "preservers" who must provide each creature with food that suits it, not as license to do as they please.

### 3. Peaceable inhabitation and the kingdom as a window on design (Isaiah 11:6-7, Romans 12:18, Proverbs 3:31)
Wescoe's method is to read the future kingdom as showing God's original design: "before sin entered the world... everything's very good," and the kingdom restores that. From Isaiah 11:6-7 he takes the picture of wolf with lamb, leopard with kid, and a little child leading them, and concludes that God intended animals to "peacefully inhabit the earth." He ties this to Romans 12:18 ("live peaceably with all") and Proverbs 3:31 ("do not envy the oppressor") and argues that killing animals for food, clothing or entertainment is choosing violence. He also applies Isaiah 55:9 to argue that human preferences (steak, fur, zoos) should not override God's purposes.

### 4. Naming and personal relationship (Genesis 2:20)
Wescoe argues that Adam's naming of the animals establishes a relationship in which an animal becomes "someone" rather than "something." He cites the practice of farmers not naming livestock and the tagging of animals with their slaughter dates as evidence of how naming is used to make killing emotionally easier. He answers a chat question by arguing that the naming was individual rather than merely taxonomic, since there was no death yet and the relationship was meant to last indefinitely. He also tells an analogy about the "Judas goat" that leads sheep into a slaughterhouse, comparing this to betrayal of trust.

### 5. Praise and belonging to God (Psalm 150:6, Revelation 5:13, Colossians 1:16, Genesis 9)
Psalm 150:6 ("let everything that has breath praise the lord") is read as including animals, since they have the "breath of life." Revelation 5:13 shows every creature praising God, which Wescoe takes as fulfilling Joel 2:28. Colossians 1:16 ("all things were created through him and for him") is used to argue that animals were created for God, not for humans, so humans are stewards on God's behalf. Wescoe also argues that God's covenant with Noah includes animals, and that the gospel is therefore good news for all creation, citing a Galatians passage about creation "groaning" (he was unsure of the location).

### 6. Predation as a product of the fall and human corruption (Isaiah 11:7, 2 Chronicles 7:14)
Responding to a question about carnivores, Wescoe argues that predation is a "symptom of the fallen nature" and that humans have corrupted both animals and their habitats. He points to breeding for milk and meat yields and to training animals as hunters. He reads Isaiah 11:7 ("the lion shall eat straw like the ox") as the restored design, and cites 2 Chronicles 7:14 as a promise of healing if people repent. He says the scientific picture of prehistory is unreliable guesswork and that "only the word of god can tell us definitively what's what's true." He tells the questioner that predation is not the design of God. Wescoe describes the appendix argument as speculation and does not engage the evidence for it.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Animals were created as food for humans.** Wescoe argues that Genesis 1:29-30 assigns plants to both humans and animals, and that Colossians 1:16 says animals were created for God.
- **Predators show that carnivory is part of God's design.** Wescoe argues that predation is a symptom of the fall and of human corruption of animals and habitats, citing Isaiah 11:7.
- **God's covenant and the gospel are only for humans.** He argues that the Noah covenant includes animals, that Revelation 5:13 shows animals praising God, and that the gospel is good news for "all creation."
- **Adam's naming was merely taxonomic.** He argues the naming was individual, based on the relationship intended before sin and death.
- **Concern for animals distracts from human suffering.** He answers that one can care about more than one issue, and that at minimum one should not perpetuate harm to animals.
- **Scientific reconstructions of prehistory can settle the question.** He argues these are unreliable guesswork and that scripture alone is definitive.
