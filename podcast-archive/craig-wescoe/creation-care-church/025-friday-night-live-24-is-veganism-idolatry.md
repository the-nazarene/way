## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a live "Friday Night Live" session for the Creation Care Church channel, asks whether veganism can be called idolatry. He says the accusation has been made from within churches, against people who promote veganism. His method is to define idolatry from scripture rather than from dictionaries or sermons. He sets out a literal sense (carving and bowing to a statue, using Isaiah 2:8, Psalm 135:15-18 and Daniel 3) and a figurative sense (anything "formed by the art and imagination of man" and set against God, using Acts 17:29). He then defines veganism as "abstaining from activities that cause needless harm and suffering to animals" and argues that this principle is consistent with God's instructions (Genesis 1:29, Luke 6:36, 1 Corinthians 13:4, Galatians 5:14, 1 John 4:16). His conclusion is that veganism is not idolatry in either sense.

Craig then reverses the charge. He argues that rejecting veganism, for reasons of appetite, tradition or industry-driven advertising, fits the biblical definition of idolatry better than being vegan does, citing Philippians 3:19, Mark 7:9, Proverbs 3:31 and Psalm 145:8-9. The second half is a question-and-answer session with viewers' questions read aloud. Topics include whether all statues are idols, whether anthropocentrism is idolatry, Romans 1:25, Genesis 9 and animal sacrifice. Craig points viewers to other archived talks in a planned series of about 40, and to the writings of Andrew Lindsay. He ends by announcing the next week's topic and closing with prayer.

The case rests almost entirely on Craig's reading of individual verses. He cites no historical sources, dates or manuscript evidence, and he presents his interpretations as the plain meaning of scripture.

## Key Teachings

### Idolatry in the literal sense
Craig takes idolatry in its "strict sense" to be making a physical image (stone, metal or wood) and bowing down to it as a god. He cites Isaiah 2:8 and Psalm 135:15-18, where the idols have mouths, eyes and ears but cannot speak, see or hear, and the closing line "those who make them become like them" becomes his warning that idol-makers grow blind and deaf to God. He presents Daniel 3:17-18 (read from the Berean Study Bible) as the model of refusal to bow down to Nebuchadnezzar's golden statue, even at risk of death.

### Idolatry in the figurative sense
Craig takes Acts 17:29 ("an image formed by the art and imagination of man," ESV) as the basis for a broader definition. On his reading, idolatry in this sense is anything human-devised that stands against God's instructions, and he gives the example of a philosophy denying that God created the universe. He adds that idolatry is "more intentional" than falling short. He says that someone acting from ignorance may be "wrong and still not what God wants" but is not necessarily idolatrous. Relying on Genesis 3, he defines the core of idolatry as "trusting in something other than God" and treats Adam and Eve's choice to trust the serpent as the original case.

### Veganism is consistent with God's instructions
Craig defines veganism as kindness to animals and the avoidance of needless harm. He argues that it cannot be idolatry because it is not contrary to God. He cites Genesis 1:29 (plant-based food as given and "very good"), Luke 6:36 (be merciful as God is merciful), 1 Corinthians 13:4 ("love is patient, love is kind") with 1 John 4:16 ("God is love"), and Galatians 5:14 (love your neighbour as yourself). His reasoning is that these passages make kindness to animals an expression of God's character rather than a rival to it.

### Rejecting veganism as the closer form of idolatry
This is the talk's central move. Craig argues that a person who rejects veganism is "putting their own determination above God's instruction." He draws on:
- **Philippians 3:19** ("their god is their belly"), applied to people who refuse God's diet because of appetite;
- **Mark 7:9**, Jesus's rebuke of the Pharisees for keeping tradition over God's command, applied to people who follow the eating habits of those around them;
- **Proverbs 3:31** ("do not envy the violent"), with the argument that killing animals for food is choosing violence when peace is available;
- **Psalm 145:8-9** (God's compassion on all he has made), applied to the claim that withholding compassion from animals misrepresents God's character.

Craig also gives a dairy-industry example. He describes trusting the advertising of dairy companies over God's word as a form of putting trust in a corporation, and he says that a cultural drinking-milk comparison shows more osteoporosis, although he names no source for this.

### Anthropocentrism as arrogance and idolatry
Responding to a viewer's citation of Andrew Lindsay (that it is idolatry to think God cares only for humans), Craig agrees that a human-centred view can be idolatrous in the figurative sense. He calls it "the epitome of arrogance," contrasts it with the humility of Jesus, and goes further: "that's not just idolatry that's satanism." He answers the claim "God doesn't care about animals" with references to the covenant with animals and to Psalm 145:9.

### Genesis 9 and animal sacrifice (Q&A)
When a viewer asks about meat-eating and animal sacrifice, Craig reads Genesis 9 as "more like a warning" than a permission, arguing that God's words after the flood reflect human evil and that the statement that the animals are "given into your hand" concerns power rather than approval. On sacrifice, he says God instructed it because surrounding peoples were already sacrificing to other gods, and God's first step was to move them toward worshipping him. He points viewers to his archived talks on these subjects rather than settling them within this session.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Idolatry is only physical statues or things of the past.** Craig says that the literal sense is "very much alive in the world today," even in churches, and that the figurative sense is "not just common it's the norm."
- **Veganism is an idol (an accusation from within churches).** Craig argues that veganism is an expression of God's character (kindness, mercy, love), not something set above God, so it cannot be idolatry.
- **Idolatry is any sin of the heart, such as pride, greed or gluttony.** Responding to Kathy's question, Craig says such things are "falling short," and he keeps idolatry for the intentional choice to trust something other than God.
- **Every statue is an idol.** Craig says not every statue qualifies, but makers should avoid creating images that others may worship.
- **God does not care about animals.** Craig answers with the covenant with animals, Psalm 145:9, and God's compassion on all he has made.
- **Genesis 9 gives permission to eat animals.** Craig calls that reading "questionable" and argues that the passage is a warning.
