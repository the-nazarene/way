## Key Teachings

### 1. The gospel as good news of reconciliation of the whole creation
Craig reads 2 Corinthians 5:18-19 as saying the gospel is about "reconciling God the creator and his created world" back to himself. He says that because humans were given dominion, reconciling humanity to God is "that first step" toward reconciling all creation. In his telling, Christ's work restores creation to its original "very good" state rather than only offering individual rescue.

### 2. The Hebrew and Greek "good news" word study
Craig starts with 2 Samuel 18:19, where a runner brings news of victory to a king. He identifies the Hebrew word *basar* as the "gospel" word, and links it to Isaiah 52:7 ("how beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news"). He says Jesus, quoting Isaiah 61:1, uses *euangelion* for the same idea, and that Paul's quotation of Isaiah 52:7 in Romans 10:15 and his "prepare your feet" image in Ephesians 6:15 show the same concept. The argument is that the Old Testament word is a "shadow" of the New Testament gospel.

### 3. Sin, death and the reversal of Eden
Working through Genesis 3 (the serpent, the forbidden tree), Romans 5:12, Matthew 1:21, 2 Timothy 1:10, 1 Corinthians 15:26, and 1 John 3:8, Craig argues that human disobedience, guided by the devil, brought sin and death into a world God had made good. Jesus, by living sinlessly and rising from the grave, "overcame" both. The restoration is a return to the immortality that God originally intended, and Craig reads it as the "kingdom" in which "there is no sin there is no death."

### 4. "Gospel of the kingdom" versus the resurrection-and-eternal-life framing
Craig notes that Paul uses many names for the gospel ("the gospel of Christ," "the gospel of peace," and others), while Jesus in Mark 1:15 calls it "the gospel of the kingdom of God." He says the gospel "is often framed entirely as the resurrection of Jesus and the invitation to eternal life." He calls those "very important parts" but argues that preaching only them misses three connections: the reversal of the original sin, the kingdom as restored original creation, and eternal life as a return to original immortality. He reads John 5:28-29 as describing two resurrections (life and judgment) with the kingdom coming after the day of judgment.

### 5. Creation's waiting and humanity's role as keepers
Romans 8:19 ("the earnest expectation of the creation eagerly waits") is read as meaning that creation, including animals, waits for humans to act as God's children. Craig ties "children of God" to the Beatitude about peacemakers (Matthew 5) and to John 8's "children of the devil" saying. He reads Genesis 2:15 ("to tend and keep it") with *shamar* as "keep, preserve, maintain," and Genesis 1:26-29 as God's grant of dominion and food. Isaiah 11:6-9 is his picture of the restored kingdom, with predator and prey at peace and a child leading them. Psalm 36:6 and Proverbs 12:10 support the claim that God preserves animals and that the righteous care for their needs. He concludes that "creation care was thus our divinely ordained role from the beginning."

### 6. Diet as part of the original instruction (Daniel 1, Genesis 1:29)
In answer to a viewer, Craig says Daniel and his companions refused the king's food and ate vegetables and water, and that they were "healthier" and "ten times wiser" than the others (Daniel 1:15-20). He ties this to Proverbs 23:1-3 and 23:20, and to Genesis 1:29, which he treats as the "very good diet" given before sin entered. Craig reads this as a basis for a plant-based diet, though he presents it as a connection he draws rather than a demand the text states outright. He does not name any source for Daniel's diet beyond the biblical text.

### 7. Dominion as care, not domination; nonviolence and love as the method
Responding to a viewer's question about whether dominion means "tyrannical oppressive violent" rule, Craig says *shamar* means "keep, preserve," and that God's own rule over the church, as "the good shepherd lays down his life for his sheep," is the model. He says humans should treat animals "with love and protecting them preserving them leading them in the way of peace." He ties this to 1 John 4:16-18 (perfect love casts out fear), arguing that animals' fear of humans comes from human violence and is contrary to God's design. He does not explicitly discuss the Genesis 9:3-4 meat permission in the captions beyond citing it, and he treats animal flesh as one area where the "very good" original instruction was not followed.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The gospel is only about individual salvation and resurrection.** Craig says this framing is incomplete; the gospel is the reconciliation of God's whole created world, and Jesus' "gospel of the kingdom of God" includes the restored creation.
- **Animals are not part of God's covenants or promises.** Craig says Genesis 9 mentions "all the animals" five times in the Noah covenant, so animals are recipients of God's covenant; he says people are often surprised by this.
- **Dominion means domination and exploitation.** Craig says *shamar* means "keep, preserve, maintain," and that dominion should look like God's own care for his creatures, not tyranny.
- **The lion and the lamb lie down together.** Craig says Isaiah 11:6-7 has the wolf and lamb, and the lion eats straw like the ox; the "lion and lamb" phrasing is a misremembering.
- **The forbidden fruit was animal flesh.** A viewer (Melissa) says God confirmed this. Craig says he has not seen compelling evidence for it, though he would look at any evidence offered; he says the core sin was trusting the devil over God.
- **Animal flesh was a permitted food from the start.** Craig reads Genesis 1:29 as the original "very good diet" for humans and animals, and treats the Daniel account as supporting a plant-based diet.
