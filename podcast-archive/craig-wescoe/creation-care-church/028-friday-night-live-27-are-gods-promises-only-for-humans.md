## Summary

Wescoe asks whether God's promises and covenants are only for humans. He says people overlook the animal promises because of a human-centered pride that reads Scripture as "it's all about me." His main evidence is Genesis 9, where God says the covenant with Noah is "between me and you and every living creature of all flesh," and he counts the animals being named as covenant recipients five times. He then gathers other texts he reads as keeping animals in view across Scripture: Genesis 9:2 and Habakkuk 2:17 (animals made afraid by human violence), Hosea 2:18 (weapons banished so animals lie down safely), Exodus 20 and 31 (cattle included in the Sabbath rest), Jeremiah 31 (the new covenant written on the heart), and Isaiah 11 and Revelation 21:4 (a restored earth with no killing and no death). He argues that humans should exercise the "peaceful dominion" of Genesis 1, eating plants, and that the new covenant and the "little child" image call believers to humility and love toward animals.

The talk ends with a live Q&A. Wescoe answers questions from the chat, including whether Christians can fulfill Isaiah 11 now, whether Matthew 25:37-40 extends to animals, and how to counter "anthropocentrism." He closes with a prayer and previews next week's talk on whether eating animals is a sin, which he says he will treat by examining every passage describing such eating as sinful.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The Noah covenant includes every living creature
Wescoe reads Genesis 9:9-17 closely. God's covenant is with Noah, but also with "every living creature of all flesh" and with "the birds the cattle and every beast of the earth." He says the animals are named more often than Noah and his descendants, and so the promise is "not just to people but to all of creation." The rainbow is the sign that the covenant remembers animals.

### 2. "Given into your hand" is dominion, not license
Genesis 9:2 says the fear and dread of humans will be on the animals, and they "are given into your hand." Wescoe argues this does not approve of mistreatment. He compares it to Job, where Satan is given power over Job within limits. For him, the Genesis 1 dominion was meant to be "loving caring dominion," and the animals' fear is a fallen condition, not God's will. Habakkuk 2:17 ("the plunder of beasts which made them afraid") is read as the violence that produces that fear. He says God's promise is "temporary" and that the covenant still includes the animals.

### 3. The end-times restoration includes animals
Hosea 2:18 (the bow and sword shattered so the animals "lie down safely") and Revelation 21:4 (no more death, sorrow, or pain) are read as promises that animals will share in the restoration. Isaiah 11:6-9 describes the wolf and lamb, the leopard and goat, and "the little child shall lead them," with the lion eating straw "like the ox." Wescoe ties this to Genesis 1:29-30, where humans and animals eat plants. He argues this is "the very good state God created it to be," and that Isaiah 11 is the fulfilment of the Hosea and Jeremiah promises.

### 4. Sabbath, the Ten Commandments, and the new covenant on the heart
He reads Exodus 20:8-11 as including "your cattle" in the Sabbath rest, and Exodus 31:16-17 as making the Sabbath "a sign" of the covenant. He links the stone tablets (Exodus 31:18) to Jeremiah 31:33-34, where the law is written on the heart and "everyone will know" God. He presents Colossians 2:11 as the "circumcision of the heart" (he says "forest skin," a caption error for foreskin, and explains that circumcision outwardly marked God's people while inwardly marking obedience). He identifies Luke 22:20 ("this is the new covenant in my blood") as Jesus being the fulfilment of Jeremiah's promise.

### 5. Love without exclusion, including animals
Wescoe argues that the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) and the command to love enemies (Matthew 5:43-48) should not be switched off for animals. He reads the Good Samaritan-style answer to "who is my neighbor" (he does not give a reference) as "the one who shows mercy," and Matthew 25:37-40 as applying to any needy creature, human or animal, on the principle that what is done "to one of the least of these" is done to Christ. He presents this as the "perfect" or "complete" love of Matthew 5:48.

### 6. Humility against human-centred pride
He opens with James 4:6, which quotes Proverbs 3:34 ("God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble"), and presents pride as the reason people "overlook" animal promises. Matthew 18:3-4 (become like little children) is read as the call to humility. He also tells the chat that "the spirit of the law" should not replace "the letter of the law," and that believers should not "have one foot in the world."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God's promises are for humans only.** Wescoe says this is the prideful, human-centred reading; he argues that Genesis 9 and other passages name the animals as covenant recipients.
- **The Noah covenant is only with Noah and his descendants.** He says the animals are named as recipients too, and the text "makes it very clear" that they are included.
- **"Man has dominion" means license to exploit animals.** He argues that dominion in Genesis 1 is caring stewardship and that Genesis 1:29 gives plants as food.
- **Isaiah 11's peaceable kingdom applies only in heaven.** He says the restoration is God's will for "the earth" as well, and asks why it should not be practised now. (He describes "carnists," a term he attributes to animal-rights or vegan circles, as those who hold this view.)
