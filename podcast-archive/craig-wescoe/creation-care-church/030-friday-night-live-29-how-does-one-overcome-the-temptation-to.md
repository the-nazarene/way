## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting the live "Friday Night Live" talk for the Creation Care Church, asks how a person can overcome the temptation to consume products of animal cruelty. He frames the matter as temptation to sin. Citing James 4:17, he argues that knowing the good (showing mercy to animals and eating the diet God "established from the beginning") and not doing it is sin, so the spiritual question and the dietary question are the same question. He opens with practical steps that do not depend on any single verse: veganizing favorite recipes, trying new cuisines, and asking experienced vegans what they buy, eat and do for entertainment.

The core of the talk is a list of five spiritual "tools" drawn from scripture: trust God rather than your own understanding (Proverbs 3:5, Luke 18:27); forgive yourself when you fall short (Psalm 103:13-14, Matthew 26:41, Romans 7:19-20); flee from temptation and look for the "way of escape" (2 Timothy 2:22, 1 Corinthians 10:13, Romans 12:2), using Jesus's answers to the devil in Matthew 4 as a model; ask other believers to pray for you (James 5:16, Philippians 4:13, 1 Thessalonians 5:11); and be motivated by love for God's creatures (1 Corinthians 13:13, 1 Corinthians 16:14, 1 John 4:16). His evidence is almost entirely the wording of verses read in English translations, plus his own experience of switching diets.

The second half is a live question-and-answer session with chat commenters, covering plant-based products, hidden animal ingredients (beeswax and lac coatings on apples, bone char in sugar), deception by doctors, and whether lapsed vegans were ever truly committed. Craig closes by previewing next week's talk on whether animals will be judged, noting that he is not sure which of two views is correct.

## Key Teachings

### Practical steps before the spiritual "equipment"
Craig suggests veganizing favorite recipes (flax egg for chicken eggs, soy or hemp milk for cow milk, tempeh or textured soy protein for chicken wings), trying new cuisines (he says he ate "five things" before changing his diet), and seeking advice from experienced vegans about what they buy, eat and do for recreation. He presents these as ways to reduce temptation, not as scriptural commands, and says that "most of tonight's talk is going to deal with what the bible says."

### 1. Trust God rather than your own understanding
Craig reads Proverbs 3:5 ("trust in the lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding") and calls it the place to start. He ties it to Luke 18:27 ("the things which are impossible with men are possible with god"), answering the feeling that a plant-based life is impossible for someone who eats animal products at every meal. His advice is to start small, such as switching one meal, and to rely on God for the strength.

### 2. Forgive yourself when you fall short
Using Psalm 103:13-14 (read from the "brienne" study bible, which the transcript garbles and is most plausibly the Berean Study Bible), he argues God knows human frailty ("he is mindful that we are dust"). He reads Matthew 26:41 ("the spirit indeed is willing but the flesh is weak") and says it must not become an excuse. The right response is to forgive yourself and resolve again, not to give up. He also reads Romans 7:19-20 as Paul admitting a gap between what he knew was good and what he did. Craig glosses this as "cognitive dissonance," the psychological term for actions that do not match beliefs, and says that "even one of the most zealous apostles of jesus was also struggling with this."

### 3. Jesus in the wilderness as the model for resisting temptation
Craig walks through Matthew 4:1-11 in order. Jesus answers each temptation with scripture: Deuteronomy 8:3 ("man shall not live by bread alone"), Deuteronomy 6:16 (do not test the Lord), and Deuteronomy 6:13 (worship the Lord alone). Craig's application is that believers should answer temptation with specific verses, and that Matthew 4:11 shows angels ministering after the devil leaves.

### 4. Flee from temptation and find the "way of escape"
Citing 2 Timothy 2:22 (flee youthful desires), Craig says one should not weigh the merits of a temptation: the example he gives is a dessert display that "looks really good." He reads 1 Corinthians 10:13 as promising a way of escape, and uses a fire-exit image to describe it. He ties Ephesians 4:27 ("give no place to the devil") to the Garden of Eden account, where he says Eve entertained the serpent's suggestion. On social pressure, he reads Romans 12:2 ("be transformed by the renewing of your mind") and argues that refusing to conform, even at the cost of ridicule, can be a witness to others.

### 5. Confess and pray with other believers
Citing James 5:16 ("confess your trespasses to one another and pray for one another"), Craig says that believers should share struggles, even unrelated ones, and pray together. He reads Philippians 4:13 ("I can do all things through christ who strengthens me") and points to Paul's sufferings (shipwreck, stoning, flogging, imprisonment) as the context. He then reads 1 Thessalonians 5:11 ("encourage one another and build one another up") and says criticism such as "when are you going to grow up" is not encouragement. He adds that asking non-vegan believers to pray for you can itself be a witness.

### 6. Be motivated by love, not only self-interest
Craig cites 1 Corinthians 16:14 ("let all that you do be done with love") and 1 John 4:16 ("god is love"). He contrasts giving up sugar, which he says is motivated only by personal health, with giving up animal products, which he says also has the welfare of the animals as motivation. He ends with 1 Corinthians 13:13, arguing that love is the greatest of faith, hope and love and the most powerful force for overcoming obstacles. He also says that people who go vegan out of love for animals "don't ever quit," while people motivated by health or weight loss often return.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Overcoming temptation is a solo effort of willpower.** Craig says trusting God (Proverbs 3:5, Luke 18:27) and seeking prayer from other believers are the first steps, and that doing it alone is the mistake.
- **Having a moment of weakness means you are a failure.** He says to forgive yourself and resolve again (Psalm 103:13-14), while not using "the flesh is weak" as an excuse.
- **Going plant-based means giving up most of your food.** He says veganizing recipes and trying new cuisines makes the change far wider, not narrower.
- **Animal products are unavoidable or harmless.** He argues that people do not need cow's milk, and that hidden animal ingredients such as beeswax and bone char are common.
