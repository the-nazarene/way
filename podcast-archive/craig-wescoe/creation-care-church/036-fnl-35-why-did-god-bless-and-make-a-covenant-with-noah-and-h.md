## Summary

Craig asks why God blessed Noah and made a covenant with him after the flood, and whether the answer is that God was pleased by Noah's burnt offering in Genesis 8:20. He reads that common view as a mistake. He argues the blessing repeats the creation blessing of Genesis 1, and the covenant had already been promised before the flood (Genesis 6:18). He also argues that the covenant includes the animals, which he takes to show that God is not pleased by their slaughter.

Craig's main textual evidence is the parallel between Genesis 1:21-22, 1:27-28 and 9:1-2, plus the repeated covenant language in Genesis 9:9-17. He reads the new fear and "given into your hand" language of Genesis 9:2 as a consequence of human violence, not divine approval, and he supports that reading with 1 John 4:16-18, Genesis 3:9-10 and Job 2:6. He treats the post-flood diet change as a restriction (Genesis 9:4, no eating blood) that begins to move humans away from violence, and he says God "did not establish his covenant just with humanity but also the animals."

The talk ends with a Q&A on whether Noah was the first to sacrifice animals, the giants and 1 Enoch, a possible link between meat-eating and shortened lifespans, and the risk of using Genesis 9:3 to justify eating animals. Craig closes by promising a later talk on clean and unclean animals.

### Key Teachings

### 1. The blessing repeats the creation blessing, not a reward for sacrifice
Craig sets Genesis 1:21-22 (blessing on sea creatures and birds) and Genesis 1:27-28 (blessing on humans with the added instruction of dominion) beside Genesis 9:1. He says God's blessing after the flood is "because in the beginning" God blessed creation, and that God is restarting the world. He also notes that after the post-flood blessing, God does not repeat the dominion instruction.

### 2. The contrast with "very good" creation
Craig contrasts Genesis 1:31 ("very good") with Genesis 8:21, where the human heart is described as evil from youth. He argues that the post-flood world is "inferior" to the original state, and that God is not simply changing his mind about diet. He says the post-flood arrangements (fear of humans, permission to eat animals, the blood restriction in Genesis 9:4) are concessions to a corrupted human condition.

### 3. The covenant was promised before the flood
Craig points to Genesis 6:18 ("I will establish my covenant with you") and argues the promise was made before Noah offered any sacrifice. He says God "still upholds his promise" even though Noah had acted on his own initiative after the flood (sending out the birds, then building the altar). On this reading, the covenant is not a response to the offering.

### 4. The covenant includes the animals
Craig counts the repeated statements in Genesis 9:10, 9:12, 9:15, 9:16 and 9:17 and says the animals are "recipients of the covenant just like noah." He argues this matters for the altar in Genesis 8:20: if the animals killed and burned there were covenant recipients, it is unlikely God was pleased by that killing. He also reads Genesis 8:15-17 as God blessing the animals in the same words as Genesis 1.

### 5. "Given into your hand" means power, not approval
Craig argues that the phrase in Genesis 9:2 ("they are given into your hand") means power, not endorsement. He supports this with Job 2:6, where Satan is given power over Job while God does not approve of Satan's actions. He also ties the fear of Genesis 9:2 to Genesis 3:9-10 (Adam and Eve hid because they were afraid), and to 1 John 4:16-18 ("There is no fear in love"). For Craig, fear is a sign of the fall and of violence toward animals, not part of God's plan.

### 6. Noah and animal sacrifice; the giants and 1 Enoch (Q&A)
In answer to a question, Craig says it is "debatable" whether Noah was the first to offer animal sacrifice. He says Genesis 8:20 is the first explicit altar and burnt offering, but he thinks the practice probably came from the violence described in Genesis 6:5, 6:11 and 6:4. He reads 1 Enoch 7:4-6 as describing giants who devoured animals, people and each other's blood, and says the book of Jude "directly quotes from this book of enoch," so he considers it reliable on that point.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God was pleased by Noah's animal sacrifice, and that is why he blessed Noah and made the covenant.** Craig says the covenant was promised in Genesis 6:18 before any sacrifice, and that the animals are included in the covenant, which makes a pleasure reading unlikely.
- **God changed his mind after the flood and added animals to the menu.** Craig argues the post-flood situation is a concession to human evil ("this is humans acting a way we're not supposed to") and not a change in God's original will.
- **"Given into your hand" (Genesis 9:2) means God endorses whatever humans do to animals.** Craig says it means power, as with Satan's power over Job, and does not imply approval.
- **Genesis 9:3 gives permission to eat animals without limit.** A viewer raised this; Craig answers that Genesis 9:3 must not be taken out of context, and warns with Numbers 11 that craving is not God's will.
- **Noah was the first to offer animal sacrifice.** Craig says this is "debatable" and that scripture does not say it for certain, though he thinks the practice probably came from the pre-flood violence.
- **1 Enoch is not reliable because it is not canonical.** Craig argues that Jude's quotation of Enoch makes its account of the giants reliable enough to use.
