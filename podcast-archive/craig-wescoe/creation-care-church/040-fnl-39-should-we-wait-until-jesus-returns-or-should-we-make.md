## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of the Creation Care Church Friday Night Live series, asks whether Christians should simply wait for Jesus to return or should make themselves ready for the kingdom now. He frames the question as a rebuttal often raised against veganism and creation care: that Jesus will fix animal suffering and creation's damage when he returns, so no present action is needed. Wescoe argues that the answer is to prepare now, and that preparation includes how believers treat animals and the earth.

His evidence is almost entirely scriptural and comes in three groups. First, he cites teachings and parables on priority and readiness: Matthew 6:33, Luke 18:28-30, Matthew 13:44-46, the wise and foolish virgins (Matthew 25:1-13), the faithful steward (Luke 12:42-43), and the wedding garment (Matthew 22:11-13). Second, he uses Genesis 1:28-31 and 2:15 together with Isaiah 11:9 and Revelation 21:4 to argue that the kingdom restores the original plant-based diet and the human role as steward over animals. Third, he uses Galatians 3:26-27 and Colossians 3:12 (Paul's letters) to argue that readiness means being "clothed in Christ" through baptism and faith, expressed in compassion, kindness, gentleness and patience, which he then applies to animals.

The talk ends with a prayer and a Q&A. Wescoe answers viewer questions on repentance, salvation evidence, what resurrected bodies will eat (he says food, citing Genesis 1:29 and Jesus eating after resurrection), and how to talk with people without hardening the heart. He closes by announcing that this is the 40th of a set of prepared rebuttal talks and that the next session will be on Pentecost.

---

### Preparation is the highest priority, not a box to tick

Wescoe reads Matthew 6:33 as an instruction to put "the kingdom of God and his righteousness" first, and treats the disciples' abandonment of their livelihoods in Luke 18:28-30 as the model. He reads the hidden treasure and the pearl of great price (Matthew 13:44-46) as all-or-nothing devotion. He rejects a view in which accepting Jesus as savior is a single checkbox after which "we can go about our business living according to the ways of the world and the flesh." Kingdom priority, in his account, has to reshape every area of life.

### Readiness as watchfulness: the parables of the virgins and the steward

Wescoe reads the parable of the wise and foolish virgins (Matthew 25:1-13) as a lesson against last-minute preparation: the five foolish virgins had no oil and were shut out. He links it to Matthew 24:44 ("be ready") and the faithful steward (Luke 12:42-43), whom he identifies with the one whom the master "will make ruler over all that he has." He says the master is Jesus, and that when he returns he wants to find his servants already doing what he asked.

### The wedding garment and being "clothed in Christ"

Using Revelation 19:6-8, Wescoe reads the bride's fine linen as "the righteous acts of the saints" and connects it to the guest without a wedding garment in Matthew 22:11-13. He then cites Galatians 3:26-27 to say the garment is Christ's righteousness received through faith and baptism, not the believer's own. He treats Colossians 3:12 (compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience) as the visible character of someone so clothed, and applies each quality to how one treats animals. He also cites Galatians 5:22-23 as the "evidence" of the Holy Spirit and James 2 ("faith without works") as a reason faith must be shown in action.

### Genesis dominion and the plant diet as the kingdom pattern

Wescoe argues that the kingdom restores Eden. He reads Revelation 21:4 (no more death or pain) and Isaiah 11:6-9 (predator and prey at peace, the lion eating straw) as the kingdom picture, and ties this to Genesis 1:29-30, where plants are given for food to humans and animals. He reads Genesis 2:15 as humans being placed to "tend and keep" the garden and, by extension, to grow food for the other creatures. From this he concludes that the faithful steward's job now is to avoid killing, enslaving or harming animals, and to return to the plant diet. He also makes a short appeal to Revelation 11:18 ("destroy those who destroy the earth") as a warning against damaging creation.

### Repentance as ongoing and specific

In Q&A, Wescoe responds to the question "without repentance, what is faith?" He says Jesus and John the Baptist both preached "Repent, for the kingdom of God is at hand," and that repentance means to turn around and stop going in a direction, not a single feeling of remorse. He says repentance is tied to each specific sin, including the consumption of animals "to serve the desires of the flesh." He presents this as part of drawing closer to God, and says God answers prayers for repentance.

- **"Heavenly life will be disembodied / we won't eat."** In Q&A, Wescoe says resurrected bodies will eat food, citing Genesis 1:29 and Jesus eating after resurrection (the resurrection passage is not given by reference).
