## Summary

Craig Wescoe opens a Friday night live session on Genesis 1, asking what God's "very good design" for creation means and what kind of dominion humans were given. He begins by playing a roughly two-minute clip from Live Mercifully (Lily), the first in a series on Bible verses said to support veganism. Craig then works through Genesis 1 day by day. He reads creation as deliberately structured, with habitats built before their inhabitants, and says God gave humans and animals a shared plant diet (Genesis 1:29-30). He treats the phrase "very good" as an evaluation that humans should share, and he reads Genesis 1:26-28 as a command to rule in a loving, non-tyrannical way.

His main evidence is cross-reading within Scripture. He connects the Hebrew "rule" words in Genesis 1:18 and 4:7 and Leviticus 25:43, reads John 1 as placing the "word" (Jesus) at the beginning of creation, and then ties this to 1 John 4:16 ("God is love") and the Isaiah 11:6-9 peaceable kingdom. He also argues that predation is not part of God's original design and that the present violence of the world comes from a corruption. He applies the argument to factory farming in the closing section, and in the Q&A he rejects evolution as a "philosophical narrative." The talk ends with questions from viewers, a closing prayer, and an announcement of fellowship the following week. Most of the content is Craig's own argument; the Live Mercifully clip is a short opening framing.

## Key Teachings

### Creation as a designed habitat, evaluated as "very good"
Craig reads Genesis 1 as layered construction. God first creates the framework (light, the firmament, waters), then the habitats (dry land, seas, sky), and then the creatures to inhabit them. He argues that the sequence shows purpose: "he's creating a home and then he's going to create the creatures to inhabit the home." He also stresses that God attributes value ("it's good," then "very good" on day six) and that humans should align their own evaluation with God's. He reads "very good" at the close of day six as a sign of completion.

### Dominion as image-bearing, loving rule
Building on the Live Mercifully clip, Craig argues that the link between image of God and dominion in Genesis 1:26 means human rule must be godly. He reads Genesis 1:26-28 as dominion given jointly to male and female, with no hierarchy between them (he says any male-over-female ordering comes after the fall in Genesis 3). He distinguishes two Hebrew words: the "rule" in Genesis 1:18 (*mashal*, as he pronounces it) is, in his reading, not the same word as *radah* in Genesis 1:26 and 1:28, though he says both mean roughly "rule." He points to Genesis 4:7, where Cain is told to "rule over" sin, and argues that rule means keeping a power in check rather than tyranny. He then cites Leviticus 25:43, where *radah* is qualified as "not with rigor," as the model for ruling with gentleness, and ties this to the Sabbath as a mercy to workers.

### The diet of all creatures and the question of predation
Craig reads Genesis 1:29-30 as giving plants as food for humans and for every beast and bird, and he says "every creature is created an herbivore." He addresses the obvious objection (sharks, lions) by saying the carnivorous world is a result of corruption and that he has addressed this in an earlier live talk, which he does not summarize here. He says God's design was a world where creatures "live together in harmony," and he rejects "survival of the fittest" as the purpose of creation. He reads Isaiah 11:6-9 as the fullest picture of this restored order, in which the lion eats straw and a little child leads the animals.

### John 1, 1 John, and love as God's nature
Craig makes the point that John begins at creation, not at Jesus' birth, so "in the beginning" in John 1:1 refers to Genesis 1:1. He identifies the "word" in John 1 with Jesus, and the "light" of John 1:4 with the first light of Genesis 1:3, and says the accounts "map onto each other." He then reads 1 John 4:16 ("God is love") as God's essential attribute, and he argues that the image of God in humans is therefore an image of love. He cites 2 John 1:6 and 1 John 2:6-8 to say the "old commandment" from the beginning is the command to love, which he links back to the command to have dominion. He also cites Deuteronomy 6:4-5 as the greatest commandment, combined with the second, love of neighbor, to describe rightly ordered relationship.

### The Sabbath as first appointed time
Craig reads Genesis 2:1-3 as God setting an example in resting on the seventh day, which he calls the first *moedim* ("appointed time") explicitly established. He says God did not rest from exhaustion but "to establish a pattern for us." He links this to Jesus as "Lord of the Sabbath" and to the idea of resting from one's own labor and letting God work in the believer. Craig notes that the chapter break between Genesis 1 and 2 is "arbitrary" and "added in much later."

### Factory farming and the question of attitude
Applying the argument to modern practice, Craig says the cultural forms of animal agriculture, such as gestation crates, battery cages, and breeding for output, are the opposite of the rule God describes. He says the crux is a change of attitude toward God, creation, animals, and each other, and that once attitude is right, "everything else will follow." He also contrasts the energy spent on technology and weapons with the effort that could go to finding diets that let animals thrive. This is the most direct application of the talk, and it is his own argument, not a quotation from the sources.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means license to exploit or kill animals.** Craig says dominion is a loving, non-tyrannical rule. He argues that the Genesis 1 diet excludes killing animals for food.
- **Man rules over woman in Genesis 1.** Craig reads Genesis 1:27-28 as joint rule with no hierarchy, saying hierarchy belongs to the post-fall order of Genesis 3.
- **Predation and carnivory are part of God's original design.** Craig says a carnivorous world is a result of corruption, and that the restored order (Isaiah 11) has lions eating straw.
- **Genesis 1 and 2 are separate, original divisions with seven literal days of creation.** Craig says the chapter headings were added much later, and he treats the seventh day as a pattern of rest rather than a fact of divine exhaustion.
