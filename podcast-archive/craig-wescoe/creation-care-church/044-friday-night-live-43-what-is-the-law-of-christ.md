## Summary

Craig Wescoe opens "Friday Night Live #43" by asking what the "law of Christ" is, citing 1 Corinthians 9:20-21 and Galatians 6:2, and how believers are to keep it. He answers that the law of Christ is faith expressing itself as love (Galatians 5:6), fulfilled by bearing one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2), which he equates with the golden rule and the "royal law" of loving one's neighbor as oneself (Leviticus 19:18; James 2:8). He argues that this love extends to animals, so that how people raise, work, and slaughter animals, and whether they eat them, is a question of whether they are fulfilling the law of Christ.

His main evidence is cross-referencing: Habakkuk 2:4 for faith as a whole-life orientation, James 1:25 and 2:8-13 for the "law of liberty" and mercy, Galatians 5:13-26 for the fruit of the Spirit, and 1 John 2:6-8 and 3:23 for a commandment "from the beginning." He reads Jesus's summary of the law and Leviticus 19:18 as the same command Paul relays, and he says the Old Testament creation instructions (dominion, tending the garden) support the animal-care conclusion.

Most of the second half is Q&A from the live chat: a prayer event against the Yulin dog and cat festival, objections about whether animal ethics distracts from the gospel, and questions on humane slaughter ("an oxymoron"), cognitive dissonance among "animal lovers," and how to be patient with people who resist veganism. The talk closes with a preview of the next episode (Genesis 2, with a Genesis 1:29 "Live Mercifully" segment) and a closing prayer.

## Key Teachings

### Faith working through love
Craig reads Galatians 5:6 ("faith working through love") as the core of the law of Christ. Faith is not a side identity but "the whole of your character," the thing that determines who your lord is. He links faith to humility by contrasting Habakkuk 2:4 ("the just shall live by his faith") with the proud whose "soul is not upright." Since God's character is love, a person who truly submits to God's will will express love, and a claim of faith that produces no love is a "pretense."

### Bearing burdens and the golden rule (Galatians 6:2)
He reads "bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ" as the practical form of the law. He describes communal sharing of hardship, then ties this to the golden rule and to loving the neighbor "as yourself." He extends the category of "burden" to beasts of burden. Using the donkey in the Balaam story (he does not give a chapter), he argues that overworking or caging an animal is a failure to bear its burden, while liberating animals from captivity, factory farms, and aquariums counts as bearing it.

### Living faith and the "law of liberty" (James 2:18; James 1:25)
Craig reads James 2:18 (ESV) and says works are not the means of salvation, but faith must be "alive," using a phone-charger analogy. He reads James 1:25 to argue that the "perfect law of liberty" is the law of Christ, and that hearing without doing makes one a "forgetful hearer."

### Love, forgiveness, and the greatest commandments (Galatians 5:13-15; Leviticus 19:18; James 2:8)
He says Paul did not invent the "love your neighbor" summary; Jesus's greatest-commandment teaching draws on Deuteronomy 6:4-5 and Leviticus 19:18. Reading Leviticus 19:18 ("you shall not take vengeance nor bear any grudge"), he argues the command includes forgiveness and the refusal to wish harm. He reads Galatians 5:15 ("bite and devour one another") as an image that, taken literally, describes how animals are treated in slaughter.

### Mercy, the animal question, and "humane slaughter" (James 2:12-13)
He reads James 2:12-13 as requiring mercy, then states that mercy means not buying humanely slaughtered flesh or cage-free eggs. He argues that "humane slaughter" is self-contradictory, since slaughter is inherently against the animal's will. He ties this to Isaiah 11:9 ("no hurting, no destroying") and to 2 Peter 3:9 and Romans 12:2 in answer to a chat comment, and he suggests that "cognitive dissonance" explains professed animal lovers who pay for animals to be killed.

### The commandment "from the beginning" and creation (1 John 2:7-8; 1 John 3:23; Genesis 2:15)
Craig reads 1 John 2:7-8 as saying the commandment to love is "old," having been "from the beginning," and links that to John 1:1 ("in the beginning was the word"). He then cites the creation instructions (put in the garden "to tend and keep," "have dominion") as the framework for stewardship, arguing that love of neighbor includes the animals entrusted to humans.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Faith alone is enough, works are optional.** Craig says faith is not earned by works, but a faith that does not produce works is "dead" (James 2:18); he calls believers who hear and forget "forgetful hearers."
- **The law of Christ is a new, burdensome set of rules.** Craig reads Galatians 6:2 and says Jesus's commandments are "not burdensome," and that the law is love, not a list of regulations.
- **Concern for animals distracts from the gospel.** In answering a chat question, Craig argues that loving the animal neighbor is part of living by the gospel toward "all of my neighbors," not a rival to it.
