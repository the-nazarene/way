## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of the livestream "Friday Night Live #47" for Creation Care Church, asks what Isaiah 46:10 ("I make known the end from the beginning") means. His answer is that "the end" is a return to the beginning: the good, perfect creation of Genesis 1-2, which God intended from the start and which Jesus restores. He reads "end" in the Greek sense of *telos*, meaning purpose or goal and not only the last point in time. He states that the Bible's "end" is "return to the beginning," and that the purpose of God's instructions in Eden (fill the earth, have dominion, eat plants) is fulfilled in the restored kingdom.

His evidence is a chain of cross-references across the canon, mostly from Genesis 1-3 and the Johannine literature, plus Matthew, Luke, Revelation and three Pauline passages (Romans 6:22, Romans 10:4, Philippians 3:19). He reads Jesus's divorce teaching in Matthew 19:8 as pointing back to Eden, John 8:44 and 1 John 3:8 as identifying Satan as the "murderer from the beginning" who brought death through deception in Genesis 3, and 1 John 3:11 as the "message from the beginning" to love one another. He ties dominion (Genesis 1:26-28) to doing God's will rather than one's own, and presents a plant-based diet and non-killing of animals as part of the original order. He reads the nakedness of Genesis 2-3 as a matter of fear versus trust, not a clothing requirement.

The talk closes with a live question-and-answer segment drawn from chat (on nudity, animal welfare, Torah and the Ephesus church in Revelation 2), a discussion of resurrection and the restored kingdom, and a closing prayer. Craig announces next week's topic will be a Psalm about animals. Throughout, the positions are Craig's own readings; he does not cite historical scholarship to support them.

### Key Teachings

### 1. The end is the return to the beginning
Craig argues that Isaiah 46:10 implies the end of God's plan mirrors its start. He says "the end is return to the beginning," moving from the creation (Genesis 1:1, cited with Hebrews 1:10 quoting Psalm 102:25), through the Fall, to the restored "very good" world. He reads Ecclesiastes 7:8 ("the end of the matter is better than its beginning") as meaning that the beginning's instructions are fulfilled at the end. He compares this to an oak being the purpose of an acorn, attributing the image to Plato and Aristotle with the qualifier "I believe." He draws on the Greek *telos*, noting the same word is used for "end" in the New Testament, and insists the end is a purpose fulfilled and not only chronologically last.

### 2. The beginning as the standard for God's will (Matthew 19:8)
In his reading of Matthew 19, Craig says the Pharisees asked about divorce and Jesus answered by pointing to Eden. He glosses Moses's permission as allowed "because of the hardness of your hearts," and says God's perfect will is found by going "back to the beginning." He argues that post-Fall provisions draw people back toward God's will but are not the full standard. He presents this as Jesus's own method of appeal.

### 3. Satan as "murderer and liar from the beginning"
Craig reads John 8:44 and 1 John 3:8 through Genesis 3. He says the only murder in the beginning was Cain killing Abel, and he rejects that as the referent. Instead, he argues that Satan is the "murderer from the beginning" because he deceived Adam and Eve into eating the forbidden fruit, which brought sin and death. He reads the serpent's "you won't die" as the lie that God's word disproved ("it was Satan that was proved to be the liar, not god"). He links 1 John 2:14 ("he who was from the beginning" = Christ, the Word of John 1:1) with overcoming "the wicked one."

### 4. Dominion, diet and animal welfare
Craig reads Genesis 1:26-29 and Genesis 2:15 as giving humans dominion to be exercised as God's will, not one's own. He says the grant of dominion comes with the diet of "fruit and vegetation" and that many Christians misread dominion as license, skipping the next verse. He argues that killing animals to eat them "brings death into the world," and presents this as a form of the Fall's deception. He links Philippians 3:19 ("their god is their belly") to the question of whom one serves, and Galatians 5:22-23 (fruit of the Spirit) to restraint and gentleness toward animals. He also says the Ephesus church (Revelation 2) should "repent and do the first works," listing the Edenic works as dominion, eating from plants, gardening and loving one another. He presents these as his own application, not as a settled consensus.

### 5. Love as the first command, and Christ as the goal of the law
Craig takes 1 John 3:11 ("the message that you heard from the beginning, that we should love one another") as the original and "eternal" command, comparable to the "royal law" of Love your neighbor. He reads Romans 10:4 ("Christ is the end of the law") as meaning goal, not abolition, with the law pointing to Christ's character. On Torah, he says the word means "instruction" and that God gave instructions to Abraham (citing Genesis 22 by implication via Isaac) before Moses. He adds that Jesus did not break the law and that the Spirit compels people to keep its letter, though the letter does not save.

### 6. Fear, nakedness and the Fall
On the nudity question from chat, Craig reads Genesis 2:25 ("naked and unashamed") against Genesis 3:7-10, where Adam and Eve hide their bodies and themselves from God because they are afraid. He cites 1 John 4:18 ("perfect love casts out fear") and argues the core meaning of being "naked and unashamed" is living without fear and by faith. He explicitly does not require clothing to be discarded and says he is "not willing to say that that's something you have to do," while noting that laws of the land may apply.

### 7. Restoration, resurrection and the kingdom
Craig links Luke 1:31-33 (an everlasting throne and "no end" to the kingdom) to the Davidic covenant and Jesus as the completion of God's dominion promise. He cites Romans 6:22 (fruit leading to "eternal life") as the opposite of slavery to sin, and Matthew 24:14 (the "gospel of the kingdom" preached to all nations before the end) as the restoration of the good world. He presents Revelation 21:4 (no more death, tears or pain) and the resurrection of Jesus as the guarantee of the believer's resurrection to eternal life in glorified bodies. He cites Jesus's words "I have power to lay down my life and take it up again" (in the transcript, "God gave me this power," paraphrased by Craig as Jesus's claim) as proof of God's power to resurrect.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means the right to exploit creation.** Craig says dominion is for doing God's will, not one's own, and that many Christians read it as license while skipping Genesis 1:29.
- **Romans 10:4 means the law has ended, so anything goes.** Craig says "end" means goal or purpose, and that the law points to Christ's character; he argues that Spirit-led believers keep the law's moral content.
- **"Naked and unashamed" requires nudity.** Craig says the point is living without fear and by faith, and that clothing is not required, though he notes the laws of the land.
- **"End" means only the last event in time.** Craig argues the Greek *telos* sense of purpose fulfilled is the intended meaning in Isaiah 46:10 and Ecclesiastes 7:8.
- **Satan's murder is Cain's killing of Abel.** Craig rejects this reading for John 8:44 and says Satan is the murderer through bringing death via deception in Eden.
