## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a Friday live message for Creation Care Church, asks what it means to "drink from the rock of Christ." He starts with two accounts of water from a rock in the wilderness: Exodus 17:1-7, where God tells Moses to strike the rock, and Numbers 20:7-12, where God tells Moses to speak to it and Moses strikes it instead. Craig argues that the second account is a failure of faith and of giving God credit, and that this failure is why Moses could not enter the promised land. He then reads 1 Corinthians 10:3-4 as identifying the rock as Christ, and links the water to the Holy Spirit through John 7:37-39, Joel 2:28, Acts 2 and Revelation 5:13. His distinctive claim is that the animals drank from the rock too, so the Spirit is poured out on creatures as well as people.

Craig then contrasts the violent conquest under Joshua (Joshua 11) with Jesus' statement that his kingdom is "not of this world" (John 18:36) and his commission to make disciples (Matthew 28:19-20). He reads "striking" as the old, violent way and "speaking" as the new way of the Spirit and disciple-making. The second half is a question-and-answer segment. Craig uses Genesis 1:27-29 and Genesis 3 to argue that "dominion" means service and that humans were given a plant diet, and he says Christians should share their understanding of the kingdom of peace with animals included. The talk closes with prayer and a preview of next week's talk on covenants (Genesis 9).

## Key Teachings

### Moses' two water events: strike versus speak
Craig reads Exodus 17:3 and 17:6, where the people thirst and God tells Moses to strike the rock. He then reads Numbers 20:7-12, where God says to take the rod, gather the assembly and "speak to the rock." Moses instead strikes it twice. Craig stresses that God and not Moses provides the water, and that Moses' words ("you rebels, must we bring water from out of this rock") claim the act for themselves. In Numbers 20:12 God says, "because you did not believe me, to hallow me," and Craig treats this as the reason Moses cannot enter the land. He notes that the animals drink in both accounts (Numbers 20:8).

### The rock is Christ (1 Corinthians 10:3-4)
Craig says the New Testament gives the rock its significance. He reads 1 Corinthians 10:3-4, where the people "drank of the spiritual rock that followed them. And that rock was Christ." He reads the manna and water as God providing for the Exodus community. Matthew 5:6 ("hunger and thirst for righteousness") is then read as spiritual thirst that only Christ satisfies. Craig links the manna to Jesus as "bread of life."

### Water as the Holy Spirit (John 7:37-39, Joel 2:28, Acts 2)
Craig reads John 7:37-39, where Jesus at the feast says "if anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink," and John adds that the Spirit was not yet given because Jesus was not yet glorified. Craig takes this to equate drinking the water with receiving the Spirit. He ties it to Joel 2:28 ("pour out my spirit on all flesh," with "all flesh" read as including non-human creatures in his argument) and to Acts 2:1-4, where the disciples receive the Spirit at Pentecost with tongues of fire and speak in languages that the crowd understands.

### The animals drank too, so the Spirit reaches creation (Revelation 5:13)
This is the argument Craig calls easy to miss. The animals were thirsty and drank in the Horeb accounts, the rock is Christ, and the water is the Spirit. So, he reasons, God's Spirit is poured out on animals as well. He cites Revelation 5:13, where "every creature" in heaven, on earth and in the sea praises the one on the throne and the Lamb, as the fulfillment of this. He presents this as a point most readers overlook.

### Striking versus speaking: violence versus the Spirit and disciple-making
Craig reads Matthew 11:12 ("the kingdom of heaven suffers violence") and John 18:36 ("my kingdom is not of this world... my servants would fight"). He contrasts these with Joshua 11:18-23, which describes Joshua's war of conquest. He argues that the striking of the rock belongs to the old, violent pattern that Joshua followed, and that speaking belongs to Jesus' method. He reads Luke 24:49, where the disciples are told to wait for power from on high, and Acts 2 as the fulfillment. Matthew 28:19-20 is his picture of the kingdom spread through baptism and teaching. He ties the Pentecost timing to the annual festivals, with Passover, firstfruits and Pentecost marking the sequence.

### Dominion as service: the Genesis 1 and diet argument (Q&A)
In answer to a question about Christian vegetarians who do not speak about diet, Craig says he gives people the benefit of the doubt and tries to "share the gift" of knowledge. He reads Genesis 1:27-29 and argues that the "dominion" of 1:28 is misread as license to kill animals. He points to 1:29, where humans are given "every herb" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed" as food, and calls it the diet God gave. He links the misreading to the serpent's temptation in Genesis 3:1-6, "you will be like god, knowing good and evil," and calls the reading a fallen, prideful attitude. He says Jesus "came to serve, not to be served," and that dominion should look like service, for example "washing the animal's feet."

### Anthropocentric versus theocentric, and humility
Craig says the Genesis misreading is anthropocentric (humans at the center), and he proposes a theocentric view with God at the center. He warns that pride "creeps up before you even notice," and that Christians should check themselves. He also says God's instructions can change (striking versus speaking, and Jesus' two missions with and without provisions, Matthew 10 not cited by verse), and that believers should wait on God's timing.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Moses struck the rock and was simply forgiven for a small slip.** Craig says the second strike (Numbers 20) was a failure to believe God and to honor him, and that it kept Moses from entering the land.
- **The rock was just any rock that produced water.** Craig argues, on 1 Corinthians 10:3-4, that the rock was Christ.
- **Only people received the water and the Spirit.** Craig argues the animals also drank and that Joel 2:28 and Revelation 5:13 extend the Spirit to creatures.
- **Genesis 1:28 "dominion" permits killing and eating animals.** Craig says the reading skips Genesis 1:29, which gives a plant diet, and that dominion is meant as service.
- **Christians may be fully spiritual and still eat animals with no concern.** Craig says growth in Christ varies, and he gives people the benefit of the doubt while sharing knowledge.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **Jesus versus Paul:** The rock-equals-Christ identification depends on 1 Corinthians 10:3-4, a Pauline text. Craig does not contrast Paul with Jesus, but the argument leans on Paul's reading of Israel's history.
- **Torah, sacrifice, diet:** The talk reads Exodus and Numbers as typology and cites Genesis 1:29 as the original diet. It does not address Levitical sacrifice directly, though Craig says "the priests... ate animals" was a rebuttal he heard from others.
- **Nonviolence:** Craig contrasts Joshua's conquest with the non-violent kingdom of Matthew 28 and John 18:36.
- **The Kingdom on earth:** He reads the Lord's Prayer petition, Matthew 28 and the "kingdom of peace" in which animals participate, as a present-earth kingdom.
- **Who Jesus thought he was:** Craig identifies Jesus with the rock, the living water, the Lamb and the bread of life.
- **Resurrection:** He mentions Jesus rising "as the first fruits" on the festival day, as part of the Pentecost timing.
- **Gospels and canon:** The talk reads John and Acts as interpretive keys to Exodus and Numbers, which shows a typological method rather than a historical-critical one.
