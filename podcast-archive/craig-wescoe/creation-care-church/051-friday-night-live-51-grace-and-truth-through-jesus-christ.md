## Summary

The talk, a one-year-anniversary "Friday Night Live" session from Creation Care Church, asks what John 1:17 means: "for the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ." The host, Craig, says the standard reading puts the law first and Jesus and grace later, and he argues that John 1 sets grace and truth before the law. His main evidence is a chain of cross-references. Galatians 3:16-17 places the promise to Abraham 430 years before the law, Genesis 17:7 gives the promise, and John 8:58 ("before Abraham was, I am") places Jesus before Abraham. He reads John 1:1-14 as Jesus, the Word, present at creation.

From there Craig traces grace and truth through Genesis. He treats God's free gift of creation (Genesis 1:29-31) as grace and God's warning about the tree (Genesis 2:17) as truth, set against the serpent's lie (Genesis 3:4). He identifies the serpent with the devil using John 8:44 and Revelation 20:2. He then moves to repentance and restoration, drawing on Mark 1:14-15, Acts 3:19-21, 2 Peter 3:9, and Galatians 5:22-23. In the live Q&A that follows, he applies these ideas to animal ethics and to "dominion," arguing against human-centred readings of Genesis. The session ends with a closing prayer and a preview of next week's study of Psalm 145:8-9.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Grace and truth precede the law (John 1:17 read chronologically)
Craig argues that John 1:17 is not describing a sequence in which Jesus comes after Moses. He cites Galatians 3:16-17 (promise to Abraham, then the law 430 years later), Genesis 17:7, and John 8:58 to argue that Jesus existed before Abraham and so before Moses. He concludes that "grace and truth coming through Jesus Christ... has to come even before the law was given to Moses." He also argues that the "standard reading" would leave no grace or truth in the law, which he says cannot be right because Scripture speaks of both before and within the law.

### 2. Jesus as the creating Word
Craig reads John 1:1-14 as a reference to Genesis 1:1 and to God creating by speech. He says John "places the beginning of Jesus" at creation and calls Jesus "that word of god that spoke everything into existence." He presents this as the basis for placing grace and truth at the start of the story.

### 3. Grace as undeserved gift; truth as God's word against the serpent's lie
- **Grace:** Craig reads Genesis 1:29-31 as God freely giving creation and "very good" status to humans and animals, with stewardship but no payment required. He calls this "an undeserved free gift" grounded in God's nature.
- **Truth:** He reads Genesis 2:17 (the warning), Genesis 3:4 (the serpent's denial), and Genesis 3:6 (the eating) as a contest over whom to trust. He uses Genesis 5:5 ("and he died") as proof that God told the truth and the serpent lied. He then cites John 8:44 ("there is no truth in him... he is a liar and the father of it") and Revelation 20:2 to identify the serpent with the devil and Satan. Craig states this identification as his reading; the cited verses are the basis he gives.

### 4. Repentance, confession, and restoration
Craig treats the Gospel of the kingdom as good news that God forgives and receives back the people who "distrusted in him." He says repentance means "to turn around" and that belief must show in conduct. His sources are:
- James 1:17 (every good gift is from above)
- John 14:6 (Jesus as the way, truth, and life)
- Titus 2:11-13 (grace that brings salvation trains believers to live "soberly, righteously, and godly")
- 1 John 1:9 (confession and cleansing)
- 2 Peter 3:9 (God not wanting anyone to perish)
- Mark 1:14-15 (repent and believe the good news of the kingdom)
- Acts 3:19-21 (times of restoration of all things)
- Galatians 5:22-23 (fruit of the Spirit)

He describes the "restoration of all things" as the future state in which grace is fully restored, and the present as a time to stop trusting the enemy's lies.

### 5. Dominion, stewardship, and animal ethics
Craig argues that "dominion" in Genesis 1 is stewardship, not superiority. He quotes Genesis 1:29 ("eat the fruit of the trees and the green plants of the ground") to argue that God did not give animals to be used for whatever humans wish, and he compares human care for creatures to a parent serving children. He calls the human-centred attitude "anthropocentrism," which he ties to pride, the "attribute of Satan." He cites Romans 12:2 on being transformed and mentions Jesus' statement that "no one is good except God" (no verse given). In the Q&A he says Christians often fail to show love even to human neighbours and criticises animal agriculture, entertainment, and experimentation as things people would not want done to themselves.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"The law came first, and grace and truth came later with Jesus."** Craig says John 1:17 places grace and truth before Moses, citing Galatians 3, Genesis 17:7, and John 8:58.
- **"Only a select few, or people with a good life or the right heritage, can be forgiven."** Craig says 2 Peter 3:9 shows forgiveness is open to everyone, "the people who've done absolutely horrible things."
- **"Dominion means humans are superior and may use animals as they wish."** Craig says Genesis 1:29 and the call to care for creation make human dominion a form of service and stewardship.
- **"Grace is earned by works" (from a commenter, Rosalyn).** Craig agrees that grace is God's work, that no one can boast of good works, and that people receive it by faith.
