## Key Teachings

### Mercy and compassion extend to every creature (Psalm 145:8-9)

The host and the embedded video take verse 8 ("the lord is gracious and full of compassion... great in mercy") and verse 9 ("his tender mercies are over all his works") to mean God's mercy is not limited to humans. The video links the Hebrew *rahum* to the word for womb, suggesting God's compassion resembles a mother's protective love. The host then argues that since "many animals are considered commodities," willful blindness toward animal exploitation is not merciful. The host also makes a word study point: *kol basar* ("all flesh") is said to always include animals (citing Genesis, Joel 2:28, Isaiah 66:23), while *kol adam* means humans only. The host says that checking the original Hebrew shows which verses refer to all creatures.

### Dominion as stewardship, not license (Genesis 1:26; Luke 12:42-46; Isaiah 11)

The host reads the dominion given in Genesis 1:26 as a delegated, managerial role that must reflect God's character: "compassion and mercy and tenderness." He says the Fall was humanity claiming to be "in charge" instead of God, and that Jesus models submission of will. Luke 12:42-46 (the faithful and wise steward) is used to argue that the steward is one who already feeds the animals and the hungry "in due season," rather than one who delays and acts cruelly. The host says that most people who abuse animals justify it with the dominion verse, which he rejects. Isaiah 11:6-9 (the lion and lamb imagery) is cited as the restored kingdom in which there is "no hurting or destroying."

### The original diet and feeding the hungry (Proverbs 12:10; Genesis 1:29-30)

On Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal, but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel"), the host argues that paying for animals to be killed to feed the hungry is still cruelty, and that Christians should feed the hungry without causing animal suffering. He says God's "original diet" is the fruit of the tree and green plants, citing Genesis 1:30 as the source. He mentions charities serving vegan meals and endorses supporting them. The host frames this as "two good things instead of a good thing and a bad thing."

### All creation praises God (Psalm 145:10, 21; Isaiah 64:8; Revelation 5:13)

The host reads Psalm 145:10 ("all your works shall praise you") through Isaiah 64:8 (the potter and the clay) to mean that God's creatures, including animals, were made to praise and honor their Creator. Psalm 145:21 ("all flesh shall bless his holy name") is read through Isaiah 66:23 and Revelation 5:13, where "every creature" gives blessing to God and the Lamb. The host concludes that killing and eating animals conflicts with this shared chorus of praise.

### The kingdom of God and the good news (Luke 4:43; Isaiah 11:6-9; Romans 8:21-22; Revelation 21:4)

Psalm 145:11-13 is read through Luke 4:43, where Jesus says he must preach the kingdom of God. The host says the good news includes God's restoration of creation and eternal life, citing Revelation 21:4 (no more sorrow, pain, or tears). Romans 8:21-22, where creation "groans" for liberation from corruption, is read as including animals who await that day. The host says the restored kingdom is also "a paradise for animals," not "a hell for animals."

### Love as keeping commandments, extended to enemies and animals (John 14:15; Luke 6:36)

On Psalm 145:20 ("the lord preserves all who love him"), the host contrasts a warm feeling of love with Jesus's words in John 14:15 ("if you love me, keep my commandments"). He argues that love must be extended to all, citing Luke 6:36 ("be merciful just as your father also is merciful") and the commandment to love enemies. Responding to a viewer, he says "perfect" in Luke 6 means "complete," so love should be "withheld from nobody," including animals.

### Q&A: sacrifice and the crucifixion (no verse given)

Asked how the crucifixion covers sin if living sacrifices were "evil," the host says a previous live talk covered this at length. Briefly, he attributes to "Clement" the view that the sacrificial system dealt first with the evil of worshiping other gods, and that Jesus ended the animal shedding. He says that between Moses and Jesus, animal sacrifice persisted because only the first evil had been dealt with. He offers to expand in the comments. The host does not give a reference for Clement's statement.

- **Loving God is a warm feeling.** The host argues, citing John 14:15, that love of God means keeping his commandments.
- **Jesus's return excuses delay.** The host argues against postponing faithful action until Jesus returns, citing Luke 12:42-46.
- **Love of enemies applies only to humans.** The host argues that love "withheld from nobody" includes animals.
