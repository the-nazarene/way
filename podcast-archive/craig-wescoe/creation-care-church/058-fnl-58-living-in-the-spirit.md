## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a Creation Care Church "Friday Night Live" talk, asks what it means to "live in the Spirit" in both biblical and practical terms. He frames the answer as three stages: knowing the Spirit, walking in the Spirit, and growing in the Spirit. Each stage is supported by a chain of scripture passages. Knowing the Spirit means seeking God wholeheartedly (Jeremiah 29:13), letting God's word be a lamp (Psalm 119:105), testing spirits against scripture (1 John 4:1), meditating on the word day and night (Psalm 1:2), and recognizing the voice of Jesus (John 10:27). Walking in the Spirit means taking scripture as a guide (2 Timothy 3:16), resisting temptation with scripture as Jesus did in Matthew 4:4, bearing the fruit of the Spirit listed in Galatians 5:22-23, and keeping the commandments (John 14:15). Growing in the Spirit means staying connected to Christ as the vine (John 15:1-2), maturing (Ephesians 4:13-15), renewing the mind (Romans 12:2), and gathering with other believers (Hebrews 10:24-25). Craig presents these as a continuous process, not a checklist. He offers no historical or textual analysis; his method is to read the passages together as a single voice.

The talk ends with a live question-and-answer segment from chat. Here the subject turns to animals. Craig argues that the fruit of the Spirit and the kingdom of God include not eating animals, citing Isaiah 11:6-9, Revelation 21:4, the Garden of Eden, Romans 14:17 and a preview of Proverbs 12:10 for the Friday Night Live talk on October 21. He also says Jesus's sacrifice ended animal sacrifice. He closes with a prayer, a reminder that there will be no talk the following week (Sukkot), and an invitation to the book club and the October 21 session.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Knowing the Spirit: seeking, the word, testing, and hearing
Craig says the starting point is "seeking god with all your heart," citing Jeremiah 29:13, and that this must be a yearning, not a casual interest. He ties this to Psalm 119:105 ("your word is a lamp to my feet") and says God is found through scripture. He then cites 1 John 4:1 ("test the spirits") and explains that discernment applies both to the spirit around us and to our own thoughts, which may come from God, from our own inclinations, from "flesh," or from the demonic. The test is comparison with what scripture says God's character is. Psalm 1:1-2 is used to argue that reading should be sustained meditation "day and night," not a weekly event. He compares this to physical training. John 10:27 ("my sheep hear my voice") is used to say that knowing Jesus's voice comes from familiarity with the text.

### 2. Walking in the Spirit: scripture as guide, temptation, fruit, and obedience
Craig says scripture is "for our benefit" and cites 2 Timothy 3:16. He presents Jesus's answer to Satan in Matthew 4:4 as an example: Jesus quotes Deuteronomy 8:3 rather than offering his own opinion, and Craig says this shows how to resist temptation. He reads the fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5:22-23 as a description of God's character that believers should embody, and reads each item in terms of conduct: love toward humans and animals, peace (non-violence), patience, self-control over "our desire to eat animals." John 14:15 ("if you love me, keep my commandments") is used to argue that obedience is evidence of love, and Craig explicitly rejects the idea that grace makes commandments unnecessary. He reads Galatians 5:16-17 as flesh versus Spirit, and rejects "just follow your heart" as "not biblically grounded," arguing that the heart is "treacherous above all things" and must be checked against the word.

### 3. Growing in the Spirit: the vine, maturity, renewal, and community
Craig uses John 15:1-2 (Jesus as the vine, branches that bear no fruit are pruned) to argue that connection to Christ produces fruit and disconnection leads to being cut off. He reads Ephesians 4:13-15 as a call to maturity, so that believers are not "tossed to and fro" by "every wind of doctrine," which he links to scientific and political ideologies. Colossians 2:6-7 adds thanksgiving to the list of virtues, and 1 Corinthians 14:20 is read as a call to be mature in understanding. Romans 12:2 (renewal of the mind) is used to argue that thoughts should be shaped by scripture rather than by a political or television source. Colossians 1:9-10 is read as a call to continual increase in knowledge. 2 Thessalonians 1:3 is cited as Paul praising a church for growth in faith and love, which Craig presents as an example of what living faith looks like. Hebrews 10:24-25 is used to urge regular assembly, including online, with the day of the Lord as motivation.

### 4. Animals, diet and the kingdom (in the Q&A)
When a chat participant (Jessica) asks about Noah, Craig says Noah saved the animals from the flood but then offered animals on an altar, which he says God never commanded. He uses Isaiah 11:6-9 and Revelation 21:4 (no death) to argue that in the kingdom people will not eat animals. He describes the Garden of Eden diet as "the fruit of the tree and the green plants of the ground" and presents this as the original pattern. He reads John 10:25 and the "greater works" passage (John 14:12, referenced but not cited with a verse in the captions) to mean that believers will live by kingdom standards that include not eating animals. He says Jesus's sacrifice on the cross "ends animal sacrifices and that bloodshed" and that Jesus is "the way" (John 14:6). In response to a chat participant who cites Romans 14:17 to defend eating animals, Craig argues the verse is being used "in the opposite way," since the kingdom is about "righteousness, peace, and joy," and he previews Proverbs 12:10 ("the righteous care for the needs of the animals") for the October 21 talk.

### 5. Forgiveness and nonviolence
In answer to a question about forgiveness, Craig says that refusing to forgive is the one unforgivable thing, citing the parable of the servant with a debt (Matthew 18, referenced without a verse). He distinguishes forgiveness from endorsing violence: believers forgive, but they do not endorse continued cruelty to people or animals. He uses the example of a hunter who repents and stops hunting as the ideal outcome, and says humans are created to "serve" animals by leading them in peace and providing habitat, not by raising cattle for profit.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Just follow your heart."** Craig says this is "not very good advice" and "not biblically grounded," because the heart is "treacherous above all things."
- **"It's not about works; Jesus knows my heart."** Craig agrees that Jesus knows hearts but argues that people may not know their own, and that disobeying commandments is logically inconsistent with love for Jesus (John 14:15).
- **Romans 14:17 used to justify eating animals.** Craig argues the verse is being used "in the opposite way"; the kingdom is about righteousness, peace and joy, and causing animal suffering works against that.
- **Noah as a model for animal sacrifice.** Craig says Noah rescued the animals but that God never commanded him to burn animals on an altar.
