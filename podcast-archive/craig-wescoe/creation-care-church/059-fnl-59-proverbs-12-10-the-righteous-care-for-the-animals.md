## Summary

Craig Wescoe's live talk asks what Proverbs 12, especially verse 10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal, but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel"), requires of believers in how they treat animals. He opens by playing a roughly three-minute video by Lily of Live Mercifully, which argues that cruelty or neglect toward animals is sinful, that even "humane" animal industries are cruel, and that a Christian who pays for animal products is participating in that cruelty. Craig then reads through Proverbs 12 verse by verse, using cross-references from Revelation, Luke, John, Galatians, Proverbs 31 and Isaiah 11 to argue that the "righteous" are those rooted in Christ, whose speech and actions bear the fruit of the Spirit, and that caring for animals and eating a plant-based diet are part of that righteousness.

His main evidence is textual and thematic rather than historical. He reads the contrast between the righteous and the wicked across the chapter, uses Genesis 1 to ground a vegetarian diet as God's original instruction, and treats Isaiah 11:6-9 as a picture of the peaceable kingdom that believers should already be living toward. He does not engage with the historical formation of the text, the Gospels, or the early Jesus movement; the argument is a close reading of the canonical text as a unified whole.

The second half is a live Q&A in which Craig concedes that a stronger claim, that eating animal flesh is always sin, cannot be made "definitively," though he says it would hold in "the vast majority of cases." He answers objections (the "Jesus ate fish" point, "I never killed an animal myself") and closes with a prayer. The talk does not resolve the sin question as a definitive matter; it presents the case as a pastoral and biblical appeal to Christians who are not vegan.

## Key Teachings

### Verse 10 as the anchor: the righteous care, the wicked are cruel
Craig treats Proverbs 12:10 as "probably the most well-known scripture" on animal treatment, as Lily's video puts it, and reads the rest of the chapter through its contrast between "the righteous" and "the wicked." He ties the "wicked" who "lie in wait for blood" (Proverbs 12:6, which he reads as Proverbs 12:5 in one place) to hunters and killers, and the righteous to those who care for animals. He also points to the word "compassionate" in the verse: he reads the "kindest acts of the wicked" as cruel even when they look kind, and applies this to animal industries that claim humane practices.

### Cruelty in the supply chain
Following Lily's video, Craig argues that even if eating meat is not in itself a sin, "treating animals with cruelty or neglect is sinful," and that the production of animal products involves cruelty. From this he argues that paying for those products participates in it. He uses an analogy: hiring a hitman does not absolve responsibility for killings, and paying someone to kill animals for your meal does not absolve you of their deaths.

### Christ as root, foundation and vine
Craig reads the recurring "root of the righteous" in Proverbs 12 (verses 3, 7, 12) through Revelation 22:16 ("I am the root and the offspring of David") and John 15:5 ("I am the vine. You are the branches"). He reads Luke 6:47-49 (the house built on rock) as the same theme: hearing and doing Jesus' words is the foundation. For Craig, righteousness "can only come through that faith in Christ," and the fruit that follows is evidence of that rootedness.

### Fruit of the mouth and of the Spirit: speaking for the voiceless
Craig takes "the fruit of his mouth" (Proverbs 12:14) with Luke 6:45 (the heart's treasure comes out in speech) and argues that Christians should align heart, speech and action with good. He cites Proverbs 31:8 ("open your mouth for the speechless") and says the clearest modern referent is animals in factory farms, laboratories, zoos and circuses. Galatians 5:22-23 supplies the list of Spirit-fruit that he sets alongside the vegan commitment to kindness, peace and joy. He presents being "vocal" for animals as multiplying the effect of one person's own veganism.

### Diet as God's original instruction; Daniel and Jonah as examples
Craig frames the plant-based diet as the diet God "said is very good" at creation, citing Genesis 1:30 (fruit and vegetation) and noting that Isaiah 11 pictures animals eating straw. In the Q&A he cites Daniel 1, in which Daniel and his companions eat vegetables and water and appear healthier than those eating the king's food, as a model of faithful refusal. He also uses Jonah's call to Nineveh as a model for persistent, non-coercive preaching: you deliver the message and leave the outcome to God.

### Isaiah 11 and the kingdom now
Craig's closing argument is that Isaiah 11:6-9 describes "the world God wants," and that believers should act on it now rather than waiting for Christ's return to fix creation. He says God will hold his people to what he commanded ("when I come back, I want to see you doing the things I commanded"), and that "the victory has already been won on Calvary" but its fruit is seen in the coming kingdom, which believers participate in "by doing his will."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"It's not a sin to eat meat because God gave us permission."** Lily's video concedes the point for argument's sake but insists that cruelty or neglect toward animals is sinful regardless, and that the production of animal products involves cruelty.
- **"'Humane' animal industries are kind."** Lily argues, drawing on Proverbs 12:10, that even the so-called compassionate acts of the wicked are still cruel.
- **"I've never killed an animal, so I'm not responsible."** Craig responds with the hitman analogy: paying others to kill animals for food makes you responsible for the killing.
- **"Jesus ate fish, so eating meat is fine."** In the Q&A, Craig says he does not need to win the sin argument to make the broader point that eating animals is still wicked and unrighteous.
- **"God will fix creation when he returns, so we need not act now."** Craig argues that God wants to find his people already doing his will, including in how they treat creatures.
