## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of the Creation Care Church "Friday Night Live" talk, asks whether Christians have a "collective blind spot" about how they treat animals, and he addresses the talk to Christians and to vegans or other non-Christians who care about animals. He argues that God's character is merciful and compassionate toward all creation, and that Christians should therefore extend that mercy to animals. He points to Psalm 145:8-9, 1 John 4:16 and Luke 6:36 for this, and then asks why most of the world, Christians included, kills or exploits animals.

His answer is spiritual. Citing 2 Corinthians 4:4, John 10:10 and John 8:44, he identifies "the god of this age" with Satan, who blinds believers and unbelievers through deception and by twisting scripture, such as the Genesis "dominion" passages. Christians are not immune, and Wescoe says many fall into this blind spot until God opens their eyes. The remedy he offers is turning to Christ, reading scripture in its light (Acts 26:18, Matthew 6:22, Ephesians 5:8), walking in the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23), and praying, especially for people who are "willfully" blind. He also says the full solution waits for the coming of Jesus' kingdom.

The second half is a live Q&A. Questions cover feeding vegan food to dogs and cats (Wescoe suggests commercial vegan pet foods, homemade recipes, and monitoring health), willful blindness, and how to respond to people who call themselves animal lovers but eat or raise animals for slaughter. Wescoe answers that the main goal is a change of heart, that imperfect effort should not produce despair, and that Christians should expect rejection for this teaching. The talk ends with a preview of the next talk (Psalm 34:14, "seek peace and do good") and a closing prayer.

## Key Teachings

### God's mercy extends to all creatures, so humans should imitate it
Wescoe reads Psalm 145:8-9 ("the lord is good to all and his tender mercies are over all his works") and argues that because God's mercy covers animals, it reveals who God cares about. He adds 1 John 4:16 ("God is love") and Luke 6:36 ("be merciful just as your father also is merciful") to argue that humans, made in God's image, should show the same mercy to animals. He treats this as a direct inference from the text, not a debated reading.

### Satan as the "god of this age" who blinds and deceives
Citing 2 Corinthians 4:4, Wescoe equates the "god of this age" with Satan, "the prince of darkness." He links this to John 10:10 ("the thief does not come except to steal, to kill, and to destroy") and John 8:44 (the devil as "a liar and the father of it"). In his reading, animal exploitation is the thief's work: "stealing their babies," "stealing their own lives," "stealing their freedom." Wescoe says Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, so Christians are not immune. (He gives no chapter for the temptation account.)

### Scripture twisted to justify harm
Wescoe says the enemy "twists scripture" to justify mistreatment of animals. His example is the claim that God gave humans "dominion." He answers that Genesis pairs dominion with eating plants and with a call to keep and protect creation, and he compares this deception to Eden. He also quotes Jesus' reply to the temptation from Deuteronomy ("you shall not test the Lord your God") as a model for seeing through such deceptions, and he invokes Matthew 10:16 ("wise as serpents but innocent as doves") in the Q&A, though he gives no verse reference there.

### Light, opened eyes and the fruit of the Spirit
Wescoe cites Acts 26:18 (opening eyes, turning from darkness to light), Luke 7:22 (the blind see, read spiritually as well as physically), Matthew 6:22 (the eye as the lamp of the body), and Ephesians 5:8 ("walk as children of light"). He says walking in this light means applying Galatians 5:22-23 (love, peace, kindness, self-control) to animals. He poses a series of self-examination questions: "Are they suffering as a result of my decisions?" "Am I instilling joy in them?" "Am I controlled by my appetites for fashion and for food?" He supports this with Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal") and 1 John 2:11 (the one who hates his brother walks in darkness).

### Animals as covenant family
Wescoe cites Hosea 2:18 and says the animals are "part of god's covenant family," calling them "our spiritual siblings at least" and "our friends." He says Genesis mentions covenant promises to animals "five times," though he gives no reference for the count. This is his main basis for treating animals as neighbors in the same way humans are.

### Pride, false teachers and the "Judas goat"
Citing Matthew 15:14 ("blind leaders of the blind"), Wescoe says false teachers who tell people God does not care about animals are themselves blinded. He ties animal abuse to pride, quoting the saying that "pride comes before the fall." In the Q&A, he gives the "Judas goat" anecdote (a goat used to lead sheep into a slaughterhouse) as an image of betrayal. He says that raising animals for sale to slaughter is still "betraying them," even when it avoids the violence of factory farming. The anecdote is his own illustration and he cites no source for it.

### Remedy: prayer, humility and the coming kingdom
Wescoe's practical answer to "willful blindness" is prayer, personal and in groups of two or three, citing Matthew 18:20 ("where two or three are gathered in my name," as a paraphrase, without a reference) and James 5:16 ("the prayer of a righteous person can avail much," also not referenced by chapter). He pairs this with humility (Colossians 3:12, meekness and tender mercies) and says the complete solution is future: death is "the final enemy" that will be taken away when Jesus establishes his kingdom. He says the problems "have already been undone on the cross" but will be fully realized later. He does not give a verse for the "final enemy" line.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The belief that God does not care about animals, and that humans are more important.** Wescoe says this is the enemy's deception and contrary to Psalm 145 and the nature of God, which extends mercy to all creation.
- **The belief that being saved makes a believer immune to deception.** Wescoe says Satan tempts and deceives Christians too, and that being saved "just makes him try even harder."
- **The belief that since perfection is impossible, effort does not matter.** Wescoe says believers should do their best to minimize cruelty, and that imperfection should not cause despair or paralysis.
