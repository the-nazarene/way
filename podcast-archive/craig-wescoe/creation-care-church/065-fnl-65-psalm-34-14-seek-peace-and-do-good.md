## Summary

This is a livestream Q&A from Creation Care Church, a Christian group focused on animals. Craig Wescoe asks how believers can "pursue peace and goodness" toward animals, using Psalm 34:14 ("depart from evil and do good. Seek peace and pursue it") as the anchor. He reads the verse as a structure: good versus evil, and peace that must be sought actively. He then builds the case from a chain of other passages, including Psalm 145:9, Micah 6:8, Genesis 1:28-31, Matthew 5:9, Jeremiah 29:13, Matthew 6:33, Proverbs 12:10, Romans 14:17 and Proverbs 3:31. His main claims are that God's goodness is mercy and compassion over all creation, that the Genesis 1 diet was plant-based, and that paying for animal products makes one complicit in violence.

Wescoe's most distinctive argument is textual. He says several English translations add "men" to Romans 12:18 and Hebrews 12:14, while the Greek word *pantone* means "all" or "every kind," so the text is not limited to humans. He also argues that peace in Matthew 5:9 is an active peacemaking rather than passive absence of conflict, and that Jesus' "sword" saying in Matthew 10:34-38 means a division between those who follow Christ and those who reject him, not a call to keep the peace. He presents these as his reading of scripture; he gives no critical-scholarly sources for the translation claim beyond the Greek word itself.

The talk closes with a question round in which Wescoe answers audience points on righteousness as faith-driven obedience (Abraham, Noah, Hebrews 11), on God's "anger" as the self-destructive path of sin (citing James 1 and mentioning Greg Boyd), and on patience toward people who are deceived. He announces a book club on Sarah Withrow King's *Veganelical* and previews the next talk on John 1:14. Attribution of the position is to Wescoe throughout; the talk is a pastoral teaching, not a historical argument.

## Key Teachings

### Goodness as mercy and justice over all creation

Wescoe takes Psalm 145:9 ("the lord is good to all and his tender mercies are his compassion is over all his works") to define God's goodness, then reads Micah 6:8 (do justly, love mercy, walk humbly) as the practical form. He applies these to animals by asking whether people are killing innocent creatures, torturing them, or capturing them against their will. He then ties this to the Genesis 1:28-31 account, arguing that the "very good" creation included a plant-based diet for animals too ("there's no killing whether for food or for any other purpose"). Note: the Genesis reading is Wescoe's own; he does not discuss alternative readings of "dominion" or the post-flood permission to eat meat.

### Peace as active peacemaking

Citing James 3:17 (the wisdom from above is "peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy") and Galatians 5:22-23 (fruit of the Spirit), Wescoe argues that peace is a quality of God's wisdom. On Matthew 5:9 ("blessed are the peacemakers"), he says peace "isn't the kind of thing that's passive" and is a positive act of establishing peace. He reads Matthew 10:34-38 as Jesus saying he came not to keep everyone comfortable but to divide believers from unbelievers, so that "peace" does not mean tolerating evil or avoiding religious and political disagreement. In the Q&A he links this to Paul's letters on church unity, saying that unity should be pursued within goodness, while evil is removed.

### Seeking first the kingdom and righteousness

Wescoe reads "seek" as wholehearted pursuit, citing Jeremiah 29:13 ("you will seek me... with all your heart") and Matthew 7:7. He defines the kingdom of God as the world "the way god designed it to be," which he links to Eden. He takes "righteousness" to include regard for animal life, citing Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal"). In the Q&A he defines righteousness as "rightly related" to God, others, animals and the earth, achieved through faith and obedience, with Noah and Abraham as examples. He reads Romans 14:17 as saying the kingdom is "not food and drink," and argues that believers should not let appetite override the peace and righteousness of the Spirit. The Romans 14 discussion is said to have been covered in an earlier live talk.

### The translation point: "all," not "all men"

This is the most specific textual claim. Wescoe says that Romans 12:18 ("live peaceably with all") is often rendered "all men," and that the Greek *pantone* means "all or the whole or any or every kind," with nothing restricting it to humans. He says the same applies to Hebrews 12:14 in the New King James Version, and he suggests that translators sometimes render the same word differently for humans and animals "where in the text, there is none." He does not cite a Greek lexicon or manuscript evidence by name; the claim rests on his reading of the Greek word and the absence of "men" in the original.

### Violence, complicity and diet

Wescoe reads peace as the opposite of violence, and argues from Psalm 11:5 ("the one who loves violence, his soul hates") that God opposes violence, including violence paid for by others. He says that buying animal products means "paying somebody to hurt animals," so one should stop even if one personally would never harm an animal. He cites Proverbs 3:31 ("do not envy the violent and choose none of his ways") and describes factory-farm conditions, battery cages and laboratory use as "the ways of the violent." He says marketing that shows "happy animals" is deceptive, and he applies Matthew 10:16 (wise as serpents, innocent as doves) to call for exposing the deception. He concludes with a direct recommendation: "Go vegan."

### Sin, character development and God's "anger"

Responding to audience questions, Wescoe reads God's testing and anger as a feature of moral growth and consequence. He cites Psalm 11:5 ("the lord tests the righteous") and David's prayer "test my heart," and presents Abraham as a figure whose faith developed over time (Genesis 15:6, Genesis 17-18, Genesis 22, Hebrews 11). He reads the Bible's language of divine wrath as a way ancient peoples described the self-destructive path of sin, drawing on James 1 ("evil desire... produces the sin which produces the death"). He says Greg Boyd reads it similarly. He tells a questioner that people should be patient and gentle with those who are deceived, reserving harsh language for those who repeatedly reject Christ, and says Satan is the source of violence and deception.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Peace is the absence of conflict or a passive tolerance.** Wescoe says peace in Matthew 5:9 is active peacemaking, and that Matthew 10:34-38 rules out the "let's all get along" reading.
- **Bible translations are neutral about animals.** Wescoe says some translations add "men" to Romans 12:18 and Hebrews 12:14 and create a human-animal distinction the Greek does not contain.
- **God's anger means God personally punishes with death.** He reads the "wrath" language as describing the self-destructive path of sin, citing James 1.
- **Eating animal products is acceptable if one does not personally kill animals.** He says buying animal products pays others to commit violence, so it is still complicity.
- **Being a good person or having faith is enough.** Wescoe says believers should keep growing in character, citing Abraham's development and David's prayer "test my heart."
- **Dominion over animals implies permission to kill them.** He reads Genesis 1:28-31 as giving humans dominion but a plant-based diet, so there is "no killing."
