## Summary

Craig asks what Proverbs 31:8 ("open your mouth for the speechless and the cause of all who are appointed to die") means for believers, and whether it extends to animals in agriculture. He argues that it does. He reads "appointed to die" as including farmed animals, citing the practice of stamping a "kill date" on animals' ears. He then builds a chain of verses (Ephesians 5:11, 1 Peter 3:15-16, Acts 18:9, Ephesians 4:15, James 1:19, Isaiah 1:17, Colossians 4:6, 1 John 3:18, Zechariah 8:16, John 2:16-17) to argue that Scripture commands believers to expose oppression, speak the truth in love, and act on that speech. He defines "voiceless" as "powerless" rather than "without vocal cords," and argues that the Jesus of the temple overturned animal sales and, through the cross, ended animal sacrifice.

The second half is a Q&A with live-chat questions. Craig answers on Romans 14 (he says it concerns idol food, not veganism, but that its principle of not causing a brother distress still applies), on the "two freedoms" (freedom from oppression versus freedom to oppress), on timing and prudence when speaking to someone who controls your housing, on Earthling Ed and his own channel, and on slaughterhouse workers and the claim that even people who do not care about animals should stop paying for factory farming. He ends with announcements (no live talk the following week, a book club, a Facebook fellowship) and a closing prayer. Most of the argument rests on his own readings of verses, and many factual claims (the ear tags, PTSD rates, the Sadducees' allegiance to Rome, the Greek word meanings he was asked about) are asserted without sources in the talk.

## Key Teachings

### 1. "Appointed to die" includes farmed animals
Craig reads Proverbs 31:8-9 as applying to people on death row or the oppressed, and then extends it to animals in agriculture. He says animals are sometimes stamped with a "kill date" on the ear at birth. He takes God's will as established in Eden and concludes that speaking for animals "appointed to die" fits the verse. He presents this as the main application, rather than as one of several readings.

### 2. Expose the works of darkness (Ephesians 5:11; Isaiah 1:17)
Ephesians 5:11 ("have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather expose them") is read as an instruction to expose rather than join in what conflicts with God's will. Craig cites undercover footage from slaughterhouses and factory farms, and "ag-gag" laws as an example of societies punishing exposure of abuse more than the abuse itself. Isaiah 1:17 ("seek justice, reprove the oppressor, defend the fatherless, plead for the widow") is applied to factory farms and to animals in shelters, laboratories, and entertainment, including captive orcas.

### 3. Bold speech, gentle manner (1 Peter 3:15-16; Acts 18:9; Romans 12:2; Ephesians 4:15; Colossians 4:6)
Craig asks listeners to be able to "give a defense" of their faith and of animal advocacy, and to do so with "meekness and fear" and "deep respect." Acts 18:9 ("do not be afraid, but speak, and do not keep silent") is read as a general mandate to speak despite fear of ridicule, including the label "preachy vegan." Romans 12:2 is cited as the basis for not conforming to the world. Ephesians 4:15 ("speaking the truth in love") is used to argue that harsh language (such as Jesus's "brood of vipers") fits only people one has long engaged with, and that gentleness should be the default.

### 4. Listen first, then tailor the message (James 1:19; 1 Corinthians 9)
Craig says that the aim is to be "quick to listen" and to answer what a person actually believes. He gives an example of a person who cites Genesis 1 or Genesis 9 to defend eating animals. He points to Paul's "everything to everyone" (his words, which he attributes to Paul without giving a verse) as applying to teaching care for creation.

### 5. "Voiceless" means powerless, and "peace" means exposing oppression (Zechariah 8:16; Matthew 10)
Craig argues that the "voiceless" are the powerless, not those lacking vocal cords. Animals "cry out," but "all those cries fall sort of on ears that are not listening," so people must speak for them. He uses the example of disabled residents asking a city council for accessible sidewalks. He reads Zechariah 8:16 ("speak each man the truth to his neighbor... give judgement in your gates for truth, justice, and peace") as rejecting a peace that avoids rocking the boat. He ties this to Jesus's saying that he came "to bring a sword" (no verse given; the saying is in Matthew 10).

### 6. The temple act and the end of animal sacrifice (John 2:16-17; Psalm 69:9)
Craig reads John 2:16-17 as Jesus's bold act against the trade in animals sold for sacrifice, and says that the Sadducees controlled the temple and were tied to Rome. He then argues that Jesus "ended the system of animal sacrifices" through his high-priesthood "of the priesthood of Melchizedek" (no verse is given) and his once-for-all sacrifice on the cross. He says other readings of the passage exist but that this one "makes the most sense" to him.

### 7. Romans 14 applied to meat (Romans 14:3, 14:15, 14:21; Romans 14 live-talk reference)
Responding to a question about a meat-eater who cited Romans 14 as abolishing food limits, Craig argues:
- Romans 14 concerns food offered to idols, not veganism.
- "All things were made clean by Jesus" is, in his view, a translator's insertion, and Mark 7-type handwashing is the real context (he gives no verse).
- Romans 14:15 says that if food grieves a brother, one is "no longer walking in love." Romans 14:21 says it is good "neither to eat meat nor drink wine" if it causes a brother to stumble.
He concludes that even if the principle is applied to animal-eating, the answer is the same: stop. He says he has a full live talk on Romans 14.

### 8. Diet and the Eden ideal (Genesis 1:29; Daniel 1; Revelation 5:13)
Craig uses Genesis 1:29 ("eat the fruit of the trees and the green plants of the ground") as God's ideal diet, and calls it a "whole food plant-based diet." He cites Daniel 1 (vegetables and water, better health) for a Veganuary speech. He compares the law's allowance for divorce, as explained by a commenter, to a concession. He says the Eden ideal should be the goal, and cites Revelation 5:13 to picture all creatures praising God together.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Romans 14 abolished all food limits."** Craig says Romans 14 concerns food offered to idols and that the "all things were made clean" line is a translator's insertion. He reads Romans 14:15 and 14:21 as limiting food that causes a brother distress.
- **"Genesis 9 gave humans permission to eat animals."** Craig answers by pointing to Genesis 1:29 as God's original provision, and treats the Eden diet as the ideal to strive for.
- **"Jesus cleansed the temple only over money."** Craig argues that the act was a bold rejection of animal sacrifice itself, and that it, along with the cross, ended the system of animal sacrifice.
- **"Voiceless means animals can't speak."** Craig says animals do cry out, but that "voiceless" means powerless, because their cries are not heard without human advocates.
- **"Peace means avoiding conflict."** Craig, reading Zechariah 8:16 and the "sword" saying, argues that real peace requires exposing oppression.
- **"Christians ignore animals."** Craig says other churches "overlook" the animal component, and that Creation Care Church is intended to highlight it.
