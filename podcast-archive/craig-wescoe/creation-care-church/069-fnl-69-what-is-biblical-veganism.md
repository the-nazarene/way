## Key Teachings

### Faith in God, not in systems
Wescoe connects Philippians 4:13 ("I can do all things through Christ who gives me strength") to the work of animal advocacy. He says secular movements often "trust in like political systems or politicians or changing laws," which "could be part of the solution but ultimately, that's not where we put our faith." He argues that if God "created the whole world and sustains the whole world," then God's power is the only power believers need (about 11:01). He describes the believer as an instrument: "I just allow god to work through me" (about 11:01-12:32).

### The original vegan diet and a non-predatory creation
Wescoe reads Genesis 1:29-30: God gives "every herb that yields seed" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed" to humans, and "every green herb for food" to every beast and bird. He concludes that "not only were the people designed to be vegan... but also all the animals were designed to be these herbivores" (about 16:45). He says this answers a common secular argument that animals are built to kill (pointing to teeth and intestines). He also contrasts Genesis 1:1, "God created the heavens and the earth," with an unnamed naturalistic account of molecules and single-celled organisms, which he calls "a very different story" (about 18:51).

### Restoration and the peaceable kingdom
Wescoe reads Acts 3:21 as a promise that God will "restore" all things to the good state of the beginning. He then quotes Isaiah 11 ("shall eat straw like the ox"; the cobra and the child; "they shall not hurt nor destroy") as God's vision for the world and his kingdom, adding that "it's not a matter of, of, if it will, it's a matter just of when it will" (about 14:02). He says Isaiah 2:4 (swords into plowshares) and Isaiah 11 include animals in the promise of peace. He ties the Lord's Prayer ("your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven") to this picture (about 01:52, 39:03).

### Dominion as stewardship, not equality
Wescoe distinguishes biblical stewardship from a secular claim that animals must be treated as moral equals. He says he would not push back against treating animals as equals, but "that's not a necessary thing." He grounds care in Genesis 1:26 (dominion over animals) and in a "how much more" argument: if God cares for animals, then humans, who are more valuable, can trust God's care too (about 20:22-22:37). He compares the relationship to the household-codes model of parents and children, with Christ's dominion over the church as the model for "good shepherds" (about 47:55-50:05). The passage he is paraphrasing for this household analogy is not named in the captions.

### Repentance, not vindication
Wescoe distinguishes the biblical attitude from a secular one. When a hunter dies in an accident and secular vegans celebrate, he says: "That's not the biblical vegan attitude." He says he rejoices when harmers "repent and stop harming the animals," summarized as "Not the demise of a hunter but rather a hunter who stops hunting" (about 28:55-29:49).

### Diet, health and protein
Wescoe reads Daniel 1:12 and 1:20, in which Daniel and his companions eat only vegetables and water and are found "ten times better" than the king's magicians. He argues that a diet contrary to God's design is not good biblical advice. When people say "you need more protein," he asks how much protein they eat each day, implying the concern is overstated. A viewer named Shirley adds that vegan athletes in bodybuilding, weightlifting, triathlon and tennis perform well, and Wescoe agrees that athletes gain from it (about 42:14-53:29).

### Conscience and inclusion in the church
Wescoe uses a passage he thinks is in 1 Corinthians chapter 10 (he says "I think it's chapter ten"; the passage is about food offered to idols) to argue that believers should not exclude others or act against their conscience. He applies the principle to animal treatment: failing to care about others' beliefs, or about God's design for animals, is the same kind of failure (about 35:55).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That animals are naturally carnivorous and "supposed to be killing each other."** Wescoe says Genesis 1:29-30 shows all animals were given green herbs, so predatory anatomy does not reflect original design.
- **That Christians have Scripture on their side in defending meat-eating.** Wescoe says the first Google results of defenses of meat-eating ("The earth is the lord so the animals are ours to use") reflect "serving the desires of the flesh instead of the very good will of God."
- **That vegan diets are unhealthy or lack protein.** He asks how much protein someone actually eats, and cites athletes who thrive on plant-based diets and Daniel 1.
- **That biblical veganism requires treating animals as moral equals.** He says equality is not necessary and that the biblical basis is stewardship under God's dominion.
- **That biblical veganism celebrates the punishment of those who harm animals.** He says the goal is repentance, not vindication.
