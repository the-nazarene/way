## Summary

Craig, host of the Creation Care Church Friday Night Live stream, asks how the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13) relates to animals. He reads the prayer line by line and pairs each line with an Old and New Testament passage. He argues that the prayer's petitions (God's name, the kingdom, God's will, daily bread, forgiveness, deliverance from evil) echo prophecies in which animals are included, and that the Genesis diet and a nonviolent, plant-based life are the ideal the prayer points toward.

The method is devotional and scriptural. Craig does not use manuscript, archaeological or historical-critical evidence. He treats the Bible as a unified text, so Genesis, Isaiah, Daniel, Romans and Hebrews are read as speaking to one another. The talk is partly a live Q&A: he answers viewer questions about "dominion" in Genesis 1:28, animals in heaven, the claim that animal sacrifice will resume (from Ezekiel 40-48), and how to respond to mockery. He ends with a closing prayer and previews next week's topic, the sparrows in Matthew.

Because the talk is one speaker's reading, each position below is Craig's, not an established interpretation.

### Key Teachings

### "Our Father" and "all flesh": God as creator of animals too
Craig reads "Our father in heaven" with Jeremiah 32:27, "the God of all flesh." He says the Hebrew *kol basar* ("all flesh") includes animals, unlike *kol adam* ("all humanity"), which he says covers only humans. He cites the flood account as an example of *basar* covering animals. From this he concludes that God is addressed as Father by all creatures.

### Kingdom, will, and the peaceable kingdom of Isaiah 11
For "your kingdom come" he cites Revelation 21:3-4 (no more death, crying or pain), which he reads as undoing the death that came with the Fall. For "your will be done on earth as it is in heaven," he cites Hosea 2:18 (a covenant with "the beasts of the field" and the banishing of bow, sword and battle) and Isaiah 2:4 (swords into plowshares). He calls Isaiah 11:6-9, the wolf lying with the lamb and the lion eating straw, "one of my favorite verses," and argues that the animals in it are part of the kingdom's promise.

### "Daily bread" and the Genesis diet
Craig reads "give us this day our daily bread" alongside Genesis 1:29-31, where humans and animals are given plants and fruit for food. He adds Genesis 2:15 (tend and keep the garden) and John 6-style language about Jesus as "the bread of life" (he calls Jesus "the bread of life" but gives no chapter). He describes the Genesis diet as "a non-violent plant-based diet," and he uses Daniel 1:8-20 (Daniel and his companions choosing vegetables and water and being found "ten times better" in wisdom) as a model for faithful eating.

### Forgiveness, healing of the land, and the parable of the unforgiving servant
Craig links "forgive us our debts" to 2 Chronicles 7:14 (humble yourselves, turn from wicked ways, and God will forgive and heal the land). He reads the healing as a reversal of the curse in Genesis, so that the land stops producing thorns and thistles. He reads the parable in Matthew 18 (the servant forgiven a huge debt who refuses to forgive a small one) as the reason forgiveness must extend to others. He also says animals rescued from abuse can show forgiveness quickly, and that this is part of God's kingdom.

### "Deliver us from the evil one": creation's deliverance
For "do not lead us into temptation" and "deliver us from the evil one," Craig cites Romans 8:19-21 (creation "eagerly waits" and will be "delivered from the bondage of corruption") and James 1:14-15 (desire leads to sin, sin to death). He presents the temptation of Jesus (Matthew 4, with quotations from Deuteronomy) as an example of resisting the adversary by quoting Scripture. He says the deliverance applies to animals as well as humans.

### Dominion reinterpreted
In the Q&A, responding to a family debate, Craig argues that Genesis 1:28 ("have dominion") is immediately qualified by 1:29 (the plant diet), so dominion cannot be a license to kill. He suggests that dominion should be read as Christ-like service, citing Jesus's saying that he came "not to be served but to serve."

### Animals in heaven: Ecclesiastes 3:19 and Balaam's donkey
Asked whether slaughtered animals will be in heaven, Craig says Isaiah 11 shows animals will be present. He argues the promise to animals would mean little if they were not included in the resurrection hope. He cites Ecclesiastes 3:19 ("man has no advantage over beasts," all have "one breath") and Balaam's donkey in Numbers 22, whom he calls a Christ-like figure who returns good for evil. He concludes it is hard to imagine the donkey excluded, and that many other animals would then be included too.

### Sacrifice ended by Christ; the Ezekiel objection
Asked whether animal sacrifice will return (from Ezekiel 40 to the end), Craig gives three replies. First, Revelation's new Jerusalem has no temple, so there is no temple sacrifice. Second, Ezekiel's temple prophecies belong to the period before the second temple, which was later destroyed. Third, Hebrews 9:26, 10:10 and 10:4 teach that Christ's single offering ended the sacrificial system, and that the blood of bulls and goats "could not take away sins." He says the "spiritual sacrifices" of praise, prayer, good deeds and sharing replace animal sacrifice.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Genesis 1:28 gives humans permission to kill and eat animals.** Craig says the very next verses (1:29-30) give a plant diet, so dominion cannot mean license to kill.
- **The Lord's Prayer concerns only humans.** Craig argues that "all flesh," the peaceable kingdom and creation's deliverance in Romans 8 show animals are included.
- **Animal sacrifice will return in the future kingdom (Ezekiel 40-48).** Craig says the new Jerusalem has no temple, Ezekiel belongs to a past period, and Hebrews teaches that Christ's sacrifice ended the system.
- **Animals will not be in heaven.** Craig argues they will be, citing Isaiah 11, Ecclesiastes 3:19 and Balaam's donkey.
