## Key Teachings

### The Lamb typology: Isaiah 53:7 through Revelation, John and Paul
Craig treats Isaiah 53:7 as a prophecy of the Messiah and then assembles a chain of identifications: Revelation 13:8 (the "Lamb slain from the foundation of the world," linked to the Book of Life), John 1:29 (John the Baptist's "Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world"), and 1 Corinthians 5:7 (Christ "our Passover was sacrificed for us"). He takes these as converging on one figure. He does not discuss the Greek or the date or authorship of any of these texts.

### Passover, Exodus 12, and ending the ritual slaughter
Craig reads Exodus 12:1-7 (a male lamb without blemish, killed at twilight, blood on the doorposts) as the background. He notes a debate about whether the killing was original to the ritual or added later, and describes a reading in which the week of unleavened bread is the "path of righteousness" and the lamb-killing is the "path of unrighteousness" that God did not intend. On this reading Jesus's death "ends" the lamb ritual. Craig says he could give "probably a two-hour talk" on the subject and does not develop the alternatives. The argument is that Jesus, as Passover Lamb, brings the practice to an end so that no more lambs need to be slaughtered.

### Timing: the crucifixion on 14 Nisan and resurrection as first fruits
Craig maps the events onto the biblical calendar. He says the crucifixion falls on 14 Nisan (which many Christians call Good Friday), the burial follows, and the resurrection is on the Sunday of the week of unleavened bread, "the day of first fruits." He says Jesus "created" the calendar, so his life followed it. The dating and calendar claims are asserted from the Hebrew calendar as he understands it. He cites no sources for the calendar or for the claim that the crucifixion fell on that date.

### Eating flesh and drinking blood: communion as symbol
Craig takes John 6:56 ("he who eats my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me") and reads it through Matthew 26:26-28. He says Jesus is not calling for literal cannibalism. The bread and cup are the Lord's Supper, a "symbolic significance" that replaces the eating of the sacrificed lamb. He suggests that Christians who celebrate the Passover lamb by eating lamb are working against what Jesus instituted.

### The Passion as solidarity with slaughtered animals
Craig reads Mark 15:16-20 and 15:29-32 (the soldiers' mockery and the crowd's taunts) and draws a comparison to factory farms and slaughterhouses, where animals are treated with "absolutely no respect" and "mocked." He says Jesus "understands what Factory farmed animals and slaughtered animals go through because he went through the same thing." He links this to Psalm 22, which Matthew 27:46 has Jesus quote. Craig argues that the quotation identifies Jesus with the Messiah of Psalm 22 (the taunts in 22:7-8, the piercing in 22:16, the garments in 22:18), and that Jesus's cry is not a sign that God had turned away. He cites Mark 15:24 for the lots cast over his garments.

### Without blemish and without sin: lamb and high priest
Craig reads 1 Peter 2:21-22 ("committed no sin nor was guile... found in his mouth") as meeting the requirement for a sacrificial animal "without blemish." He adds Hebrews 4:15 ("without sin") and says Jesus is both the lamb and the high priest who offers it, "according to the order of Melchizedek." He ties this to the believer's ongoing resistance to temptation, including temptation to eat lamb at this season. He also cites 1 John 1:7 for the blood cleansing from all sin.

### Neighbors, mercy, and the whole creation
Craig reads Luke 10:25-37 and Leviticus 19:18 (love your neighbor as yourself) and says the question "who is my neighbor" should include animals. He says Genesis 1 shows God creating and blessing animals and humans together, so that both are designed to be neighbors. In his reading, the Samaritan's mercy is the "Hallmark quality of neighborliness," and the same mercy should be extended to animal neighbors. He cites Isaiah 11:6-9 (the peaceable kingdom), Revelation 5:13 (every creature in heaven, earth and sea praising the Lamb), and an Isaiah and Jeremiah line ("All Flesh will come to worship before me") to argue that the gospel covers all creation. Craig says the animals are included in the Lord's Prayer, "your will be done... on Earth as it is in heaven."

His correction is that the Passover lamb ritual reflects a deviation from God's ideal, and that Jesus ends the ritual.
- **Eating Jesus's flesh and drinking his blood means literal cannibalism.** Craig says Jesus is speaking symbolically and that the Lord's Supper is the meaning.
- **Jesus's cry on the cross means God abandoned him.** Craig says the quotation of Psalm 22 signals that Jesus is the Messiah the psalm describes.
- **Eating lamb at Easter honours the Lamb of God.** Craig argues that this contradicts what Jesus's death was meant to end.
- **Animals are not our neighbors.** Craig argues from Genesis 1 and Luke 10 that animals are included in "neighbor."
