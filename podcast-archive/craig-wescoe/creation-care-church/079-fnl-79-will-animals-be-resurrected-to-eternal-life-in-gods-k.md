## Summary

Craig Wescoe asks whether animals will be resurrected to eternal life in God's kingdom. The talk is a live Friday stream from his church group, recorded near Good Friday and Easter. It opens with a communion service (Genesis 14:18, 1 Corinthians 11:24-25, Matthew 6:9-13), then moves to the main question. Craig answers yes and argues the case mostly from scripture quoted in English with occasional appeals to Hebrew and Greek words.

His main lines of evidence are: animals are blessed in Genesis 1 before humans and so belong to God's original plan, which he says the kingdom restores; Isaiah 11:6-9 describes a peaceable world that includes animals; Ecclesiastes 3:19 and the shared "breath" language of Genesis 2 put humans and animals on the same footing; Romans 8:18-23 says all creation groans awaiting liberation; God makes covenant promises to animals in Genesis 9 and Hosea 2:18; and passages such as Revelation 5:13, Isaiah 66:23, Jeremiah 32:27 and Joel 2:28 use "all flesh" or "every creature" to include animals in worship and in the outpouring of the Spirit. His most emphatic example is Balaam's donkey (Numbers 22:28-33), which he reads as an image of Christlike love for enemies. He concludes that if the donkey is in the kingdom, so are other animals.

The talk ends with a Q&A that takes questions from chat, a closing prayer, and an invitation to a Discord fellowship. In the Q&A Craig argues that translations sometimes obscure the animal references, asks listeners to check the original languages on Bible Hub and Strong's, and says animals show love and that people who eat animals should reconsider that practice in light of this hope. These are his positions, offered to his congregation, not claims established by independent evidence.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Animals are in the original creation blessing
Craig reads Genesis 1:20-28 and notes that God blesses the sea creatures and birds before blessing humanity. From this he argues that animals were part of God's plan "from the beginning." His inference is that the kingdom restores that original plan, so "if they're in the beginning, it stands to reason that they would also be in the end."

### 2. The restored peaceable world (Isaiah 11)
He reads Isaiah 11:6-9 (wolf with lamb, a little child leading them, no hurting or destroying). He links this to Genesis 1:29-30, where he says animals eat green herbs, and argues that the kingdom is a return to a world with no sin, death, fear or violence. He presents Isaiah 11 as the picture of the future in which animals are included.

### 3. Shared "breath" and "living soul" (Ecclesiastes 3:19; Genesis 2)
Responding to the idea that human and animal spirits go different directions, Craig reads Ecclesiastes 3:19 ("Man has no advantage over beasts") and argues that humans and animals receive the same ruach (breath) and become a nefesh chayah (living soul). He says Genesis uses the same terms for both. In the Q&A he says some translations render these terms differently for animals and humans, and that this is a translation choice that can serve an agenda.

### 4. Creation groaning and liberation (Romans 8:18-23)
Craig reads Romans 8:18-23 and argues that "all creation" groans and waits for the revealing of the children of God. He says animals were "subjected to corruption" through no fault of their own, and that they share the hope of deliverance. This is the passage he calls the first New Testament support for animals' hope.

### 5. God's covenant with animals (Genesis 9; Hosea 2:18)
He says the covenant language in Genesis 9:15 is repeated about five times in Genesis 9 and includes "every living creature," so animals are recipients of God's promises. He reads Hosea 2:18 (covenant with the beasts of the field, birds and creeping things) as a parallel to Isaiah 11. He argues that if the promise of eternal life is the most significant promise, then a covenant that omits animals would make little sense.

### 6. Praise, love and the "all flesh" passages (Revelation 5:13; Isaiah 66:23; Jeremiah 32:27; Joel 2:28; 1 Corinthians 2:9)
- Revelation 5:13: he reads "every creature" in heaven, on earth, under the earth and in the sea praising the Lamb, and argues animals can praise God and so have the capacity for love.
- He says the Hebrew "all flesh" (basar) includes animals, and reads Isaiah 66:23 and Jeremiah 32:27 this way.
- Joel 2:28 (pouring out the Spirit on "all flesh") is read as including animals.
- 1 Corinthians 2:9 quotes Isaiah 64:4. Craig notes that Isaiah has "wait for him" where Paul has "love him," and says the group God prepares things for is those who love or wait on him, and that animals can do this.
- Ephesians 1:13 (sealed with the Spirit of promise) and Acts 24:15 (resurrection of both the just and the unjust) are used as further support for the hope of resurrection.

### 7. Balaam's donkey as an example of Christlike love (Numbers 22:28-33; Romans 12:21)
This is what Craig calls "the best example." He reads the donkey's refusal to pass the angel, the three beatings, and her question "what have I done to you?" He argues the donkey saved Balaam's life and so overcame evil with good (Romans 12:21), which is the love Jesus calls his followers to. He concludes that if the donkey is raised, the door is open for other animals, "all through Christ" (John 14:6). He says the donkey's question is what the animals would say collectively if they could speak.

### 8. The "language of love" and the universal call (Acts 2:4; Galatians 5:22-23; 1 John 4:8, 16; Mark 16:15)
Craig reads Pentecost as a "universal language" understood by all listeners, and argues the true language is love, not supernatural speech. He cites Galatians 5:22-23 (the fruit of the Spirit) and 1 John 4:8, 16 (God is love) and says humans and animals alike understand acts of rescue and feeding. He reads Mark 16:15 ("every creature") to argue that the gospel and the call to lead the animals in peace apply to all creation.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Animals have no place in God's plan or in the afterlife.** Craig argues they were part of creation from Genesis 1 and are included in the restoration in Isaiah 11 and Romans 8, so they are in the kingdom.
- **Only humans can love or praise God.** He argues from Revelation 5:13, Psalm 150 and the Balaam's donkey episode that animals show love and praise; he says that people who claim animals act only from instinct are explaining away evidence.
- **"All flesh" or "all mankind" means only humanity.** He says the Hebrew basar means all humans and animals, and that translations often obscure this (for example, "all mankind" in one viewer's Bible).
- **Animals are products or food, not creatures God blesses.** He argues against treating animals as commodities, says "livestock" is a wrong rendering of Genesis 1 cattle, and calls on listeners not to see them as food.
