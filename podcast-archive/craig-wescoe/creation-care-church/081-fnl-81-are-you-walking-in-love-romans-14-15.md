## Summary

Craig Wescoe's live talk asks whether a Christian who eats animal products is "walking in love," using Romans 14:15 ("if your brother is grieved because of your food, you are no longer walking in love"). He treats this as the central question and argues that the vegan concern for animals and the Christian duty of love converge. Wescoe presents the Romans passage as the conclusion of Paul's reasoning on food, then links it to 1 Corinthians 8:13, where Paul says he "will never eat meat again" if it makes a brother stumble. He argues Paul's example is not limited to eating in front of a vegan: "I will never eat meat again, period."

His supporting evidence runs in three layers. First, he cites Jesus and Paul on love as the mark of discipleship (John 13:34-35, 1 Corinthians 13:2, Matthew 5:23-24). Second, he argues from Old Testament texts that God's heart breaks for suffering animals and that God intended a plant-based diet (Psalm 34:18, Zechariah 11:5, Proverbs 12:10, Genesis 1:29). Third, he reads the Numbers 11 quail episode as a warning against craving meat, and he asserts without citation that animal products are a major cause of modern disease. He attributes the claim that the onus for change lies on the meat-eater to Paul, not to himself.

The talk ends with a live question-and-answer segment in which Wescoe responds to chat comments about being excluded at church potlucks and social gatherings. He advises listeners to confront churches with Paul's passage, to model humility (Luke 18:10-14), and to expect transformation. He closes with prayer and previews the next talk, on joy in caring for creation (John 15:11). Throughout, the talk presents its positions as Wescoe's reading of scripture, and it does not engage scholarly work on the Pauline passages.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Eating that grieves a brother is not "walking in love"
Wescoe's central move is that Romans 14:15 makes the eater responsible for the effect of food choices on another believer. He says the onus is on the one doing the thing, not on the person grieved by it, and rejects the common reply that God granted permission to eat animals (he mentions Genesis 9 as the usual text, without quoting it). He treats the passage as applying to churches that exclude a vegan member, and he says such churches are "not walking in love."

### 2. Paul's example in 1 Corinthians 8:13
He reads 1 Corinthians 8:13 as Paul giving himself as a model: "I will never eat meat again, lest I make my brother stumble." Wescoe argues this extends beyond eating in front of a vegan, because "we know what people are doing whether it's in front of us or not," and the grief comes from the effect on God's creatures. The talk does not discuss what kind of meat or food situation the surrounding chapter addresses; it reads the verse as a general rule.

### 3. Love as the mark of discipleship
Wescoe lines up three texts: 1 Corinthians 13:2 ("if I have not love, I am nothing"), John 13:34-35 (love as the sign that people will know you are Jesus' disciples), and Matthew 5:23-24 (leave the altar and be reconciled first). He argues that if one's food causes disunity, one is missing "the hallmark" of discipleship, and that this alone should move a believer to change even if they are not persuaded by the animal-welfare arguments.

### 4. God's heart breaks for suffering animals
He describes factory farming, dairy, and laboratory cruelty, and argues that a grieved heart toward animals is a God-given disposition, illustrated by children who want to hug animals. He reads Psalm 34:18 (God is near to the brokenhearted) alongside Matthew 5:3 ("poor in spirit") and Zechariah 11:5, arguing that the shepherds who sell sheep for slaughter "do not pity them" and that Jesus' line about serving God and money applies to animal agriculture. He also reads Proverbs 12:10 (the righteous regard the life of their animal) and Luke 6:36 (be merciful) as instructions about how to treat animals.

### 5. Genesis 1:29 as the "very good" diet
Wescoe reads Genesis 1:29 as God giving "every herb that yields seed" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed" as food, and calls this the "very good diet" from the beginning. He presents trusting that diet as the right response to God's instruction.

### 6. The kingdom is not food and drink; the belly is not God
Using Romans 14:17 ("the kingdom of god is not food and drink but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit"), Philippians 3:19 ("whose god is their belly"), and Galatians 5:13 and 5:24, he argues that appetite for animal flesh is a rival to God and that the fruit of the Spirit (peace, joy) excludes violence toward animals. He says "if we're chopping up animals to eat their flesh, that's violence, that's not peace." He does not consider how Paul's own use of "flesh" in Galatians might be read in its original setting, and he applies it directly to diet.

### 7. Numbers 11 as a warning, and the church as the place of change
He reads Numbers 11 (the craving for meat, the quail, and the plague at Kibroth-hattaavah, "grave of craving") as God's judgment on a diet of meat. He then states that animal products are "probably the greatest cause" of diseases such as strokes and heart disease. This is an unsourced claim in the transcript. He concludes that a single vegan in a church should be enough to make the whole congregation go vegan, and he uses the Jonah narrative (Nineveh repented, and God relented) to argue that churches can change if confronted with Scripture. He also cites 1 Corinthians 10:23 ("all things are lawful... but not all things edify") to argue against looking for scriptural loopholes such as the one he attributes to people who "search the scriptures" (John 5:39).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"God gave us permission to eat animals (Genesis 9)."** Wescoe's correction: Genesis 1:29 gives a plant-based "very good diet," and God's warnings in Numbers 11 undercut craving for meat.
- **"Paul's example applies only when a vegan is present."** His correction: Paul says "I will never eat meat again, period," and the grief is about the animals and the effect of the eater's choices, whether or not they are seen.
- **"Vegans are the ones who make church fellowship hard."** His correction: the church that excludes vegans is the one not walking in love, and the vegan is not at fault for feeling alone.
- **"There are no vegans in my church, so I'm not causing anyone to stumble."** He argues the absence of vegans is itself a sign that the church excludes them ("Vegans are what? Two to 3 percent of the population").
- **"Jesus declared all foods clean, so animal eating is fine."** In the Q&A, Wescoe says "the answer is absolutely not," says Jesus was talking about washing hands, and says the question has nothing to do with animal cruelty. He points listeners to a separate talk for the argument; he gives no verse here.
- **"Scripture permits eating meat, so I can ignore the rest."** He calls this searching the Scriptures for a loophole (John 5:39, 1 Corinthians 10:23).
