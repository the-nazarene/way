## Summary

Craig, host of Creation Care Church's weekly live "Friday Night Live" broadcast, asks why God created the Garden of Eden vegan, not only that it was. He takes Genesis 1:29-30 (humans and animals given plants for food) as the starting text. His answer is theological rather than historical: a plant-based diet fits a creation without death, with peace among creatures, in which God dwells with his people and in which God's character, which he identifies as love, is reflected. He reads Genesis 2:16-17 with Romans 5:12-15 and Revelation 21:4 to argue that death entered through sin and that the Eden arrangement, and the restored kingdom, contain no killing. He draws on Galatians 5:22-23, Romans 12:18, Isaiah 11, and John 10:10 for peace and love, and on Exodus 25:8, Isaiah 66:1, 1 Corinthians 6:19 and 12:27 for God's dwelling with humans. The second half is a question-and-answer segment with chat participants. ## Key Teachings

### 1. No death in Eden, so no killing
Craig reads Genesis 2:16-17 ("in the day that you eat of it you will surely die") as showing that Eden had no death and no provision for dying. From this he reasons that if no animal was killed, none was eaten, so the plant diet follows. He pairs this with Romans 5:12 and 5:15, which he takes to mean that death entered "through one man's offense" and not in God's original plan, and with Revelation 21:4, where death, sorrow and pain are removed after the resurrection. He says the restored state "is the state that we're in prior to death entering the world."

### 2. Peace and love as God's arrangement
Using Galatians 5:22-23, Craig argues that love, joy and peace are the fruit of God's Spirit and reflect God's character, which he says should be embodied across all creation. He cites Romans 12:18 and argues that the Greek word translated "all" (which he gives as "Pantone") means "the whole or all," so the command to live peaceably is not limited to people. He reads Galatians 5:13-15 as a warning against "bit[ing] and devour[ing] one another," which he applies literally to killing and eating animals.

### 3. Dominion as shamar (care), and animals as family
Craig takes the Hebrew word shamar in Genesis 2:15 to mean "take care of, maintain," and argues that human dominion in Genesis 1:26 means protection and care, not license. He cites Isaiah 11:6 and 11:9 (the wolf and lamb, "they shall not hurt nor destroy") as the picture of the restored creation, with humans leading animals in peace. He then uses Matthew 12:50 (those who do the will of the Father are "my brother and sister and mother") and his own household analogy to argue that God intends humans and animals to be family, and that animals should be seen "as friends," not "as food." He paraphrases Jesus calling his followers "friends" without giving a reference.

### 4. God dwelling with his people
Craig traces a theme of God's dwelling from Exodus 25:8 (the sanctuary "that I may dwell among them") and Isaiah 66:1 (heaven as throne, earth as footstool) to 1 Corinthians 6:19 (the body as the Temple of the Holy Spirit) and 1 Corinthians 12:27 (believers collectively as Christ's body). He argues that the Tabernacle and Temple prefigured this inner indwelling, and that the fruit of the Spirit is therefore God acting through believers. He presents this as the reason God created Eden: "to literally dwell among us."

### 5. God's character is love
Craig's central claim: "the reason that God created the world this way is because that's God's character his character is love," supported by 1 John 4:8 and 4:16. He cites Psalm 36:6 ("you Lord preserve both people and animals," which he reads from the pamphlet), and John 10:10, where Jesus is "the Good Shepherd" who gives life while the thief "comes to steal and to kill and to destroy." He contrasts the Garden, where "the enemy has no power," with the fallen world.

### 6. Standing out and not conforming
In the application section, Craig reads Romans 12:1-2 (present your bodies "a living sacrifice," do not be conformed to this world) and argues believers should resist social pressure to eat animal products and "not just kind of fit in." He cites Galatians 5:24 and self-control as a fruit of the Spirit against fleshly desires, and says veganism is "a journey not a destination." He counsels patience and encouragement rather than criticism, using Jonah and Nineveh as an example of a resistant city that repented.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means a license to use or kill animals.** Craig argues that dominion in Genesis 1:26 means care (shamar, Genesis 2:15) and that "the one thing that it definitely isn't is a license to kill and eat the animals," which he says "most people want to say it is."
- **Being vegetarian is enough.** Replying to a chat comment, Craig agrees (citing a viewer's testimony about dairy and egg production) that "it's not enough," and that veganism is the goal, while also conceding that "everything contains some amount of animal products" and the aim is minimizing harm.
- **It is too late to change.** Responding to a viewer who went vegan at 11, Craig says "it's never too late to start," citing the parable of the workers (Matthew, no reference given).
- **Animals are food for humans.** Craig argues that Genesis 1:29-30 gives plants to humans and animals alike, and that humans should see animals "as friends... as family," not "as food."
