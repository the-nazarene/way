## Summary

Craig Wescoe asks what kind of fast God actually chooses, and whether abstaining from food is the most important form of fasting. He surveys the scriptural fasts: Moses and Jesus each going forty days without food or water (Exodus 34:28; Luke 4:1-2), Daniel refusing the king's meat and wine and eating vegetables (Daniel 1), and communal fasts in Ezra, Joel, Nineveh and Acts 13. He reads Matthew 6:16-18 as requiring secret fasting. He then argues that the fast God chooses is in Isaiah 58:6, "to loose the bonds of wickedness" and "let the oppressed go free." Wescoe says the imagery of chains, yokes and burdens applies most directly to animals (monkeys chained for coconuts, circus elephants, donkeys and oxen), and that the fast therefore means refusing exploitation and working for liberation. He links this to Jesus's quotation of Isaiah 61:1 in Luke 4:18 and to his reading of the temple incident in John 2:16-17 as Jesus freeing animals sold for sacrifice.

His evidence is almost entirely scriptural and rests on his own readings, with no outside scholarship named. The talk is presented as a live Friday session, recorded from the Animal Rights Center in Berkeley. ## Key Teachings

### Fasting as a scriptural practice with precedents
Wescoe begins with the common picture of fasting as abstaining from food and sometimes water. He cites Moses on the mountain (Exodus 34:28) and Jesus in the wilderness (Luke 4:1-2) as forty-day fasts, and argues that these give a precedent that Jesus "commands fasting for various purposes." He lists further examples: fasting before baptism (the Didache, cited as chapter 7 verse 4), Ezra 8:23 (fasting alongside entreaty to God), Nineveh's sackcloth and ashes, Joel 2:12-13 (turning to God "with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning"), and Acts 13:2-3 (fasting while the Spirit sets apart Barnabas and Saul). He says this shows fasting was "continued to be practiced in a big way throughout the church," so it is not "archaic."

### The Daniel fast and the vegetable diet
Wescoe describes the "Daniel Fast" as abstaining from animal products and alcohol, not from all food. He reads Daniel 1:8-20 as a ten-day test in which Daniel and three companions eat vegetables and drink water, and emerge "better and fatter" and "ten times better" in wisdom than the court magicians. He ties this to Genesis 1:29 as God's original diet and calls it "the ideal living according to god's very good plan." He also cites Genesis 6 and "Peter" and "Paul" for the idea that eating animal flesh has demonic origin, using the "table of demons" passage (he gives no verse for it). He mentions the Liberation Pledge and "Animals First" as contemporary parallels.

### Isaiah 58:6 as the chosen fast, applied to animals
This is the talk's central argument. Wescoe reads Isaiah 58:6 as God rejecting ritual fasting in favour of loosing chains and freeing the oppressed. He says the imagery of "bonds," "yokes" and "heavy burdens" "primarily applies to animals," and that it applies to people "only by extension." He reads Proverbs 12:10 ("A righteous man regards the life of his animal") and Matthew 11:28-30 (Jesus's "yoke" as light, with Jesus bearing the load) as supporting this, and concludes that the fast God wants is "justice for the oppressed," which he ties to Jesus's words in Matthew 23 about "weightier matters" (he gives no chapter).

### Jesus's zeal for the Father's house: the temple as a model
Wescoe links Psalm 69:9 ("zeal for your house has consumed me") to John 2:16-17. He reads the overturning of tables and release of animals in the temple as Jesus acting against the commerce in animals sold for sacrifice, and as an example of the liberating fast. He treats the whole creation as God's "house," with the earth as his footstool, and says God "dwells among us" (on his reading, Jesus is God in the flesh).

### Secret fasting and textual variants
Wescoe reads Matthew 6:16-18 as requiring that fasting be done unseen, "so that the only one who knows that you're fasting is God," and contrasts it with fasting for display. He also points to Matthew 17:21 ("this kind does not go out except by prayer and fasting") and notes that it appears in the New King James Version but not the NIV or ESV, which he takes as evidence that "not all Bibles are using the same manuscripts." He describes the Didache as "not in the Bible," with no named author, and says "the early apostles came up with" it. These are his characterisations, not verified claims.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Fasting means only going without food and water.** Wescoe says that is the most common form but not the only one. He describes the Daniel fast (no animal products or alcohol), juice fasts, and the "Animals First" practice of fasting once a month and donating the food money.
- **Matthew 17:21 is in every Bible.** Wescoe says it appears in some translations (NKJV) and not others (NIV, ESV), reflecting different manuscripts.
- **Animals have no soul in the biblical sense.** Wescoe argues from Genesis 1:30 and the ark account that animals are called "living soul" (nefesh haya) and receive the breath of life.
- **Fasting belongs only to the Old Testament.** He cites Acts 13 and other New Testament passages to argue it was continued throughout the early church.
- **Fasting should be a public display.** Wescoe reads Matthew 6:16-18 as requiring secret fasting, unseen by others.
- **People who are not vegan cannot join an animal-liberation fast.** In answer to a chat question, Wescoe says non-vegans can join fasts against specific injustices (fur, horse racing, the Yulin dog festival) and recommends not shaming people, since shame is "not the most effective strategy."
