## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a Friday night live message for Creation Care Church, asks "What is coming out of your mouth?" using Proverbs 10:11: "the mouth of the righteous is a well of life but violence covers the mouth of the wicked." He argues that the verse's contrast between the righteous and the wicked extends to how people treat animals. His main method is to read a concept through the same book first, then through parallel passages. Proverbs 12:10 says "a righteous man regards the life of his animal but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel," and Wescoe treats this as the key to what "violence" means. He then adds Psalm 11:5, John 10:10, John 4:14, Luke 6:45, and several Gospel passages, arguing that the speech of a righteous person flows from a heart set on God's kingdom and righteousness. He treats the eating of animal products and the use of animals in farming and experimentation as forms of concealed violence.

The central counterargument he addresses is the use of Matthew 15:11 ("not what goes into your mouth defiles a man") and Luke 10:8 ("eat such things as are set before you") to justify eating animal products. He reads Matthew 15 in its context (the dispute is about unwashed hands, and the list of defiling acts is murder, adultery, theft, and false witness) and argues that Luke 10 begins with the instruction to seek out "sons of peace," who would not serve a diet of violence. He also presents exposure of cruelty as a duty, using Luke 8:16-17, Ephesians 5:11, and the ag-gag laws against undercover slaughterhouse filming as an example. He calls for confession and forsaking of sin (Proverbs 28:13, 1 John 1:9), warns of self-deception (James 1:26, Psalm 34:13), and ends with the hope of a kingdom "without killing, no death" and with prayer.

The second half is a Zoom Q&A where Wescoe answers chat questions about cruelty-free labeling (he says "cruelty free" can still contain animal products), animal testing, media narratives, and whether churches that support hunting or the Heifer Project are in line with the talk's message. He closes by announcing next week's topic, which is based on Matthew 10:34-35.

## Key Teachings

### Reading Proverbs 10:11 through Proverbs itself
Wescoe's stated rule is that when a biblical book uses a concept, one should first see "how does it use those same words elsewhere" within that book. Proverbs 12:10 pairs "righteous" and "wicked" in the same verse and mentions the animal's life, so he concludes that the righteous "care for the needs of the animals whereas the wicked do not." He then reads Psalm 11:5, where God's soul "hates" the one who loves violence, as God's own verdict on cruelty. He ties this to the summary of God's good will as love of God, neighbor, and "all of his creation," and says Proverbs 12:10 "specifically picks out the animals."

### Mouth, heart, and treasure
Using Luke 6:45 ("out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks"), he argues that speech reveals treasure. He links this to Matthew 6 ("where your treasure is, that's where your heart is") and to "seek first the kingdom." He names the desires of the flesh as the thing to avoid and gives the example of taking milk, cheese, and eggs from animals. His point is that a heart set on the world's values produces speech "concealing violence" or "making excuses for violence and bloodshed."

### Matthew 15:11 and the limits of "what goes in does not defile"
Wescoe identifies a common reading: "it doesn't matter what I eat, it's what comes out of my mouth that defiles me," used to justify killing animals for food. He says the reading is only "the first half of the verse." He points to Matthew 15:20, where Jesus lists murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, and false witnesses as the things that defile, and to the context of the passage, which he describes as a dispute about "eating with unwashed hands." His conclusion is that defilement comes from violence and hypocrisy, not from the question of what is eaten. He refers listeners to an earlier live talk, "did Jesus declare all foods clean?" for a fuller treatment.

### Luke 10 and the "sons of peace"
He reads Luke 10 as Jesus sending out 70 (or 72) disciples "as lambs among wolves," and says the harvest imagery would have been familiar from the agricultural calendar. He stresses verse 5 ("if a son of peace is there, your peace will rest on it") and treats "peace" as the opposite of violence. His argument is that the instruction to "eat such things as are set before you" (verse 7-8) applies only after one has found a "son of peace" whose way of life is peaceable. He explicitly rejects the idea that a host's pig roast licenses a guest to eat pork, saying that host is not the son of peace one should seek.

### Exposure, confession, and the ethics of visibility
Using Luke 8:16-17 ("nothing is secret that will not be revealed"), Wescoe argues that Christians are "light bearers" meant to expose evil rather than conceal it. He offers the windowless slaughterhouse as an example of concealed violence and the ag-gag laws as an example of penalizing exposure while the cruelty is legal. He then connects this to Proverbs 28:13 ("he who covers his sins will not prosper") and 1 John 1:9 (confession leads to cleansing from "all unrighteousness"), so that exposure leads to confession and forsaking. He applies this to churches as well, saying they can be complicit in "the powers of darkness" and need to confess that too.

### Deception, judgment by words, and the warning of Revelation 3
He cites Matthew 12:36-37 (people will give account for every idle word) and James 1:26 (a religion that does not bridle the tongue is useless) to argue that deception of one's own heart is the real danger. He names the enemy as "the father of lies" and quotes the claim "God doesn't care about animals. He only cares about people" as a self-deception. He closes this part with Revelation 3:16-18, where the lukewarm are spewed from the mouth, and Jeremiah 1:9 ("I have put my words in your mouth") as the model for speech that God would want. He ends with the "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians is not named, but he lists love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, gentleness, faithfulness, self-control).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"What goes into your mouth doesn't matter, so eating animals is fine" (Matthew 15:11).** Wescoe says this reads only half the verse. He argues from Matthew 15:20 and the unwashed-hands context that defilement comes from violence and hypocrisy, not from diet.
- **"Luke 10 tells you to eat whatever you are served."** He says verses 5-8 tell disciples to first find a "son of peace," so the host's diet is not automatically endorsed. A pig roast does not license pork.
- **"Cruelty free" means vegan.** He says the label only means the product was not tested on animals and that cruelty-free products "can still contain animal products," so one must check both.
- **"God only cares about people, not animals."** He calls this a deception of the heart and cites James 1:26 and Psalm 11:5.
- **"Individual choices don't matter because the systems of evil remain."** He calls this a surrender to the "powers of darkness" and argues that at minimum believers should extract themselves from that fellowship and that joining together can change systems.
