## Summary

Craig asks whether the Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12) and the command to love one's neighbor apply to animals, and whether Christians are "treating animals how we want to be treated." He answers yes. He builds the case almost entirely from scripture passages read in sequence, with little reliance on outside scholarship. His main line of argument is that Genesis 1 presents humans with "dominion" over animals, but that dominion is a caring stewardship rather than killing and eating them, since God assigns plants as food for both humans and animals (Genesis 1:29-30). He then reads Psalm 104, Luke 12:42, Proverbs 12:10, and Isaiah 11 as describing God's own feeding and peaceable care for creatures, and Isaiah 61:1 and Matthew 21:12-13 as Jesus's mission of liberating captives and overturning a "den of thieves." He treats Paul's Romans 13 and 1 Corinthians 13 as confirming that love of neighbor is the fulfillment of the law and should extend to animals.

The talk moves from theory to practice. Craig says the Lord's Prayer ("on earth as it is in heaven") implies no slaughterhouses or fur farms in heaven, and he recommends leaving animals off the plate and checking products, noting that a "cruelty free" logo does not mean the product is free of animal ingredients. He also urges patience with non-vegan Christians and warns against misanthropy toward humans.

Most of the last third is a live Q&A in which Craig reads chat comments aloud and responds. He closes by previewing next week's talk on shedding innocent blood (Isaiah 1:15-16). The talk is a devotional sermon rather than a historical or textual study, so its claims about what Jesus meant rest on Craig's own reading of the passages.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The Golden Rule and the love commands include animals
Craig reads Matthew 7:12 as a summary principle, then links it to Matthew 22:36-40 (which he says quotes Deuteronomy 6:5 and "love your neighbor as yourself") and Romans 13:8-10 (love fulfills the law). The open question he poses is "who is my neighbor? Is it only humans?" His answer is that animals are included, because the whole earth is God's creation [03:35]-[06:49].

### 2. Genesis 1: dominion as stewardship, with a plant diet
Craig reads Genesis 1:20-31 and stresses that God creates and blesses the animals as well as humans [07:39]-[09:12]. He argues that the "dominion" given to humans (Genesis 1:26-28) does not mean killing animals for food, because God gives "every herb that yields seed" and "every green herb" as food for both humans and beasts (Genesis 1:29-30) [10:00]-[10:54]. This is Craig's reading; he does not discuss how other interpreters understand "dominion."

### 3. Stewardship, righteousness and peace: Psalm 104, Luke 12:42, Proverbs, Isaiah 11
- Psalm 104:24-28: God opens his hand to feed creatures, and Craig proposes that humans, as God's image-bearers, should feed animals in the same way [11:48]-[12:49].
- Luke 12:42: the "faithful and wise steward" who gives food "in due season" is, for Craig, a person acting in God's image [13:57].
- Proverbs 12:10: "A righteous man regards the life of his animal" [14:42].
- Isaiah 11:6-9: the peaceable kingdom image shows the ideal of stewardship. Craig adds that the "knowledge of the Lord" fills the earth, so the whole creation is included [15:23]-[16:56].
- Proverbs 3:31 and Psalm 11:5: Craig contrasts this with envying "the man of violence," and says God "hates" violence toward creation [17:44]-[19:30].

### 4. Liberation and the temple: Isaiah 61:1, Matthew 11:12, Matthew 21:12-13, John 10:10
Craig says Jesus read Isaiah 61:1 in the temple and declared it fulfilled, and that the "captives" include animals in gestation crates, battery cages, aquariums, animal-testing stalls, and those sold for slaughter [19:30]-[20:48]. He reads Matthew 11:12 (the kingdom "suffers violence") as meaning humans who oppress animals are seizing God's kingdom by force [21:55]. He then reads Jesus's cleansing of the temple (Matthew 21:12-13) as an act against people who "bought and sold in the temple," which he extends to buying and selling animals for slaughter. He contrasts this with John 10:10 ("the thief does not come except to steal and kill and destroy"). The text names money changers and dove sellers; the slaughter-for-profit reading is Craig's application [23:29]-[24:33].

### 5. Love as God's nature: 1 John 4 and 1 Corinthians 13
Craig reads 1 John 4:16 and 4:8 ("God is love"; "he who does not love does not know God") and argues that showing love to animals is part of knowing God. He reads 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 point by point: love is kind (so supporting factory farms is not love), love is not arrogant (so human superiority does not license killing animals), and love does not seek its own (so eating animal flesh for taste is "seeking its own") [27:47]-[33:04].

### 6. Practical steps: diet, purchasing, and the Lord's Prayer
- Craig says that at every meal one can "leave animals off our plate," citing Genesis 1:29 [25:59].
- He describes the "cruelty free" bunny logo as a misnomer: it means no animal testing, but the product may still contain animal products, so one must check both [26:43].
- He ties Matthew 6:10 ("your will be done on earth as it is in heaven") to the absence of slaughterhouses and fur farms in heaven [35:36]-[36:54].
- He recommends patience with others, and lead-by-example rather than forcing change [42:47]-[44:03], [53:28]-[54:15].
