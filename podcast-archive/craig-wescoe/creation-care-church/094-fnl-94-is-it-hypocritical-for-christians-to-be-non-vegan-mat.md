### Key Teachings

### 1. Four scriptural senses of hypocrisy
The host argues that Jesus uses "hypocrite" in more than one way and sets out four senses drawn from Matthew:
- **Saying one thing and doing another** (Matthew 23:23): a loose paraphrase of the ordinary dictionary sense, which he links to "double-mindedness" and "cognitive dissonance."
- **Holding others to a standard you do not keep** (Matthew 7:5, "remove the plank from your own eye"): he applies this directly to telling others to go vegan while not being vegan. He stresses that the point is not to stop correcting others but to correct oneself first.
- **Doing good to be seen** (Matthew 6:1): he calls this "virtue signaling" and gives the example of a politician holding a shovel at a Habitat for Humanity event without doing any work.
- **Lip service** (Matthew 15:7-9): saying "Yes Lord" while acting against the words spoken. He applies this to people who say "I love animals" while eating them or supporting factory farming.

### 2. Faith without works is dead
He cites James 2:26 ("faith without works is dead") and Galatians 5:6 ("faith working through love") to argue that belief has to show up in conduct. He adds 1 John 3:17-18 ("let us not love in word or in tongue but indeed and in truth"). The argument is that a Christian who professes love for animals but eats them is living out "dead faith." He says he is not telling people to abandon faith. He is saying faith must be "expressed through works."

### 3. The "weightier matters": justice, mercy, faithfulness
He treats these three terms as the content of Matthew 23:23 and supports each with Hebrew Bible and gospel passages:
- **Justice:** Micah 6:8 ("do justly, love mercy, walk humbly"), Psalm 106:3 (those who keep justice are blessed), and Isaiah 61:8, which he reads as God hating "robbery for burnt offerings," with the robbed party being the animals. He lists fur, factory farming, circuses and horse racing as injustices a just Christian should not support.
- **Mercy:** Luke 6:36 and Matthew 5:7 (be merciful and receive mercy). He presents the "mercy" passage as the direct counterpart of the sacrifice passage.
- **Against animal sacrifice:** Matthew 9:13 quotes Hosea 6:6 ("I desire mercy and not sacrifice"). He reads the prophets (he names Amos, Jeremiah, Isaiah and Ezekiel) as condemning the slaughter of animals, and concludes that "God's will is Mercy" and that Christians should not slaughter animals at all.
- **Against "improved" cruelty:** Proverbs 12:10 ("the kindest acts of the wicked are cruel"). He rejects welfare reforms such as larger cages as still cruel and calls for "total Liberation for the animals."

### 4. Faithful and wise stewards and the original diet
He reads Luke 12:42-44 (the servant left in charge of the master's household) as a picture of believers who must already be doing the master's will when he returns. He links this to Psalm 145:15 ("you give them their food in due season") and argues that humans, as stewards, are now responsible for feeding the animals. He then reads Genesis 1:26-31 as God's original plan: dominion is stewardship, and the text gives "every green herb for food" to humans and "every beast of the earth" as food. This is his basis for arguing that the Creator's design is plant-based and that Christians should return to it. He treats the reading as plain, with no discussion of how Genesis 1:29-30 has been interpreted.

### 5. Love God, love neighbor, and the animal neighbor
He summarizes the law through the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:5) and Leviticus 19:18, and then identifies the "neighbor" through the Good Samaritan parable (Luke 10). In the Q&A he argues that "neighbor" includes animals, so mercy toward animals is part of the second great commandment. He says love is not enjoyment of pets; love is "showing need, showing concern for the other regardless of what's in it for yourself."

### 6. Outreach: prayer, gentleness and listening
Answering viewers, he gives practical guidance. Begin by assuming the other person has not heard the message; "season your words with salt" and speak gently; listen more than you talk; and rely on prayer, since "it's up to God to open those eyes." He draws on Jonah to illustrate that the messenger's job is only to preach and that the result belongs to God. He also says that "we don't want to lose heart," and that outreach should be done out of love and for God's glory.

### Misconceptions Addressed

- **Hypocrisy means only saying one thing and doing another.** He argues that Jesus uses the word in at least four senses, including judging others by a standard you do not keep, and doing good to be seen.
- **Tithing and religious observance are the main duties.** He argues from Matthew 23:23 that these are not the "most important" matters and that justice, mercy and faithfulness outrank them. He also says tithing should not be dropped.
- **Faith alone is enough.** He argues from James 2:26 and Galatians 5:6 that faith without works is dead.
- **Loving animals means liking pets.** He says love means concern for the animal's well-being whatever the benefit to oneself. Petting animals while eating or supporting factory farms does not count.
- **Welfare reforms like bigger cages are a moral step forward.** He cites Proverbs 12:10 to argue that the "kindest acts of the wicked are cruel," and calls for "total Liberation" rather than incremental improvement.
- **God wants animal sacrifice, or at least permits it.** He argues from Hosea 6:6 and Matthew 9:13 that God desires mercy, not sacrifice, and says God "doesn't care about animals" is not true.
- **Animals are not neighbors.** Answering a viewer, he argues from the Shema, Leviticus 19:18 and the Good Samaritan parable that animals are included in "neighbor."
