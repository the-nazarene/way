## Summary

Craig asks what Jesus meant when he said "my kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36), and what that means for a believer's allegiance to nations, political parties and their leaders. He argues that the kingdoms of this world are built on weapons, conquest and division, while Jesus' kingdom is marked by love, feeding one's neighbor and unity. His evidence is a chain of proof texts: Isaiah 2:4 (swords into plowshares), Mark 3:25 (a house divided cannot stand), John 13:35 (love as the mark of disciples), the temptation in Matthew 4:8-10 with Deuteronomy 6:13, Daniel 3 (the three men who refused to bow to Nebuchadnezzar's statue), Acts 5:29 (obey God rather than men), and Revelation 21:2-5 (the New Jerusalem as the kingdom of God coming down). He presents these as a command to examine yourself, using 2 Corinthians 13:5, and to place identity "in Christ" rather than in a party or nation.

The talk is almost entirely Craig's own exposition, with a question-and-answer section at the end where he reads comments from the live audience and responds. In those answers he extends the argument to peace, immigration, the Pledge of Allegiance, and to Daniel 1 as a basis for a vegetable diet. He also asserts, without a named source, that refusing to worship the emperor was the main reason for Christian martyrdom in the first three centuries. He closes with prayer and announces that next week's talk will be on Romans 13:11 and a "vegan Awakening" in the church.

## Key Teachings

### The kingdom is a different kind of kingdom
Craig reads John 18:36-37 and says Jesus' words "my servants would fight" show that worldly kingdoms are characterized by building weapons for conquest and defense. He sets against this Isaiah 2:4, which he links to his former ministry name "Swords to Plowshares": in God's kingdom people grow food for their neighbors rather than weapons or animals for slaughter. He summarizes an image he attributes to someone he "once heard": "if your neighbor is hungry don't build a wall build a bigger table." He contrasts this with the "kingdoms of this world" that "justify their conquests" with claims such as searching for weapons of mass destruction.

### A house divided cannot stand, and love is the mark of discipleship
Using Mark 3:25, Craig says that Satan works to divide the body of Christ, and that intentional political polarization is one form of this division. He cites John 13:35 ("by this all will know that you are my disciples if you have love for one another") and gives a hypothetical in which a Roman and a Syrian, both Christians, are ordered by their governments to kill each other. He says the believer's answer is already settled by Christ's command to love, and that a person who tries to "have one foot" in a political party and "one foot" in God's kingdom cannot succeed.

### Refusing the kingdoms' offers: Matthew 4 and Deuteronomy 6:13
Craig reads Matthew 4:8-10, the temptation in which the devil offers "all the kingdoms of the world." He argues that Jesus refuses even the offer to "do so much good" through worldly power, quoting Deuteronomy 6:13 as his reply. Craig applies this to news consumption: a believer should not repeat the talking points of Fox News or CNN but should quote Scripture. He ties this to Matthew's saying that a person's words reveal the heart (he gives no verse for this).

### Daniel 3 and the refusal to bow
Craig reads Daniel 3:10-27 at length: Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refuse to worship Nebuchadnezzar's gold image, are thrown into the furnace, and are kept safe by a figure "like the Son of God." Craig says that they "remain faithful" even under threat, and that the king's response acknowledges God's power. He notes that the outcome is not a promise that refusal will always be rescued. He then makes a historical claim: that in the first three centuries the number one reason for Christian martyrdom was refusal to worship the emperor and Rome's gods. He gives no source for this claim.

### Obey God rather than men, but do not rebel for its own sake
Craig reads Acts 5:27-29, where Peter and the apostles tell the council that they must "obey God rather than men." He says this is not a license for defiance "for the sake of being defiant," and he refers to Romans 13 as urging against needless rebellion. Where a government command conflicts with God's command, though, the believer follows Jesus.

### Examine yourself, and find identity in Christ and his kingdom
Craig reads 2 Corinthians 13:5 ("examine yourselves") as a daily discipline, and asks listeners whether their allegiance is to Jesus or to a party or nation. He develops this with John 15:19 ("you are not of the world"), Psalm 20:7 (some trust in chariots and horses, but we remember the name of the Lord), Daniel 2:44 (the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that breaks the other kingdoms in pieces), Joshua 24:15 ("for me and my house we will serve the Lord") and 2 Corinthians 5:17 ("a new creation"). He calls Jesus "our commander-in-chief," and ends with Psalm 146:3-10 ("do not put your trust in princes"), which he contrasts with governments that "spend billions... on nuclear warheads" while people starve.

### Peace, the Pledge, and the Daniel 1 diet (Q&A)
In answer to viewer questions, Craig says that the "peace" many nations seek through conquest or occupation is not the peace Jesus advocated. He cites Matthew 5:9 ("blessed are the peacemakers") and describes peacemaking as active. He discusses the Pledge of Allegiance and children's rhymes as ways of conditioning people to "pledge allegiance" to a nation. On diet, he reads Daniel 1:5-8, in which Daniel refuses the king's food and wine and eats vegetables and water, and he states that the four men were "vegan Disciples of Daniel" who were "blessed with Better Health and 10 times the wisdom." He says the diet is also an obedience to "that original diet that God gave us," which is a reference to Genesis 1:29 that he does not read aloud in this talk.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Believers can be loyal to a political party or nation while also serving God.** Craig argues that such divided loyalty is the "one foot in each" error, and that Jesus' command to love one another rules out fighting fellow believers for a government.
- **Peace is secured through conquest, occupation or striking first.** Craig says this is "the wrong idea of peace"; Jesus' peacemakers are active but not violent.
- **Obeying government means never disobeying it.** Craig's reading of Acts 5:29 is that when a government's command conflicts with God's, God is to be obeyed; he adds that this does not mean defiance for its own sake.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **Jesus vs. Paul:** Craig draws mainly on the Gospels, Daniel and Psalms. He cites Pauline letters (2 Corinthians 5:17, 13:5; Romans 12:2) as supporting texts, but does not contrast Paul with Jesus or discuss Paul's role.
- **Nonviolence:** This is the talk's central theme. Craig reads Isaiah 2:4, Matthew 5:9 and John 13:35 as a rejection of war and of killing fellow believers.
- **Kingdom on earth:** Craig treats the kingdom of God as something that will "come down" from heaven (Revelation 21:2; Daniel 2:44), and as a rival to earthly kingdoms rather than their reform. This differs from a reading in which the kingdom is realized in present political or social life.
- **Resurrection, afterlife and hell:** Only briefly touched, through Revelation 21:2-5 ("no more death") and the Daniel 3 story of deliverance from death. No resurrection doctrine is argued.
- **Who Jesus thought he was:** Craig affirms Jesus as king, Messiah and "commander-in-chief," and reads John 18:37 ("I am a king") as Jesus' own self-claim. He does not discuss the historical question of what Jesus claimed.
- **Diet:** Craig reads Daniel 1 as a model of vegetarian, vegan eating and as a form of faithfulness to the diet "God gave us." This is a moral and devotional argument, not a historical one.
- **Torah:** Only the Deuteronomy 6:13 citation and the idea of "the original diet" touch on Torah; the talk does not argue from Torah law.
