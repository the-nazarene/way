## Summary

Wescoe asks what the Book of Enoch is, how the Dead Sea Scrolls changed its standing, how Jesus, the apostles and the early church regarded it, and what it says about the origin of animal sacrifice and flesh-eating. He frames the talk as a Creation Care Church live session based on Jude 1:14-15, and says he wants to lay out the evidence so listeners can reach their own view.

His main argument is that the pre-1976 scholarly consensus, which treated 1 Enoch as a Christian composition built on Jude and Genesis, has been reversed. He cites the Cave 4 Aramaic fragments published by Milik in 1976 as showing that the book predates the New Testament. He then traces how Justin Martyr, Athenagoras, Irenaeus, Clement, Tertullian, Commodianus and Anatolius used the book, and how Augustine excluded it from the canon while still granting that Jude attests to something authentically from Enoch. ## Key Teachings

### The Dead Sea Scrolls reversed the scholarly picture
Wescoe says that before 1976 there was an almost consensus view outside Ethiopia that 1 Enoch was pseudepigrapha written by Christians, a spin-off of Jude and Genesis 5-6. He credits the Cave 4 Aramaic fragments of 1 Enoch, published by Milik in 1976, with overturning that view. On his account, the New Testament depended on Enoch rather than the reverse. He also notes that the Ethiopian Tewahedo churches are the only ones that have always kept the book in their canon. The talk gives no primary-text images or fragment numbers, so these claims rest on the speaker's summary.

### Genesis 6 is read through 1 Enoch from the start
Wescoe presents a line of witnesses that reads "sons of God" in Genesis 6 as angels who lusted after human women and fathered giants. He cites the Damascus Document, which he dates between about 175 and 100 BC, describing fallen "heavenly watchers." He then cites Philo (whom he dates to 25 BC-40 CE) and Josephus (mid-to-late first century). He says the early church, from Justin Martyr (c. 155) onward, consistently read Genesis 6 the same way. He also claims Genesis itself used Enoch as a source, though he sets that aside as a topic for a later talk.

### Jude, Peter and Jesus as witnesses to 1 Enoch
Wescoe argues that Jude 1:14-15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 and calls it the authentic prophecy of Enoch. He says Peter's reference to angels cast into Tartarus (2 Peter 2:4) draws on 1 Enoch 10:4-6. For Jesus, he points to Matthew 22:29-30 and Mark 12:24-25, where Jesus says the risen dead are "like angels in heaven" and do not marry. He argues that the only place Scripture states this is 1 Enoch 15:3-7, and so Jesus is reasoning from that text. He treats the Sadducees' question as an opening for Jesus to cite it.

### Augustine's exclusion and the three positions
Wescoe reads Augustine's City of God, which grants that "Enoch, the seventh from Adam, left some divine writings" because Jude asserts it, but excludes the Enoch writings because their antiquity made authenticity uncertain and because they contain "fables about giants." Wescoe reads this as the decision that shaped the New Testament canon. He lays out three positions: reject the whole book as pseudepigrapha; accept all of it; or accept the first 19 chapters as authentic and remain uncertain about the rest. He argues that the first position forces a reader to reject Jude, Peter and Jesus, and the second is hard because of the "animal apocalypse" in section four, which he says predicts Israelite history in detail. He favours the third.

### Origin of sacrifice and flesh-eating
Wescoe's central application is that, in 1 Enoch 6-7, fallen angels teach humans and the giants eat animals and human flesh and drink blood. He quotes 1 Enoch 7:5-6, 10:8 and 19:1, which describe Azazel teaching sin and the demons leading humans into sacrifice. He concludes: "now we know that the origin of animal sacrifice and eating animal flesh has to do with demonic possession. Uh that it's not something that God ever instituted." He also links the demons' lust for violence, blood and worship to the violence he sees in modern life, a point he develops in the Q&A.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That the Book of Enoch was simply a later Christian invention.** Wescoe says the pre-1976 consensus has been overturned by the Qumran fragments, which he treats as showing it predates the New Testament.
- **That the Book of Enoch can be dismissed without consequence.** He argues that calling it pseudepigrapha forces a reader to call Jesus, Peter and Jude false prophets.
- **That Augustine's exclusion settled its authority.** Wescoe says Augustine himself admitted that parts were authentic and excluded the book because he could not tell which parts.
- **That Satan alone bears responsibility for sin in Enoch.** In Q&A he argues that Enoch assigns culpability to Satan's angels, to those who follow them, and to humans who choose to follow them.
- **That the origin of sacrifice is in God's institution.** He reads 1 Enoch 19 as showing that sacrifice and flesh-eating came from demonic possession.
