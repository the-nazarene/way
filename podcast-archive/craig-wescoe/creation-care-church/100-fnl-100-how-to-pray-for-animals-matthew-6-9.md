## Summary

Craig Wescoe asks how Christians should pray for animals, and whether it matters who they pray to and how. He builds on the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-15) and the surrounding verses on private prayer, forgiveness and God's will. His answer is that prayer should be addressed only to the Creator, offered in forgiveness, and aimed at what is "consistent with his will." Applied to animals, he says Christians should pray against named forms of exploitation (animal agriculture, testing, entertainment, the pet trade, hunting and similar) and for the repentance of those who carry them out. He supports this with Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal") and with the Creation-care reading of Genesis 1:29 and Isaiah 11, which he uses to argue that God's intended diet is vegan. Most of his evidence is scriptural and comes from his own reading of these texts. He makes several historical and personal claims without sources, including a claim about why Christians were executed in the first and second centuries and his account of pig vigils at a Los Angeles slaughterhouse.

Craig says that an unanswered prayer is not necessarily contrary to God's will, citing 2 Peter 3:9. ## Key Teachings

### Pray only to the Creator, and pray in forgiveness

Craig reads Matthew 6:5-9 as instructing private prayer that is not for show, addressed to "Our Father in heaven." He pairs this with the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4-5, cited through Matthew 22:36-38) to argue that prayer goes to the Creator alone, not to angels, saints, the dead, Mother Earth, or creation itself. He also ties prayer to forgiveness (Mark 11:25-26; Matthew 6:14-15) and to loving enemies (Matthew 5:44), so that intercession includes praying for the repentance of persecutors, not their destruction.

### Intercession belongs to Jesus and the Spirit, not to saints

Taking Daniel 6 as his opening case, Craig argues that the faithful man prays only to God even when the king's decree threatens his life. On intercession, he cites Hebrews 7:25 (Jesus as the one who "ever lives to make intercession") and Romans 8:26 (the Spirit "makes intercession for us"). His conclusion is that a Christian should not seek intercession from the dead or from saints. Note that he presents Daniel's refusal as an example of faithful prayer under threat, and that his comparison of the Daniel decree to the first- and second-century execution of Christians for refusing to worship the emperor is an unsourced historical claim.

### Prayer must be "according to his will"

Craig argues that effective prayer must match God's will, citing 1 John 5:14, John 15:16 (asking "in my name"), James 4:3 (asking "amiss" for pleasures), and 1 Peter 3:12 and Proverbs 15:29 (God hears the righteous and is far from the wicked). His example is a person who prays for bacon, which he says God has explicitly said he does not want eaten. He separates unanswered prayer from prayer contrary to God's will. Delayed answers can reflect God's patience (2 Peter 3:9), but when a request is clearly against God's will, the believer should stop praying for it and examine themselves.

### Praying for animals: a concrete list and the call to repentance

Craig displays the prayer list on creationcarechurch.org/prayer, grouped under animal agriculture, fashion, science, entertainment (including bull runs, sports fighting and racing), hunting, the pet trade (live exports and puppy mills), and lost companion animals. He says the righteous "regard the life of his animal" (Proverbs 12:10), and that the wicked are cruel to animals. He asks Christians to pray for the repentance of those who harm animals, not for their punishment, and for the victims themselves.

### Diet: a vegan reading of Genesis, Isaiah and Revelation

Craig's clearest doctrinal claim is that God's intent is vegan. He says "in the beginning we're vegan" (Genesis 1:29) and "in the end we're vegan" (Isaiah 11, with 11:9 quoted in the Q&A), and that Revelation 21:4 describes a world without death. He argues from Malachi 3:6 ("God never changes") that God wants veganism now, and that Christians should adopt it before the kingdom comes, so that Christ finds them already doing his will. This is a theological argument from Scripture, not a historical reconstruction, and the talk does not engage the Hebrew text or the reading of Genesis 1:29 by other interpreters.

### Witness, protest and the "Fruit" of prayer

Craig describes the protests and outreach he has taken part in: a prayer vigil he attended outside a UK laboratory (as reported by a commenter), video outreach outside Christian concerts with literature handed out, and pig vigils he says ended when a slaughterhouse was shut. He argues that praying together, even remotely, has force, and that visible Christian advocacy can open conversations. He links this to Matthew 5:16-style witness and Galatians 5:22-23 (the fruit of the Spirit), which he will treat in the next talk.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God created animals for humans to eat, so eating them is fine.** Craig says Christians often use this to justify harm to animals and ignore the call to care for them, and he argues that God's will for creation is vegan.
- **If a prayer is not answered, it must be outside God's will.** Craig says delayed answers can reflect God's patience (2 Peter 3:9); the test is whether the request is clearly contrary to God's will.
- **Prayer requires a fixed posture, closed eyes or long speech.** Craig says praying without ceasing means constant awareness of God and short prayers count, while extended prayer (Luke 6:12) is also valid.
- **Saints, the dead or other mediators can intercede for us.** Craig says intercession belongs to Jesus and the Holy Spirit alone (Hebrews 7:25; Romans 8:26), so believers should pray directly to God.
