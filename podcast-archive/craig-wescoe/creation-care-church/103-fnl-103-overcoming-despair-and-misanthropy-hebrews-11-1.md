## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of Creation Care Church's weekly live talk, asks how a person who cares deeply about animal suffering, and about human suffering, can avoid despair and misanthropy (hatred of humans). He frames this as a question about the Christian's inner life: the risk is that love "grows cold," as he reads from Matthew 24:12.

His answer is built almost entirely from scripture, read devotionally and applied to veganism and animal cruelty. Wescoe argues that the world "lies under the sway of the wicked one" (1 John 5:19), but that the decisive victory is already won (1 John 5:3-4), so the present suffering is temporary. He counsels against hating the people who do harm: vengeance and grudges should be left to God (Leviticus 19:18), enemies should be loved and forgiven (Luke 6:27-28; Ephesians 4:32), and the evil should be opposed without condemning the person. He also points to prayer and casting one's cares on God (1 Peter 5:6-7), joy as a fruit of the Spirit, hope in a coming kingdom of peace (Isaiah 2:3-4; Revelation 21:4; Hosea 2:18), and mutual encouragement in the community (Hebrews 10:24-25).

The talk ends with about twenty minutes of live Q&A with commenters, whom Wescoe answers in his own words. It closes with a prayer for endurance and love. The talk makes no historical or textual-critical argument; its claims about the end times, the spiritual powers, and God's plan are presented as Craig's reading of scripture.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Despair answered by the already-won victory
Wescoe reads 1 John 5:19 as explaining why the world is full of suffering: it "lies under the sway of the wicked one." He then reads 1 John 5:3-4 ("this is the victory that has overcome the world, our faith") and offers an analogy: the victory is like a lightning strike that has already happened, while the thunder (the full manifestation) has not yet arrived. The victory is therefore "a done deal" and "not in the balance," and the present suffering is temporary. He ties this to Hebrews 11:1 (faith as "the evidence of things not seen") and to a sense of a clock "ticking down."

### 2. Guarding against love growing cold and misanthropy
Wescoe reads Matthew 24:12 ("because lawlessness will abound the love of many will grow cold") and presents misanthropy as a trap of the enemy. He says misanthropy "often does sort of creep in" when people reflect on human responsibility for animal suffering, and that it can come "from a place" of genuine care but is still "a very Sinister thing." The opposite of misanthropy, in his terms, is philanthropy, which he treats as the will of God.

### 3. No vengeance, no grudges: love of enemies
Drawing on Leviticus 19:18 (love your neighbor as yourself; no vengeance or grudge), he distinguishes vengeance (seeking retribution for a wrong) from aggression. He says vengeance belongs to God, so believers should seek the wrongdoer's repentance and be ready to forgive. He reads Luke 6:27-28 as the "radical" solution, and says that loving enemies is "as much for your sake as it is for the other person's sake." He also insists this does not mean condoning evil: "we want to be adamantly speaking out against the evil but we don't want to be hating the people who are doing the evil."

### 4. Separating the person from the evil and from spiritual forces
Wescoe says a person doing evil is "at their core a child of God" who has been "used by the enemy." He appeals to unspecified Pauline teaching that believers war not against flesh and blood but "principalities of Darkness," and to Genesis 3 (the serpent) and Genesis 6 as examples of spiritual corruption. He warns against the opposite error, blaming the devil for one's own sins. He also applies this to outreach: a gentler approach that treats people as uninformed, not as villains (Luke 23:34 is cited from memory for "forgive them, for they know not what they do").

### 5. Casting cares, joy, and hope
He reads 1 Peter 5:6-7 ("casting all your care upon him") as the practical way to release the weight of suffering to God rather than bearing it alone. Romans 15:13 is read to show that joy and peace need not be incompatible with caring about suffering; joy is "the second fruit of the spirit." He encourages prayer as "the first thing" one should do about suffering.

### 6. The coming kingdom as the object of hope
Wescoe presents Isaiah 2:3-4 (swords into plowshares), Revelation 21:4 (God will wipe away every tear), and Hosea 2:18 (covenant with the beasts of the field, bows and swords shattered) as promises of a peaceable world. He explicitly reads Hosea as including animals: "there will be no more hunting no more slaughter houses no more uh barbecues of animal flesh." He uses the image "instead of building a bigger wall we build a bigger table." He says the end "is surely coming soon" and that New Jerusalem "will be at any time."

### 7. Community and encouragement
Hebrews 10:24-25 is his closing scriptural practice: not "isolated Islands," but stirring one another up to love and good works. He presents the live talks and chat as part of this. In the Q&A he also reads Romans 12:2 (transformed by the renewing of your mind) and 2 Peter 3:9 (God is "longsuffering," not willing that any should perish). He says he has been "the one causing" harm himself for twenty years, and that most people who resist veganism are simply uninformed.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The world's suffering means evil has the final word.** Wescoe argues the victory over the wicked one is already won (1 John 5:3-4), and the present suffering is temporary.
- **Hating those who cause harm is the righteous response.** He says vengeance belongs to God, and that one should oppose evil and call people to repent without hating them (Leviticus 19:18; Luke 6:27-28).
- **Animal-rights activists who wish humanity would go extinct are expressing a justified view.** He calls this a misanthropic mindset that conflicts with God's plan and with love for all creatures, including humans.
- **Joy is inappropriate while so much suffering exists.** He argues that joy and caring about suffering are not incompatible, and that inner joy empowers service (Romans 15:13).
- **People who eat animal products are villains.** He says most are uninformed, and that treating them as villains is counterproductive.
