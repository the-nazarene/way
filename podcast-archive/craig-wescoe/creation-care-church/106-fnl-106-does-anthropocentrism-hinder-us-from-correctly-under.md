## Summary

Craig Wescoe, host of the Creation Care Church "Friday Night Live" series, asks whether human-centered thinking (anthropocentrism), which he ties to human pride and vanity, keeps readers from understanding Scripture correctly. The talk is built on Matthew 5:8 ("blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God") and is mostly a string of Proverbs, Isaiah and Gospel verses. Wescoe's main application concerns animals: he argues that Christians often misread Genesis 1:26 ("dominion") as a license to exploit, harm and kill animals.

His central evidence is a reading of Genesis 1:26-31 that he presents as corrective. He says the first thing God specifies about human dominion is a plant diet (Genesis 1:29-30), that the Isaiah 11 peaceable kingdom (Isaiah 11:6, 9) describes the intended end state, and that Jesus's comparisons in Matthew 6:26 and 12:11-12 only work if animals have value to God. He also reads "dominion" as stewardship and "fear of the Lord" as submission and worship, and he ties these to the fruit of the Spirit and the "paths of peace" of Proverbs 3.

The second half is a live Q&A with a small audience. Wescoe takes up animal testing, whether buying from non-vegan stores makes one complicit, and whether pride is the worst sin (he answers that distrust of God, as in Eden, is the root). He closes by pointing to the two great commandments and to a next-week talk on coveting, and he says that perfect purity in avoiding harm is not achievable, so one should "do our best."

## Key Teachings

### Pride and "right in your own eyes" as the hindrance
Wescoe opens with Isaiah 55:8-9 (God's thoughts are higher than ours) and Proverbs 3:5-6 ("lean not on your own understanding"), then Proverbs 21:2 ("every way of a man is right in his own eyes"), tying this to Deuteronomy, where he says people followed what was right in their own eyes before the Commandments. He pairs Proverbs 16:18 ("pride goes before destruction") and James 4:6 (God resists the proud) with Romans 12:16, reading humility and "associating with the lowly" as including the treatment of animals. The argument is that pride is the lens that makes harmful readings of Scripture seem right.

### The "dominion" misreading, and Genesis as the corrective
This is the talk's core. Wescoe says Genesis 1:26 and 1:28 are cited to justify exploiting, harming, killing and eating animals, and taking calves' milk. His correction reads Genesis 1:26-31 as a whole: dominion comes with the image of God, but the first food given is plants ("every herb that yields seed," Genesis 1:29), and beasts and birds also receive green herbs (Genesis 1:30). He treats "very good" in Genesis 1:31 as God's own assessment of a peaceable arrangement. He argues that dominion, as God describes it, means stewardship, and that substituting a human definition ("to rule over," "to be a tyrant") distorts the text.

### Matthew 6:26 and 12:11-12: human value and the animals' value
Wescoe says Christians use Matthew 6:26 ("are you not of more value than they") and Matthew 12:11-12 (the sheep in the pit on the Sabbath) to justify harming animals. His reasoning is that the comparison only proves anything if animals have value to God, which he says Psalms and Genesis show (breath of life, covenant, feeding). He answers the "more valuable" point with an analogy: a pilot or ship captain is more "important" because of role and responsibility, not because others are worthless, so greater human role means greater accountability toward animals. He says he is not claiming animals are equal to humans or have salvation at stake; his point is only that they should not be harmed.

### Wisdom, peace and the pure heart (Matthew 5:8)
Wescoe reads Proverbs 3:13-18 (wisdom is more precious than rubies; "all her paths are peace") and the continuing verses on God founding the earth by wisdom and knowledge. He links the "pure in heart who shall see God" (Matthew 5:8) to having "knowledge of God," and then to Isaiah 11:9 ("they shall not hurt nor destroy... the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord"). The peace of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22, read by an audience member) is applied to animals as well as people, including opposition to war and to animal exploitation.

### "Fear of the Lord" and servant leadership
Wescoe reads Job 28:28 ("the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom") and says the fear is submission and worship, not terror, citing 1 John 4 ("perfect love casts out fear") and asserting that New Testament quotations of such passages are translated "worship." (The NT translation claim is his assertion; the group should check it.) He pairs this with Matthew 20:28 (the Son of Man came "to serve") to argue that the Christian posture toward animals and people is service rather than "what can I get out of you."

### Q&A: animal testing, complicity, and "doing your best"
In answer to audience questions, Wescoe describes animal testing as torture for profit, asks listeners to pray for its end, and argues that the FDA and the wider legal system make complete avoidance of animal-derived products impossible. He gives an example of companies (Impossible and Beyond, named by him) whose product decisions drew criticism from vegans. He concedes that buying from stores and landlords who deal in animal products implicates the buyer, and his advice is to be "as conscientious as possible," to speak out, and to aim at systemic change.

### Two great commandments as a practical test
For practical discernment ("how do I know if I'm following my own understanding?"), Wescoe offers the two great commandments (love God wholeheartedly; love your neighbor as yourself) and asks whether an action forsakes God or harms a human or animal neighbor. He then says study Scripture for specifics, pointing to Exodus 20 as the expansion of those two commands.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means license to exploit.** Wescoe says Genesis 1:26 is commonly read as permission to exploit, harm and kill animals; he replies that Genesis 1:29-31 defines dominion through a plant diet and that God calls the arrangement "very good."
- **"More valuable than animals" means animals don't matter to God.** He says Matthew 6:26 and 12:11-12 presuppose animals' value to God, and that greater human role means greater accountability, not license.
- **Animal rights means claiming animals are equal to people or have salvation at stake.** He says he is not claiming equality or salvation for animals; his point is only that they should not be harmed.
- **"Fear of the Lord" means living in terror.** He says it means submission and worship, citing Job 28:28 and 1 John 4.
- **Avoiding all animal harm is achievable.** He says it is not fully possible under current systems and that the goal is to "do our best" and push for systemic change.
