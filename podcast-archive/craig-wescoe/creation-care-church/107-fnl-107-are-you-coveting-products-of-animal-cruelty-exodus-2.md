## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a live Friday talk for the Creation Care Church channel, asks whether buying products of animal cruelty (meat, milk and dairy, skins, fur, wool) counts as coveting under the tenth commandment, Exodus 20:17. He argues that coveting is more specific than "excessive desire." It means wanting something that belongs to someone else and is not intended for you. From there he argues that milk is meant for a calf, flesh is meant to keep the animal alive, and skin and fur are meant for the animal, so consuming or wearing them is covetous.

To support this, Wescoe reads a series of New Testament and Hebrew Bible passages. These include Colossians 3:5 and Ephesians 5:3 (covetousness as idolatry and something not to be named among believers), 1 Timothy 6:9-10 and Zechariah 11:5-6 (greed and uncaring "shepherds"), and Numbers 11 (the quail narrative, which he reads as divine disapproval of craving for meat). He also relies on Proverbs 23:20, which he says uses a Hebrew word meaning flesh rather than food in general, and on Romans 13:9-10, which he treats as making love of neighbor, including animals, the fulfillment of the law. The evidence is almost entirely scriptural reading and close attention to wording. He draws no archaeological, historical-critical, or manuscript evidence.

The second half is a live Q&A with viewers. Wescoe answers questions on whether gluttony means overeating (he says the biblical uses are tied to eating flesh), whether vegan companies can also be driven by greed, why churches overlook animals (he blames tradition and an "anthropocentric mindset"), and how to persuade others. He closes by previewing next week's talk on Job 12:7, "ask the animals."

## Key Teachings

### Coveting means desiring what is not yours or not meant for you
Wescoe's central definition rests on the examples in Exodus 20:17. He says the common modern gloss ("excessive desire") is incomplete. The shared thread is that each item (house, wife, servant, ox, donkey) belongs to a neighbor. He then applies this to animal products: the milk is "for her baby," flesh is "part of the animal" and "intended to keep the animal alive," and skin and wool are given "for the animals not for us." His point is that the animal's own needs define what is "intended for" whom. He does not argue from texts about animal use in Torah beyond these creation-order assumptions.

### Covetousness as idolatry, sin, and death
He treats coveting as a root of wider sin. He cites Colossians 3:5, where "Paul in his letter to the Colossians equates covetousness with idolatry," and Ephesians 5:3, where the community should not even name it. He links this to the Garden of Eden, arguing the first sin was covetousness because the forbidden tree was the one thing "not intended for them." He also paraphrases James: "the evil desire leads to sin which leads to death." Sin is breaking the Ten Commandments, and the desire is to be cut off before it produces death. No verse is given for the James paraphrase.

### Numbers 11: craving for meat and God's displeasure
This is his most detailed case. He reads Numbers 11:4 (the mixed multitude's "intense craving" for meat) and Numbers 11:18-20 (God's reply that they will eat meat "for a whole month until it comes out your nostrils"). He then reads Numbers 11:31-34 (the plague, "while the meat was still between their teeth," and the place named "the grave of craving"). His reading is that God is not approving meat-eating but giving them what they crave as a judgment, comparing it to a parent at the end of their patience. He then ties this to the Ten Commandments that come next in his argument. Note that the reading of Numbers 11 as a condemnation of meat-eating as such is Wescoe's own interpretation; the text itself frames the sin as complaint against God and the manna.

### Greed, profit, and uncaring shepherds
He argues that industries (factory farming, fashion, circuses, aquariums) "cultivate" covetousness in consumers because profit depends on it. He cites 1 Timothy 6:9-10 ("the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil") and Zechariah 11:5-6, where shepherds slaughter sheep "and feel no guilt." He reads the industry as this kind of shepherd, indifferent to whether the practice is "God's will." He adds Revelation 3:17 (the church that says "I am rich") and Matthew's saying about treasure and the heart, arguing that true riches are mercy, justice, and compassion.

### Gluttony, flesh, and "destroying the work of God"
On Proverbs 23:20 ("do not mix with wine bibbers or with gluttonous Eaters of Flesh") he argues that the Hebrew word translated "gluttonous" (he transliterates it "baser") appears only with flesh, meaning living animals, persons, or dead bodies, not food in general. He therefore reads the warning as against eating flesh, not overeating. He pairs this with Romans 14:17-20, which he says shows the kingdom is "not food and drink" but righteousness, peace, and joy, and that killing animals for food is "destroying the work of God." He also cites Proverbs 12:10 to argue that even a "kind" act done with animal flesh (feeding the poor with bacon) is counterbalanced by cruelty.

### Love of neighbor fulfills the law, including animal neighbors
Wescoe reads Romans 13:9-10 as summarizing the commandments in "love your neighbor as yourself," and argues animals count as neighbors, citing the creation blessing ("be fruitful and multiply," Genesis 1) and Isaiah 11:6 as a picture of the coming kingdom. He reads 1 John 2:15-16 (lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, pride of life) and Colossians 3:2 ("set your mind on things above") as directing believers away from the cruel world's products. He says the Sabbath commandment (Exodus 20, which he calls the fourth) includes animals in rest, and that God made a covenant with animals, claims he presents without citing a verse.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Coveting just means excessive desire.** Wescoe says that gloss is incomplete. Coveting is desiring what belongs to someone else or is not intended for you.
- **Gluttony means overeating, so Proverbs 23:20 only warns against too much food.** He argues the Hebrew term is attached to flesh every time, so the warning concerns eating flesh. He also says eating a healthy amount of vegan food is not gluttony.
- **Numbers 11 shows God approving meat-eating.** He reads the passage as God's judgment on craving meat, not permission.
- **God's love and covenant are for humans only.** He argues God's love and covenant extend to animals, citing the creation blessing, the Sabbath, and Psalms (no specific Psalm given).
- **Vegan products avoid the greed problem.** In a viewer Q&A he says that greed for profit can also drive vegan companies, and that whether the money is spent on luxuries or charity matters.
