## Summary

The host asks three questions drawn from the description and the title verse, Job 12:7: did animals ever speak, can people communicate with animals now, and what can people learn from them. He answers mainly from scripture, moving through Genesis 1-2, Isaiah 1:2-3, Proverbs 6:6-8, Acts 2, Isaiah 11, and a set of praise and kingdom passages. He does not claim the Bible settles whether animals spoke. He says that "the Bible isn't using enough detail to tell us definitively what was said," and turns to the Book of Jubilees, which he says was found among the Dead Sea Scrolls and used by the Qumran community, for an account in which all animals spoke "one tongue" before the fall (Jubilees 3:28-29).

His central claim is that "dominion" in Genesis 1:26-28 means stewardship, not license. He argues that the plant-based diet given in Genesis 1:29-30, the peaceable animals of Isaiah 11:6-9, and the Spirit being poured out "on All Flesh" (Joel 2:28, quoted in Acts 2) point toward a restored world in which people and animals live in peace. He reads Isaiah 11 and Galatians 5 as descriptions of what this looks like now, and treats the Pentecost tongues of Acts 2 as a reversal of Babel.

The talk ends with a Q&A from the live chat. The host takes questions on translation (Nicole on "guardians" versus "owners"), on whether ants have queens, and on whether animals sense danger. He answers that animals may be "attuned" to storms and earthquakes, and that a literal reading of Isaiah 11 is better than an anthropocentric one. He closes with prayer and announces next week's talk on John 17:23.

## Key Teachings

### Animals are included in "All Flesh" and the breath of life
The host builds on Job 12:7, using the New King James rendering "the breath of all mankind." He says the Hebrew phrase is *basar* ("All Flesh") and that it "is not just people but also animals despite some translators uh suggesting otherwise." He links this to Genesis 1:20-25, where God creates sea creatures, birds, and land animals and blesses them with "be fruitful and multiply."

### Dominion is stewardship, not exploitation
Using Genesis 1:26-28 and 2:15, he argues that humans and animals receive the same blessing, but only humans are told to "have dominion." He reads the Hebrew *shamar* in Genesis 2:15 as "to be a protector to be a maintainer." He says the "Dominion" in Genesis 1 is a responsibility for "the good order that I created things to be," and that it is not "some license to do what we want." He identifies translation layers (Hebrew to Greek to Latin to English) and says "livestock" is an anachronism in the Garden of Eden.

### Diet: the plant food given to people and animals
He reads Genesis 1:29-30 as giving humans "every herb that yields seed" and animals "every green herb" for food. He takes this as the original diet and reads Isaiah 11:7 ("the lion will eat straw like the ox") as the restored one. He says that when people cite "dominion" against vegetarian readings, the answer is that "the very next thing he said" after Dominion is the instruction to eat plants.

### Animals as teachers: Isaiah 1:3 and Proverbs 6:6-8
Isaiah 1:3 ("the ox knows its owner and the donkey its Master's crib") becomes a rebuke: Israel does not recognize God as master, unlike the animals. Proverbs 6:6-8 (the ant with "no Captain overseer or ruler") becomes a model of cooperation without kings. He ties this to 1 Samuel 8, where Israel asks for a king and is told it has rejected God. He admits he is "not a biologist" and that the biology of the ant example may not hold.

### Language, Jubilees, Babel, and Pentecost
He presents the Jubilees account (3:28-29) in which, after the fall, the mouths of all animals are closed and "they had all spoken one with another with one lip and with one tongue," and then all flesh is scattered "according to its kind." He compares this to the scattering of humans at Babel (Genesis 11:1-9). He then reads Acts 2:1-14 as the reverse: the Spirit gathers the nations, who each hear "in his own language," and Peter quotes Joel 2:28 about the Spirit on "All Flesh." He presents this as a process "already begun." He does not show that Jubilees is authoritative, saying "whether we want to accept Jubilee's account or not."

### Peaceable kingdom and the child who leads
He reads Isaiah 11:6-9 as predators and prey living together, "a little child will lead them," which he describes as the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) in action. He ties this to Matthew 18:3 ("unless you change... and become as Little Children") and to Luke 12:42-43 (the faithful steward who gives food "in due season"). He reads Psalm 145:15-21 and Revelation 5 as scenes of all creatures praising God.

### Naming, anthropocentrism, and reading the Bible on its own terms
He argues that Adam's naming of the animals (Genesis 2:19-20) creates a relationship of recognition, with names reflecting "why God designed them" rather than what people can extract from them. He says the main obstacle to reading these texts is an "anthropocentrism" that treats everything as "All About Me," and that reading passages about animals on their own terms can loosen it.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Dominion" licenses people to do whatever they want with animals, including killing and eating them.** The host argues that dominion is a responsibility tied to God's will, and that Genesis 1:29 immediately gives plant food as the diet.
- **"All flesh" means only people.** He argues that *basar* in Job 12:7, Joel 2:28, and Psalm 145 includes animals.
- **Translations that say "livestock" or "owner" reflect the original.** He argues that "livestock" is an anachronism for Eden and that "owner/master" should be read as "guardian/steward."
- **Analogies and parables cannot be read literally and apply only to humans.** In answer to a chat question, the host says this reading comes from an "anthropocentrism" that a literal reading of passages like Isaiah 11 can correct.
