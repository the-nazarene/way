## Key Teachings

### Matthew's "poor in spirit" versus Luke's "poor"
Craig reads Matthew 5:1-12 and Luke 6:17-21 side by side. He says Luke has "blessed are you poor for yours is the kingdom of God" while Matthew has "blessed are those who are poor in spirit." He reports that "people sometimes say that the Matthew ones are more spiritualized whereas the Lucan version is more physical," and then suggests "poor in spirit" means being "rich toward God" while not "going after these worldly things." He treats Luke's "woes" (Luke 6:24-26) as the counterpart to the blessings, which Matthew does not have.

### Mourning and the birth-pain analogy
Craig cites John 16:20-21 (a woman in labor whose sorrow turns to joy after birth) and applies it to the suffering of "animals and the people in this world." Mourning is not indifference: "blessed are those who mourn now but then you'll Rejoice later in that Kingdom when all these things are done away with." He attributes to Paul the view that present sufferings "cannot compare" with future joy, without giving a reference.

### The meek inherit the earth (Psalm 37)
He ties Matthew 5:5 to Psalm 37:7-11 and reads the meek as those who "wait on the Lord," "keep his Commandments," and do not "fret" over the wicked. He contrasts them with the self-exalting who "harm" animals and people, and argues the meek are "those who will inherit this Earth who will be part of that Kingdom."

### Hunger and thirst for righteousness, and mercy
He notes Luke's version (Luke 6:21) has no "for righteousness," and brings in the two-shirts teaching as a literal reading of need. For mercy (Matthew 5:7), he cites Luke 6:36 and James 2:13 ("Mercy triumphs over judgment"), then argues that mercy extends to animals in factory farms, labs, and slaughter. He draws on Matthew 12:11-12 (the sheep in the pit on the Sabbath) and a Hosea 6:6 quotation ("I desire Mercy not sacrifice"), reading the latter as a call to spare animals rather than kill them. He presents the human-animal value comparison as one of responsibility, using an airplane pilot analogy.

### Purity of heart and the heart-written law
Craig cites Jeremiah 17:9 ("the heart is deceitful above all things") to reject "follow your heart" and to argue the heart must be aligned to God's will. He pairs this with Jeremiah 31:33 (the law written on hearts) and Romans 12:2, presenting purity of heart as obedience out of love rather than fear.

### Peacemakers and the child-led creation (Isaiah 11)
He reads Isaiah 11:6 and 11:9 ("a little child thou lead them," "they shall not hurt nor destroy") as a picture of peacemakers leading people and animals in God's way of peace. He also quotes Jesus that one must "change and become like a little child" to enter the kingdom, and presents peacemaking as teaching children, animals, and neighbors "the way of Peace."

### Persecution and the woes (Luke 6:22-26)
Craig says Jesus promises persecution, not approval: "be ready to be persecuted." He describes his own experience of hostility toward plant-based advocates (accusations of "a doctrine of demons," exclusion from potlucks, bacon jokes). He reads Luke's woes as a warning against false prophets who avoid hard truths to be liked, and says the persecution of prophets before Jesus is the expected pattern.

### Stewardship, the least of these, and the Kingdom on earth
He links "the least of these" (cited without a chapter, from the Matthew teaching where Jesus says what is done or not done for "the least of these" is done to Jesus) to animals, arguing failure to help animals is "in some sense" failure toward Jesus. He reads Revelation's warning that God "will destroy those who destroy the Earth" and presents the Kingdom as the restoration of "God's very good world," with "no more evil" and no exploitation.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Matthew's poor in spirit is purely spiritual, and Luke's poor is purely physical."** Craig says people "sometimes say" this, then presents "poor in spirit" as being rich toward God while not chasing worldly things, and argues both versions point toward real need in the world.
- **"Following Jesus means everyone will love and approve of you."** Craig says Jesus promises persecution, not approval, and links this to the prophets.
- **"Just follow your heart."** Craig cites Jeremiah 17:9 to say the heart is deceitful and must be aligned with God's will.
- **"Humans are more valuable than animals, so animals don't matter."** Craig argues the value comparison presupposes animals have value to God, and that human value is the responsibility of stewardship.
- **"Mercy only applies to people."** Craig argues mercy, as a form of power over those under one's control, applies to animals as well.
