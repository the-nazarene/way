Craig, host of Creation Care Church's Friday Night Live series, asks what it means to "shout it from the rooftops," based on Matthew 10:27, where Jesus tells his disciples to proclaim privately taught words publicly. He answers that proclamation should be bold, spoken "in love," backed by consistent action, and extended to "every creature" (Mark 16:15). Throughout, he argues that the message includes care for animals, and that God's "very good design" in Genesis 1:29 (plant food) and the enduring law of Matthew 5:18 are still binding. ## Key Teachings

### Proclaim openly, to all nations and all creatures
Craig reads Matthew 10:27 as Jesus telling disciples to take private teaching public. He pairs this with Deuteronomy 6:4-9 (the Shema, with its commands bound on hands, between the eyes, and on doorposts) as a call to visible, repeated teaching. He ties the Great Commission of Matthew 28:18-20 ("make disciples of all the nations") to Mark 16:15 ("preach the gospel to every creature"), arguing the mandate "is not just a thing that is concerned with humans but it's concerned with all creatures."

### Speak the truth in love, and let deeds match words
Drawing on Ephesians 4:15-16 and 1 Corinthians 13:1-2, Craig argues that truth must be spoken in love, even when opponents respond with mockery or violence. He says Paul places love above other gifts, so that "if you don't have love you have nothing." He then cites 1 John 3:17-18 and James 2:15-17 to say that words without works of charity are "dead" faith. He gives examples such as building homes, giving out Bibles, and providing vegan food to the hungry.

### Care for the most vulnerable, including animals
Using James 1:27 and Matthew 25:34-40, Craig argues that "pure and undefiled religion" means caring for the most vulnerable. He explains that widows and orphans were the people in that culture most likely to be neglected once the male provider was gone. He extends the category to animals in cages, in experiments, and on factory farms, and says that serving them is serving Christ, "our King," in the parable's terms.

### The jot and tittle: Genesis 1:29 and Proverbs 12:10 as permanent
Craig reads Matthew 5:14-18 as Jesus affirming that not one "jot or tittle" of the law will pass away until heaven and earth pass. He then applies that statement to Genesis 1:29 (plants as food), Proverbs 12:10 (care for animals), and the love command. He concludes these "won't go away," so Christians have no "permission to do the opposite." This is the talk's main textual argument for a plant-based diet, and it depends on reading Genesis 1:29 as God's design for humans and animals alike.

### Testimony, prayer and going where people are
Craig uses Revelation 12:10-11 (overcoming "by the word of their testimony") to argue that personal testimony is hard to dispute, unlike theology. He suggests asking fellow believers for prayer about animal suffering, citing James 5:16. He draws on John 4:5-7 (Jesus waiting at Jacob's well) and Acts 5:42 (teaching "in every house" and daily in the temple) to argue for meeting people in public places, such as the airport or supermarket. He also points to Isaiah 61:1 and Isaiah 52:7 as scriptural pictures of the good news for the poor and the captive. He reads these as including resurrection and eternal life, and asks whether Christians are also visiting imprisoned animals.

### The Didache and the canon (Q&A)
Answering a question about baptism in Matthew 28:19 versus "in the name of Jesus" in Acts, Craig says the New Testament gives no detailed method. He points to the Didache, which he calls "the diday," as the earliest catechism. He claims it "dates back to the apostles," describes immersion in running cold water, a two-day fast, and sprinkling as a last resort, and says it was left out of the canon "because they didn't know which of the Apostles actually composed it." He says it is "one of the least refuted historical sources." These are his claims and are not checked in the talk.

- **Jesus' mission was only for humans.** Craig cites Mark 16:15's "every creature" to argue the commission includes animals.
- **The law has been abolished, so Genesis 1:29 and Proverbs 12:10 no longer apply.** Craig reads Matthew 5:18 as saying not a jot or tittle passes away, and treats these texts as still binding.
- **God made animals to be eaten.** In the Q&A, Craig's suggested response to someone who says this is to reply that "that's not what it says in Genesis 1:29."
- **The Didache was rejected because its teaching was disputed.** Craig says it was left out because its author was unknown, and that its authenticity was never questioned. (This is Craig's claim, not verified in the talk.)
