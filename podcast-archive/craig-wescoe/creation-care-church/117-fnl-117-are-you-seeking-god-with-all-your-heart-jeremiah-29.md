## Summary

Craig, presenting a Friday live talk for Creation Care Church, asks what it means to "seek God with all your heart" (Jeremiah 29:13). He argues that seeking God is a continual, diligent, humble and faithful practice, not an occasional effort. Seeking leads to finding, to loving God and one's neighbor, and to living as an "image bearer" of God. He treats the Genesis 1 account of a plant-based diet for humans and animals as the original design that God is bringing creation back to, so he concludes that wholehearted seeking includes veganism.

He presents this as the Bible's own account of what seeking involves. He also says Paul quotes Isaiah 64 and that the Christ-righteousness of 2 Corinthians 5:21 is what transforms believers. ## Key Teachings

### Seeking God is wholehearted, continual and diligent
Craig reads Jeremiah 29:13 and Matthew 7:7–8 as promises that God will be found by those who seek with all their heart, not "a little bit of effort." He says that "it's implied" that the promise requires seeking wholeheartedly. He ties this to 1 Chronicles 16:11 ("seek his face Evermore") and Proverbs 8:17 ("diligently") to argue that seeking is ongoing. His example is someone half-attending to a project, which he says is not diligence.

### Seeking means putting God's kingdom and righteousness first
Using Matthew 6:31–33, Craig says the followers of Jesus should not be consumed by food, drink and clothing, which he links to what "the Gentiles seek." Instead they should "seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness." He adds that "where your treasure is" connects to where the heart is. For Craig, seeking the kingdom is part of seeking God.

### Humility, repentance and faithfulness
Craig draws on 2 Chronicles 7:14 (humble yourselves, pray, seek my face, turn from wicked ways), 1 Chronicles 28:9 (seek and be found; forsake and be cast off), Zephaniah 2:3 (seek righteousness and humility), and Habakkuk 2:4 (the proud soul is not upright; the just live by faith or faithfulness). He describes faith as a daily reliance that governs action, and he treats pride as the main obstacle. Isaiah 64:4–9 provides the image of God as Potter and believers as clay, and Craig says the passage admits that human righteousness is like "filthy rags" and that sin separates people from God.

### Christ's righteousness, Christ's walk and the image of God
Craig reads 2 Corinthians 5:21 as teaching that the sinless Jesus "became sin" so that believers might become God's righteousness "in him." He says Christ's Spirit in believers transforms them from within. He reads 1 John 2:6 ("walk just as he walked") as the test of that indwelling, and Hebrews 1:3 ("express image") to mean that Jesus is indistinguishable from the Father. Believers are called to bear that image.

### Genesis 1 as the original vegan design, and "dominion" as stewardship
This is the talk's central claim. Craig reads Genesis 1:26–31 and says that "Dominion in God's image and likeness doesn't mean is killing and eating the animals." He cites Genesis 1:29–30, where humans and animals are given plants for food, and says "even the animals are vegan." He acknowledges that "if you go by Genesis 2 uh it's a little complicated." He argues that animals were created and blessed before humans and were "not here just for us." He says the "end" God declares in Isaiah 46:9–10 "is just like the beginning," so seeking God leads back to the original design.

### Loving God and neighbor, including animals
Craig reads Matthew 22:36–40 as Jesus' summary of the law. He quotes Deuteronomy 6:4 (the Shema) and Leviticus 19:18 as its sources. He argues that love of God leads naturally to love of neighbor and that "neighbor" is not restricted to humans. In the Q&A he uses Matthew 5:43–48 to argue that God's love is impartial and that believers should love indiscriminately. He also draws on 2 Peter 3:9 and says that God's love does not diminish, even though humans can push God away through sin.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God is a servant who grants material wishes if you believe hard enough (Law of Attraction, prosperity teaching).** Craig says God is not our servant, and that we should be submitting to his will rather than visualizing what we want. He reads James as saying that asking to fulfill one's own pleasures leads to unanswered prayer.
- **God loves only the faithful.** Responding to a viewer who asked whether God loves all people, Craig says God loves everyone impartially, as Matthew 5:43–48 indicates. He distinguishes God's love for his children, who know and love him in return, from the rest of humanity, who do not yet acknowledge that love. He still says God is "longsuffering" toward all (2 Peter 3:9).
- **God moves away from people who sin.** Craig says God is "a gentleman" who never pushes himself on anyone, and that it is human sin and rejection that create distance. God's grace remains available for the repentant.
- **Animals were created for humans to kill and eat.** Craig argues that this is not what Genesis says and that animals were created and blessed before humans.
- **Jesus would eat fish, so eating animals is consistent with imitating him.** Craig says this misses the point. For him, imitating Jesus means humility, service, prayer and opposing injustice, and he cites the temple scene and the servant-leader teaching.
- **Pride is a minor issue for believers who already do the right things.** Craig argues that pride can creep in even among people who are vegan or who attend church often, and that believers must continually check themselves.
