## Summary

The talk takes up Matthew 6:10 ("your kingdom come, your will be done on Earth as it is in heaven") and asks whether believers should be living by God's will now or deferring it until Jesus returns. The unnamed host opens with a three-minute clip from the Live Mercifully channel, narrated by Lily, which argues that the petition requires present obedience, including rejecting the killing of animals for food. The host then develops the same case at length, using Revelation 21:4 and Isaiah 11 to describe a kingdom with "no death," and treating that as a standard for conduct now.

The host's main evidence is a chain of texts. Revelation 21:4, Hosea 2:18 and Isaiah 11:6-9 describe a peaceable creation that includes animals. Genesis 1:28-31 is read as the original diet of plants, which the host takes as God's "will" at creation. Acts 2:38, Galatians 2:20 and Ephesians 2:8-10 are used to explain how the "kingdom" comes into individual lives through the Holy Spirit and faith. The Gospel parables (Matthew 7:24-27, Luke 6:46-49, Matthew 25:1-13, Matthew 22:1-14, Luke 12:42-48) are cited to argue that readiness means already doing what Jesus commands, not postponing it. Luke 22:42 is offered as a model of setting one's own will aside.

The second half is a live Q&A with commenters, which covers animal tears, "dominion," whether "God knows my heart," the meaning of the wise and foolish virgins' oil, the protein objection, and spiritual gifts. The host closes with a prayer and announces that next week's talk will take up Hosea 2:18 ("when will bow and sword be abolished").

## Key Teachings

### 1. The petition is a present commitment, not a future hope
The host argues that praying "your will be done on Earth as it is in heaven" means aligning one's own conduct with God's will now. Praying the words while living against them makes the prayer "a mere religious chant." The host criticizes the attitude "I'll wait till I get to heaven," which he says goes against Matthew 6:10.

### 2. Heaven's "no death, no killing" is the standard for earth now
Drawing on Revelation 21:4, the host reasons that if the kingdom has no death and no killing, believers should not kill now, "whether it's killing people killing animals." He says Jesus "is here to transform us now," and so the transformation should begin before his return. Animals are included throughout: Hosea 2:18 has God making a covenant "with the beasts of the field," and Genesis 9 is cited (as "five times") for God's promises extending to animals. The host also says Isaiah 11:9 ("they shall not hurt nor destroy") covers humans, animals and the earth together.

### 3. Original creation: plant-based diet as God's will
The host reads Genesis 1:28-31 as the original arrangement. Humans have "dominion," and the food given is "every green herb" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed," shared with the beasts and birds. He says God then "saw everything... it was very good," and that the restored kingdom will return to this pattern. In Q&A he answers the "dominion" objection by quoting Sarah Withrow King: dominion "isn't a license to kill but it's a responsibility to keep." He also says Noah's Ark was "vegan" in that all the animals survived.

### 4. Grace, faith and the Spirit: transformation from within
Acts 2:38 is used to show that the Holy Spirit is a gift received by repentance and baptism, and that this Spirit transforms the believer "from the inside." Galatians 2:20 ("it is no longer I who live but Christ who lives in me") is read as meaning believers should "live for the spirit... not for the flesh." Ephesians 2:8-10 supports the claim that good works were "prepared" beforehand and that believers are God's "workmanship." The host applies "the desires of the flesh" directly to dairy and meat consumption (the example given is calves separated from their mothers for cheese). He treats this as a question of whose will is followed, not only a matter of diet.

### 5. Readiness: the parables as warnings against deferral
The host cites the Parable of the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1-13), the Wedding Feast (Matthew 22:1-14) and the Faithful Steward (Luke 12:42-48) to argue that the "Lord, Lord" who does not act will be shut out. He reads the oil in the lamps as the Holy Spirit (he says "usually I hear that"), and treats the wedding garment as "the righteous acts of the Saints." He concludes that the faithful servant is one who is found "doing his will" when the master returns, and is opposed to waiting for "deathbed repentance." He cites James (no verse given) on not knowing tomorrow.

### 6. Warnings against self-justifying hearts
In the Q&A the host answers the claim that "God knows my heart" by citing the call to "examine yourselves" and the idea that the heart is "treacherous above all things." He says it is "more dangerous" to assume one's heart is right while resisting God's will. He also rejects the idea that veganism is creation-worship, saying the diet is a command of "the Creator." He says that vegan Christians have "that part of God's message correct" while others have it "incorrect," and adds that other commands (faithfulness, avoiding idolatry, caring for people) must also be kept.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"I'll go vegan when Jesus returns / I'll wait until heaven."** The host says Matthew 6:10 calls for God's will to be done on earth now, and that many Gospel teachings call for present readiness.
- **"Dominion means permission to kill and eat animals."** The host says the next verse, Genesis 1:29, gives plants as food, and quotes King that dominion is a responsibility to keep, not a license to kill.
- **"God knows my heart, so my conduct doesn't matter."** The host says the heart is "treacherous" (he cites scripture on examining oneself) and that assuming one's heart is right while resisting God is the more dangerous position.
- **"Deathbed repentance is enough."** The host argues against this, citing the parables of the virgins and the steward and the James "you fool" passage.
- **"Veganism is worshipping creation."** The host says veganism is obedience to the Creator, and that the alternative is "worshiping your belly."
- **"The Bible doesn't say what heaven is like, so there's nothing to infer."** A pastor's remark is repeated: the Bible says what does not happen in heaven (no death, suffering or separation from God). The host says the same reasoning implies no slaughter or factory farming.
- **"Protein requires animal products."** The host says the protein deficiency argument is "largely a myth" and that the animal industry may have promoted it. No sources are given.
