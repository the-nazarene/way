## Summary

The host opens with a question from Hosea 2:18: when will "bow and sword" be abolished, what will that look like, and to whom was the promise made. He answers that God will end the instruments of violence, that the promise covers animals as well as people, and that it is fulfilled as people come to "know the Lord" and live in peace. His main evidence is a chain of prophetic texts (Isaiah 2:4, Micah 4:3, Psalm 46:9-10, Habakkuk 2:17, Isaiah 11:6-9), the Noahic covenant in Genesis 9, which he says includes "every living creature," and a set of New Testament passages (Ephesians 6, 2 Corinthians 10:4, John 18:36, Matthew 26:52, Matthew 5:7-9, Romans 8, Matthew 24) that he reads as describing a spiritual rather than physical battle.

On timing, he relies on Matthew 24:36 ("no one knows" the day or hour) and concludes that believers should be "ready" now, living out the nonviolent, vegan-compatible life he associates with the coming kingdom. He reads Genesis 1:29-31 as an original plant-based world and Hosea 6:6 as evidence that God never required animal sacrifice. The talk is a live "Friday Night Live" session: after the teaching, the host reads and answers viewer comments, including questions on Micah 4:4, Jesus' "sword" saying, animal souls, and the current war in Gaza (which he declines to analyse politically). ## Key Teachings

### 1. Hosea 2:18 includes the animals in the covenant
The host reads Hosea 2:18-20 as God making "a covenant for them with the beasts of the field, with the birds of the air and with the creeping things of the ground." He ties this to Habakkuk 2:17, where animals are afraid "because of men's blood," and to Genesis 9:8-17, the covenant with Noah. He says God "repeats himself five times" that the animals are part of the covenant, so the promise is "not just a promise to the humans" but to the animals too. This is his basis for saying the peace is for all creatures.

### 2. The prophetic chorus: swords into plowshares, and violence returning
He lists parallel texts: Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3 (swords beaten into plowshares, "Nation shall not lift up sword against Nation"), Psalm 46:9-10 (God "breaks the bow and cuts the spear in two"), and Isaiah 11:6-9 (the wolf and lamb, a child leading the animals). He reads plowshares as a shift from producing weapons to producing food for neighbours. He links these to Micah 4:4 (a viewer's question, answered with the same theme) and to Isaiah 46:10 ("declared the end from the beginning," which he cites after a viewer's prompt). The theme he draws out is that "knowledge of God" is the condition for this peace.

### 3. Spiritual warfare: how the New Testament reads "sword"
The host argues that Paul's "we do not wrestle against flesh and blood" (Ephesians 6:11-12) and "the weapons of our Warfare are not carnal" (2 Corinthians 10:4) mean the believer's fight is spiritual, aimed at converting "our neighbor into repentance." He reads John 18:36 ("my kingdom is not of this world") and Matthew 26:52 ("put your sword in its place") as Jesus rejecting violent kingship. He takes Matthew 5:7-9 (the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers as "sons of God") as describing people who are active, not passive, in making peace. In the Q&A he also uses Galatians 5:22-23 ("the fruit of the spirit") as a test of which groups to align with.

### 4. The timing question: Matthew 24 and "be ready"
He says Matthew 24:36 and 24:44 make the exact day unknowable, so "watch" and "be ready" are the only instructions. He reads the days of Noah (Matthew 24:37-39) through Genesis 6, which he describes as the "sons of God" producing violent giants who ate flesh and drank blood, and he refers to an earlier talk on the Book of Enoch for elaboration. The "eating and drinking" of Matthew 24 is, in his reading, violence and consumption of flesh rather than ordinary drinking. The "faithful and wise servant" (Matthew 24:45-51) is the one who lives in the peace the prophets describe when the master returns.

### 5. Diet: the plant-based world of Genesis 1
The host reads Genesis 1:29-31 as God giving "every green herb for food" to humans and animals alike, calling it "the very good world" of the beginning. He says the end-times peace restores that order. In answer to a viewer, he cites the Darby translation's rendering of Genesis 1:29-30 and argues that the Hebrew *nephesh* ("living soul") is used of both humans and animals, so denying animal souls would also deny human ones. He admits translations differ ("livestock" and "fowl" carry "animal agriculture" connotations) and recommends checking the Hebrew and Greek.

### 6. Sacrifice: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice"
He cites Hosea 6:6 and calls it "the most quoted passage by Jesus in the gospels," reading it as the prophets' rejection of burnt offerings. He adds Jeremiah 7:21-26 (raised by a viewer) and says God did not command animal sacrifice in the sense used in Leviticus. He reconciles this with the Torah's sacrificial laws only by pointing to a separate talk and the Vegan Christian Discipleship School, saying he cannot do it "in like one minute."
