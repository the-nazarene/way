## Summary

Craig, presenting for Creation Care Church's weekly "Friday Night Live" live stream, asks what it means that God "remembers" the animals (Genesis 8:1) and whether humans are called to remember them too. He argues from a chain of passages across the Hebrew Bible, the Gospels, and Revelation that animals are included in God's covenant community, are valued by God for their own sake, and are to be treated with compassion rather than exploitation. He reads the Hebrew verb *zakar* ("remember") in Genesis 8:1 as active rescue, not mere recall, a point a chat participant raised and he endorsed.

His conclusion is practical and ethical: followers of God should not eat animals, should avoid cow's milk (which he calls covetous and cruel), and should see Genesis 1:28-29 (dominion followed by a plant diet) as one instruction. He also reads Jesus's temple action (Luke 19:45-48) as a protest against sacrificial violence rather than against money-changers. Most of the case rests on Scripture as the speaker reads it, with little engagement with critical scholarship. He offers some unsourced health and industry claims about dairy, which the group should treat as his assertions.

The talk ends with a live question-and-answer session in which Craig fields chat comments (on Hosea, mercy versus compassion, and dairy), then previews next week's talk on 1 Thessalonians 5:11 (building church community) and closes with prayer.

## Key Teachings

### 1. God "remembers" the animals, and the remembering is saving action
Craig begins with Genesis 8:1 and the ark, then moves to Genesis 9:12-16, where God's covenant with Noah is extended to "every living creature" and "all flesh" (*kol basar*, which he says appears three times in that passage). He says that when Scripture speaks of God remembering, it means acting on someone's behalf, rescuing or redeeming them, and that God's forgetting of sins works the same way. He credits the Hebrew reading of *zakar* to a chat comment ("I read...") and presents it as a good point rather than something he checked himself.

### 2. Animals are included in covenant promises and the future restoration
He traces a series of promises: Hosea 2:18 (a covenant "with the beasts of the field, with the birds of the air, and with the creeping things of the ground," with weapons of violence removed); Joel 2:28 (God pours out his spirit on *kol basar*, read as humans and animals together); Isaiah 2:4 and Isaiah 11:9, which he reads as the same restoration; and Revelation 5:13, where "every creature" sings praise to God and "the lamb" (identified as Jesus). In a chat answer he argues that even prophecies that do not name animals (such as swords into plowshares) include them, because the restored world has a place for them.

### 3. Compassion texts for how humans treat animals
Craig collects several rules and stories and reads them as protections for animals as well as people: Deuteronomy 25:4 (do not muzzle the ox that treads grain), Exodus 20:8-11 (Sabbath rest extended to "your cattle"), Proverbs 12:10 (the "tender mercies of the wicked are cruel"), Matthew 12:11-12 (rescuing a sheep on the Sabbath), and Matthew 18:12 (the lost sheep). He argues that the minimum in these texts should not be treated as the limit: the question is "What's the ideal in God's mind," not "How much cruelty am I allowed?" He also contrasts a "minimum" ethic with the cruelty of modern factory farming and insurance claims after floods, though he gives no sources for those practices.

### 4. Diet: plant food as God's design, and dairy as covetousness
Citing Genesis 1:29, Craig says the plant diet is "the very good diet" God gave, and that Genesis 1:28 (dominion) and 1:29 (diet) "go together." He reads Numbers' account of craving meat as a warning. He spends extended time on dairy: arguing that milk is produced for a calf, that drinking it is "backwards on every level," that calves are separated and veal is produced, and that coveting what is "not intended for us" (his reading of "wickedness," as he attributes to a chat participant) applies to cow's milk. The health claims he makes (cholesterol, acidity, osteoporosis) are stated without sources.

### 5. The temple action was about violence and sacrifice, not money-changers
Craig argues against the common sermon reading that Jesus cleared the temple because money-changers were cheating people. His points: Luke 19:45-48 does not mention money-changers (the speaker says they appear only in Matthew, Mark, and John); Jesus quotes Jeremiah 7:11 instead of a proverb about dishonest scales; and in his reading, Jeremiah 7 says God "never commanded animal sacrifice." He glosses the key word for "thieves" as "violent ones" and cites Strong's Hebrew 6530 for it. He ties this to Hosea 6:6 ("I desire mercy, not sacrifice").

### 6. Human value and the place of animals
Craig reads "are you not of more value than the animals" as implying that animals have value to God, and argues it is an intrinsic value, not an instrumental one. He says humans' greater importance lies in their role as stewards, using an airplane pilot analogy (if the pilot fails, everyone is in danger). He refers viewers to an earlier live talk on this question and does not cite a verse for the "more value" saying.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Temple cleansing was about money-changers cheating worshippers.** Craig says Luke omits money-changers, Jesus quotes Jeremiah 7:11 rather than a dishonest-scales proverb, and the real target was sacrificial violence.
- **God's concern is only for humans.** Craig says God repeatedly names animals (Genesis 9, Jonah 4:11, Numbers 20:8) precisely so people do not forget them.
- **Dominion gives humans authority to do as they like with animals.** Craig says being made in God's image means acting as God acts, and that Genesis 1:29 comes straight after dominion.
- **Dairy is harmless or healthy.** Craig argues milk is produced for calves, that dairy involves separation and cruelty, and that drinking it is coveting. The health claims are asserted without sources.
