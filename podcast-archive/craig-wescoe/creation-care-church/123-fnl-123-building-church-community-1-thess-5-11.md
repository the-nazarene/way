## Summary

This Friday Night Live talk from Creation Care Church asks what 1 Thessalonians 5:11 ("therefore comfort or encourage each other and edify or build up one another") means for building church community, and what that looks like in practice. The unnamed host argues that the church is the people, not a building, and that its core task is mutual encouragement in walking with God, "stirring up" love and good works, and reaching out to others. He ties this to a set of proof texts about God dwelling among his people, which he reads through the Emmanuel ("God with us") theme.

The main evidence is a sequence of scripture passages read in a fixed order: Hebrews 10:24-25, Ephesians 2:20-22, Colossians 1:18, 1 Timothy 3:15, Revelation 21:3, John 1:14, Ezekiel 37:27, Isaiah 57:15, Zechariah 2:11, Matthew 1:23, Matthew 18:20, Matthew 28:18-20, Matthew 22:36-40 and John 13:34-35. The host also argues that Christian love extends to animals, using Genesis 1:26-31, Genesis 1:29, Genesis 9:4, Hosea 2:18 and Revelation 21:4 to support a vegan diet as part of God's original and intended will. He gives practical examples: building houses for the poor, feeding the homeless with vegan food, rescuing animals, hosting vegan meals with the weekly talk, and letting one's conduct witness to others.

The talk ends with a Q&A from livestream commenters (questions included whether "assembling" means weekly church attendance, and which Christian vegan animal sanctuaries exist), a closing prayer, and a preview of next week's talk on 1 Kings 17 (Elijah and the ravens), which the host says was suggested by Steven Lee August.

## Key Teachings

### Encouragement and mutual building-up as the church's defining activity

The host reads 1 Thessalonians 5:11 and Hebrews 10:24-25 as the core instruction. He says encouragement must be specific: "encouraging each other in our walk with God," including keeping God's commandments and walking with God as Enoch and Noah did. For Hebrews 10:24, he reads "stir up love and good works" as an active process, comparing it to stirring water so it keeps moving. He glosses "love" as *agape*, meaning doing things for others, especially the needy, the vulnerable, and people who have not heard the gospel. He says "the day" in Hebrews 10:25 is "the day of the Lord," the day of judgment.

### The church as Christ-headed body and dwelling place of God

Using Ephesians 2:20-22 and Colossians 1:18, the host describes the church as a body with Christ as its head and cornerstone, built on "the apostles and the prophets," and as a "habitation... of God in the spirit." He says the Spirit dwells both in individuals and in the community collectively. He ties Colossians 1:18 ("firstborn from the dead") to the hope of resurrection to eternal life in Christ's kingdom. He also reads 1 Timothy 3:15's "house of God" and "pillar and ground of the truth" as a call to stand against deception, including in politics, marketing and animal agriculture, on the basis of John 14:6 ("I am the way, the life and the truth"), which he quotes as "I am the way the life and the truth."

### God dwelling among his people (the Emmanuel thread)

This is the talk's central structural argument. The host cites Revelation 21:3 ("the Tabernacle of God is with men"), and says the fulfillment begins in John 1:14, where "the word became flesh and dwelt among us." He reads Ezekiel 37:27 and Zechariah 2:11 as promises that God will dwell among his people and that "all nations" will become his people, so the body of Christ will be worldwide and open to all. He reads Matthew 1:23 (quoting Isaiah 7:14) as naming Jesus Emmanuel, "God with us." He argues that Matthew 18:20 ("where two or three are gathered together in my name I am there") shows Christ dwelling in community, and that Matthew 28:18-20 ("lo I am with you always") is the promise that God's spirit makes possible. He glosses the baptismal formula in Matthew 28:19 with Hebrew names (*Elohim*, *Emmanuel*, *ruach ha-kodesh*), a point the caption garbles, so the exact wording should be checked.

### Love of neighbour includes animals; diet as part of God's design

The host reads Matthew 22:36-40 (quoting Deuteronomy 6:4-5 and Leviticus 19:18) as summing up the law and the prophets, and then extends "neighbor" to animals: "when we think of who our neighbor is we don't want to be thinking well it's only humans." He reads Genesis 1:29 as God giving plants as food and Genesis 9:4 as forbidding eating flesh with blood, and says the reading is "kind of unmistakable" from the Hebrew. He connects this to Revelation 21:4 (no death, no suffering in the new creation) and Hosea 2:18 (a covenant with the beasts and the removal of weapons). He argues that killing and eating animals runs against God's will "from the beginning to the end." The talk does not engage with the scholarly reading of Genesis 9:4 or with competing interpretations of these texts.

### Practical outreach and witness

The host proposes concrete activities: building houses for the poor; feeding homeless people with vegan food; rescuing animals; starting groups where people share a vegan meal and watch an FNL talk; and striking up conversations with people met through charity work. He argues that personal conduct is itself a witness, citing Romans 12:2 ("stand apart from the world"), and warns against looking "just like the people in the world."

### Assembly as people, not a building or a weekly service

Responding to a viewer question about Hebrews 10:25, the host says that attending church once a week is good but not what the text means. "There's no church building in the context of this exhortation," he says, and "the people are the church." He cites Creation Care Church's own lack of a building (it records in a house church) and counts online participation, including the livestream, as assembling together.

### Foundations: hearing and doing Jesus' words

Replying to a commenter's "strong foundation" remark, the host reads Matthew 7:24-27, the wise and foolish builders, to argue that hearing Jesus' sayings is not enough and that they must be put into practice. He presents this as the proper foundation for the church.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The church is a building, and assembling means going to a service once a week.** The host says "the people are the church" and that Hebrews 10:25 describes people coming together (including online) for encouragement, not a building or a weekly service.
- **"Neighbor" means humans only.** The host argues Matthew 22:39 extends to animals, since God created the world for humans and animals together.
- **Scripture permits eating animals (Genesis 9:4).** The host reads Genesis 9:4 as forbidding eating flesh with blood, and says that is "kind of unmistakable" from the Hebrew. He does not engage with other readings of the verse.
- **Scripture verses about eating meat can be dismissed as irrelevant.** The host says he has handled such objections in earlier FNL talks and will address Elijah and the ravens (1 Kings 17) next week. He did not address that text in this talk.
