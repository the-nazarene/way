## Summary

The talk asks how a believer should respond when Satan appears to be influencing people, and how to protect oneself from him. Wescoe builds his answer from a chain of proof texts (Mark 8:33, Job 1-2, John 10, James 4:7, Psalm 91, Matthew 4, 1 Peter 5:8, Ephesians 6, Romans 12:14, Luke 23:34, Isaiah 54:17). His position is that the adversary behind harmful behavior is Satan, not the human beings who act on his behalf. Believers should therefore "rebuke" Satan, not the person, answer evil with blessing, test teachers by their fruit and by how they use scripture, and stand together in prayer and love. The main evidence is the scriptural sequence itself, read with brief commentary. Wescoe does not engage the historical or textual questions these passages raise.

About two-thirds of the way through, the stream turns into a live appeal. Wescoe says Creation Care Church is under "serious spiritual attack" and that Satan is trying to remove him as leader so the church can be scattered. He asks listeners not to send money and to offer prayer and moral support instead. Much of the closing hour consists of reading chat messages aloud and responding to them. Several of these concern the church's board, its governance and legal status, and whether particular people are aligned with Satan. Wescoe says he never names names, and the talk ends with a closing prayer asking God to "use me" and to defeat Satan.

## Key Teachings

### Rebuke the adversary, not the person
Wescoe reads Mark 8:33, where Jesus says "Get behind Me, Satan" to Peter. He argues that Jesus addressed Satan rather than Peter, so "whenever Satan is influencing a person to do Satan's bidding we should rebuke Satan" and seek to free the person from Satan's control. He extends this to Luke 23:34 (Jesus praying "Father, forgive them") and to Matthew 4, where Jesus rebukes the tempter. In his reading, the lesson is to identify the true adversary and pray for those who are being used by him.

### Satan as the adversary behind harm
Using Job 1:6-12, Wescoe presents Satan as "the opponent or the adversary" (he gives the Hebrew *hashatan*), a figure who roams the earth. He links this to 1 Peter 5:8 (the roaring lion), John 10:10 (the thief who comes "to steal and to kill and to destroy"), and Ephesians 6:12 (the struggle is "not against flesh and blood"). He reads these together as one picture: the thief, the lion and the tempter are the same spiritual enemy, and human opponents are his instruments.

### Divine protection and communal defense
Psalm 91 and Psalm 17:8-9 provide the image of God as a mother bird sheltering her young under his wings. Wescoe adds Matthew 26:53 (Jesus could call twelve legions of angels, but does not) and Isaiah 54:17 ("no weapon formed against you shall prosper"). He argues that God allows believers to suffer attack so that they will "come together as a community" and protect each other. He draws the same conclusion from Jesus's lament over Jerusalem, which he quotes without a verse reference, and from 1 John 4:4.

### Respond to enemies with blessing
Romans 12:14 ("Bless and do not curse") and Luke 23:34 are his main texts. Even when people curse believers and "steal and kill and destroy," Wescoe says the response is "kindness and mercy," not retaliation. The speaker presents this as Jesus's own example. Psalm 17 is also used for the image of "hot coals" falling on persecutors.

### Test the teacher by fruit and by scripture use
Wescoe cites 2 Corinthians 11:14-15 ("Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light") and notes that in Matthew 4 the tempter quotes Psalm 91. He concludes that "we want to make sure that we're not just listening to someone because they use scripture, but we want to make sure that they're using scripture the right way." He then invokes the saying about trees and fruit, giving no verse number, and applies it to people who bear bad fruit and "are serving" the adversary. These are the talk's sharpest claims about discerning others.

### Unity, prayer and the "unified" rebuke
James 4:7 ("resist the devil and he will flee from you") is central. Wescoe argues that Satan is weak against a united community and that a group prayer can drive him off. He leads the congregation in a spoken prayer (quoted in the talk at roughly 24:43 to 25:30) that declares solidarity with the church and its leader and rebukes Satan. He cites 1 John 4:8 ("God is love") to say that love is the weapon of choice.

### Framing the church dispute as spiritual warfare
The second half applies the scriptural frame to a live internal dispute. Wescoe says a church is "the people," not a legal entity, and that he has built the community over four years. Responses to board members and governance questions are read from the chat, and Wescoe responds to some of them. Wescoe states that he makes "negative money" from his talks and asks for no donations. Readers who want to assess the dispute itself will need sources beyond this transcript, since the talk gives only one side and the chat material is paraphrased.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Popular belief:** A person who opposes you is your enemy and should be fought directly.
  **Wescoe's correction:** Human opponents are "flesh and blood" and are being used by the adversary. The real target is Satan, and the appropriate response is prayer, blessing and forgiveness.
- **Popular belief:** Anyone who quotes scripture correctly can be trusted.
  **Wescoe's correction:** Satan himself quoted Psalm 91, and 2 Corinthians 11 warns of false ministers, so listeners must check whether scripture is used "the right way."
