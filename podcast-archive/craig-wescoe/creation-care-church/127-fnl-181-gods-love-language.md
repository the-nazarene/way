## Summary

Craig, host of this Creation Care Church "FNL" live talk, uses Gary Chapman's five love languages (quality time, physical touch, words of affirmation, gifts, acts of service; Chapman's book is dated 1992 in the description) as a frame for two questions: how God shows love to people, and how God wants people to show love back. He works through Bible passages on each category and eliminates them one by one.

His answer is that God's primary love language toward people is gifts, citing James 1:17 and Romans 6:23, and that the love God asks for in return is acts of service, meaning keeping his commandments, citing John 14:15 and John 13:15. ## Key Teachings

### 1. The love-language frame and the method of elimination
Craig assigns a Bible passage to each of Chapman's five categories. Quality time with God is prayer (1 Thessalonians 5:17; Jeremiah 33:3; Psalm 16:11), but he says that prayer "is for our own benefit," since God is everywhere (Psalm 139:8). He says this is not God's primary request. He then tests the other categories in turn and concludes that God's main love language toward us is gifts, and that the main way he asks us to love him is acts of service. He says that "you could probably figure it out by process of elimination."

### 2. Physical touch rejected (John 20; Hosea 12:3)
Craig reads Hosea 12:3 as Jacob wrestling with God and offers three readings: an angel, a theophany, or an internal struggle. He leans toward an angel because of the injured hip. He then reads the Mary Magdalene scene ("Don't cling to me") and the Thomas scene (John 20:24-31) as showing that God does not want to be touched. He reads Jesus's line "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" as "Blessed are those who don't even need to touch me to know that I'm real." He says the Thomas passage is part of an original ending of John, and he notes that "the scholarly majority" holds that John 21 was added later. He treats that majority view as persuasive without naming any scholar.

### 3. Words of affirmation rejected (Matthew 15:7-8; Matthew 7:21-29)
Craig says praise is good but "not his primary love language." He cites Matthew 15:7-8, quoting Isaiah 29:13 about honoring God "with their lips" while "their heart is far from me." He then reads Matthew 7:21-29 (the "Lord, Lord" passage and the house built on rock) to argue that saying "Lord, Lord" does not earn entry to the kingdom. He treats the parable of the talents (Matthew 25) as an example of what God does affirm: faithful service, not words.

### 4. Gifts as God's primary love toward us, sacrifice rejected (Isaiah 1:11; Psalm 51:16-17; Jeremiah 7; James 1:17-18)
Craig says the gift language is the one most religions attribute to God, and that they wrongly think God wants animal blood. He cites Isaiah 1:11 ("I do not delight in the blood of bulls or lambs or goats") and Psalm 51:16-17 ("a broken and contrite heart") to argue that God never required sacrifice. He presents two claims without sources in the talk: that his work on the Book of Enoch shows animal sacrifice "has a demonic origin," and that Jeremiah found an older Torah scroll without animal sacrifices, which he says a "lying scribe" later altered. He then cites James 1:17-18 ("Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above") and says God's gift of life, existence, and the Holy Spirit is his main form of love toward us.

### 5. Acts of service as the love we owe God (John 14:15; John 13:15; Matthew 20:28; Matthew 7:21)
Craig's central claim is that God's requested love is obedience in action. He cites John 14:15 ("If you love me, then keep my commandments"), John 13:15 ("I have given you an example"), and Matthew 20:28 ("the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve"). He argues that Jesus's own sacrifice was a once-for-all gift that does not call for human imitation, and that the call to disciples is "Follow me," which is an action. He treats the gifts God gives as a "talent" people must put to use. He says the criterion is "doing the will of my Father," citing Matthew 7:21.

### 6. Animal welfare, diet, and the "least of these" (Isaiah 11:9; Genesis 1:29; Genesis 49:6)
In the chat Q&A, Craig treats the rule against harming animals as a wider principle than diet. He cites Isaiah 11:9 (the Hebrew "lo ra'ah," "they will neither hurt nor destroy"), and Genesis 1:29 as "the biggest one" of human harm to animals, which he says is diet. He also reads Genesis 49:6 (quoted by a chat member) on bulls crippled for sport. He applies "whatever you do to the least of these" (Matthew 25; the verse number is not given) to animals, saying that if Jesus were in the slaughterhouse or the laboratory, a follower should act against those places rather than pay to support them.

### 7. Grace and works: a critique of "carnal Christians" (Romans 6:22-23)
Responding to a chat remark about "saved by grace," Craig says the sentence after the grace passage (which he does not name by book) says "let us do the good works." He says "that's not even what Paul is saying," and he cites Romans 6:22-23 ("the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord") as a free gift that requires a transformed heart to receive. He calls the faith-only reading a misreading of Paul and says Jesus's Sermon on the Mount rules it out. He also uses the word "repentance" (metanoia) to mean changing one's mind and direction, not only confession.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That God wants animal sacrifice.** Craig says the prophets (Isaiah 1, Psalm 51, Jeremiah 7) repeatedly reject it, and that the practice has a "demonic origin" (his claim, based on his Enoch work). He treats this as the common view of religions generally.
- **That praise is God's primary love language.** Craig says words of affirmation alone are "lip service" (Matthew 15:7-8).
- **That physical contact with God is a form of devotion.** He reads the Mary and Thomas passages as God discouraging touch as a way of relating.
- **That faith alone, without works, is Paul's teaching.** Craig says the grace passage is followed by a call to good works and that the faith-only reading is a misunderstanding of Paul. He directs this at "carnal Christians."

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** Craig uses Matthew 7 and John 14 for Jesus's teaching on doing the commandments, then uses Romans 6 and his reading of the grace passage to say Paul agrees.
