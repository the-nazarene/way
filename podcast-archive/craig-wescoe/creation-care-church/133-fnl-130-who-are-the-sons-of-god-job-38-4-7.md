## Summary

The host asks who the "sons of God" (Hebrew *bene Elohim*) are in the Bible, starting from Job 38:4-7, where "all the sons of God shouted for joy" at creation. He argues that the phrase has several distinct senses across Scripture: heavenly or divine beings who form a council with God (Job 1-2, Psalm 82:6, Genesis 6:1-4); human beings (Adam in Luke 3:38, Solomon in 1 Chronicles 28:6, and Israel in Hosea 1:10); Jesus as the unique "only begotten Son" who is the creative Word of God (Hebrews 1, John 1, Luke 1:35); and believers who are made children of God through faith and the Spirit (1 John 3:1-2, Matthew 5:9, Galatians 3:26-28, Romans 8:14-22).

The host's method is to lay out passages in canonical order and connect them by shared vocabulary (*Elohim*, *Ruach Elohim*, "word," "image and likeness"). He reads Genesis 1 and the Johannine prologue together to argue that Jesus is the Word through whom creation was spoken, and he uses Romans 8 and 2 Chronicles 7:14 to describe a future in which creation is restored. The talk ends with a Q&A on the chat. In answers, the host applies the sons-of-God reading to a vegan ethic, arguing that eating animal flesh makes a person responsible for the animal's death, and he addresses the Book of Enoch's place in the canon. The talk closes by announcing next week's session on Paul (2 Peter 3:15-16).

The talk is confessional and devotional rather than historical-critical. Its positions are the host's own, and it does not discuss the original meaning of *bene Elohim* in its ancient setting or the dating of the texts it harmonizes.

### Key Teachings

### 1. Heavenly "sons of God" form a divine council, and some fell
The host reads Job 38:4-7 and Job 1:6 and 2:1 as showing divine beings present with God, whom he calls a "divine council." He cites Psalm 82:6 ("You are gods... children of the most high") and mentions Deuteronomy 32 without developing it. He reads Genesis 6:1-4 as describing some of these beings disobeying God by taking human wives, producing the Nephilim, and says this led to the flood. He describes these beings as having "the capacity to obey God and to disobey God." In the Q&A he distinguishes faithful heavenly sons (Gabriel, Michael, Uriel, Raphael) from fallen ones, citing Ezekiel's passage about the king of Tyre (verse not given).

### 2. Human beings called sons of God
He lists three human cases: Adam, called "the son of God" in Luke 3:38 and tied to Genesis 1:26-27 ("in the image of Elohim"); Solomon, whom God calls "my son" in 1 Chronicles 28:6; and the people of Israel, addressed as "sons of the living God" in Hosea 1:10. He concludes that the term covers both heavenly and earthly beings.

### 3. Jesus as the Word of God and the only begotten Son
The host argues that Hebrews 1:1-3 calls Jesus the "express image" of God and the one through whom the worlds were made. He ties this to the spoken word of Genesis 1:1-3 and to John 1:1-3, 12-14. He reads Luke 1:35 as naming the child "the Son of God," and John 3:16-17 as describing a Son sent to save a world that God made. He claims that Jesus says "I am" throughout John, and that this is the Word "declaring God" (citing John 1:18 and an unspecified Exodus passage). He holds that Jesus, unlike Adam and the heavenly beings, "will not ever sin." The argument here is theological and harmonizing: it reads the whole Bible as one plan spoken from Genesis onward.

### 4. Believers become children of God through faith and the Spirit
Using 1 John 3:1-2 and Matthew 5:9 ("Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God"), he defines the believer's identity in terms of the attribute of peacemaking, which he calls an active practice of living by God's will. He then cites Galatians 3:26-28 to say that baptism into Christ makes believers "sons of God" and "one in Christ," and Romans 8:14-22 to say that creation "eagerly waits" for the revealing of the sons of God. He links this to 2 Chronicles 7:14 (forgiveness and healing of the land) and to a restoration of creation to its original "very good" state.

### 5. The Genesis diet and a vegan reading of sin
The host reads Genesis 1:29-30 (though he paraphrases it, citing the "fruit of the tree" and "plants of the ground" as food) and argues that the original human diet was plant-based. He concludes that people who eat animal products are "responsible for the killing of that animal," and that this is a sin requiring repentance. He cites Galatians 5:22-23 (fruit of the Spirit) and says believers cannot bear those fruits toward people while doing the opposite toward animals. He also argues that farmed animals are "betrayed" when they are sent to slaughter, comparing it to the "Judas goat" in slaughterhouses, and says there is "no such thing as humane slaughter."

### 6. The Book of Enoch and the canon
In answer to a viewer, the host says Enoch is in the canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church but not in Roman Catholic or most Protestant Bibles. He says Augustine, in the late fourth century, was unsure which parts of Enoch were genuine. Augustine followed what he describes as rabbinic Judaism's exclusion of Enoch, even though Jude quotes Enoch. The host says that most Christians in the first two centuries accepted Enoch as scripture. He refers viewers to an earlier live talk for the full argument.

### Misconceptions Addressed

- **Enoch was never part of the Bible.** The host says Enoch has been canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and was accepted by most early Christians, and that its exclusion from Catholic and most Protestant Bibles came from Augustine's decision in the fourth century.
- **"Humane" animal farming makes eating meat acceptable.** The host argues that "there is no such thing as humane slaughter," that most farmed animals are also abused, and that even kindly raised animals are "betrayed" when sent to slaughter.
- **Matthew 13's "them" refers to a specific group.** He reads "them" as anyone who hears Jesus's teaching and does not recognize the voice of God, not a fixed group or a physical disability.
- **Paul is anti-vegetarian.** He raises this as a claim made in the *Conspiracy* film and says his group disagreed with that reading, but he does not argue the point in this talk and defers to next week's Paul session.
