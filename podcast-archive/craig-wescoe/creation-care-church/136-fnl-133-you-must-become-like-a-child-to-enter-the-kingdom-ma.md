## Summary

The talk asks what Jesus meant in Matthew 18:3-4 when he says people must "change and become as little children" to enter the kingdom of heaven, which Scriptures he had in mind, and why this matters to Christians and non-Christians. The unnamed host, who runs the Creation Care Church Friday Night Live series, answers that childlikeness means humility and obedience to God's word, and that the content of that obedience is the original design in Genesis 1, which the host reads as vegan: humans eat plants, animals are not killed, and "dominion" means stewardship rather than control. Isaiah 11, where a little child leads peaceably reconciled animals, is offered as the prophetic picture of the restored kingdom. ## Key Teachings

### 1. Childlikeness is humility and obedience
The host takes Matthew 18:3-4 ("unless you are converted or change and become as Little Children...whoever humbles himself as this little child is the greatest") as the key text. He links it to the saying "let the little children come to me" (cited as Matthew 19:4, see the table) and says that humility is the defining characteristic of a child. He then describes a child as one who "humbles himself and uh listens to the voice of God and follows and walks uh according to this voice," comparing it to an obedient child following good parents. Pride is the opposite: making up one's own rules and doing "whatever I want."

### 2. Creation as God's kingdom, and the original diet
The host argues that Genesis 1 is already God's kingdom and that Isaiah 11 describes its future restoration. In Genesis 1:26-31 he reads "dominion" as a blessing that includes eating "the fruit of the tree and the plants of the ground," and says God called the arrangement "very good." He contrasts this with a "Fallen sense of dominion" in which humans control and dominate. He adds that a child in a pen with a rabbit and strawberries would not think of killing the rabbit, and calls on readers to have "that thought" never cross their minds.

### 3. Jesus as the Word, and the call to listen
The host equates Jesus with the "word of God" that speaks creation into being in Genesis 1, citing John 1:14 ("the word of God became flesh and dwelt Among Us"). He reads John 10:27-30 ("my sheep hear my voice... I and my father are one") to mean that listening to Jesus is listening to God, and that nothing can snatch believers away from that obedience except their own choice to follow "the voice of the adversary." He also reads John 8:12 as walking in the light by following Jesus rather than "the desires of your flesh."

### 4. Love as the hallmark, and the fruits of the Spirit
Colossians 3:12-15 and Galatians 5:16-23 are read as describing the "elect of God" who show tender mercies, kindness, meekness and love. The host says that love, joy and peace are the first three fruits of the Spirit, and argues that these cannot be lived "without being vegan": paying for cruelty to animals is a failure of love, sending animals to slaughter removes joy, and violence toward animals breaks peace. He uses 1 John 4:8,16 ("God is love") and 1 Corinthians 13 to support the claim that love is God's primary attribute. Micah 6:8 is read the same way: "to do justly" means walking in God's ways of righteousness, and "to love mercy" includes mercy to animals. He notes that Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6 ("I desire Mercy not sacrifice").

### 5. Good deeds mixed with cruelty
Using Proverbs 12 ("the kindest acts of the wicked are cruel") and James 1:17 ("every good gift...from above"), the host argues that good works done with non-vegan food are "marred" by cruelty. He gives the example of feeding the hungry with pepperoni pizza and calls it "two good things instead of a good thing and a bad thing" if vegan food were used. In the Q&A he names organizations he says send farm animals to the poor (Hunters for the Hungry, the Heifer Project, World Vision, and Sojourners magazine, the last of which he says "regularly calls for support"). He describes these as "deceptions of the adversary" and says they "hinder" the kingdom even as they help.

### 6. Seeking the kingdom, repentance and God's covenant with animals
The host reads Matthew 6:33 ("seek first the kingdom of God") as the centre of the Christian life and 2 Chronicles 7:14 as the promise of forgiveness and healing for people who humble themselves and "turn from their wicked ways." He tells the story of the prodigal son (Luke 15) as a model for repentance and celebration, and says that "the best case scenario" for people who harm animals is that they change. To the claim that God does not care about animals he replies with Genesis 9:16 (the "Everlasting Covenant" between God and "every living creature") and Revelation 5:13, where creatures praise God. He concludes that "we know for certain" there will be a restored kingdom in which "there will be a vegan world."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Becoming like a child" means acting childish, like a tantrum-throwing toddler.** The host opens with a joke about vegans being told to eat vegetables and says that this is "not what Jesus had in mind"; the childlike virtue is humility and obedience.
- **"Dominion" means control and domination over animals.** The host says this is the "Fallen sense of dominion"; the true dominion is stewardship that includes eating plants.
- **Some commandments no longer apply or can be separated out.** He says people "try to separate things... say well that doesn't apply anymore," and insists "the will of God does not change."
- **God does not care about animals.** The host answers with Genesis 9:16 (the covenant with every living creature) and Revelation 5:13 (creatures praising God).
- **Charitable giving of meat is an unmixed good.** He argues that good deeds done with cruelty are "marred" and that feeding the hungry with animal products "hinders" the kingdom.
