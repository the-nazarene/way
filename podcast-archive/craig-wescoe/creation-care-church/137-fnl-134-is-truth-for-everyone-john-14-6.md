## Summary

The host asks whether Christians should present Jesus as objective truth, or whether truth is subjective, and how a believer should answer someone who says they do not need the Bible to know what is right or true. The talk is a "Friday Night Live" session from Creation Care Church, with a live Q&A at the end.

The host argues that truth is singular and objective and that Jesus is that truth. He reads John 14:6 ("I am the way, the truth and the life") as a singular claim that does not allow for "your truth" or "my truth." He treats John 8:32 and 8:44, Genesis 3, Colossians 2:8 and Romans 12:2 as showing that deception, from "the father of lies," is the alternative to that truth. His main argument for diet is that Jesus, as the Word through whom all things were made (John 1:1-3), gave the original instructions in Genesis 1:26-31, which he reads as a plant-based diet for humans and animals. He supports this with Malachi 3:6, Hebrews 13:8 and Isaiah 46:10 (God does not change) and with Matthew 7's "good tree, good fruit" test and Luke 12:42-44's faithful steward.

The Q&A brings up misuses of John 8:32 to justify eating animals, the ending of Matthew 7:23 ("depart from me, you who practice lawlessness"), the Fall, and whether Christians outside the tradition can be saved. On the last point the host says it is "God's job" to judge individuals and that believers should simply lead people to follow Jesus' instructions. The talk closes with prayer and a preview of next week's talk on Psalm 51.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Truth is singular and objective, and "truth is relative" refutes itself

The host reads John 14:6 as a statement that "implies that truth is objective," since Jesus does not say "I am truth according to your perspective." He then argues that the claim "truth is relative" assumes an objective standard for at least that statement, so if it is objectively true it contradicts itself, and if it is only subjectively true it is not a claim anyone else must accept. Supporting texts include John 4:24 (worship "in spirit and truth") and John 8:32 ("you shall know the truth and the truth shall make you free").

### 2. Relativism is the adversary's deception

Using John 8:44 and the serpent's words in Genesis 3, the host presents the deceiver as the "father of lies" who makes objective truth look optional. He says that once people doubt that objective truth exists, "all the deceptions" are placed "on equal footing with the truth." Colossians 2:8 and Romans 12:2 are cited against "human philosophies" and "the world." The host also takes slogans such as "live my truth" and "follow your heart" as examples of this deception, contrasting them with Proverbs 12:22 and Jeremiah-style warnings about the heart (he paraphrases "the heart is exceedingly wicked" without giving a reference).

### 3. The fruit test and the narrow gate (Matthew 7 and Galatians)

The host reads the Sermon on the Mount's narrow gate (Matthew 7:13-14), the Golden Rule as the summary of "the law and the prophets" (Matthew 7:12), and the test of false prophets "by their fruits" (Matthew 7:15-20). He equates the good fruit with the "fruit of the Spirit" in Galatians 5:22-23, calls Galatians 6:7-8 a principle of sowing and reaping, and says that bearing good fruit is how believers should examine themselves. These are the host's readings. He does not compare them with other scholarship or with earlier Jewish or Christian interpretations.

### 4. Hearing versus doing: the wise builder and the faithful steward

The host reads the end of the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 7:21-29) and says that calling Jesus "Lord, Lord" is not enough. Doing "the will of my Father" is required, and Jesus "taught them as one having Authority." Luke 12:42-44 is read as a faithful steward who is already doing the master's commands when the master returns. The host concludes that believers who wait for Jesus to fix everything, rather than obeying him now, have misread the parable.

### 5. Genesis 1 as the original instruction, read as a plant-based diet

This is the central argument for the talk's conclusion about diet. The host says the Word (John 1:1-3) who created all things is the speaker in Genesis 1. He reads Genesis 1:26-31 as God giving humans "dominion" with "every herb that yields seed" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed" for food, and he takes "dominion" to mean loving stewardship that does not kill animals. He adds Genesis 2:15 (tending the garden), Hosea 2:18 and Genesis 9 (which he says mention a covenant of peace with animals), and Isaiah 11:6-9 (peaceable animals) as later confirmation. He says the instruction is "literally like one page" and that the claim that God changed it after the Fall is false. These are his interpretations, and the talk does not engage with other readings of Genesis 1 or 9.

### 6. An unchanging God and the "objective truth" that does not change

To answer the claim that God's instructions changed, the host cites Malachi 3:6 ("I am the Lord, I do not change"), Hebrews 13:8 ("Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever"), and Isaiah 46:10 ("I declare the end from the beginning"). He argues that the beginning (Genesis 1) and the end (Isaiah 11) are the same truth. He cites 2 Peter 3:9 to argue that God wants "all" to repent, not only "some," so the truth applies to everyone.

### 7. Q&A: animals, salvation and judgment

- **Animals and "the truth shall make you free":** The host says people quote John 8:32 while eating animal products, and he argues that the truth should also free animals. He also reads Isaiah 58 (breaking the yoke) and Psalm 124:7 as referring to animals in their literal sense.
- **Matthew 7:23 and the ending of the verse:** He says many leave off "you who practice lawlessness," and that this shows the judgment is about habitual sin, not doctrinal mistakes.
- **Exclusivism:** Asked about John 14:6 and people in other traditions, he says he cannot answer in detail, that the judgment is God's, and that the task is to point people to Jesus' instructions.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Truth is relative" or "live your truth":** The host says the claim undermines itself if it is offered as objectively true, and that it is the deceiver's way of putting lies on equal footing with the truth.
- **God changed his mind after the Fall:** He answers with Malachi 3:6, Hebrews 13:8 and Isaiah 46:10, arguing that God's instructions from Genesis 1 still stand.
- **Matthew 7:23 is about doctrine:** He says the verse ends with "you who practice lawlessness," so it is about choosing sin over salvation. He also says people often leave this part out.
- **John 8:32 justifies eating animals:** He says people misuse the verse this way, and that the freedom it promises should extend to animals.
- **Saying "Lord, Lord" is enough:** He says Matthew 7:21-23 requires doing the Father's will.
- **Veganism is too burdensome:** He says Genesis 1's instruction is short and that plant-based food is now easy to get.
