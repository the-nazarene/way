## Summary

Craig, presenting for Creation Care Church, asks what the "key of knowledge" in Luke 11:52 is and who holds it. He argues that the question is answered in the Clementine Homilies, which he treats as Peter's teaching as recorded by Clement, and that the key is a test for Scripture: a passage is true if it is "in harmony with the creation" (Homily 3.42) and false if it contradicts the original creation, with Genesis 1 and 2 as the baseline. He also argues that the scribes and Pharisees held this key and hindered others from using it (Matthew 23:13; Luke 11:52), and that the animal sacrifices and related commands are later falsehoods added by "lying scribes" (Homilies 2.38, 3.45, 3.46). His support is a chain of quotations from the Homilies, Jesus sayings in the Gospels, and Hebrew prophets such as Jeremiah 7:22-23, Isaiah 1:11, Psalm 40:6 and Hosea 6:6.

Craig's case rests mainly on internal consistency: the Homilies, the Synoptic sayings he chooses, and Jeremiah and Isaiah are read together, and he takes Jesus' "not one jot or tittle" saying (Matthew 5:17-18) as proof that anything Jesus set aside was never part of the law. He presents the practical upshot as diet (the plant-based food of Genesis 1:29), non-violence, and humility toward creation. He notes that the Homilies also say the hidden teaching should be given only to the discerning, which he uses to explain why this material is not publicly stated.

The talk ends with a live Q&A in which Craig answers objections, including the argument that Genesis 9 permits eating animals and the question of whether God changes his mind. He says that whether one reads these passages as added falsehoods or as a concession to "the hardness of our hearts," the beginning of creation is the standard to follow. He closes by previewing the final talk in the series on "the table of demons," which he says will be explained from the Homilies because the New Testament says little about it.

## Key Teachings

### The key as authority over the door
Craig begins with Isaiah 22:22 ("What he opens, no one can shut"), Revelation 3:7-8 (which he says Jesus holds, the key of David), and Matthew 16:19, where Jesus gives Peter "the keys of the kingdom of heaven." He describes a transmission chain of Jesus to Peter to Clement, and argues that the Homilies are where one would expect the "elaboration" on the key because Peter received it. He says one would not expect to find it in Paul's letters, John's letters, or the Gospels.

### The scribes and Pharisees held the key and blocked entry
Using Homily 3.18 and Homily 18.15, Craig argues that the scribes and Pharisees possessed the key of knowledge but hid it and "did not allow the people to enter." He reads Matthew 23:2-3 (they "sit in Moses' seat," but do not practice what they preach) and Matthew 23:13 alongside Luke 11:52 as confirmation. Using Homily 18.16 and Matthew 25:29, he says knowledge is "taken away" from those who do not use it and given to the "worthy," including "babes" (Matthew 11:25). He presents the apostles as the "unlettered" recipients.

### Harmony with creation as the test of Scripture
This is the core principle, from Homily 3.42: scriptural sayings that agree with the creation made by God are true; those contrary to it are false. Craig points to Genesis 1 and 2 as the description of the good creation: humans as God's image, a plant-based diet, creatures kept as herbivores, and the Sabbath. He cites Jesus' answer on divorce in Matthew 19:3-8 as a model. Jesus, Craig says, rejects the Mosaic permission as due to "the hardness of your hearts" and returns to "the beginning," and so the method is to go back to creation rather than to later law.

### Falsehoods added by lying scribes
Craig's main claim is that the Homilies (Homilies 2.38, 3.46) say the written Torah was corrupted by falsehoods "against God," inserted by people who "were not prophets" but liars. He supports this with Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribes"). Using Homily 2.44 and Homily 3.45, he argues that God never commanded sacrifice: he cites Jeremiah 7:22-23 ("I did not speak to your fathers... concerning burnt offerings or sacrifices"), Psalm 40:6, and the Numbers 11:34 story of Kibroth-hattaavah. He cites Isaiah 1:11 and Homily 3.26's list of God's traits (God "hates sacrifices, bloodshed, and libations"). Homily 3.56 and Hosea 6:6 are linked to Matthew 9:13 and Matthew 12:7 ("I desire mercy, not sacrifice"). Craig acknowledges that this is a strong claim, and he says it is supported by "the scriptural basis."

### The false image of God and the Holy Spirit
Homily 18.19 and Homily 2.40 are used to argue that conforming to a false picture of God (one who loves sacrifice, war, or murder) leads people to act badly and to reject the Holy Spirit. Craig reads this through the saying attributed to Jesus in the Gospel of Thomas 44, Luke 12:10 and Matthew 12:32 on blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. He argues that errors about the Father and Son are forgivable if one still receives the Spirit, but a wrong view that leads one to reject the Spirit's fruit is the danger.

### Discernment, hidden teaching, and the temple
In Homily 2.51 Peter says Jesus taught "Be ye good money changers" (which Craig says is not in the canonical Gospels but is attested "by some other sources," unnamed), and Craig reads this with Matthew 22:29 as Jesus' rebuke of the Sadducees for not understanding "the scriptures nor the power of God." Using Homily 3.51 and Matthew 5:17-18, he argues that Jesus removed only what never belonged to the law. He links Homily 3.52 with Hebrews 10:18 and Matthew 15:13 (plants not planted by the Father will be uprooted) to argue that false additions will be exposed. Homily 2.39 is used to explain why Peter says he will not state publicly that chapters were "added to the Bible," since the unlearned would be confused. Craig ties this to Matthew 13:10-12 and to Jesus' cleansing of the temple as an act of using the key.

### Diet, dominion, and practical application
Craig applies the test to diet. He reads Genesis 1:28 (dominion) together with Genesis 1:29 (the plants of the ground as food), and argues that dominion means stewardship under God's instructions rather than license to kill. He ties humility to the key, citing Jesus' "came not to be served, but to serve," and presents the aim of confronting wrongdoing as repentance, citing the rejoicing over one sinner who repents. He raises Genesis 8-9 as a problem and, in Q&A, presents several readings (God's changed mind, a temporary concession after the flood, a sign of the lying scribe) and says the practical conclusion is the same: return to the beginning of creation.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God's perfect will includes animal sacrifice, as commanded in Leviticus.** Craig says God "never commanded sacrifices" (Homily 3.45) and that Jeremiah 7:22-23 and Psalm 40:6 confirm this; the sacrifice laws are later falsehoods.
- **Genesis 1:28 gives humans license to dominate and kill animals.** Craig says dominion is stewardship under God's stated instructions in Genesis 1:29 (plant food).
- **Christians can say sacrifices ended while the law stayed the same.** Craig argues that if the law is unchanged (Matthew 5:17-18), the sacrifices were never part of the law.
- **Jesus abolished the law.** Craig says Jesus came to "fulfill" the law and dispel the lies of "the liar and his scribes" (1 John 3:8).
- **God changed his mind after the flood to permit eating animals (Genesis 9).** Craig says that reading would contradict the beginning of creation, so it should be read as an addition, a temporary concession, or a concession to "hardness of heart" rather than as God's ideal will.
- **Vegans worship creation, a pagan view.** Craig replies that vegans worship the Creator who gave the plant diet and commands mercy, and that the accusation shows a wrong view of God.
