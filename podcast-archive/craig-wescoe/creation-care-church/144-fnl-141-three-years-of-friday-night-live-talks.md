## Summary

Wescoe marks the close of Creation Care Church's third year of Friday Night Live (FNL) talks by reviewing the church's history, its volunteer roles, its talk series, and its finances, then opening Q&A. He says the church formed in 2021 when six ministries focused on animal welfare and discipleship merged, and that the first 40 live talks were built from his study guides on biblical objections to veganism. He lists later series and projects: a "vegan Christian discipleship school," history-slideshow talks on the Book of Enoch, the forbidden fruit, and the Clementine homilies, and a review of the film *Christspiracy*. Wescoe presents these as his own research and invites disagreement; the talk does not test the sources it cites.

Much of the talk is administrative. Two exchanges touch on substance: a viewer's claim that Enoch is "not from God," which Wescoe answers by pointing to Jude and to his own talk, and a discussion of how Paul's food advice applies to church life.

## Key Teachings

### Plant-based diet as God's original design
Wescoe argues that Genesis 1:29 is the baseline for human diet: "eat the fruit of the tree and the plants of the ground," followed by God seeing everything as "very good." He says this is "the very good diet that God designed us for," and that it should guide how people treat both animals and neighbors. He cites Isaiah 11:6-9 and Luke 6:36 as recurring support. In the Q&A he answers a Noah objection (that meat was needed after the flood) by reasoning that the dove's freshly plucked olive branch implies fruiting trees, so vegetation was available. This is a rebuttal he gives from a street-outreach video, not from a text analysis in this talk.

### "I desire mercy and not sacrifice"
Wescoe reads Matthew 12:7 as Jesus's citation of Hosea 6:6, telling listeners to "go and learn what this means." He reads it as a rejection of animal sacrifice in favor of mercy. The talk list refers to a talk asking "why did God instruct animal sacrifice" (no. 8) and a study guide on whether God "ever desire or require animal sacrifice" (12 pages). Wescoe does not walk through the Hebrew or the Hosea context in this transcript.

### The forbidden fruit as flesh and blood
In a past talk, Wescoe argues that the forbidden fruit of Genesis was most likely the "Flesh and Blood of grapes," which he says symbolically represents the flesh and blood of animals, so that the forbidden food is animal flesh and blood. He says the case rests on "all the scripture verses that I think support my reading," and he concedes that "20 different sources" give different answers and that "who knows when will actually know what it really is." Here he presents this as a contested interpretation.

### The Clementine homilies: key of knowledge and table of devils
Wescoe summarizes a series from the past month. He reads Luke 11:52 ("key of knowledge") as Jesus hinting that some Torah commands arose "because of the hardness of your heart" and that "from the beginning it was not so" (Matthew 19:8 is not cited; this is his paraphrase of Jesus). He says the homilies teach that God's pre-fall creation is the standard for truth: "whatever is in harmony with that initial creation that God called very good... that's what we should be looking to as our standard for truth." For the "table of devils" (introduced as 1 Corinthians 10:21), he says the list of things to abstain from in the homilies (idol food, carcasses, animals killed by wild beasts, blood) amounts to "eating flesh and blood." He notes that the passage he is using is in the homilies rather than in 1 Corinthians, though he introduced it as 1 Corinthians 10:21.

### The Book of Enoch and the meaning of "canon"
Wescoe argues that Jude 1:14-15 calls Enoch "seventh from Adam" the "authentic testimony" of Enoch, and that other New Testament passages assume its contents. He says Jesus also quoted Enoch (his claim: "you don't know the scriptures" about angels not marrying), so dismissing Enoch means dismissing Jesus or Jude. He argues that the canon should serve as a "measuring stick," and that churches that dismiss Enoch do so without engaging the early historical evidence. He says he will return to Enoch in the coming year. The transcript shows no reading of Enoch itself or of the early church writers he mentions.

### Paul's food teaching and "understanding Paul"
Wescoe says a past talk introduced "the complexities of understanding Paul," including the Greek manuscript layers and Paul's "dialectical method." He says readers who rely on modern English translations "get these sort of widely different views of Paul." In the Q&A, a commenter (or Wescoe responding to one, the transcript is unclear) quotes Paul's advice about food causing a brother to stumble and says "I will no longer eat meat." The verse is not given. Wescoe says "if more people just took the advice in Paul," churches would become more welcoming. He also refers to Romans 14 as the basis of a past talk on "weak faith."
