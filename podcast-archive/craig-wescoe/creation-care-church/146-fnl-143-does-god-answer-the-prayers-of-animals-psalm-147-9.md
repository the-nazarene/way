## Summary

The talk asks whether God answers the prayers of animals, starting from Psalm 147:9 ("He provides food for the cattle and for the young ravens when they call"). The host answers yes. He argues that Scripture shows God hearing animal cries, providing for animals, and treating them as valuable, and that God's original design for creation was peaceable and plant-based. Humans, in his reading, hold a stewardship role (dominion) that should be exercised like God's open-handed provision, not exploitation. He says the biblical promises of a restored creation (Hosea 2:18, Isaiah 11:6-9, Revelation 21:4, Romans 8:18-22) show God's concern for animals, and that the same concern should shape Christian conduct now.

## Key Teachings

### 1. God hears and provides for animal cries
The host reads Psalm 147:9 as the "first indication" that God is concerned with animals, and he links it to the general principle that God hears prayers (he notes that "the prayers of the righteous he hears"). He pairs it with Job 38:41 (God provides food for young ravens when they cry) and Psalm 104:27-28 (God gives food "in due season" and "opens your hand"). He argues that hunger is the most basic prayer, and that animals "crying out" for food are praying in the same way humans do when they are most in need.

### 2. Matthew 6:26 presupposes animal value
Against non-vegan readings that use Matthew 6:26 to say animals matter less, the host argues that the "you are of more value" comparison "wouldn't even work if humans didn't matter to God" and only works "if the animals do matter to God." He also reads the first half (birds neither sow nor reap, yet are fed) as a teaching that God provides for creatures who trust him.

### 3. Dominion as stewardship, and the original plant diet
The host reads Genesis 1:26-30 as God giving humans "dominion" over animals and assigning everyone a plant-based diet (Genesis 1:29-30: "every herb that yields seed" and "every green herb for food"). He says the arrangement "God says is very good" was harmonious, with no creature killing another, and that humans are "in charge of maintaining that very good arrangement." He ties this to Luke 12:42-44, the "faithful and wise Steward" who gives food "in due season," and concludes that humans are "quite literally the hands and feet of God" in feeding animals. He defines "faithful" as trusting God and following his instructions, and "wise" as hearing Jesus's words and putting them into practice.

### 4. Balaam's donkey as a voice and a witness
In Numbers 22:28-33 the host argues that the donkey's words express what "every animal" would say to humans about abuse, and that the donkey acted "out of love for balum" even when beaten. He reads the episode as an example of Christlike love toward enemies and as a hope that "the cries of animals in slaughterhouse lines" will open the eyes of Christians who are "blinded."

### 5. Promised restoration of the animals
He reads Hosea 2:18 (a covenant with "the beasts of the field" and the shattering of weapons "from the Earth"), Isaiah 11:6-9 (predator and prey living together, with "a little child" leading by example), and Revelation 21:4 (God wiping away every tear, "no more death") as promises that include animals. He argues that Romans 8:18-22 ("the whole creation groans") shows that animals are waiting alongside the "children of God" for the restoration of the kingdom, and that Matthew 18:3-4 calls believers to change and become humble "as little children."

### 6. Prayer according to God's will, and the Kingdom prayer
He reads 1 John 5:14 ("if we ask anything according to his will he hears us") as applying to animals: their need for food and water is "according to his will," and so God hears them. He then links this to Matthew 6:10 ("your kingdom come, your will be done on Earth as it is in heaven") and argues that Christians should align their conduct with God's will, including "not dropping bombs on each other" and "not putting them in slaughterhouse lines."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Matthew 6:26 means animals don't matter to God.** The host argues the verse presupposes animal value: the comparison "you are of more value" works only if animals matter to God.
- **Human dominion permits exploitation.** The host reads dominion (Genesis 1:26-28) as a role of stewardship and loving provision, and says "ungodly Dominion" is a misuse of the role.
- **Being in God's image means having a human form.** The host says the image of God is "more to do with our character and our role" and does not describe a physical likeness.
- **Heaven includes animal eating or slaughter.** In the Q&A the host says the evidence is "overwhelmingly in our favor" against slaughterhouses in heaven and that only "a couple passages about a temple" could be raised, which he finds insufficient.
