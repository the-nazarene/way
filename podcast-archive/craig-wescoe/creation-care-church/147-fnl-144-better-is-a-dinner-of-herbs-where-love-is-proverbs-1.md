## Summary

The host asks why Proverbs 15:17 says "a dinner of herbs where love is" is better than "a fatted calf with hatred," and why herbs would be tied to love and flesh-eating to hatred. He argues that the verse belongs to a wider pattern in Proverbs (10:11-12, 12:10) in which violence toward animals and people is linked with hatred and strife, and kindness to animals with righteousness. From Genesis 1:29-31 he concludes that a plant diet was God's "very good" design from the beginning, and that eating flesh therefore runs against God's will and God's character as love.

His supporting evidence is a chain of texts: Habakkuk 2:17 (animals afraid of people because of violence), Daniel 1 (Daniel and his friends on vegetables who appear healthier and "10 times wiser"), Proverbs 3:13-17 ("all her ways are ways of peace"), 1 John 4:8 and 4:16, Psalm 11:5, Micah 6:8, and Isaiah 11:6-9. He reads Romans 14:15 and 14:21 as urging abstention from animal flesh, and argues from the Greek verb proskoptō in Matthew 7:27 that the danger in Romans 14 is harm to oneself rather than making a brother stumble. He also says that the Greek word for flesh (sarx) in Romans 14:21 is more specific than the general word for food.

The talk ends with a long live question-and-answer session in the chat. The host answers questions about whether the verse is really about table companions, about medications and everyday products tested on or made from animals, and about whether the verse concerns food or speech. He closes by announcing a blessing-of-animals service on October 4, the next talk on Micah 6:8, and a replay on Genesis 2:15. The talk presents these positions as the host's own views, which the host states the church may not share.

## Key Teachings

### Proverbs links love with peace and violence with hatred
The host reads Proverbs 15:17 in its immediate literary context. He cites Proverbs 10:11 ("the mouth of the righteous is a well of life but violence covers the mouth of the wicked") and Proverbs 10:12 ("hatred stirs up strife but love covers all sins"). He argues that violence toward an animal is part of the same strife and hatred the verse describes, and that "a lot of people don't make that connection but here in Proverbs it is making that connection." Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel") is used to show that even the wicked's kind acts are cruel, while the righteous care for animals. On this reading, the herb table is the place of love and the flesh table the place of contention.

### Genesis 1:29-31 as the original plant diet
The host reads Genesis 1:29-31 as God giving "every green herb for food" to humans and to every beast, bird, and creeping thing, followed by "it was very good." He treats this as God's established arrangement "from the beginning," in which all creatures eat the herb diet and love rather than violence governs the creation. He says the word translated "herb" in Genesis is the same word used in Proverbs 15:17.

### Daniel 1 as a test case of health and wisdom
Daniel asks for "vegetables to eat and water to drink" for ten days rather than the king's delicacies. The host notes that after the test "their countenance appeared better and fatter in flesh," and that in Daniel 1:19-20 the four young men are found "10 times better" in wisdom and understanding than the magicians and astrologers. He argues that the diet God designed both spares animals and benefits the body, "fueling our bodies with the things that our bodies were designed to run off of." The argument here is from the text as he reads it, not from any outside nutrition study.

### God's ways are peace, and God is love
Proverbs 3:13-17 says "all her ways are ways of pleasantness and all her paths are paths of peace." The host reads this as meaning that wisdom contains no violence, so a meal built on slaughter cannot fit it. He then pairs 1 John 4:8 ("he who does not love does not know God for God is love") and 1 John 4:16 to argue that eating flesh is inconsistent with knowing God. He briefly links this to a "table of demons versus the table of the Lord" contrast in the New Testament, which he says his group discussed in a live talk on the Clementine Homilies.

### Romans 14 and the meaning of "stumble"
The host says Romans 14 is often used to defend meat-eating, and that people who cite it should read the whole chapter, "because it doesn't say what a lot of these meat eaters think it says." He reads Romans 14:15 ("do not destroy with your food the one for whom Christ died") and Romans 14:21 ("it is good neither to eat meat nor drink wine nor do anything by which your brother stumbles"). He argues that sarx here means animal flesh specifically, not food generally. He argues that the verb translated "stumbles" is proskoptō, meaning "to beat on" or "strike against," not "cause to stumble" into sin. He cites Matthew 7:27 (the house "beat on" by the winds) and Matthew 4:6 to support this reading, then argues that eating flesh makes the eater more violent, both toward animals and toward a human neighbor. This is the talk's most technical argument, and the Greek points are offered by the host without any cited lexicon or commentary.

### Peaceable kingdom, mercy over sacrifice
The host cites Psalm 11:5 ("the one who loves violence his soul hates") and Micah 6:8, and he quotes "I desire mercy not sacrifice." He presents Isaiah 11:6-9 as a picture of what the world would look like if people followed the plant-based diet, with animals grazing together, the lion eating straw, and "they shall not hurt nor destroy." He ties this to his opening claim that God's arrangement is "very good" and to the idea that right eating reflects whom we serve.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Gluttony means overeating:** The host says that in Scripture "gluttony" is tied to consuming animal flesh, "it's not quantity of food it's what that food is."
- **Romans 14 permits eating meat:** He says that reading the whole chapter shows "it doesn't say what a lot of these meat eaters think it says."
- **"Stumble" means causing a brother to sin by seeing you eat meat:** He argues the Greek verb means "beat on" or "strike against," so the warning concerns harm to the eater's own character and to others.
- **Proverbs 15:17 is about whom you eat with, not what you eat:** He disputes this reading, saying the verse would then make the herb meal an undesirable one that God nonetheless calls very good.
- **"What goes into the mouth doesn't matter, only what comes out":** He argues that "what we eat reflects who we serve."
- **Science and religion are at odds:** He says that science "is confirming what we already knew," that the diet is better for health, environment, and animals.
- **You can't live without bacon or cheese:** He says "you can," pointing to "millions and millions of vegans" who are healthy on a plant-based diet.
