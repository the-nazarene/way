## Summary

This is a live "Friday Night Live" session from Creation Care Church, hosted by Craig, built around Micah 6:8 ("to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God"). The talk asks whether loving mercy extends to animals, and specifically whether the Bible's own texts support a vegan diet and oppose animal sacrifice. Craig's answer is yes on both counts. He argues that Jesus' commands to be merciful (Luke 6:36) and his quotation of Hosea 6:6, "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (Matthew 9:13), apply to animals as well as people, and that God's dominion charge in Genesis entails eating plants rather than animals. Most of the argument is built from Hebrew Bible passages (Proverbs, Psalms, Jeremiah, Malachi) read through the lens of a contrast between Jacob, a "man of peace," and Esau, a violent hunter.

The second half is a chat Q&A. A participant asks why God ordered Israel to kill firstborn lambs and to sacrifice animals. Craig answers that, in his current view, Jeremiah 7:21-23 and 8:8 show that the sacrificial laws were added by a "lying scribe" and were not commanded at the Exodus. He presents a second, less preferred option, drawn from the Clementine Homilies, in which God weaned people off sacrifice in stages (or "met us where we were"). He says he no longer holds that second view as his own.

The talk ends with a closing prayer, announcements about a special blessing-of-animals service two weeks later, and an invitation to a Zoom fellowship. The talk is a devotional and advocacy presentation, not a historical study. Craig makes no claims about the historical Jesus beyond what the texts say, and he cites no critical scholarship except a passing reference to the Clementine Homilies.

## Key Teachings

### Mercy as one of the "weightier matters"
Craig reads Micah 6:8 as a summary of three requirements (justice, love of mercy, humble walking with God). He then ties this to Matthew 23:23, where Jesus calls "justice and mercy and faith" the "weightier matters of the law." From this he argues that concern for animals is not optional. He takes issue with people who say diet "is not a salvational requirement," arguing that if God values something, it should matter to believers regardless of its salvific status. He also cites Luke 6:36 ("be merciful just as your father also is merciful") and asks what God's mercy looks like, saying the answer should shape human mercy toward "humans or to animals."

### The heart, treasure, and wholehearted seeking
Working from Matthew 6:20-21 ("where your treasure is there your heart will be also"), Craig argues that one should treasure what God treasures, including mercy. He reads Jeremiah 17:9 ("the heart is deceitful above all things") as a rebuke to the cultural slogan "follow your heart," and notes that some translations render the verse "desperately wicked" or "incurably sick." Jeremiah 29:13 ("you will seek me... with all your heart") is his model of the right orientation: wholehearted devotion to God's will, including refusing to kill animals for food when the heart might desire it.

### Esau, Jacob, and the way of peace
Craig takes Malachi 1:2-3 ("Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated") and reads Genesis 25:27 alongside it: Esau is "a skillful Hunter," Jacob "a mild or peaceful man dwelling in tents." Craig adds that eating flesh inevitably involves consuming blood. He draws on Proverbs 3:17 and 3:31 ("choose none of his ways" of violence) and Psalm 11:5 ("the one who loves violence his soul hates"). He argues that God's hatred of Esau is directed at his violence and his refusal to show mercy to animals, not at arbitrary predestination. He then cites Psalm 145:8-9 and 145:15-16 to argue that God's compassion covers all creatures, and that humans, as image-bearers, are to imitate it.

### Sacrifice: the "lying scribe" explanation
This is the most distinctive argument. Craig cites Jeremiah 7:21-23, where God says "I did not speak to your fathers or command them in the day that I brought them out of the land of Egypt concerning burnt offerings and sacrifices." He reads Jeremiah 8:8 ("the false pen of the scribe certainly works falsehood") and concludes that a scribe "added a bunch of these things in," producing "anachronistic ideas." He concedes that the Passover lamb is the first sacrifice in the Exodus story and that Jeremiah's words imply it was not commanded, but he does not develop a full textual-critical argument in this talk. He points to a longer treatment in a discipleship school lesson on sacrifice. He cites Hosea 6:6 and Matthew 9:13 to show that Jesus himself quoted the principle "I desire mercy, not sacrifice."

For the alternative, Craig describes the Clementine Homilies' explanation: Moses wove people away from sacrifice in stages, first getting them to stop worshiping other gods and then to stop sacrificing. He presents this as "probably the second most plausible View" and says he no longer holds it personally. He encourages listeners to "agree to disagree" on the explanation so long as they agree on mercy toward animals.

### Dominion, diet, and the Kingdom
Craig reads Luke 12:42-44 (the faithful steward) against the Genesis creation account. He says God's first words granting dominion were "eat the fruit of the tree and the plants of the ground," and argues that faithful stewardship therefore entails a vegan diet and merciful care for animals. He ties this to the Kingdom: citing Isaiah 2:4 ("beat their swords into plowshares"), Hosea 2:18 (covenant of peace with the animals), and Revelation 21:4 ("no more death"), he presents the Kingdom as a return to the original peaceable order. He argues that believers should practice this now, because Jesus wants to "find us doing these things" when he returns.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Follow your heart."** Craig argues that Jeremiah 17:9 says the heart is deceitful and that the Bible tells believers to guard against its desires.
- **"Diet and animal treatment aren't salvational, so they don't matter."** Craig argues that since God calls mercy a "weightier matter" (Matthew 23:23), believers should treat it as important regardless of its salvific status.
- **"Jesus will fix everything when he returns, so we don't need to act now."** Craig argues that Jesus says he wants to find his servants already doing these things when he returns (Luke 12:42-44).
- **"God arbitrarily loves some and hates others (Jacob and Esau)."** Craig argues that God's hatred is directed at Esau's violence and refusal of mercy, not at his birth or predestination.
- **"God commanded animal sacrifice in the Exodus."** Craig argues, citing Jeremiah 7:21-23 and 8:8, that the sacrificial laws were not commanded at the Exodus and were added later by a scribe, though he acknowledges the Passover lamb as the Exodus story's first sacrifice.
- **"Animals are our property."** A chat participant says animals belong to God, not to owners. Craig agrees and says no one "belongs to you" except God in this sense; he does not argue the point in depth.
