## Summary

This live episode of Creation Care Church's "Friday Night Live" series asks whether the Bible says animals can praise God, as some people claim they cannot. The unnamed host answers yes, working mainly from Revelation 5:13, which lists "every creature" in heaven, on earth, under the earth and in the sea as praising God and the Lamb. He argues that the four-part list is a deliberate pattern meaning "no animal is excluded," and he then traces the theme back through Genesis, the Noah narrative and the prophets.

The main evidence is textual. The host argues that God blesses the animals before the humans (Genesis 1:20-25), that the diet given in Genesis 1:29-30 is entirely plant-based, and that God's covenant in Genesis 9:16 is "with every living creature of All Flesh." He also argues that English translations such as the New King James obscure animals by rendering the Hebrew *basar* ("All Flesh") as "mankind" (Job 12:10). He reads Psalm 150:6 ("let everything that has breath praise the Lord") and Joel 2:28 as including animals. The talk closes with the host's vision of a future kingdom of peace, Isaiah 11 and a plant-based Eden, followed by a long live Q&A about whether "all flesh" will be redeemed, the host's answer being no, not universally.

The talk ends with a closing prayer, a reminder of a prayer meeting and Zoom fellowship, and announcements of a replay (Genesis 4:4) and a future live on Proverbs. Most of the second half is responses to chat comments.

### Key Teachings

### 1. "Every creature" in Revelation 5:13 means every animal
The host reads Revelation 5:13 as describing all animals. He argues that the four categories (heaven or sky, sea, earth, under the earth) are a recurring biblical pattern for saying "no category of animal no animal is excluded," and that "under the Earth" means burrowing creatures such as moles. He does not discuss the Greek text or how Revelation's visionary scene is usually read.

### 2. The Lamb is Jesus, and the Lamb is the focus of praise
He identifies the Lamb with Jesus through John 1:29 ("Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world") and places Jesus "at the right hand of the Throne of God" via Hebrews 12:2. He then links this to Hebrews 13:15 ("the sacrifice of praise") and to Hosea's "I desire Mercy not sacrifice," which he reads as God wanting praise and mercy rather than "Bloodshed of my creatures." This is the one place where he discusses sacrifice as a theme.

### 3. Genesis: blessing, dominion and a plant-based diet
He argues that God blesses the sea creatures and birds (Genesis 1:22) and the land animals before humans are created (Genesis 1:28 is the human blessing that follows). Dominion over animals is "unique to humans," and he reads it as stewardship in God's image rather than license. From Genesis 1:29-30 he concludes that the diet God gave was "entirely plant-based" and that eating animals is outside God's original order. He also argues that the animals' blessing shows that God created the world "for them just as much as he is creating it for us."

### 4. Noah and the everlasting covenant with "All Flesh"
Using Genesis 6:19-22 and 8:15-17, he argues that God preserved the animals through the flood, instructing Noah to bring plant food into the ark. In Genesis 9:16 he identifies the "Everlasting Covenant" as "between God and every living creature of All Flesh." He stresses the Hebrew term (*basar*, rendered by the transcript as "ker," "baser," and "Cole") and claims it always means "all creatures that are made of Flesh," citing "several dozens of passages," though he names only a handful. Hosea 2:18 and Isaiah 11:6-9 are read as the covenant's future fulfilment.

### 5. The Psalms, Job and Joel: all breath and all flesh praise God
He lists Psalm 71:8, Psalm 63:3-4, Psalm 145:8-9 and Psalm 150:6 as praise texts that extend to animals. He reads Job 12:7-10 as a passage where animals "teach" humans, and he argues that the New King James's "mankind" is a translator "reading animals out." From Joel 2:28 he argues that God pours out his Spirit "on all flesh," which he connects to Romans 15:13 and the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) as joy and peace.

### 6. Kingdom readiness, covetousness and the diet
In the Q&A he argues that diet is a present-day matter of preparing for the kingdom. Eating animal flesh and drinking animal milk is, in his words, "covetousness," desiring what "is not intended for us." He argues that a person who is not "making yourself fit for the kingdom now" would not want to be there. He cites Luke 6:36 and Genesis 2:15 in his Creation Care Church pamphlet, which argues for diet, mercy and stewardship together.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Animals cannot praise God."** The host argues Revelation 5:13 and Psalm 150:6 say they can, and that "every creature" and "everything that has breath" include animals.
- **"'All flesh' means only humans" or "mankind" in translation.** He says the Hebrew *basar* includes animals and that English versions such as the NKJV (Job 12:10) read them out.
- **"The kingdom is a barbecue heaven."** A chat commenter says people expect steak and barbecues in heaven; the host agrees that the future kingdom is plant-based and Eden-like.
- **"All flesh will be redeemed, including the animals destroyed in the flood."** The host says the covenant is for all flesh but must be entered; universal salvation is "probably not accurate" in his view, so "all flesh" does not mean every creature ever made.
- **"God wants animal sacrifice."** He reads Hosea's "I desire Mercy not sacrifice" and Hebrews 13:15 as God wanting praise rather than bloody offerings.
