## Summary

Craig asks whether the body, which 1 Corinthians 6:19 calls a temple of the Holy Spirit, is being treated as one, or whether it has become what he calls a "grave of craving." He reads the "grave of craving" from Numbers 11, where Israel tires of the manna and craves flesh, and a plague strikes "while the meat was still between their teeth." He takes the Hebrew-derived name of that place as the symbol of appetite that leads to death.

His answer is that God's "very good" diet in Genesis 1:29-31 is fruit and plants, and that eating animal products is a form of violence against animals that contradicts the mercy and peace the Bible asks for. He supports this with a chain of texts: Romans 12:1-2 (living sacrifice, renewed mind), John 10:10-11 (the thief versus the Good Shepherd), Proverbs 12:10 and 21:21, Luke 10:29-37 and 6:36 (who is my neighbor, be merciful), Galatians 5:16-23 (fruit of the Spirit), Matthew 7:15-18, Matthew 4:17 and Romans 14:17 (repent, the Kingdom is not food and drink). The main narrative evidence is Daniel 1, where Daniel and three companions eat vegetables and water for ten days and appear healthier and "10 times better" in wisdom than those who ate the king's delicacies. He reads Numbers 11:18-20 as a rebuke, not an endorsement, of meat eating. He frames the Torah as possibly combining separate sources, which he says would explain some inconsistencies in the Numbers story.

The talk ends with a live question-and-answer session (on 1 Timothy 4:1 and the "doctrine of demons," on Habakkuk 2 and Isaiah 11:9, and on Christians who eat meat or follow carnivore diets), followed by announcements and a prayer. Craig says the views "are based on my own research" and presents them as his own position, not as settled scholarship.

### Key Teachings

### 1. The body belongs to God
Craig reads 1 Corinthians 6:19 ("You are not your own") as meaning that the body belongs to God once a person gives their life to him, and that the Spirit dwells in the body both individually and in the community as "the body of Christ" (00:02 to 04:41). Romans 12:1-2 is read as the call to offer the body as a "living sacrifice" and to prove "the good and acceptable and perfect will of God" by how one treats others, including animals (07:49).

### 2. Numbers 11 and the "grave of craving"
He summarizes Numbers 11:4-34: the people tire of manna and crave flesh; Moses complains; God sends quail; and "while the meat was still between their teeth" a plague kills those who craved (04:41 to 06:14). He reads Numbers 11:18-20 (meat "until it comes out your nostrils") as sarcasm, arguing that God is not permitting meat but judging the desire, and that the plague came before they could eat it for a month (26:40). He also notes the Torah apparently contains two intertwined accounts. Craig says that in one strand the people drink from the rock and in another they crave flesh, and that there is no mention of killing the animals they had brought from Egypt. He offers no firm resolution: "I don't know why," and says the moral is that craving flesh is the problem (53:32 to 55:07).

### 3. Genesis 1 as the design for diet and dominion
Genesis 1:26-31 is the anchor. Craig reads the plant diet ("every green herb for food") as "very good" and says no other diet in the Bible is called very good (17:10). He reads "dominion" as loving protection rather than exploitation, citing the Proverbs and Matthew 7 "fruit" passages (09:23, 39:30). In the Q&A he reads Habakkuk 2:12-17 and Isaiah 11:9 together, arguing that violence toward animals is the sign of the Fall and that animals fear humans because of it, citing Genesis 9 as the post-flood permission he treats as a sign of the Fall (48:52 to 50:25).

### 4. Daniel 1 as a test case
He reads Daniel 1:8-20 as a ten-day trial: vegetables and water versus the king's delicacies and wine, after which Daniel and his companions look healthier and are found "10 times better" in wisdom (18:49 to 22:36). He links this to Proverbs 23:1-3 and 19-20 (do not eat at the ruler's table or with "gluttonous Eaters of meat") (23:33 to 25:06). He treats the story as a model of faithfulness that brings blessing.

### 5. Jesus and the "thief" versus the "Good Shepherd"
John 10:10-11 is set against the Numbers passage: the thief kills and destroys, Jesus gives abundant life (09:23). Luke 10:29-37 and 6:36 are used to argue that "neighbor" is not limited to humans. He rejects the idea that "it's not animals, it's just people" (14:04 and 15:38).

### 6. Fruit of the Spirit and the Kingdom
Galatians 5:16-23 is read as a list of tests: are we showing animals love, peace, kindness, gentleness and self-control (28:22 to 29:57)? Matthew 7:15-18 supports "you will know them by their fruits." He reads Matthew 4:17 ("repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand") as turning away from appetite, and Romans 14:17 ("the kingdom of God is not food and drink") as a statement that the Kingdom is not about gratifying the flesh (31:31 to 33:05).

### 7. Responses in the live Q&A
- **1 Timothy 4:1 and "doctrine of demons":** Craig says that using this verse to dismiss vegan practice is backwards, because God called the plant diet "very good" and blessed Daniel (44:11 to 47:20). He points to earlier talks on 1 Timothy 4 and the "table of demons."
- **Addiction:** He claims cheese contains "casomorphins" that are addictive, and that Christ can break such addiction. This is a claim with no source given in the talk.
- **Carnivore and "God gave Noah permission":** He calls carnivore claims "pseudo science" and says Christians rely on a handful of passages instead of "God's very good will" (51:57).

- **"Carnivore diets are natural for humans."** Craig calls this "pseudo science" and says it conflicts with God's original design.
- **"It's only people who are my neighbor."** Craig rejects this, citing Luke 10:29-37 and 6:36.
