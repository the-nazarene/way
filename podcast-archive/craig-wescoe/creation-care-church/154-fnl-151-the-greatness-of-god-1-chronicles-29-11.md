## Summary

This is a Friday Night Live devotional from the Creation Care Church channel, built on 1 Chronicles 29:11 ("Yours, O LORD, is the greatness..."). The host asks what the greatness of God means and argues that God's design for creation was vegan and peaceful from the start, that God's love extends to animals as well as people, and that God will one day restore creation to that original state. The talk is a sermon, not a historical study of Jesus or his first followers, and it does not engage the critical questions a historian would raise about dating, authorship or textual transmission.

The host's main evidence is a chain of proof-texts. He reads Genesis 1:26-31 as giving humans "dominion" over animals while assigning plants, not animals, as food, and he treats Genesis 8:21 as God calling the later practice of eating animals "evil." He then connects the opening of John's Gospel (John 1:1-14) and the "I am" sayings (John 8:58, read against Exodus 3:14) to Genesis 1, arguing that the divine Word who created the world is Jesus, who is "the same yesterday today and forever" (Hebrews 13:8). Isaiah 11:9, Hosea 2:18, Revelation 21:4 and Psalm 150 are used to describe a future restoration that includes animals.

The talk ends with a prayer and an announcement of Zoom fellowship. Much of the final third is the host reading and answering live chat comments, including questions on the Hebrew word for "dominion," the NIV's "rule over" wording, Hebrews 10:4 and animal sacrifice, and an anecdote about a convert who was told to resume eating meat. The host defers several questions to earlier talks and discipleship-school lessons.

## Key Teachings

### Dominion is stewardship shaped by God's love, not a license to kill
The host reads Genesis 1:28 ("have dominion over the fish of the sea...") and says many readers stop there. He then reads on to Genesis 1:29-30, where humans and animals are given "every herb that yields seed" and "every green herb for food." From this he argues that dominion "should not be used as a license to kill and eat the animals." He adds that because God is love, human dominion should look like God's dominion: love, peace and kindness toward animals, with the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23, which he does not cite by reference) as the model. A chat commenter's Hebrew lexicon entry for *rad* ("rule, subjugate") is raised, and the host says he has covered the term in other material rather than resolving it here.

### The creation was "very good" in a vegan form, and God does not change
The host argues the original diet was entirely plant-based and that this is part of the "very good" that God established. He reads Genesis 8:21 as God describing human violence and the evil of human imagination, and claims that God "doesn't say it's very good" when describing animal eating, but "says it's evil." He rejects the idea that God later changed his mind or that the diet was merely a temporary permission. Hebrews 13:8 ("Jesus Christ is the same yesterday today and forever") and Isaiah 55:9 are used to argue that God's "ways are higher" and do not evolve. The host's reading of Genesis 8:21 is his own interpretation and should be checked against the Hebrew and the surrounding chapters.

### The Word who created the world is Jesus, who pre-existed
The host reads John 1:1-5 and 1:14 as describing the divine Word (*logos*) through whom "all things were made," who "became flesh." He draws parallels between John 1:1-5 and Genesis 1 (light, darkness, creation by speech). He then cites John 17:5 and 17:24 ("before the world was"), Colossians 1:19-21 (fullness dwelling in Jesus), 1 Peter 1:20 ("foreordained before the foundation of the world") and John 8:58 read against Exodus 3:14 as evidence of Jesus's pre-existence and divine identity. He says the "I am" sayings in John point to the divine name. The host notes that the Gospel of John is treated as one body of text with Genesis, without discussing how or when it was composed.

### God's love and greatness include the animals
The host argues that people "underestimate" God's love by treating animals as "menu items." He cites Genesis 1:30 (the "living soul," *nephesh*) and Psalm 145 and Psalm 150 (the chapter's closing "let everything that has breath praise the Lord"), along with Revelation 5:13 and Joel 2:28 ("all flesh"), to argue that animals praise God and are included in his covenants. He also criticizes "anthropocentric" reading, meaning reading Scripture only for its benefit to humans. Romans 8:38-39 is read as showing that nothing created, including "animals," can separate people from God's love, and the host draws the conclusion that God's love is more powerful than anything in the world.

### The future restoration: no pain, no killing, animals included
The host says God "declared the end from the beginning" (Isaiah 46:10), so the future restoration mirrors Genesis 1. He cites Isaiah 11:9 ("They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my Holy Mountain"), Hosea 2:18 (a covenant with the beasts of the field and the removal of "bow and sword") and Revelation 21:4 (no more death, sorrow or pain) as promises that include animals. He ties this to the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-10: "Your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as it is in heaven") and says this is the "very good" kingdom to be restored.

### Hebrews 10:4 and the end of animal sacrifice (answered in chat)
In reply to a chat question about God hating animal sacrifice, the host reads Hebrews 10:4 ("it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins") and says it shows that Jesus, as high priest, ended the sacrificial system. He reasons that if the system was ended, "then he probably never wanted that to begin with." He does not address the book of Leviticus in any depth and refers listeners to earlier talks on sacrifice.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"God changed his mind about diet over time."** The host says Hebrews 13:8 and Isaiah 55:9 show that God's ways do not change or develop, so the plant-based diet is his original and enduring will.
- **"Genesis gave humans dominion, so they may kill animals."** The host argues that Genesis 1:29-30 assigns plants as food, so dominion is stewardship shaped by love.
- **"God withheld the permission to eat animals until after the Fall."** Attributed in the talk to the Creation Museum; the host says the text describes meat-eating as evil (Genesis 8:21), not as a permission withheld.
- **"God only cares about people."** The host says God's love and covenants extend to animals, and that human-centred reading (anthropocentrism) clouds this.
- **"Animals are merely natural resources."** The host, responding to an AI answer quoted in chat, says animals are beings, not resources.
- **"Christians may keep eating meat because of Jesus."** The host says Jesus ended sacrifice (Hebrews 10:4), and that a convert who resumed eating animals was following bad teaching. He treats this as a misreading of the faith.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** The talk relies on Paul (Romans 8, Colossians 1, and the host's paraphrase "as Paul says" on love) as authority, and does not separate Jesus's teaching from Paul's. It also does not contrast Paul with the Jerusalem community.
- **Torah, sacrifice, diet and nonviolence:** The talk's strongest bearing on the repo is here. It reads Genesis 1:29-30 as a plant-based design and Genesis 8:21 as a later departure, and it reads Hebrews 10:4 as the end of animal sacrifice. It does not address how Torah was practised or interpreted in Jesus's time.
- **The Kingdom on earth:** Matthew 6:9-10 is read as a prayer for earth to become as heaven, and the talk argues this means restoration of creation including animals (Isaiah 11:9, Revelation 21:4). This is a futurist reading presented without historical analysis.
- **Who Jesus thought he was:** The talk makes a strong christological claim: that Jesus is the pre-existent divine Word (John 1, John 17, John 8:58). It presents this as the plain sense of the text, with no discussion of how the Gospel of John's self-presentation differs from the synoptic Gospels.
- **Gospels and canon:** The host treats John, Genesis, Colossians, Hebrews and Revelation as one harmonized witness. The only canonical remark is the Marcionite ordering of Colossians, which he does not develop.
- **Resurrection, afterlife and hell:** Not discussed directly; the restoration is described in terms of a renewed earth and "no more death."
- **Essenes, Ebionites, Nazarenes, James and the Jerusalem community:** Not discussed.
