## Key Teachings

### 1. "Live by his faith": the proud versus the just
The host reads Habakkuk 2:4 as contrasting the proud, whose "soul is not upright in him," with the just who "live by his faith." He stresses that the verse says "his faith," so faith is what the person lives by, not one item among several. He links the Hebrew word for "proud" (*apala*, Strong's 6075) to Proverbs 11:2 and says pride is set against humility toward God. The proud trust themselves; the humble trust God and his wisdom.

### 2. The just (*sadiq*) and walking with God
The host follows *sadiq* from Genesis 6:9 (Noah, "perfect in his generations," who "walked with God") and Genesis 5:24 (Enoch, who "walked with God") to Genesis 14:18-20 (Melchizedek, whose name he reads as "king of righteousness" and "king of peace," which he links to Hebrews). He cites Isaiah 60:21, where "your people shall all be righteous" and "inherit the land forever." From the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:5-10) he draws a list of traits: the meek and the land, hunger and thirst for righteousness, mercy, purity of heart, peacemaking, and persecution for righteousness. He reads "peace" against "violence," contrasting Jacob with Esau and the days of Noah. Malachi 3:18 and the "by their fruit" teaching give the idea of discernment between the righteous and the wicked. 1 John 2:29 is read as "born of him."

### 3. Faith (*emunah*) as steadfastness and the rock
The host says *emunah* (Strong's 530) means "firmness" or "steadfast," like an unmovable rock. He notes it is usually translated "faithful" (26 times) or "true" (15 times). He reads Deuteronomy 32:4, where God is "the rock," as the model: God's ways are justice, and he is "righteous and upright." Children of God therefore bear the same fruit, which he ties to Galatians 5:22-23. He also says Jesus is the rock on which to build (the end of the Sermon on the Mount). Hebrews 11:1-3 and 11:5-6 are used to describe faith as "assurance" and as diligent seeking of God, and he pairs this with the claim that faith in God alone is what "overcomes the world" (1 John 5:4).

### 4. Peace, mercy and animals: Isaiah 11 and Hosea 2
The host reads Isaiah 11:5-9 as a picture of the righteousness and faithfulness ("belt" of the loins and waist) of a child who leads animals in peace, with "none hurt nor destroy." He reads Hosea 2:18-20 as a covenant that God makes with "the beasts of the field" and "the birds of the air," so that weapons are shattered and all lie down safely. Genesis 9 is cited as the parallel covenant. He concludes that God's people, as his image-bearers, should show loving-kindness toward animals now, and cites Proverbs 12:10 ("The righteous will care for the needs of the animals"). He also says Genesis 1:29 is a call to a vegan diet (see Misconceptions).

### 5. Faith and works: James 2 and Ephesians 2
The host reads James 2:17-23 (faith without works is dead; Abraham was "justified by works"; demons believe and tremble) and says it is not opposed to Paul. He reads Ephesians 2:8-10 (saved by grace through faith, "not of works," but "created in Christ Jesus for good works") as saying that faith gives rise to good works. In this reading, the works are evidence that the faith is alive and rooted in Christ. He describes Abraham's faith as trusting God's promises and acting on them, and as making him "a friend of God." Along with "children of God" and "bride," this is one of three close relational images he uses for the believer's relationship to God.

### 6. Animal sacrifice and the "lying pen": Noah and Jeremiah
The host gives two readings of Noah's sacrifice after the Flood. The first, which he used to hold, is that even the righteous prophets fell short. The second, which he now prefers, is that the animal sacrifice was a later insertion by the scribes. He cites Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribe") and Jeremiah 7, where he says God did not command the sacrifices when he brought Israel out of Egypt. He reads Enoch (in response to a viewer, who notes that it teaches sacrifice began with fallen angels) as opposing animal sacrifice, and he suggests the Genesis 6 "violence" was part of this. He says the Genesis story of Noah appears as "two concurrent stories" woven together, one in which Noah does not sacrifice and one in which he does. He attributes support for this view to the Clementine homilies and to his teaching in the Vegan Christian Discipleship School. The captions garble who found the hidden Torah scroll in the temple; check the wording in the recording before repeating that detail.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Faith is mere belief that God exists.** The host says James 2:19 (demons believe) shows belief alone is not enough; faith means trusting God's promises and acting on that trust, as Abraham did.
- **Paul and James contradict each other on faith and works.** The host says Ephesians 2:8-10 and James 2 describe one faith that produces good works, and that Paul is not saying works are irrelevant.
- **Habakkuk 2:4 means "live by faith and other things."** He says the text says "his faith," so faith itself is the thing the person lives by.
- **Being a good person is enough, with no need for faith in God.** He says a person must have faith in God and his promises, and that good fruit comes only from God.
- **Translations of Mark 16:15 ("every creature") are accurate.** He says some translations render it as "people," and that this is an example of translators drawing distinctions between humans and animals that he thinks the text does not contain.
- **Animal sacrifice was commanded by God in the Torah, and Noah offered sacrifice.** The host argues, from Jeremiah 7-8, that the sacrifice was not commanded and was added by "lying scribes," and that Noah did not sacrifice.
