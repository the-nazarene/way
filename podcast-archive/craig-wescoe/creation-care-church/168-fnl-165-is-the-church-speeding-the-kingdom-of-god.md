## Summary

Craig asks whether the church is "speeding the kingdom of God," the question raised by 2 Peter 3:11-12, and what Jesus says his followers should do to prepare for that kingdom. He argues that the preparation is moral transformation, and that how Christians treat animals, especially what they eat, is part of that transformation. He reads 2 Peter 3:9-12 as calling for holy living that "hastens" the day of God, then links the Greek term he renders *spodontis* ("to hurry or earnestly desire") to the "eager expectation" of creation in Romans 8:19. From there he argues that creation, including animals, is groaning for the children of God to be revealed (Romans 8:18-23), and that God's kingdom is one where righteousness dwells, with animals at peace (Isaiah 11:6-9; Proverbs 12:10).

His main evidence is a chain of Scripture passages, which he reads with a tight focus on the "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22-23) and on Genesis 1:29 (the plant diet). He reads 1 Corinthians 8:13 and Romans 14:15 as requiring Christians to stop eating meat for the sake of brothers grieved by animal suffering, and reads James 4:17 as making non-veganism a sin. The closing section relies on the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15:28-29), which he presents, through an Irenaeus quotation, as defining Christian life around abstaining from blood, strangled meat and idol-food. He says James, whom Jesus "appointed to lead the church," would therefore make animal-eating a central concern.

The talk ends with a prayer, the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13), and a Q&A in which Craig answers chat questions about milk, animal sacrifice and the "table of demons," and points viewers to earlier talks and to sharing his videos.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Preparing for the kingdom means transformation, and speeding it means eager desire
Craig reads 2 Peter 3:9-12 ("what manner of persons ought you to be in holy conduct and godliness, looking for and hastening the coming of the day of God") and says the Greek term rendered "hastening" means "to hurry or earnestly desire." He ties this to Romans 8:19, where creation "eagerly waits" for the sons of God to be revealed. He asks whether the church is earnestly desiring the kingdom "on earth as it is in heaven" and says Jesus requires transformation for entry: Matthew 18:3-4 ("unless you are converted... you will by no means enter the kingdom") and Romans 12:2 ("be transformed"). He is explicit that he is leaving open whether the new earth comes by physical destruction or by refining fire; he says the practical instruction is the same either way ([04:00], [08:53], [10:25]).

### 2. Animals belong to the righteousness of the kingdom
He cites Isaiah 11:6-9 (the wolf with the lamb, "a little child shall lead them") and Proverbs 12:10 ("A righteous man regards the life of the animal") as descriptions of who lives in God's kingdom. He takes Genesis 1:29 as God's original food instruction and Genesis 9 as including animals in the everlasting covenant. Revelation 5:13 (every creature praising God and the Lamb) is used to argue that animals are included in the final praise ([12:05], [13:36], [31:14], [33:47]).

### 3. The fruit of the Spirit applied to animals
Galatians 5:22-23 is read item by item and applied to animal treatment: love (do we show love to animals?), joy (are we "taking away their joy by treating them as commodities?"), peace (eating them means "we are not being at peace with them"), kindness, faithfulness, and self-control, which he treats as the culminating fruit. He contrasts this with Philippians 3:19 ("whose god is their belly") and 1 John 4:8, arguing that appetite and self-control are the test ([14:25], [16:06], [21:30]).

### 4. 1 Corinthians 8:13 and Romans 14:15 as a basis for abstaining from flesh
Craig reads 1 Corinthians 8:13 in the New King James Version ("I will never again eat meat") and argues three things: the Greek word is *kreas* (flesh), so the verse means flesh generally and is not limited to eating around others; the word "stumble" is not in the Greek and was added; and the verse has a conditional form that his translation hides. He then pairs it with Romans 14:15 ("you are no longer walking in love") as a way of telling Christians who eat animal products that they are not walking in love ([17:01], [19:34]). He calls this "the ace in the hole" in conversations with other Christians. Note: the claim that "stumble" is an addition and the reading of *kreas* are his; the talk does not show the manuscript evidence.

### 5. The Jerusalem Council and James
This is the central historical argument. Craig reads Acts 15:28-29 from what he calls the PSB version, which he says rests on "later manuscripts," and reads *porneia* as "defilement," including eating forbidden food. He then quotes Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.12.14, which he calls "the earliest witness" to Acts and the Jerusalem Council, placing Irenaeus in the late second century. He argues that the Council defined "following the Holy Spirit" as abstaining from idol-food, blood and strangled meat, so "half of what they're talking about is not eating animals." Because James "is the one Jesus appointed to lead the church," he concludes that the matter should be important to the church today ([24:56], [25:58], [26:42]).

### 6. Sin of omission and the table of demons
He cites James 4:17 ("to him who knows to do good and does not do it, to him it is sin") and Genesis 1:29-31 to argue that since God called plant-eating "very good," those who know this and eat animals are sinning. He also cites 1 Corinthians 10:21-23 ("You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons") and says eating animal products is sitting at "the table of demons," which he says he has discussed in an earlier talk on the Clementine Homilies ([18:00], [20:31], [23:14], [47:57]).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"1 Corinthians 8 only asks you to avoid meat around weaker believers."** Craig argues the verse says "I will never again eat meat" without qualification, and that "stumble" is added by translators.
- **"Christians are free to be cruel to animals; it isn't sin."** He argues that James 4:17 and Romans 14:15 make it sin to ignore known good (plant-eating) or to grieve a brother through food choices.
- **"Veganism is a new modern movement."** He says the term "vegan" is new, but the idea is ancient, based on the prophets and Isaiah 11, and he uses this in outreach to non-believing vegans.
- **"The Bible is just a human book and cannot speak to this."** He says that even if one doubts inspiration, the texts are ancient and have promoted a peaceable, vegan vision for thousands of years.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** The talk is heavily Pauline. Craig cites Romans, 1 Corinthians, Galatians and Philippians far more than the Gospels, and uses Jesus' words mainly from Matthew (4, 6, 11, 18, 24, 28). The Jerusalem Council is presented as the controlling reference point, which rests on Acts and James, not on Paul's letters.
- **James and the Jerusalem community:** Yes. Craig says James was "appointed to lead the church" and that the Council's decision defines following the Holy Spirit, with animal abstention at its center. This is his reading of the Council and Irenaeus's quotation of it. The talk does not discuss James's observance of Torah or the wider Jerusalem community.
- **Torah, diet and nonviolence:** Heavily present. Genesis 1:29 is the basis for a plant diet; Isaiah 11 and Joel 2:28 (swords into plowshares) are used for nonviolence; Acts 15 is read as a dietary decree. He does not discuss Torah's sacrificial or dietary law in detail.
- **The Kingdom on earth:** Central. The talk argues the kingdom is a restored earth with righteousness and peace, and that Christians are to prepare for it now through transformation and animal-related conduct.
- **Resurrection, afterlife and hell:** Not discussed beyond a passing reference to the new heavens and new earth.
- **Who Jesus thought he was:** Only lightly. Jesus is "the Lamb" (Revelation 5:13) and the one who calls disciples "friends" (John 15, quoted in chat).
- **How the gospels and canon were formed:** Touched on through the claim that the PSB Acts text rests on "later manuscripts" and the appeal to Irenaeus as an early witness. These are not examined.
