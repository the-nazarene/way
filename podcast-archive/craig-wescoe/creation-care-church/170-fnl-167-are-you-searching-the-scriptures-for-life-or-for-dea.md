## Summary

Craig Wescoe, who leads Creation Care Church and runs a vegan Christian discipleship school, opens with John 5:39-40 and asks whether readers search the scriptures "for life or for death," meaning whether they look for justifications for cruelty or for mercy. He argues that Jesus distinguishes the scriptures from the living Word, and that the goal of reading is to come to Jesus, who is "the life." He builds the case through a chain of proof-texts: keeping the commandments and loving one's neighbor (Matthew 19:16-19), taking Jesus' "yoke" (Matthew 11:29, which he illustrates with an ox-training image and material from the Clementine literature in an earlier talk), the warning about "lawlessness" (Matthew 7:21-23), and the "weightier matters" of justice, mercy and faithfulness (Matthew 23:23-28). From these he concludes that mistreating animals and eating animal flesh are departures from God's will.

His central claim about animals rests on Genesis 1:26-31. He argues that human "dominion" is a stewardship that makes humans more valuable only in the sense of carrying responsibility, and that God gave a plant-based diet as the only diet God calls "very good." He supports this with Matthew 12:11-12 (the sheep on the Sabbath), Matthew 6:26 (the birds), Proverbs 12:10, Deuteronomy 30:19, the quail story of Numbers 11, Philippians 3:19, and 1 Corinthians 6:19-20. Craig is clear that he is speaking from his own research and not for his church.

The talk ends with a long live Q&A. Craig takes questions on whether faith alone saves, which he answers by linking Habakkuk 2:4, the Abraham narrative and James's "faith without works" into a view that faithfulness, shown in works, is what saves. He also addresses the claim that God changed his mind about diet, which he rejects, and closes with prayer and announcements. The talk is a sermon-style presentation carried by Craig, with chat comments read aloud from the Zoom fellowship audience.

## Key Teachings

### Scripture is a witness to the Word, not the Word itself
Craig reads John 5:39-40 and says Jesus separates "the scriptures" from himself, the actual Word of God. Scripture is a "recording of things that the word of God speaks." Those who search the scriptures for life while refusing to come to Jesus are, in his reading, the target of the warning. He ties this to John 14:6 ("I'm the way, the truth, and the life").

### Keeping the commandments, the yoke of the Lord, and loving one's neighbor
From Matthew 19:16-19, Craig notes that Jesus answers the question about eternal life with the commandments and the summary "love your neighbor as yourself" (Leviticus 19:18). He reads Matthew 11:29 ("take my yoke upon you") through an ox-training image: the new ox walks beside the experienced one and learns the path without bearing the load. Craig says this was a common early-church idea, which he has covered in an earlier live talk. The yoke, for him, means walking with God and keeping his commandments.

### The weightier matters: hypocrisy, mercy, and faithfulness
Matthew 23:23-28 is the main text here. Craig argues that the scribes and Pharisees tithed mint, anise and cumin but "neglected the weightier matters of the law, justice and mercy and faith." He says Jesus' direct language is a model for calling out teachers who lead people astray, and that accusations of "judging" should not stop this. Craig applies the test to readers: when searching the scriptures, are you looking for ways to show justice, mercy and faithfulness, or for loopholes to excuse injustice? He pairs this with Matthew 7:21-23 and the idea that sin is "lawlessness." He presents repentance as the way back and the "good news" of the gospel.

### Animals are valued by God; human "dominion" is stewardship
Craig argues against the reading that humans are more valuable than animals and therefore may use them freely. He draws on Matthew 12:11-12 (the sheep fallen in a pit on the Sabbath) and Matthew 6:26 (the birds God feeds). In his view the comparison only works if animals are valuable to God. Genesis 1:26-31 gives humans dominion as a responsibility given because they are made in God's image. He uses an airline pilot analogy: if humans fail to steward animals, "the whole earth is doomed." He also cites Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal") as a measure of righteousness.

### Diet: the plant-based diet of Genesis 1 and the warning of Numbers 11
Craig argues that God's food instruction in Genesis 1:29-30 is a plant-based diet and that Genesis 1:31 is the only place in the Bible where God calls a diet "very good." He reads the quail story in Numbers 11 as a warning about craving meat, which he says left the people in "the grave of craving" (kibroth hattaavah, which he gives in its Hebrew sense). He links this to Philippians 3:19 ("whose God is their belly") and to 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (the body as the temple of the Holy Spirit, "bought at a price"). Craig says he is not obligated to answer every objection; he treats these texts as enough to show God's will. He also says he does not accept that God changed his mind in Genesis 9, which he has discussed in other live talks.

### Faithfulness, not works or mere belief, saves
In the Q&A, a viewer asks about the minimum needed for salvation. Craig answers that people are saved by faith and that the Habakkuk 2:4 phrase is better rendered as "faithfulness." He says the Abraham narrative (cited in Hebrews, James, and one of Paul's letters) shows that Abraham's belief was credited as faithfulness because it was proven by his actions. He quotes James's "faith without works is dead" and says works are evidence of salvation rather than its price. He admits this is his own view.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Humans are more valuable than animals, so they may use them as they like.** Craig says the comparison in Matthew 12 and Matthew 6 presumes animals are valuable to God, and that human "value" is a responsibility of stewardship rather than license.
- **Dominion (Genesis 1:26-28) permits harming animals.** Craig argues that dominion is given in God's image and that using it to cause harm inverts God's intent.
- **God changed his mind about humans eating animals (Genesis 9).** Craig says God does not change his mind and that the claim misreads the text.
- **Jesus was gentle and avoided offense.** Craig argues that Jesus' words to the scribes and Pharisees were direct and that calling out hypocrisy is Christlike.
- **Keeping the Sabbath forbids doing good on it.** Craig says Matthew 12 shows that good works, including helping animals, are lawful on the Sabbath.
- **Faith is only doctrinal belief.** Craig says the demons "believe" (James) and that faith means faithfulness, shown in action.
