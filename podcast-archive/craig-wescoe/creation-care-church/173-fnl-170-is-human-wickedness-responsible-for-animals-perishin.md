## Summary

Craig Wescoe, speaking for his own Creation Care Church channel, asks whether human wickedness is responsible for animals perishing, starting from Jeremiah 12:4 ("How long will the land mourn... The beasts and birds are consumed, For the wickedness of those who dwell there"). He argues that the answer is yes, and he defines the wickedness in several ways: violence toward animals, neglect of the poor and vulnerable (drawing on Ezekiel 16:49-50 and James 1:27), and the "den of violence" he says the temple had become (Jeremiah 7:11, Matthew 21:12-13). He also argues that the prophets reject animal sacrifice (Isaiah 1:11, Hosea, Psalms) and that the original human diet in Genesis 1:29-30 was plants, not flesh.

His main evidence is a chain of texts. The Sodom intercession (Genesis 18:20-33) shows God sparing a town for the sake of a few righteous people. The Nineveh story (Jonah 3) and 2 Peter 3:9 show that God's aim is repentance rather than destruction, and Matthew 4:17 is presented as Jesus' call to repent. He reads "dominion" in Genesis 1:26-31 and "keep" in Genesis 2:15 as stewardship, and he closes with Revelation 11:18 and 21:4 as a hope that destroyers of the earth will be judged or will repent. The talk ends with a thirty-minute Q&A from the live chat, in which he applies the argument to veganism, the sheep, pig and cow breeding he describes, and to how to talk with people who change their diet. He says repentance must reach the mind as well as conduct, and he closes with a prayer and an invitation to the Zoom fellowship.

Craig frames all of this as his own view ("this talk is my own" and not the church's official position, as he states at the start). The speaker carries the entire talk; the Q&A segment contains the audience's questions, which are read aloud.

## Key Teachings

### Wickedness as neglect of the vulnerable and violence
Craig reads wickedness in the sense of the Sodom passage. He quotes Ezekiel 16:49-50 ("pride, full of fullness of food, and abundance of idleness. Neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy") and says the sin was failing to care for the destitute, not a sin of the kind usually associated with Sodom. He pairs this with James 1:27 ("to visit orphans and widows in their trouble") and Proverbs 31:8-9 ("Open your mouth for the speechless"). He then says the voiceless today include animals, so the righteous should speak for them. He notes that he is reading the Hebrew word *sibat* as "fullness of pride" rather than "fullness of food," and that the translation "overfed" is less likely.

### God's mercy: the ten righteous, Nineveh, and repentance
From Genesis 18:20-33 he draws the point that God would spare Sodom for the sake of ten righteous people. He presents this as a measure of how much God values righteousness, and he compares it to the line of Jonah 3:6-10, where the king of Nineveh orders a fast, sackcloth and an end to violence, and "God relented." He reads Jonah 4:11 as God pitying a city that "cannot discern between their right hand and their left," and he refers to a separate live talk he gave on left and right. He cites 2 Peter 3:9 ("not willing that any should perish") and Matthew 4:17 ("Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand") as the same message. He says the Noah/Jonah point is that repentance, not destruction, is the goal.

### The temple as a "den of violence"
Craig argues that the Hebrew *perets* in Jeremiah 7:11 should be rendered "violence" or "violent ones," not "thieves." He reads the temple sacrifices as the violence he means and connects the Jeremiah line to Jesus' cleansing of the temple in Matthew 21:12-13, which he says quotes Isaiah 56:7 and Jeremiah 7:11. He then argues that God does not dwell in a temple made by hands, that human bodies should be the temple of the Holy Spirit, and that the same logic applies to eating animal products and to the wider earth. He links this to Genesis 6:11-12, where "all flesh" had corrupted its way and "the earth was filled with violence" before the flood.

### Rejection of sacrifice and the Edenic diet
Craig cites Isaiah 1:11 ("I do not delight in the blood of bulls or of lambs or of goats"), then says the prophets consistently reject animal sacrifice. He attributes "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" to Hosea and a line about a "humble and contrite heart" to David, without giving chapter numbers. He argues from Genesis 1:29-30 that God's original diet for humans and animals was the green herb and the fruit of the trees, not flesh. Dominion in Genesis 1:26-28 is then read as stewardship: he says we are to "tend and keep" the garden (Genesis 2:15, where he glosses *shamar* as "guardians"), and that a steward is "ruler over all that he has" (Luke 12:42-44). He cites Isaiah 11:6-9 for the image of children leading animals in peace.

### Repentance as action and as a changed mind
Craig says repentance means turning away and that saying sorry without changing action is adding sin. He answers a question from Tanya by agreeing that repentance also means "changing your mind, your thinking." He argues that people who change diet for health alone may not have transformed minds, and he describes a "Daniel diet" approach (he refers to Daniel's diet and says it is healthier and "wiser," without giving a chapter) in which a church member commits to 40 days without animal products and is then educated so they do not return to old habits. He recommends affirming people's existing change and then explaining its divine dimension. He ties this to 2 Chronicles 7:14, where God "will heal their land" when his people humble themselves and turn from wicked ways, and to Joel 2:13 ("tear your hearts instead").

### Hope and judgment in the future
In Q&A he reads Zephaniah 1:3 and Ezekiel 34:25 as passages about the sweeping away of animals and of "dangerous" animals. He offers two readings of the sweeping: either violent people die in their own violence, or they repent and are no longer destroyers. He says his hope is for repentance and cites 2 Peter 3:9 again. He reads Revelation 11:18 and 21:4 as future assurances that destroyers of the earth will be judged and that there will be no more death or violence.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Dominion" means humans may kill or use animals as they please.** Craig reads Genesis 1:26-31 as stewardship and points to the herb-and-fruit diet of Genesis 1:29-30 to argue that violence toward animals is the opposite of God's design.
- **Jeremiah 7:11 says "den of thieves."** He argues the Hebrew *perets* should be translated "violence," so the temple critique is about violence, not simply theft.
- **Repentance just means saying you're sorry.** Craig says true repentance must be backed by action, and, in response to Tanya, that it also includes a changed mind.
- **The sin of Sodom is a matter of sexual misconduct.** He does not argue this against a named view, but he argues from Ezekiel 16:49-50 that the sin was pride and neglect of the poor and needy.
- **Animal agriculture is the only ecological harm.** In Q&A he says palm oil, parking lots, shopping malls, factories, overfishing and coral destruction are also harms, though he calls animal agriculture "the biggest culprit."
