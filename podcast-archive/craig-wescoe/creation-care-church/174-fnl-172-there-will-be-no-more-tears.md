## Summary

Craig Wescoe, presenting a Friday live talk for Creation Care Church, asks what Revelation 21:4 ("God will wipe away every tear... there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying") promises, and what it means for animals and for people whose hearts break over animal suffering. He carries the talk himself, moving through a long chain of scripture passages, and reads the promise as a restoration of the "very good" creation of Genesis 1 rather than an escape from the world.

His main claim is that the promise covers animals as well as humans. He points to the everlasting covenant with "every creature of all flesh" in Genesis 9, to Hosea 2:18 and Isaiah 11 on peace with the beasts, and to Romans 8 on creation "groaning" and waiting. He also argues that tears are not a sign of faithlessness: God himself weeps (he cites Hebrews 5:7 and John 11, where Jesus weeps before raising Lazarus), so grief over animal suffering is part of Christlike concern. Wescoe ties this to a present ethic: repentance, which he defines as turning away from evil, and a plant-based diet, which he grounds in Genesis 1:29. He also says animals, including companion animals and the animals people eat, will be in the kingdom.

The talk ends with a 30-minute live Q&A that answers commenters' greetings and questions, including whether animals will be in heaven, whether it is too late to change, and whether forgiveness makes behavior irrelevant. The closing prayer asks God to bring the kingdom and reunite humans and animals. Wescoe presents all of this as his own reading of the texts. He does not engage the historical or critical questions a scholar would raise about these passages.

## Key Teachings

### Revelation 21:4 is a promise of renewal, not only escape
Wescoe reads "make all things new" as renewal or restoration, set in a new heaven and new earth. He links it to the Lord's Prayer ("Your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as it is in heaven") and to Genesis 1, where God creates a "very good" world. In his reading, the hope is a restored earth without death or fear, grounded in Jesus's resurrection power and in his words "I am the resurrection and the life" (John 11:25). He also says in the Q&A that Revelation 21:4 draws on Isaiah 25:8, a point a commenter raised and he affirmed, arguing that much of the New Testament draws on the Hebrew Bible.

### The promises include animals
This is the talk's central argument, and he builds it through several texts:
- **Genesis 9:** he stresses that God makes a covenant five times in this chapter with the animals. He notes the phrase "every creature of all flesh" and says the covenant is "everlasting," so "it will never be taken away from any of the animals."
- **Hosea 2:18:** God will make a covenant with "the beasts of the field" and shatter bow and sword so the creatures "lie down safely."
- **Isaiah 11:6-9:** the wolf and lamb, leopard and kid, and a little child leading them. He reads the "little child" as the peacemakers and children of God of Matthew 5 and Romans 8, and says Isaiah pictures animals restored to a plant diet (Genesis 1:30).
- **Revelation 5:13:** every creature in heaven, earth, and sea praises God, which he takes as fulfilment of the Genesis 9 promise.

### Creation groans and waits (Romans 8)
Wescoe reads Romans 8:18-25 as showing that "the whole creation" awaits the revealing of the children of God, animals included. He compares this groaning to a pregnant mother eagerly awaiting birth: the waiting is real, and the promise makes it hope. He ties this to Matthew 5:9 (peacemakers as children of God) and Isaiah 11.

### Tears are shared with God, and grief is Christlike
He cites Psalm 34:15-18 (the Lord is near the brokenhearted) and says God "hears those cries," including those of animals separated from their young in the dairy industry. He reads Psalm 126:5-6 as "sowing in tears" that will be reaped in joy. He cites Hebrews 5:6-8 (Jesus offering "prayers and supplications with vehement cries and tears," with Psalm 110:4 in view) and John 11:31-44, where Jesus weeps before raising Lazarus. He concludes that tears are not wrong and that Jesus's grief and his confidence in resurrection are "both... consistent and Christlike."

### Repentance, forgiveness, and present practice
Wescoe reads Acts 3:19-21 ("repent... times of refreshing") alongside Romans 12:2 and Genesis 1:29-31. He defines repentance as turning away from evil, and says God declared the original creation "very good," including the diet of Genesis 1:29, which he calls "entirely plant-based." He cites 2 Chronicles 7:14 and 2 Peter 3:9 to argue that God forgives and heals the land when people turn back. He recommends stopping support for dairy separation, laboratory testing, factory farming, captivity in zoos and aquariums, and habitat destruction.

### Heaven includes companion animals and "the animals on your plate" (Q&A)
In answer to a commenter's question about whether animals will be in heaven, Wescoe says "without reservation, yes." He argues that Balaam's donkey (Numbers 22) will be there and that many other animals will as well. He says he tells people that their companion animals will likely be in the kingdom, and then asks them whether they will be ready to face "the animals on your plate." He says he does this "not to guilt trip the person, but to open their eyes."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Animals are outside God's promises.** Wescoe argues the promises of Revelation 21:4, Genesis 9, Hosea 2:18, Isaiah 11, and Romans 8 include animals as "every creature of all flesh."
- **Revelation's promise is new and purely about heaven.** He says the promise draws on Isaiah 25:8 and the Hebrew Bible, and that the restoration is of the earth "as it was in the beginning."
- **It's too late to change.** In the Q&A he says "It's not too late. It's never too late," and urges people not to wait, since "we don't know if we even have tomorrow."
- **Forgiveness makes behavior irrelevant.** A commenter suggests people will be forgiven, so change does not matter. He responds that Jesus calls for repentance and for doing his teaching, not for assuming forgiveness.
- **Animal change takes millennia.** He says a shift in animals' fear of humans can happen in one lifetime, citing the Galápagos Islands as an example he has heard about but not visited.
- **Christians who ignore animals are without scriptural grounds.** He presents his reading as the one the text supports, and argues that concern for animals is "part of our connection to God."
