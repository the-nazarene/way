## Summary

The talk asks what it means to call Abraham "the friend of God" (James 2:23), what the Bible says friendship is, what the opposite of a friend (an adversary) looks like, and how a person can be a friend of God. The host works mainly through a chain of Hebrew Bible and New Testament proof texts. Genesis 15:5-6 supplies the promise and Abraham's belief; Isaiah 41:8 and 2 Chronicles 20:7 are read as God explicitly calling Abraham "my friend." The host reads friendship as shared purpose and mutual care, drawing on John 15:12-15, Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 and Jeremiah 29:11-13. Adversaries are those who sow strife (Proverbs 16:28), return evil for good (Psalm 109:2-5), and dig their own traps (Psalm 57:6).

The host's central practical claim is that the friend of God mirrors God's character toward all creatures, and that this includes animals. He argues that "dominion" (Genesis 1:26-28) must look like the shepherd's care in John 10:10-11, not killing and eating, and that Genesis 1:29 gives a plant diet. He also argues that God makes covenants with animals (Genesis 9, Hosea 2:18, Isaiah 11:9), which he takes as evidence that animals are God's friends too. The host does not argue from history or manuscripts. He is preaching from scripture to a congregation that practices veganism, and he presents his reading as the Bible's own. Much of the talk's last third is the live chat Q&A, where he answers questions on animal sacrifice, Judaism and veganism.

The talk ends with a closing prayer for the "threefold cord" of God and his followers, a reading of Romans 16:20 as the adversary's inevitable defeat, and an invitation to the Zoom fellowship and the monthly prayer meeting.

## Key Teachings

### Abraham as God's friend: promise met by trust
The host reads Genesis 15:5-6 (the stars and the descendants, Abraham believing God "and it was credited to him as righteousness") as the passage James 2:23 is drawing on. He admits Genesis 15 "doesn't overtly call it friendship," so he turns to Isaiah 41:8 ("the descendants of Abraham, my friend") and 2 Chronicles 20:7 ("Abraham, your friend"). In his reading, friendship is the relationship God establishes through promises of land, descendants and blessing. Abraham's righteousness is his belief shown in action: he goes where God sends him.

### Friendship in John 15: knowing the master's plan
From John 15:12-17 the host takes two points. The commandment is to love one another "as I have loved you," and Jesus says "No longer do I call you servants... But I have called you friends." The host's distinction is that a servant follows orders without knowing the plan, while a friend is told the plan. He identifies that plan as the kingdom coming "on earth as it is in heaven," which he links to the Lord's Prayer. He treats "if you do whatever I command you" as the condition of that friendship.

### Friendship as mutual protection: Ecclesiastes and the threefold cord
He reads Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 as describing two people who "have each other's back," with the image of two people standing back to back so they can see in both directions. He says the third strand of the "threefold cord" is God, so that two people joined to each other and to God form a cord "not quickly broken." He notes that Ecclesiasticus is a different, deuterocanonical book and that the passage is from Ecclesiastes in the Hebrew Bible.

### Adversary: strife, evil for good, and the self-set trap
The opposite of a friend is someone who "sows strife" (Proverbs 16:28) and "a whisperer [who] separates best of friends." Psalm 109:2-5, which the host reads as David's complaint that he repaid love with hatred, shows the adversary returning evil for good. Psalm 57:6 ("They have dug a pit before me... they themselves have fallen") supports his claim that wrongdoers entrap themselves. From this he draws: "We don't have to emulate the ways of sin to punish the sin. It punishes itself." Believers respond to adversaries with good (Luke 6:26-27, Romans 12:14) and do not take on the adversary's methods. He adds that Proverbs 22:24-25 warns against "a man of wrath," which he ties to murder.

### The shepherd's dominion and the roaring lion
He sets the "roaring lion" of 1 Peter 5:8 against Jesus as the good shepherd who lays down his life (John 10:10-11). He reads the "wolf in sheep's clothing" and fruit-bearing tests as a way to tell friends from adversaries. He argues that the church's dominion over animals (Genesis 1:26-28) must look like Christ's dominion over believers: protective and not devouring. He concludes that eating animals, dismembering them, or taking their milk and babies is the lion's way, not the shepherd's.

### Animals as God's friends, and Genesis 1:29 as diet
The host argues that God makes promises to his friends, and that God makes covenants with animals (citing Genesis 9, Hosea 2:18 and Isaiah 11:9, with "Genesis 9 five times"). He therefore concludes that animals are also God's friends and that the friend of God shows the same care toward them. Proverbs 12:10 ("a righteous man regards the life of his animal") is his test of cruelty. In the chat he applies Genesis 1:29 ("This is your diet... the plants of the ground") as the limit on dominion. He also says that animal sacrifice is not required by God, citing "dozens of passages" he says he can share, and points to his study guide (see Books & Resources).

### Identity in Christ: 1 John 3:8-9 and the call to repent
Using 1 John 3:8-9, the host argues that a person should not adopt "I'm a sinner" as an identity, since that is a past state. He says the new identity is a child of God who was made in God's image. He adds that "most churches don't discuss these passages." The talk closes with the "destroy the works of the devil" line and Isaiah 11:9's picture of the whole earth "filled with knowledge" of God, which he equates with love (1 John 4:8,16).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Being a sinner is one's identity.** The host says that "I'm a sinner" is a past state; the identity is being a child of God, based on 1 John 3:8-9.
- **Dominion means the right to kill and eat animals.** The host says dominion must look like Christ's care for the sheep (John 10:10-11) and that Genesis 1:29 assigns a plant diet.
- **The Bible requires or will restore animal sacrifice.** He calls the claim that the future includes animal sacrifice "a lie" and says God "doesn't desire it, never required it."
- **Judaism requires animal sacrifice, so Jewish people cannot be vegan.** He answers that not all of Judaism teaches this and that one can follow the more informed branches (naming Rabbi Akiva Gersh).
- **Churches avoid these passages.** He says "most churches don't discuss these passages" and attributes this to concern about losing members and money.
