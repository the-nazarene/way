## Key Teachings

### 1. Genesis 3:21 does not describe an animal being killed
Craig's central point is that the letter reads an animal sacrifice into the clothing of Adam and Eve. He says: "Nowhere in the text does it say anything about an animal being slain to produce the garments of skin." He offers two alternatives: the skins might be an outer protective layer, or they might be animal skins that God spoke into existence without death, as he did in the rest of Genesis 1-2. He calls the letter's reading a "conjecture" and "bad hermeneutics."

### 2. Two separate plant diets (Genesis 1:29-30)
Craig argues that humans and animals received different food in Genesis 1. Humans get "every seed-bearing plant" and "every tree whose fruit yields seed." Animals get "every green plant." He says: "these are two separate diets, very similar diet, both plant-based, both vegan, but they're separate, they're named differently, they're not the same thing." This answers the letter's claim that humans were "vegetarian, not vegan" and that they used milk and cheese, which Craig says no biblical or "extra-biblical" source he knows mentions.

### 3. Genesis 9 and the animal covenant
Craig reads Genesis 9:3-4 as God speaking to humans ("just as I gave you the green plants, I now give you everything, but you shall not eat flesh with its life"), with the animals as the earlier recipients of the green plants. He then cites Genesis 9:16 and the repeated "everlasting covenant" with "all living creatures," which he counts at five mentions in chapter 9 and says could be seven. He also cites Hosea 2:18. He says that "everlasting" is the same word used for God's love for humanity, and he describes his own experience teaching a Bible study about who the Noah covenant was made with.

### 4. Acts 10 and the Hebrews sacrifice argument
Craig says Peter's vision in Acts 10 was about the inclusion of Gentiles, citing Acts 11:18, and not about permission to eat animals. He says that Peter "never killed or ate the animals in the vision." On sacrifice, he quotes Hebrews: "it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins," and reads Matthew 12:7 (quoting Hosea 6:6) as identifying the innocent as the animals.

### 5. Ritual, the Eucharist and the "table of devils"
Against the letter's claim that the Eucharist justifies eating flesh, Craig cites Matthew 26:26-27 (bread, not lamb) and 1 Corinthians 10:21 ("the table of the Lord and the table of demons"). He then reads a passage he attributes to the Clementine Homily 7.4, which he connects to the abstentions in Acts 15:28: food offered to idols, dead carcasses, animals "suffocated" or "caught by wild beasts," and blood. He argues that slaughter by throat-cutting and gas chamber is asphyxiation, and that "you can't eat flesh without consuming some amount of blood." He concludes that meat-eating places a believer at "the table of devils."

### 6. Nonviolence and the Temple, plus "abusus non tollit usum"
Craig rejects the letter's Latin maxim ("abuse does not take away use") by saying: "It is the command against violence that prohibits using animals as objects to exploit or kill." He cites Proverbs 3:31, Psalm 11:6, and Proverbs 31:8 ("speak up for... all who are appointed to die"). He reads John 2:13-17 and Matthew 21:13 (quoting Jeremiah 7:11) as Jesus's passionate opposition to violence against animals, and relies on his own reading of the Greek or Hebrew word behind "den of thieves," which he gives as "Strong's 6530... violence or violent ones." He presents his reading of "passionately" as a model for how to respond.

### 7. Faithfulness, the Daniel story and the "Ebionim"
Craig answers the letter's claim that veganism is "uninformed" conscience by citing Daniel 1:8, 1:12 and 1:20, where Daniel refuses the king's meat and wine and is found "10 times better." He reads Luke 12:42-43 on the "faithful and wise manager" and quotes Eusebius (*Demonstratio Evangelica* 3.5) describing the disciples as "Ebionim" who abstained from wine and meat. He uses both to argue that faithful practice in Scripture looks like his own diet. He ends with Romans 14:15 and 1 Corinthians 8:13 to say that eating meat causes a brother or sister distress and is therefore not walking in love (also 1 John 4:8, John 13:34-35).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God slew an animal to clothe Adam and Eve, prefiguring Christ's sacrifice.** Craig says the text never mentions an animal being killed. The skins could be an outer protective layer or spoken into existence, as other creation is in Genesis.
- **Humans were "vegetarian, not vegan," and consumed milk and cheese in Eden.** Craig says Genesis 1:29 names no dairy, and no "extra-biblical sources" he knows mention it either.
- **Genesis 9 gave humans permission to eat animals.** Craig argues that verses 3-4 are spoken to humans as a contrast with the animals' earlier plant diet, and that the animals are the covenant recipients in verse 16.
- **Peter's vision in Acts 10 permitted eating animals.** Craig says it concerned the inclusion of Gentiles, as Acts 11:18 shows.
- **Animals were created to serve humans.** Craig says Genesis 1:21-22 shows animals blessed before humans, and that Jesus came to serve, not to be served (Matthew 20:28).
- **The Eucharist shows that eating meat is Christlike.** Craig says the Last Supper used bread (Matthew 26:26-27), and that 1 Corinthians 10:21 contrasts the Lord's table with the table of demons.
- **"Abuse does not take away use" justifies animal use.** Craig says the biblical command against violence governs use and forbids exploiting or killing animals.
- **Daniel's diet was not a moral claim.** Craig reads Daniel's refusal of royal meat as faithful and wise.
