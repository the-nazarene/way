## Summary

Craig Wescoe opens a Friday night live talk for Creation Care Church by asking how a believer can receive God's abundant blessings in the year. He ties the topic to the biblical calendar: he argues that the week he is speaking in is the first week of the biblical year, and that resurrection Sunday falls inside the Feast of Unleavened Bread, so the new year can be counted back two weeks from it. Psalm 65:11 ("You crown the year with Your goodness") is his opening text, linked to the three annual festivals that cluster around harvests.

Wescoe's main answer is relational rather than material. Drawing on Jeremiah 29:11, Proverbs 3:5-6, Psalm 51:10-11, Ezekiel 11:19 and 1 John 3, he argues that blessing comes through closeness to God, a transformed ("clean" or "soft") heart, and love that extends to people and to animals. He says explicitly that financial hardship does not mean a person is outside God's will. He also reads Ephesians 4 and Romans 12:2 as calls to put off old conduct, and Philippians 4:13 and Isaiah 40:31 as promises of strength. He presents the talk's ideas as his reading of scripture, not as a settled chronology or exegesis.

The talk ends with a Q&A on the chat (greetings from many countries, Sunday's Zoom sermon with Tracy Ford, a monthly vegan Christian discipleship school, and the Discord). In the Q&A Wescoe takes questions on the Matthew/Luke "bread and stone" saying, which he treats as a source-critical problem he has not resolved, and on the leaven of the Feast of Unleavened Bread, which he reads as the teaching of the Pharisees and scribes. The talk closes with prayer and a note that the next live talk is on April 3.

## Key Teachings

### The biblical new year and the resurrection calendar
Wescoe argues that the biblical year begins with the 14th of the first month, the Feast of Unleavened Bread (Exodus 12:18). He reads Leviticus 23:14-17 as naming three annual gatherings tied to harvest. He states that Jesus was killed on Passover, the day before the Shabbat that begins Unleavened Bread week, and rose on the Sunday following that Shabbat, so resurrection Sunday always falls within that week. He links the wave offering in Leviticus 23 to Christ as "first fruits" (1 Corinthians 15:20), and counts fifty days from resurrection Sunday to Pentecost, which he associates with Acts. These are his readings of the calendar, and he does not cite a calendrical source for them in this talk.

### Blessing as relationship and abundance, not guaranteed wealth
Wescoe says God "has plans to prosper" his children, but that the blessings may come as supportive people, a location, or a role, not necessarily money. He states: "it's not necessarily a financial blessing that if you're struggling financially, that doesn't mean that you're not doing his will." He grounds this in Jeremiah 29:11 and Proverbs 3:5-6 (trust, acknowledging God in all ways).

### The clean heart and the gift of the Spirit
He builds a sequence from Psalm 51:10-11 to Ezekiel 11:19 ("a heart of flesh"), arguing that receiving the Holy Spirit requires a softened heart that is compassionate toward suffering creatures. In the sermon he uses the Jesus saying about a child asking for bread or fish to argue that the Father will give the Holy Spirit. In the Q&A he is more cautious: he says the wording differs between Matthew (good things) and Luke (Holy Spirit), and that he thinks the original may have been "bread" and "stone," with fish and egg as later substitutions. He says he has not reached a firm conclusion.

### Love for neighbors, including animals, and enemies
Wescoe reads 1 John 3:11 and 1 John 3:1 ("love one another") as the crux of the message, and extends "neighbor" to include animals. He argues that love should continue toward those who do evil, while boundaries are still set, and that the goal is their repentance rather than punishment. He reads Romans 12:2 with Genesis 1 (eating plants, stewardship of animals) as the "good and acceptable and perfect will of God," and presents the believer as an exemplar of that will in the world.

### Readiness for the Kingdom
Citing Isaiah 43:18-19 (a road in the wilderness), he links it to John the Baptist's voice and says the Lord's Prayer is "coming to fruition." He urges that believers "stop hurting the animals, not waiting for Jesus to fix it," and cites the parable of the lamps without oil as a warning to be ready now. He says the Kingdom is "on earth as it is in heaven" and should be prepared for by living to its standards.

### The leaven as the teaching of the Pharisees and scribes
In the Q&A, answering a viewer, Wescoe reads Jesus's warning about the leaven of the Pharisees and scribes as a warning about their hypocritical teaching, not about sin in general (though he says that reading is also applicable). He contrasts "the pure word of Elohim" with mixtures he attributes to "the lying priestly scribes," and says Jesus offers his own teaching as the daily bread. He offers Numbers 21:6 (God not giving snakes for bread) as a possible allusion, which a viewer had suggested, and calls it "very reasonable."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Financial prosperity as a sign of God's favor.** Wescoe says God's blessings may come as relationships, a location, or a role, and that financial struggle does not mean a person is outside God's will. He grounds this in Jeremiah 29:11 and the broader "plans to prosper" language.
- **Blessing as only a personal or private matter.** He argues that the blessings are meant to be shared, that the Spirit's abundance overflows toward people and animals, and that the believer is to be a visible witness to God's will.
