## Summary

Wescoe asks why Christians should remember Jesus's death on Good Friday, and what is "good" about it. His answer is that the death is not the good part. The good part is the resurrection, which he presents as Jesus overcoming death and proving God's power to raise people to eternal life. He reads John 19 and 20 to set the scene, then argues from John 11:25, 1 Peter 1:3 and Romans 8:11 that this hope rests on the resurrection.

He then widens the claim. Drawing on Romans 8:18-22, Ecclesiastes 3:19 and Acts 3:19-21, he argues that the hope is for all creatures, not only humans. He reads Genesis 1:26-31 as God's original design, in which humans and animals eat plants, and calls that design "very good." He presents this as the goodness Christians should live by. He also says the resurrection extends to animals and that the same animals will be raised, using Balaam's donkey and the Genesis 9 covenant as support. He offers his own reading of the John 20 timing of the Holy Spirit, and he calls John 21 a later addition, though he frames both points as "up for you to determine."

The second half is mostly Q&A from the live chat and a promotion for his children's book *The Law of Yah*, illustrated by Nola Lindsay, which teaches the vegan-creation reading to children. The talk closes with prayer and an announcement of the next talk on April 17.

## Key Teachings

### 1. The resurrection, not the death, makes Good Friday "good"
Wescoe reads John 19:14-19 and John 20:11-23, then argues that Good Friday is named "good" because of what follows: "you can't have a resurrection without a death." He ties this to John 11:25 ("I am the resurrection and the life") and reads Jesus as having raised himself by his own power, as "the word of God made flesh." He adds 1 Peter 1:3 ("begotten us again to a living hope through the resurrection") and Romans 8:11 (the Spirit "will also give life to your mortal bodies") to argue that the same power will raise believers.

### 2. John 20's timing of the Spirit and the later John 21 (his reading)
Wescoe argues that John 20:19-23 implies the disciples received the Holy Spirit on resurrection day, the day that begins the count to Pentecost. He says Acts places this at Pentecost 50 days later, and suggests Luke, who he says was not an eyewitness and used sources, "confused" the account. He presents this as "my interpretation."

On John 21 he states: "this is a pretty much cons uh scholarly consensus position that the original account of John ends at chapter 20 and chapter 21 is a later edition." He notes that the disciples are not called fishermen earlier in John. In the Q&A he says he is "not married to the traditionalist view" and thinks chapter 21 was written later by people with a "more Mark perspective." He does not name the scholars he means.

### 3. Genesis 1 as a vegan design
Wescoe reads Genesis 1:26-31, focusing on the food given in verses 29-30 ("every herb that yields seed... To you it shall be for food"). He answers the objection "if God wanted us to be vegan, why didn't he just tell us?" with "He did." He then makes the claim that "at no other point in the Bible does God say the arrangement of things in the world is good... This is the only time." He treats this as God's "very good will" that Christians should follow. He also uses Psalm 31:19 ("how abundant is your goodness") to say God's goodness is not lacking.

### 4. Creation's hope and the restoration of all things
Wescoe reads Romans 8:18-22, which speaks of creation waiting and groaning for "the glorious liberty of the children of God." He pairs it with Ecclesiastes 3:19 ("man has no advantage over beasts") and reads that passage as showing animals share the same destiny. He then reads Acts 3:19-21 ("the times of the restoration of all things") and claims the prophets all spoke of "this day this return to the very good design for God's creation." He also cites James 1:17-18, calling Jesus the "first fruit of all of his creatures," which he uses to argue resurrection hope is "not just humans."

### 5. Will the same animals be raised?
In answer to a chat question, Wescoe says "there's good reason to think that it's going to be the same animals." His arguments:
- Hosea, which he reads as a promise that the bow and sword will be abolished. He says that promise would mean little if the animals who lived and died never saw it. (He speaks the reference as "Hosea 21:18," which looks like a misspoken citation; verify before quoting.)
- Genesis 9, where God's "everlasting covenant is with all the animals," which he says is repeated five times.
- Balaam's donkey. He calls the donkey a model of Christlike love, and says that if she is not in the kingdom, "something's a miss." He cites no reference for this story.

He also says the Hindu and Jain practice of not eating animals, which he links to reincarnation, is "predominantly... vegetarian, not vegan" in his experience.

### 6. Women as first witnesses
Wescoe reads John 20 as showing Jesus appearing first to women. In the Q&A, responding to the claim "women shouldn't be preachers," he argues the women were the first to preach the resurrection and that "Jesus specifically called the women to be the first to preach the good news of the resurrection." He says the passage he is answering was a 1 Timothy passage ("first Timothy or whatever") but gives no details.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Good Friday is good because Jesus's death is good.** Wescoe says the death is not good in itself; what makes the day good is the resurrection that follows.
- **God never said humans should be vegan.** He argues Genesis 1:29-30 does, and that it is the "very good" original design.
- **The gospel and eternal life are only for humans.** He argues from Romans 8, Ecclesiastes 3:19 and James 1:17-18 that the hope is for all creatures, and that "you're underestimating the love of God" if you think otherwise.
- **John 21 is part of the original Gospel of John.** He says scholarly consensus treats it as a later addition, though he presents it as his view that viewers can weigh.
- **Women should not be preachers.** In the Q&A he argues that Jesus called women to be the first to preach the resurrection.
- **Animals will be new animals in heaven.** He argues the same animals will be raised, based on Hosea, Genesis 9 and Balaam's donkey.
