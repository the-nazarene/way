## Summary

Recorded as a Friday live session for Creation Care Church, this talk asks what Leviticus 3:17 ("you shall eat neither fat nor blood") actually requires, and whether the Torah permits eating animal flesh. The unnamed host argues that it does not. He reasons that flesh cannot be separated from blood and fat, so the only way to keep the command is the diet of Genesis 1:29 (plants and fruit). He reads Leviticus 11 as a distinction between herbivores and flesh-eaters rather than a list of foods to eat. He cites Numbers 11 and several Proverbs as warnings against craving flesh.

## Key Teachings

### 1. "Permanent" prohibition and the impossibility of separating blood and fat from flesh
The host reads Leviticus 3:17 as "a perpetual or permanent statute throughout your generations." He argues that since animal bodies are "composed of flesh and fat and blood and bone," no one can eat the flesh without eating the forbidden parts. He rejects the practice of hanging a carcass to drain it. His illustration is a catheter: if urine stops dripping, that does not mean none is left, so draining blood does not make meat safe to eat. He then says that trimming fat from a steak does not fix the problem, since "fat is part of the flesh."

### 2. Genesis 1:29 and 9:4 as the original diet; Leviticus 11 as herbivore vs. flesh-eater
He reads Genesis 1:29 ("every herb that yields seed... to you it shall be for food") as God's designed diet, and cites Genesis 9:4 ("you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood") as a later limit. On Leviticus 11, he notes that most "clean" animals are herbivores and most "unclean" ones eat flesh. He proposes that the original instruction was "you should eat like" herbivores, and that the list was later distorted. He also notes that Leviticus 11:41-43 says anything that walks on the earth should not be eaten, and argues that this covers most animals on a typical plate.

### 3. Numbers 11 and Proverbs as warnings against flesh-eating
In Numbers 11 the host reads Moses's account of the craving for flesh: the people are told they will eat "not 1 day, nor 2 days... but for a whole month until it comes out your nostrils," and while the meat is "still between their teeth" a plague strikes them, and the place is named "the grave of craving." He presents this as the consequence of "making your God your belly." He then reads Proverbs 6:16-17 (hands that shed innocent blood are among the things the Lord hates), Proverbs 12:10 (the righteous regard the life of their animal), and Proverbs 23:20 (do not mix with "riotous eaters of flesh"). He argues that Proverbs 23:20 forbids gorging on flesh at all, not only excessive eating.

### 4. Jeremiah 7-8 and the "lying pen of the scribe"; Jesus at the temple
This is the core of the talk. The host reads Jeremiah 7:11 ("a den of perets," which he renders "blood shedders") and Jeremiah 7:21-29, where God says "I did not speak to your fathers or command them... concerning burnt offerings or sacrifices" on the day of the Exodus. He reads Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribe") as a charge that the sacrificial laws were falsely added. He gives a backstory in which Hilkiah the high priest found an older Torah scroll without animal sacrifices and told Josiah, who instituted reforms. He says he gives this in more detail in a discipleship school lesson. He says Jesus quoted Jeremiah 7:11 when he overturned the tables, and that the issue was animal bloodshed, not money-changing. He also cites Hosea 6:6 as a "most quoted" line of Jesus, and calls the temple confrontation "literally the hill that he dies on."

### 5. Hebrews 7 and the annulment of the Levitical priesthood
He reads Hebrews 7:1-18 and walks through its Melchizedek argument: Abraham tithed to Melchizedek, Levi was "still in the loins" of Abraham, so the Levitical priests were tithing through Abraham to a higher priesthood. He points to Psalm 110:4 ("You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek") and to the statement that the former commandment is "annulled." He says that whether the Levitical priesthood was replaced or was never legitimate, the result is that Jesus is "that once for all priesthood," with no altar or animal slaughter in it. He acknowledges this is "a little complicated" and offers his summary.

### 6. Sacrifice as slaughter, idolatry, and the terms "fat" and "milk" (Q&A)
In answer to chat questions, the host argues that translators obscure the difference between "slaughter" and "sacrifice," and that buying meat from a grocery store is participation in the same practice. He calls that "literally idolatry." He claims the Hebrew words for "fat" (halev) and "milk" (halav) have the same consonants, so Leviticus may also forbid milk. He says the "land flowing with milk and honey" refers to date paste. He argues that "fat" in Leviticus is not modern nutritional fat, so avocados are not included. On the Nazarite vow, he says the prohibition on grapes stands for the prohibition on flesh and blood, and that this "or any product thereof" extends to eggs and milk. He also says he avoids grape juice only to meet other Nazarites on their own terms.

- **Draining blood makes meat kosher-like or acceptable.** He says blood remains in the flesh, citing the catheter analogy.
- **Trimming off visible fat makes the meat acceptable.** He says fat is part of the flesh.
- **Jesus cleansed the temple to protest money-changing.** The host says it was a protest against animal bloodshed, citing Jeremiah 7:11.
- **Animal sacrifice is a thing of the past.** He argues that buying meat from a store is sacrifice and idolatry.
- **"Fat" in Leviticus means nutritional fat.** He says the biblical term refers to a part of the animal body, not to avocados or nuts.
- **Honey is bee vomit.** In Q&A, he says it is bee "milk," and that "land flowing with milk and honey" refers to date paste.
