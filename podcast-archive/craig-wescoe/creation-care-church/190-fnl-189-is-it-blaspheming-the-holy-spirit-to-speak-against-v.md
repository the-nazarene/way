## Summary

Craig, pastor of Creation Care Church, takes up the question in the title: does speaking against veganism count as blaspheming the Holy Spirit, the one sin Matthew 12:31-32 says will not be forgiven? He argues that blasphemy against the Spirit means rejecting the Spirit of God in oneself. Opposing veganism on the basis of Jesus eating fish, or of God's commands to sacrifice, is not in itself that sin. Craig's real target is people who call a vegan lifestyle "a doctrine of demons," which he says is the sin the passage warns against. He says the test is the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23; Matthew 7:16-20).

His evidence runs in several steps. He reads Jeremiah 7:22 and 8:8 as saying God did not command burnt offerings when Israel left Egypt, and that the "lying pen of the scribe" introduced sacrifice. He argues that Luke 24:42-43, the "fish" that Jesus eats, shows manuscript variation (some witnesses add or swap "honeycomb"), that the earliest report of the Marcion gospel (via Tertullian) says only "food," and that "honey" in such texts means date paste. He then contrasts the Genesis 1 creation account (God speaks humanity into being and gives a plant diet, Genesis 1:29) with Genesis 2, and reads John 3:5-6 ("born of water and spirit") and John 1:14 ("the word became flesh") as pointing back to Genesis 1. He cites Galatians 5, 1 John 3 and 4, Matthew 7 and Matthew 9:13 for the fruit test. He reads 1 Timothy 4 as a warning against people who condemn God's good gifts.

The talk ends with an open question round. Craig says the passage makes forgiveness available to anyone who repents of rejecting the Spirit, and that a person can hold mistaken views about the Father and the Son and still be forgiven if they bear the Spirit's fruit. He admits he still teaches from both perspectives, and that his views on sacrifice are a conviction he holds, not a settled consensus. He closes with a prayer for repentance and the coming of the kingdom.

## Key Teachings

### 1. What "blaspheming the Holy Spirit" means
Craig starts with Matthew 12:31-32, then notes the parallels in Mark 3:28-29 and Luke 12:10. He also cites Gospel of Thomas saying 44, which says blasphemy against "the Father or the Son" is forgivable but not against the Holy Spirit. His definition: the Spirit is "the form of God that we receive in ourselves," so to blaspheme the Spirit is to reject receiving God's Spirit, to say "you're not welcome in this temple." Blasphemy against the Father or the Son, by contrast, can be forgiven, so a person can hold false beliefs about God and still be forgiven.

### 2. Jeremiah on the "lying pen of the scribe"
Craig reads Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribe") as a charge that scribes altered Scripture. He pairs it with Jeremiah 7:22, where God says he did not command burnt offerings and sacrifices when he brought Israel out of Egypt. Craig says Amos, Isaiah and Hosea also say God wants mercy, not sacrifice. He admits Exodus describes the Passover sacrifice, and he treats that as the sacrifice the scribes wrongly attributed to God's original command. In the Q&A he adds that Kings and Chronicles describe Hilkiah finding an older Torah without sacrifices, which he reads as evidence for his position.

### 3. The fish (and honeycomb) of Luke 24
Craig argues that Luke 24:42-43 is an unreliable basis for the claim that Jesus ate fish. He notes that some manuscripts omit "honeycomb," that the manuscripts disagree, and that this proves the text was changed, leaving only the question of how. He states that the earliest witness to the Marcion gospel, Tertullian, reports only "food" (*broma*). He suggests the original may have been "fish weed" or "fish relish" (seaweed-based dishes), and in the Q&A he says the honey was likely date paste, which he says Jewish speakers use. He also says Luke's account is not the only report of Jesus eating with disciples, and that bread was the more probable food in those accounts.

### 4. Genesis 1 versus Genesis 2, and "born of water and spirit"
Craig reads John 3:5-6 ("born of water and spirit ... that which is born of the spirit is spirit") as referring to being re-created in the image of God, not to being born of flesh. He sets Genesis 2:7 and 2:21-24 (humanity formed from dust, woman from a rib, "one flesh") against Genesis 1:1-3 (the Spirit hovering over the waters, God speaking creation into being) and Genesis 1:27-29 (image of God, "be fruitful," and a plant diet). He calls Genesis 1:29-31 God's first command, "Be vegan," and infers that the image-bearer should share God's diet.

### 5. The Word, Colossians and Jesus as the image
Craig connects Colossians 1:15-16 (Jesus as "the image of the invisible God," through whom all things were created) and John 1:1-3, 14 (the Word who "became flesh") back to Genesis 1, where God creates by speaking. He reads the incarnation as the Word taking on flesh to dwell among people, which he links to the sanctuary idea. He says that anyone who thinks Jesus was an animal abuser has the wrong picture of the image they are meant to follow.

### 6. The test of the Spirit: fruit, not doctrine
Craig's main test comes from Galatians 5:16-23 (flesh versus Spirit, and the fruits of the Spirit), 1 John 3:8-10 (the Son came to destroy the works of the devil, which are sin), Matthew 9:13 (quoting Hosea 6:6, "I desire mercy, not sacrifice"), 1 John 4:1 (test the spirits) and Matthew 7:13-17 ("you will know them by their fruits"). He argues that the fruits of love, joy and peace exclude killing animals, so anyone who causes animal suffering should examine whether the Spirit is in them.

### 7. Re-reading 1 Timothy 4
Craig says 1 Timothy 4:1-3 ("doctrines of demons," "forbidding to marry and commanding to abstain from foods") is the passage most often used against veganism. He argues that God created marriage and the plant diet (Genesis 1:28-29), so condemning them as demonic is "the doctrine of demons." He reads verse 4 ("every creature of God is good ... nothing is to be refused if it is received with Thanksgiving") as blessing what God created, not as permission to eat animals. He takes "godliness" in verses 7-8 to mean the image-bearer's practice. He says some translations are slanted to reverse this sense, though he does not name them.

### 8. Repentance and forgiveness
Craig says that the sin in Matthew 12 is ongoing rejection. A person who has rejected the Spirit can repent and receive forgiveness, because "it's only unforgivable as long as you continue doing it." He ties this to the "age to come" and to God's kingdom, which he describes as the restoration of a "very good world."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That Jesus ate fish, so eating animals is endorsed by him.** Craig argues that the manuscripts of Luke 24:42-43 disagree, that the earliest report says only "food," and that "honey" likely means date paste, so the passage cannot bear the weight placed on it.
- **That God commanded animal sacrifice.** Craig says Jeremiah 7:22 denies that God commanded burnt offerings and sacrifices when Israel left Egypt, and that the sacrificial system came from scribes who altered the Torah.
- **That 1 Timothy 4 calls veganism a doctrine of demons.** Craig says the chapter condemns those who forbid God's good gifts (marriage and foods), and that condemning a plant-based diet is the demonic teaching.
- **That speaking against veganism is a harmless opinion.** Craig says the passage makes this a serious matter, since rejecting the Spirit's fruit is the one unforgivable sin, though he also says it is forgivable on repentance.
- **That everyone who "blasphemes the Holy Spirit" is lost forever.** Craig says the sin is forgivable as long as the person stops rejecting the Spirit.
