## Summary

Wescoe, host of the Creation Care Church live "FNL" (Friday Night Live) talk, asks whether believers are "sighing and crying" over animal suffering, as Ezekiel 9:4 (NKJV) describes the men who receive a protective mark on their foreheads. He sets this against the "mark of the beast" in Revelation. His answer is that God's mark belongs to people who grieve over "abominations," and that a central abomination is the harm done to animals, including eating them. He reads this against the design in Genesis 1, which he takes to be a plant-based diet.

His evidence is mostly a chain of proof texts. Romans 8:22-23 is used to say creation is "groaning" and that believers should groan with it. Joel 1:18, Ezekiel 26:15 and Jeremiah 9:10 are read as describing animals suffering from human destruction. Luke 6:20-23 is taken to promise laughter to those who now weep. Deuteronomy 6:4-9 and Mark 12:28-34 are presented as the true content of God's "mark," which is love for God and neighbor.

The talk's most distinctive argument is about the number 666 in Revelation 13:18. Wescoe rejects the common answers (Nero, a papal hat, an Obama-era calculation) and says the Bible itself points to Solomon, because 1 Kings 10:14 gives Solomon's yearly gold as 666 talents. He reads Solomon's decline in 1 Kings 11:4 (his foreign wives turned his heart away) as the loss of God's mark, and his early request for wisdom in 1 Kings 3 as the opposite. He also argues that Daniel's refusal of the king's meat and wine (Daniel 1:8-16) shows the same pattern.

The talk ends with a Q&A from the live chat, where Wescoe answers the Solomon-versus-Nero question and separates the "antichrists" of 1 John from the false prophet of Revelation. He then closes with prayer and announces the next talk (FNL 191) for July 3. The talk is presented as Wescoe's own interpretation; he says he is open to better evidence.

## Key Teachings

### "Sighing and crying" as the response to abomination
Wescoe starts with translation differences: NKJV "sigh and cry," NIV "grieve and lament," ESV, BSV and NASB "sigh and groan." He reads the verb as sorrow and displeasure over the world's wrongs, set against celebrating them. He ties this to Romans 8:22-23, in which creation "groans" and believers "groan within ourselves" for the redemption of the body. He takes the creation in that verse to include animals. From this he concludes that believers should groan alongside animals, longing for their release from "bondage."

### Animal suffering as the abomination
He cites a set of prophetic texts for the claim that animals groan under human harm:
- Joel 1:18 (herds "restless because they have no pasture"), which he reads as habitat destruction.
- Ezekiel 26:15 (the "wounded cry" when "slaughter is made in the midst of you"), which he applies to animals being slaughtered.
- Jeremiah 9:10 (the land burned, the birds and beasts "fled"), which he links to extinction, overfishing and habitat loss. He also points to Jeremiah 7-8, where the temple is called a "den" of violence.
- He briefly mentions a passage in "Habakkuk?" about animals afraid of human harm, but he is unsure of the reference in the transcript.

He reads Jesus's line about the temple as a "den of blood shedders" as the same prophetic complaint. The Luke 6:20-23 Beatitudes ("Blessed are you who weep now, for you shall laugh") are read as promising joy in the coming kingdom to those now mourning these abominations. Wescoe also says that those who speak against bloodshed will be reviled, as the prophets were.

### Two marks: God's and the beast's
He sets Ezekiel 9:4 against Revelation 20:4 (those who "had not received his mark on their foreheads") and Revelation 19:20 (those who "received the mark of the beast"). God's mark goes to those who mourn abomination and reflect God's image. The beast's mark goes to those who worship the beast and its image, which he equates with celebrating harm.

### The "mark of God" is Deuteronomy 6 and Mark 12
He says the mark of God is the Shema of Deuteronomy 6:4-9 (love God with all your heart, soul and might; words on hands, between the eyes and on doorposts) lived out in practice. He quotes Mark 12:28-34, where Jesus calls the Shema the first commandment and loving neighbor second, and where a scribe says this is "more than all the whole burnt offerings and sacrifices." Wescoe says this proves God "desires mercy, not sacrifice," and that "neighbor" includes animals. He adds that Jesus's line "If you love me, keep my commandments" supports the same point.

### Solomon, 666 and the loss of the mark
For Revelation 13:18 ("Here is wisdom... his number is 666... the number of a man"), Wescoe proposes Solomon. He cites 1 Kings 10:14 (and 2 Chronicles 9:13) for Solomon's yearly gold, 666 talents. He reads 1 Kings 3:9-14, where Solomon asks for an understanding heart and is granted wisdom, as the right starting point. He reads 1 Kings 11:4 (the foreign wives turn his heart away) as the moment the mark of God is lost, and he applies "Where your treasure is, that's where your heart is" to Solomon's wealth. He says that "not exactly" the beast or false prophet, Solomon is a warning of a heart that has left God.

### Abomination as reversal; the diet of Genesis 1 and Daniel 1
He draws on Proverbs 16:5 (the proud are an abomination), Proverbs 17:15 (justifying the wicked is an abomination), Proverbs 12:10 (the righteous care for animals), and Isaiah 5:20-21 (calling evil good). He reads these as describing people who call compassion extreme and eating animals normal. He cites Genesis 1:29-31 as God's original grant of "every herb" and "every tree" for food, and calls the vegan diet "the way God designed us." He then cites Daniel 1:8-16, where Daniel refuses the king's meat and wine and eats vegetables, and Daniel and his companions look healthier and are "10 times better" in wisdom than the court magicians. He answers those who call veganism "the doctrine of demons" with 1 Timothy 4 (cited but not argued in detail).

### Antichrist distinguished from the false prophet
In the Q&A, asked about "Antichrist" (a commenter links it to Nero), Wescoe says the word does not appear in Revelation. He reads the "antichrists" of the Epistles of John as multiple figures, "shortly after his time" (late first to early second century), who corrupt Jesus's teaching. He does not identify the Revelation false prophet with them.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That 666 must be solved by numerology, conspiracy or modern figures** (Nero, the Pope, Obama). Wescoe says these calculations lack a biblical basis and that the text names a man associated with wisdom and the number 666 (Solomon).
- **That the Antichrist of 1 John is the same figure as the false prophet of Revelation.** He says the Epistles speak of multiple antichrists who corrupt Jesus's teaching, and that the Revelation false prophet is a different figure.
- **That vegan eating is "extreme" or a "doctrine of demons."** He answers that Genesis 1 gives plants for food and calls the creation "very good," and that Daniel's diet was wise.
- **That the mark of God is a visible sign on the body only.** He reads Deuteronomy 6's hands, foreheads and doorposts as lived devotion and love (Mark 12), not only as literal marks.

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** Jesus's summary of the law (Mark 12:28-34) is the center of the talk. Romans 8:22-23 is used as Paul's contribution to creation's groaning, without any contrast between Jesus and Paul.
- **Torah, sacrifice, diet and nonviolence:** The talk reads "mercy, not sacrifice" (via Mark 12) as God's preference, reads the Genesis 1 diet as design, and treats the Daniel 1 diet as faithful practice. It ties all of these to animal welfare.
