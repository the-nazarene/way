## Summary

The talk asks what Jesus meant in John 14:6 ("I am the way, the truth, and the life") and what following that way requires of a disciple. Host Craig Wescoe argues that "way," "truth," and "life" are tightly linked throughout scripture, and that together they describe a path of peace and mercy as opposed to violence and cruelty. He applies that to animals, so that his answer to "how do I follow the way" is, in his words, to go vegan. He also argues that Jesus' earliest followers were called "followers of the Way" in Acts (Acts 9:2; 24:14), and that following them means following Jesus' original teachings rather than later traditions.

His evidence is almost entirely proof-texting from the Hebrew Bible and the Gospels: Proverbs (3:13-18, 3:31, 12:10, 12:28, 21:21), Matthew 7 and 11, John 4, 8, 10, 16 and 18, Deuteronomy 30:19, and Luke 10 and 18. He reads these as connected by a common idea. He treats Genesis 1 as establishing animals as neighbors, and he reads the temple incident and the Good Samaritan parable in ways that the talk presents as his own interpretation, not as something the texts say directly. He also argues, against a "faith alone" reading, that Jesus taught keeping the commandments as the way to eternal life (Luke 18:18-20).

The talk is a live-stream devotional, not a historical argument, and it ends with a call to repentance, a closing prayer, and a Q&A where chat participants are welcomed and answered. A Q&A participant (unnamed) adds the claim that Paul is not anti-works and that his passages are misread out of context. The talk closes with announcements about fellowship, the next FNL talk, and a discipleship school.

## Key Teachings

### Way: the path of peace versus violence
Host reads Proverbs 3:13-18 ("all her paths are peace") and Proverbs 3:31 ("choose none of his ways," glossed as "the violent ones") and says the "opposite of peace throughout the Bible is violence." He reads Matthew 7:13-14 (narrow way that leads to life) and Matthew 11:12 ("the kingdom of heaven suffers violence") as saying that people are to enter the kingdom by peaceful means, not by violence. He applies this to humans and animals alike, asking whether one's decisions bring violence toward "the animals that God breathes his own breath into." He also reads Jeremiah 29 ("seek the Lord with all your heart") to argue that the way is narrow and takes committed effort, not just assent to a proposition.

### Truth: hearing Jesus' voice and the Spirit of truth
Host works through John 18:37 ("everyone who is of the truth hears my voice"), John 8:32 ("the truth shall set you free"), John 16:13 ("he will guide you into all truth"), and John 4:23-24 (worship "in spirit and truth"). He says Jesus "set the animals free" when he went into the temple, and concludes that the truth sets animals free too, so followers should not pay people to confine, hurt or kill them. He reads Hebrews as saying that animal blood cannot reconcile humans to God. He also cites 1 John 3:18 ("in deed and in truth") and asks, "what about your animal neighbors?" The temple act is not given a verse in the talk, and the claim that Jesus "set the animals free" is the host's gloss.

### Life: giving life rather than taking it
John 10:10 is contrasted with "the thief" who "steals and kills and destroys," and the host applies this to animal agriculture. He pairs Deuteronomy 30:19 ("choose life") with the Matthew 7 narrow-way passage. He reads Proverbs 12:10 as a test of whether one's "kindest acts" are cruel, and Proverbs 12:28 ("in its pathway there is no death") as ruling out eating animals. He notes that Jesus calls himself the bread of life and light of life (John 6:35 and 8:12, not read "for sake of time").

### Followers of the Way and the original teaching
Host says the earliest followers were called "followers of the way" before the name "Christians," which he attributes to Antioch in Acts. He cites Acts 9:2 (Saul seeking any "of the way") and Acts 24:14 (Paul's own description of "the way which they call a sect"). He says that to be a follower of the way one must follow Jesus' original teachings, not the later labels "Protestants, Catholics, Orthodox." He also names the Nazarenes and Ebionites as early names, referring viewers to an earlier talk he gave ("evidence for veganism in the early church"). That earlier talk is not in this transcript, so the claim is unverified here.

### Works, commandments and eternal life
Host reads Luke 18:18-20: when asked "what shall I do to inherit eternal life," Jesus says keep the commandments, and he reads this as against "faith alone" readings. He then reads the Luke 10:25-37 lawyer exchange: Jesus asks "what is your reading" of the law, the lawyer answers with Deuteronomy 6:5 and Leviticus 19:18, and Jesus says "do this and you will live." The Good Samaritan is then read as a contrast between bloodshedding priests and a Levite (identified by the host as the Sadducees and the Levitical priesthood) and a merciful neighbor, who in his reading is the model for how to treat animals. He says Jesus' "reading" test is one each listener should apply to Scripture.

### Mercy, not sacrifice
Host says Jesus quotes Hosea's "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" "multiple times throughout the gospels" (no verse given). He uses this to argue that Jesus does not want the blood of animals shed, and he ties this to Calvary. He also cites Proverbs 21:21 ("follows righteousness and mercy finds life").

### Romans 14 and the kingdom as not food and drink
Host reads Romans 14:17 ("the kingdom of God is not food and drink, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit") and Romans 14:15 ("do not destroy with your food the one for whom Christ died"). He reads these as rejecting the use of food for gratifying the flesh. In the Q&A, a participant argues that Paul is not anti-works, that the faith-alone passages are read out of context, and that the faith-alone reading "has nothing to do with how you live your life."
