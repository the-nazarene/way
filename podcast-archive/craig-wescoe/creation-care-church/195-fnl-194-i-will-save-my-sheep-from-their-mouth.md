## Summary

Craig, host of Creation Care Church's Friday night live series, asks what Ezekiel 34:10 ("I will save My sheep from their mouth, so that they will not be food for them") means and how it applies to Jesus. He argues that Jesus, as the Good Shepherd in John 10, fulfills Ezekiel's promise by opposing the killing and eating of sheep. On this reading, Jesus is not merely a metaphorical shepherd of people: he acts against animal slaughter, most visibly when he disrupts the Jerusalem temple.

Craig's main method is to chain passages together by shared vocabulary ("mouth," "flesh and blood," "grave of craving," "den of bloodsheds"). He reads Genesis 1:26-29 as giving humans dominion in God's image without permission to eat animals. He reads Numbers 11 as a warning against craving flesh, and Genesis 6 together with 1 Enoch 7 as the origin of eating flesh and blood. He then reads Jeremiah 7:11 and Matthew 21:12-13 as Jesus opposing the sacrificial system. He also uses 1 Corinthians 10:5-7 to call eating animal flesh "evil." The talk is a devotional-style exposition for a committed audience, not a historical argument, and Craig does not address the textual or historical questions a critical reader would raise (for example, whether the Gospels' temple scene is meant as a critique of sacrifice in the way he describes).

After the main talk, Craig takes questions from the live chat (Q&A, about 43:00 onward), responding to comments on animal intelligence, Psalm 36:6, sheep and guinea pigs, and objections that "sheep" means people. The talk closes with a prayer for the world to turn from "the ways of the Nephilim" and for God's kingdom to come "on earth as it is in heaven." Much of the opening (about the first four minutes) is announcements about upcoming meetings.

## Key Teachings

### The Good Shepherd versus the shepherds of Ezekiel 34

Craig reads Ezekiel 34:10 as God opposing shepherds who feed on their sheep. He then reads John 10 (verses 10-16, 27-28) as Jesus contrasting himself with thieves and hired hands. The thief "comes to steal and to kill and to destroy," while the good shepherd "lays down his life for the sheep." Craig's central claim is that Jesus's laying down of life is an act of protection *against* slaughter, not a figure for a sacrifice that would be eaten. He describes the sheep as "belonging" to Jesus and hearing his voice, and draws analogies to dogs and cats recognizing their owners. He says he will return to whether Jesus "puts his money where his mouth is," and says yes.

### Dominion and friendship: Genesis 1 and John 15

Craig argues that humans receive dominion over animals only "in the image and likeness of God," which he takes to mean dominion for embodying God's character, not for killing. He cites John 15:15 ("no longer do I call you servants... I have called you friends") and argues that the same stance should govern how humans regard animals: as friends, not servants to be used. He ties Genesis 1:29 (the plant and fruit diet) to the idea that God designed humans for a non-flesh diet, and says that "not drinking blood, not eating flesh" is what God "designed us for."

### Numbers 11: the "grave of craving"

Craig reads Numbers 11 as a story about the Israelites craving *flesh* (he notes that the Hebrew word translated "meat" is *basar*, flesh, not general food). God is "displeased," Moses is displeased, and God sends quail that causes a plague "while the meat was still between their teeth." He argues that the place-name Kibroth-hattaavah ("the graves of craving," in his gloss) shows the consequence of lusting after flesh. He rejects readings that treat the quail episode as divine permission to eat meat, arguing that "eat it until it comes out your nostrils" is a rebuke.

### Genesis 6, 1 Enoch 7, and the origin of eating flesh

Craig argues that Matthew 24:38 ("eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage") refers not to ordinary feasting but to the pre-Flood period described in Genesis 6:1-5, 11 (which he reads as corruption and violence). He identifies the marrying as the union of "sons of God" (fallen angels) with human women, drawing on 1 Enoch 7 (he cites the Lawrence translation and mentions the Charles numbering). He argues that the Nephilim then "devoured" human labor and began eating human and animal flesh and drinking blood, which he takes as the violence that caused the Flood. He reads Genesis 9 (the prohibition on eating flesh with its blood) as God's explicit ban after the Flood. Craig states that Jesus "calls" 1 Enoch scripture, citing Matthew 22:30 (which he paraphrases), and says this justifies using Enoch to interpret Genesis 6. (The Matthew reference is his inference from the paraphrase; the transcript does not give a verse.)

### The temple disruption as opposition to sacrifice

Craig reads Jeremiah 7:11 ("a den of thieves") and the Hebrew *parats* as "bloodshed," so that Jesus's words in Matthew 21:12-13 ("a den of bloodsheds") name the sacrificial system. He argues that the overturned tables are not about money-changers, since he notes Luke does not mention money-changers, and that Jesus is quoting Jeremiah rather than Proverbs about dishonest scales. He points to Jeremiah 7, where God says "I never commanded sacrifice," and to Jeremiah 8, which he says accuses "lying scribes" of adding sacrifice to the Law. He concludes that Jesus is "clearly... against the sacrifices." He also says the temple act is literal as well as symbolic, and that Jesus "died for the literal sheep" at Passover.

### Diet, charity and "deceptive foods"

Craig says the answer to food-related objections is to "do a good thing to do a good thing": to give the poor vegan food rather than animal flesh, since doing a bad thing for a good end is not acceptable. He cites Proverbs 23:1-3 in a Q&A reply to say that anything beyond Genesis 1:29 is "deceptive food." He answers the "we live in a fallen world" argument by saying believers should do God's will "to the best of our ability until he returns."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Numbers 11 is a positive story about God permitting meat.** Craig argues the opposite: God is displeased, and the quail episode is a rebuke that ends in plague.
- **Matthew 24:38's "eating and drinking" refers to ordinary parties and weddings.** Craig says it refers to the fallen-angel marriages and the eating of flesh and blood in Genesis 6 and 1 Enoch 7.
- **Jesus's temple act was about money-changers and dishonest trade.** Craig says Luke omits the money-changers and that Jesus quotes Jeremiah 7:11 about bloodshed, so the target is sacrifice.
- **"Sheep" in John 10 and Ezekiel 34 means only people.** Craig says the animals are literal and that Jesus died for literal lambs as well as humans.
- **God gives humans permission to eat animals since Genesis 9.** Craig says Genesis 9 forbids eating flesh with its blood, and that the permission reading is wrong.
- **Living in a fallen world excuses eating meat.** Craig says believers should do God's will as far as possible until Jesus returns.
