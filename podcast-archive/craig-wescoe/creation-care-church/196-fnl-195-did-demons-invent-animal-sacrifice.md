## Summary

Wescoe asks whether demons invented animal sacrifice, and his answer is yes. He argues that the origin of animal sacrifice, and of eating animal flesh and blood, lies in the story of the Watchers (the "sons of God" of Genesis 6) and their giant offspring. His main evidence is 1 Enoch, which he treats as authoritative because Jude, Peter and Jesus (Matthew 22:29-30) appear to draw on it. In 1 Enoch 19:1, Uriel says the fallen angels are "leading them astray into sacrificing to demons as gods." Wescoe reads this as the origin of animal sacrifice, and he supports it with Justin Martyr, Clement of Alexandria and Clementine Homily 7.8, which he presents as early witnesses to the same idea.

He then argues that the Hebrew Bible itself disowns sacrifice as God's command. He cites Jeremiah 7:22-23, the prophetic statements in Isaiah, the Psalms, Hosea and Samuel, and the Day of Atonement goat sent to Azazel (Leviticus 16:8). He reads Jesus's temple action and Hebrews 10 as ending the sacrificial system, and he reads the Jerusalem Council's abstention rules (Acts 15, via Irenaeus) as a prohibition on "the table of demons." He presents a plant-based ideal from Genesis 1:29 as God's original intent.

The talk ends with a Q&A in which Wescoe reads chat questions and answers them. He admits that his position is controversial. He holds that Jeremiah's "lying pen of the scribes" refers to a Torah of Yahweh woven together with an older Torah of Elohim, and that this is the source of the sacrificial laws. He closes with prayer for the animals and for people to "abstain from the table of devils."

## Key Teachings

### 1. The Watchers, giants and demons (1 Enoch 7, 15)
Wescoe reads Genesis 6:1-4 and 6:11-12 as a brief account that leaves out details. He fills them in from 1 Enoch 7:4-6, where the giants "began to sin against birds and beasts and reptiles and fish, and to devour one another's flesh and drink the blood." He says this is "the origin of eating animal flesh." In 1 Enoch 15:9-11 the disembodied spirits of the giants become evil spirits, which he equates with demons. Because they "take no food, but nevertheless hunger and thirst," he argues they need humans to offer sacrifice on their behalf. He connects the giants' violence to Matthew 24:38 ("eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage") and to Jesus's contrast with the thief in John 10 (the thief "comes only to steal and kill and destroy").

### 2. Enoch's testimony and its authority
Wescoe argues that Jude 14 and 2 Peter 2:4 quote or draw on Enoch, so Jude "is quoting from the Book of Enoch." He says Jesus's Matthew 22:29-30 about angels not marrying refers to Enoch, which is in the Ethiopian Tewahedo canon. He says Augustine excluded Enoch in the 4th century and gave as his reason that modern-day Judaism of his time had left it out. Wescoe says he will leave it to listeners whether that is a reason to reject it, "But, if Jesus calls it scripture, that's good enough for me." He also argues that doubting Enoch's authorship means calling Jude "a liar."

### 3. The prophets reject sacrifice as God's command
Wescoe reads Jeremiah 7:22-23 as saying God did not command burnt offerings and sacrifices on the day of the Exodus. He reads Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribes has produced a deception") as an attack on the sacrificial Torah. He lines up Isaiah 1:11 ("I have no pleasure in the blood of bulls and lambs and goats"), Psalm 40:6 and 51:16-17, Hosea 6:6 and 1 Samuel (which he paraphrases without a verse) as a chorus against sacrifice. He cites Jesus's use of Hosea 6:6 in Matthew 9:13 and 12:7, and Jesus's quotation of Jeremiah 7:11 in Matthew 21:12-13. He argues that the Greek word *lēstai* (rendered "robbers") should be read as "blood shedders," based on the Hebrew *perets*, and that Jesus's temple action was about sacrifice, not money. Wescoe says that Luke "doesn't even mention the money changers."

### 4. The two Torahs
Wescoe says that Kings and Chronicles describe Hilkiah finding an older Torah scroll in the temple with no sacrifices in it. He calls this the "Torah of Elohim," and the later priestly law the "Torah of Yahweh." He says Genesis 1 uses Elohim and Genesis 2 uses Yahweh, and that the sacrificial laws are always attributed to Yahweh. In the Q&A he gives his view that "half of it would be legitimate, and half of it wouldn't be," because the two Torahs are "woven together" in our Bibles. He says he has argued this in an earlier talk, "Did Ezra rewrite the Torah?" He presents this as his own minority view, not as settled.

### 5. Jesus, Hebrews and the end of sacrifice
Wescoe reads Hebrews 10:4-10 as saying "it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" and that Christ "sets aside the first to establish the second." He reads the "first" as the sacrifices God never desired and the "second" as doing God's will, which he links to Jeremiah 7:22-23. He says the Day of Atonement with its goat for Azazel is "demon worship," and that anyone who says sacrifice was never ended is "rejecting Jesus and his once-for-all sacrifice." In the Q&A he says this is what he tells people who hold the traditional view.

### 6. The table of demons: Acts 15, 1 Corinthians 10 and diet
Wescoe cites 1 Corinthians 10:21 ("You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons, too") and Clementine Homily 7.8 to argue that eating animal flesh and blood is participating in demon worship. He reads James's decree in Acts 15 as quoted by Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 3.12.14), which lists abstaining from "meats offered to idols and from blood and from defilement or pornea" as the core of walking in the Spirit. He says that buying meat at the store and grilling it still counts as eating "at the table of demons." He reads Genesis 1:29 as God's original, plant-based ideal.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **God commanded animal sacrifice.** Wescoe argues that Jeremiah 7:22-23, Isaiah 1:11, the Psalms, Hosea 6:6 and Samuel show God never desired or required it, and that the sacrificial laws come from "lying scribes."
- **Jesus cleansed the temple over money changers.** Wescoe says the scene was about animal sacrifice, not money, and reads *perets* as "blood shedders."
- **Lucifer is the name of the figure behind Azazel.** Wescoe says no scripture verse uses that name, and he identifies Azazel with Hashatan (the adversary or Satan).
- **Yahweh and Elohim are the same God.** Wescoe contrasts a sacrificial, "Yahweh" conception of God with the God of the prophets and Jesus, and he says love is incompatible with the sacrificial God.
- **Gnostic means heretic.** In the Q&A he says the term means "knower," is often used pejoratively, and that he distinguishes "true Gnosis" from a counterfeit.
