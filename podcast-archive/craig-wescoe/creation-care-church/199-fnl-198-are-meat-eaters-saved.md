## Summary

Craig opens by asking whether eating meat is a salvation issue, a claim he says Christians often use to dismiss veganism. He answers that eating meat is not a prerequisite for salvation, but argues that a non-vegan has reason to doubt his own salvation, since salvation is supposed to produce good fruit and mercy (about 05:10). He says he is not claiming that loved ones who eat meat are unsaved.

His main evidence is a series of passages arguing that works are the test of salvation. He reads Ephesians 2:8, Titus 3:5 and Romans 10:9 as not licensing disobedience. He leans on James 2 ("faith without deeds is dead"), the judgment-by-deeds passages in Revelation and Matthew 25, the "Lord, Lord" warning in Matthew 7:21-23, and the fruit test in Matthew 7:16-18 and Galatians 5:22-23. He then applies the fruit test to meat-eating, arguing that paying for animals to be killed produces the opposite of the fruit of the Spirit. He also argues that Paul himself subordinates his authority to Jesus and to the Jerusalem leaders (1 Corinthians 1, 1 Corinthians 7:12, Galatians 2), so that if Paul seems to conflict with James or Jesus, the reader should follow James or Jesus.

The talk ends with a live Q&A from the chat, where Craig responds to comments about "once saved, always saved," the Lord's Prayer and the kingdom on earth, and the board's re-election. It closes with a prayer for repentance and a reminder of the next session.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Paul's salvation passages are not a license to ignore Jesus
Craig says people hide behind Ephesians 2:8, Titus 3:5 and Romans 10:9 to argue that confessing "Jesus is Lord" frees them from Jesus' teaching. He says he will look at the context of these verses "a little bit later," but in the transcript he relies mainly on other passages. He quotes Ephesians 2:4-5 and says salvation is "freely given... because of who God is, not because of who we are." He says grace is "received only through faith" and asks what receiving by faith means. He says Jesus answers that question in a way "different than just saying Lord, Lord." He does not develop the Ephesians context in the transcript.

### 2. Paul defers to Jesus and to the Jerusalem leaders
Craig reads 1 Corinthians 7:12 ("To the rest I say this, I, not the Lord") as evidence that Paul distinguished his own view from Jesus' command. He quotes 1 Corinthians (no verse given) on division and baptism ("Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you?") as showing that Paul points away from himself. He then cites Galatians 2:1-2 (fourteen years later Paul goes up to Jerusalem to "set before them the gospel") and argues that Paul accepts the Jerusalem leaders as the judges of his teaching. He concludes that Paul would side with Jesus if the two conflicted. He says this is his view, not a proven reading, and that "it's somebody's interpretation of Paul that's wrong" if Paul appears to conflict.

### 3. The Jerusalem decree in Acts 15 covers meat
Craig quotes James's ruling in Acts 15 ("abstain from food polluted by idols, from defilement, from meat of strangled animals, and from blood"). He argues that this shows the Spirit-led community required abstention from meat and blood, and that circumcision was not required. He attributes the abstention rule to "James and Peter" at around 40:14, but the passage he quotes is James's ruling. He also asserts that "all meat offered to all meat is offered to idols," which is his own claim and not part of the Acts text he quotes.

### 4. Faith without works is dead, and mercy is the test (James)
Craig quotes James 2:17 and 2:26 and argues that living faith produces deeds, which are the evidence of the Spirit in a person. He quotes James 2:13 ("Mercy triumphs over judgment") and applies it to how people treat animals. He also quotes James 2:19 ("Even the demons believe that and shudder") to argue that mere belief in God or Jesus is no better than what demons have.

### 5. Judgment is by deeds (Revelation, Matthew 25, Romans 2)
Craig cites Revelation 20:12 ("the dead were judged according to their deeds") and Revelation 22:12, and Revelation 2:23 ("I will repay each of you according to your deeds"). He presents these as proof that judgment follows works and not confession. Note that at 19:06 the words "They weren't judged according to a confession they made with their mouth..." are his gloss, not verse text. He then cites Matthew 25:31-46 (the sheep and the goats) and Romans 2:6-7. He says that people who turn to "peace and righteousness" will be "on the right side of that judgment," and that those who continue in violence will be "on the wrong side." He links this to the "eternal fire" and "eternal punishment" language in the passage. He reads Matthew 5:7, Luke 6:36 and Matthew 19:16-17 as calls to mercy and obedience.

### 6. The fruit test applied to meat
Craig takes Matthew 7:16-18 ("By their fruit, you will recognize them") and 2 Corinthians 13:5 ("Examine yourselves") as the test for whether Christ is in a person. He sets the nine fruits of Galatians 5:22-23 against meat-eating: killing animals is "not self-controlled," "not kind," "not gentle," and "not peaceful." He argues that "if you have the Holy Spirit in you, you cannot bear bad fruit." He adds Ephesians 2:10 ("we are his workmanship... to do good works") to show that Ephesians 2:8 must be read with what follows. He concludes that not eating meat is the test a true believer should pass. He also argues that Genesis 1 (the food given in creation) is God's "very good instruction," and that the kingdom prayer ("your will be done on Earth as it is in heaven") calls for transforming the world now.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **"Veganism / meat-eating is not a salvation issue, so it can be dismissed."** Craig says this is selfish reasoning, and that the judgment-by-deeds texts make diet and treatment of animals relevant to salvation.
- **"Ephesians 2:8 and Romans 10:9 mean faith alone allows me to ignore Jesus' teaching."** Craig says Ephesians 2:10 follows immediately with "good works," and that Romans 2 and James show works matter.
- **"Paul gives a license that overrides Jesus and James."** Craig argues Paul himself defers to Jesus and the Jerusalem leaders, so any conflict would favor them.
- **"Saying 'Lord, Lord' is enough."** Craig cites Matthew 7:21-23 and James 2:19 to say that confession and belief alone are not enough.
- **"Once saved, always saved."** In the Q&A, Craig says he doesn't dispute that salvation can be permanent, but says people who fail the fruit test cannot cling to a past experience to justify their conduct.
