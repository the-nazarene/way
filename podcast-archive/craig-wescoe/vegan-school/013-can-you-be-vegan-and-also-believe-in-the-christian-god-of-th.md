## Summary

Craig Wescoe asks whether a person can be vegan and still believe in the Christian God of the Bible, since some people argue that God created animals to be eaten. He answers that Scripture points the other way, and builds the case from four verses. He starts with Genesis 1:29, which he reads as God giving humans fruit and vegetation as food, and then moves to 1 John 4:16 and Galatians 5:22, where love is the central attribute of God and the first "fruit of the Spirit." From there he turns to Matthew 5:48 and the call to a complete, impartial love that includes enemies, and he ends with John 14:12, where Jesus promises that his followers will do "greater works." He concludes that loving animals is one way of going beyond what Jesus requires, and so a way of embodying God's love.

The talk is devotional and ethical rather than historical. Wescoe gives no sources outside Scripture, does not discuss the dating or authorship of the texts, and does not address how these passages have been read by scholars or by other Christian traditions. His argument rests on how the verses fit together as a theological pattern.

The talk closes with the same framing it opens with: love is the defining feature of God, and extending it to animals is presented as the fullest form of that love. The final line is a sign-off, and the talk does not revisit the Genesis or Matthew passages.

## Key Teachings

### Creation design: humans were given a plant diet (Genesis 1:29)
Wescoe opens by answering the claim that God made animals for eating. He says "scripture says the reverse in Genesis 1:29," reading God's words as giving humans "fruit and vegetation" as their food. His reasoning is that if humans were designed for a plant diet, then eating animals is not what God intended. He describes this as "the first verse we're going to look at" and treats it as the starting point for the rest of the talk.

### God is love, and love is the fruit of the Spirit (1 John 4:16; Galatians 5:22)
He cites 1 John 4:16 ("God is love") to name love as "the key attribute of God." He then cites Galatians 5:22 for the claim that "the first fruit of the Spirit of God is love," with joy, peace and the other fruits following. His reasoning: someone who has God within them will bear this fruit, and so the question becomes how that love is shown to animals, whether by harming them or by caring for them and creating conditions where they can live joyfully and peacefully.

### Complete, impartial love (Matthew 5:48)
Wescoe places the Sermon on the Mount in view and says Jesus teaches going "the extra mile." He lists those who should be loved, including the poor, widows, orphans, strangers and enemies, and he describes "love your enemies" as the hardest command. He cites Jesus' words on the cross ("forgive them for they don't know what they're doing") as an example. He then reads Matthew 5:48 ("you shall be perfect... just as your father in heaven is perfect") as a call to a love that is "not withheld from anyone." Applied to animals, he argues that withholding love from them and killing them ("slit their throats eat their flesh") does not express that love.

### Doing greater works (John 14:12)
Wescoe reads John 14:12 as Jesus promising that believers will do "the works that I do" and "greater works." He takes this as permission to go beyond what Jesus required, and he gives loving animals as an example of such a "greater" work. His argument is that if Jesus required love even for enemies, then extending love to animals is the next step in fully embodying God's love.
