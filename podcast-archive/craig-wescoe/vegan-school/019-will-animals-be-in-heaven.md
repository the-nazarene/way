## Key Teachings

### 1. Shared fate and God's creation plan
Wescoe starts with Ecclesiastes 3:19, which he reads as saying animals and people have "the same fate," and calls this "the most convincing piece of evidence" that animals share the afterlife. He then points to Genesis 1:20-31 as a creation account in which animals have a place from the beginning and the earth is meant for people and animals to live "in harmony." He adds Isaiah 11:6-9, the image of a child leading animals, as a picture of the kingdom in which animals are included. His reasoning is that because animals were part of God's plan at creation, they should be part of its fulfilment.

### 2. Animals praise God
He cites Revelation 5:13 and Revelation 4:8 as passages where animals praise God. He argues that if animals praise God in the kingdom, they must have been "created with at least this capacity to praise God," and so there is no reason to exclude them when people praise God there too. The talk does not address whether the creatures in these Revelation passages are literal animals, which a careful reader would want to check against the text.

### 3. Balaam's donkey as a hero
Using Numbers 22:21-39, Wescoe describes the donkey as stubborn and disobedient in appearance but actually saving Balaam's life, and says the donkey "was actually the hero of the story." He compares this to Moses, Jesus, the apostles and Joseph, and asks why Balaam's donkey would be excluded from the kingdom if these heroes are included. He then widens the argument to other animals, including those not named in Scripture.

### 4. Donkey in John and God's covenant with animals
He notes that Jesus riding a donkey fulfils Scripture in John 12:14-15, so the donkey had a role in God's plan. He then calls Genesis 9:15 and Hosea 2:18 "the most convincing piece of evidence that it will be the same animals." He paraphrases Genesis 9:15 as "my covenant is with all the animals" and reads Hosea 2:18 as a promise that weapons will be banished and that animals will "lie down safely," which he links to Isaiah 11. The talk relies on his paraphrases here, so the exact wording of these verses should be checked.

### 5. Reunion with companion animals and moral response
Wescoe concludes that there is "evidence to suggest" people will be reunited with companion animals they have loved. He then argues that the same reunion includes animals that people have harmed, and says this calls for apology and repentance. This is the only place in the talk where he draws an ethical conclusion.
