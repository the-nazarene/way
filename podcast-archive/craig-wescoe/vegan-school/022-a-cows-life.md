He describes a cycle on a dairy farm: cows are impregnated so they will give milk, their calves are taken away, male calves are slaughtered or raised for veal, female calves are raised to become dairy cows, and all the cows are slaughtered once their milk declines. ## Key Teachings

### The dairy cycle as he describes it
Wescoe says cows are impregnated "so they can produce milk," and their babies are "taken away from them." Male calves are "slaughtered immediately or are raised for a short time and then sold as veal." Female calves "are raised the same way to be dairy cows," and once milk output declines, "they get slaughtered." He presents this as the normal fate of dairy animals. He names no farm, region, or industry source for these claims.

### Milk belongs to the calf
His central moral point is that "animal milk is for baby animals." He says a mother produces milk "for her baby," not so that "the baby can be taken away from her and we can drink it." He lists plant-based alternatives: "soy milk," "almond milk," "hemp milk," "rice milk," and goat milk, which he notes is still animal milk.

### Consumer demand drives the suffering
Wescoe argues that farmers respond to purchases: "if we change then the demand changes and the supply also changes." He frames individual buying choices as the lever that determines whether these animals keep living "this lifestyle." He does not address other factors such as regulation, labor, or prices.

The channel description, not the spoken transcript, ties the message to Matthew 6:10 and to humanity leading animals "in the way of love and kindness," calling this "good news for all creation." - **Dairy is separate from meat and slaughter.** Wescoe says dairy and meat are linked: calves are slaughtered or raised for veal, and dairy cows "ends up slaughter" once their milk declines.
