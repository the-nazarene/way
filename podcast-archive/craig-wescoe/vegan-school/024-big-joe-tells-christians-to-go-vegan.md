The host held a sign reading "GOD WANTS YOU TO BE VEGAN -- CHANGE MY MIND," and a man called Big Joe stopped to argue that the Bible requires a plant-based diet. ## Key Teachings

### Genesis 1:29 as the original diet, with meat permitted after the Flood

Big Joe opens by citing Genesis 1:29, where God gives humans seed-bearing plants and fruit "for food." He says "technically meat was never part of the human diet," and that God allowed it only after the Flood because nobody could plant or cultivate the ground during the flood year. He reads the "clean" animals taken into the ark as being for sacrifice and food, but argues that the text "didn't say it was for food" in the first place. The host challenges the timeline (the ground would have needed time to recover after the waters subsided), and Big Joe answers that cultivation would have taken time. The question of whether the text says meat was permitted is contested inside the conversation itself and is not resolved.

### Diet, health, and appetite as a spiritual discipline

Big Joe links the original diet to health: "we have all the sicknesses diseases cancer all this comes from the foods," and returning to the original diet would reverse many of them. He then frames appetite as the place where people fall and where believers can gain victory. He points to Adam and Eve, who "fell on appetite," and to Jesus's temptation in the wilderness, which he says began with appetite. He mentions Eastern yogis as having "so-called spirituality" without meat and says "there's something to it." The health claims are asserted without any cited study.

### Daniel 1 and the prophetic "last days"

Big Joe cites Daniel as a model: Daniel ate vegetables and water and became "much more wiser." He ties this to the Book of Daniel and Revelation as prophetic books that "unlock each other," and reads the story of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego as a picture of God's people resisting "the mark of the beast." He says the furnace is "symbolic" and that the last-days period will be "seven times hotter," requiring a special diet. This is a typological and apocalyptic reading, presented as Big Joe's own interpretation.

### Manna, quail, and the Sabbath in the wilderness

In Big Joe's account of Numbers 11, the Israelites craved the meat of Egypt, "died with it in their teeth and nostrils," and the obedient ones ate the manna for forty years. He links manna to "the body of Christ." He then claims that God was training Israel in the Sabbath during the wilderness years, that the Sabbath "was established before sin" with Adam and Eve, and therefore "it's not taken away." This is a direct claim about Sabbath observance, stated without discussion of any opposing reading.

### Animal fear, violence, and the "you are what you eat" argument

Big Joe argues that "animal fear is linked to our violence toward them," citing Genesis 9 and Habakkuk. He says the fear arose from humans' violence and that people who kill animals "unnecessarily" become desensitized, potentially moving toward violence against people. He claims that an animal being slaughtered "secretes a hormone" that transfers to the eater, and he invokes the saying "you are what you eat." He also argues that there is no real need for killing animals today, since people no longer need animal skins to cover themselves. The hormone claim is presented without any source.

### Cain and Abel, Esau, and moral choice

Big Joe reads Cain and Abel as showing that God "accepts what he requires," so worship must match God's standard. He then argues that knowingly killing an animal "when you have better choices" is wrong "in your heart of hearts," and that choosing taste over conscience makes a person "just like Esau," who "sold his birthright" for a meal. He cites James 4:17 in the description to support the idea that knowing the good and not doing it is sin. The Esau comparison is his own application of the story.

### Salvation, love, and accountability

Big Joe answers a hypothetical objection that salvation has nothing to do with food: "it doesn't, but guess what when you love God there are things." He says that in the last days "spiritual Israel will also have a special diet," and he ends with a warning that "one day we're gonna have to go in front of God and give an account" for the state of "the body I gave you." He also says that Christians who consider returning to meat should know "there's not gonna be no killing" in heaven. The heaven claim is stated without a biblical reference in the transcript.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Meat was God's intended human food from the beginning.** Big Joe says Genesis 1:29 shows a plant-based original diet, and that meat was permitted only after the Flood because of sin.
- **The Sabbath was abolished with the Mosaic law.** Big Joe says the Sabbath was "established before sin" with Adam and Eve, so "it's not taken away."
- **Diet is irrelevant to salvation.** Big Joe concedes that "salvation doesn't have anything to do with food," then argues that love for God and "a special diet" in the last days still matter.
