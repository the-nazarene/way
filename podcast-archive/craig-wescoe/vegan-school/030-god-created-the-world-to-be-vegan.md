## Key Teachings

### Isaiah 11 as God's purpose for the earth
Wescoe opens by reading Isaiah 11:6-9, where the wolf, leopard, calf, lion and child live together, and says this shows "God's plan was not for us to be killing animals harming animals living in a world of violence and sin." He reads the peaceable picture as the intended order of creation, and he ties it to "the knowledge of the Lord" filling the earth.

### Love as God's nature, and animals learning it from humans
Citing 1 John 4:8 and 16 ("God is love"), he reasons that if the whole earth has knowledge of God, it has knowledge of love. He then says animals "gain it from us," since humans have been "instructed to lead them." Humans are the channel through which creatures learn this love.

### Image of God, dominion as care, and a plant diet
He cites Genesis 1:27-28 for humans being made in God's image and given dominion over animals, and Genesis 1:29 for eating "the fruit of the tree and the green herbs." From this he concludes that "we're not supposed to be eating the animals." He presents dominion as responsibility to feed and protect animals, not as license to use them. He also reads the Isaiah peace as the result of humans providing "the green herb that's appropriate for each one."

### Fruits of the Spirit and the language of love
Wescoe quotes Galatians 5:22-23 and treats the list (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, gentleness, faithfulness, self-control) as God's plan for how people should treat each other and animals. He says "against these there is no law," and he links "speaking in tongues" to "the language of love that everyone can understand" across all creatures. He does not discuss the original context of the Galatians passage.

### Service as the expression of love
He closes by quoting an unnamed saying of Jesus that people "will know who his followers are by the love that they have for one another," and another that Jesus "didn't come to be served but to serve." His conclusion is that love means serving others, "when it comes to animals to serve animals." The "Jesus" sayings are given without references.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means the right to use and kill animals.** Wescoe reads Genesis 1:28 as a call to care for and feed animals, not as permission to harm or eat them.
- **The Bible allows humans to eat animals.** He reads Genesis 1:29 as giving only "the green herbs" and says "we're not supposed to be eating the animals." He does not address any other biblical passage that might be read differently.
