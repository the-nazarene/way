## Summary

Wescoe, speaking at a Vegan Toastmasters meeting on March 15, 2020 (per the description), tells how a near-collision with a cow on an Oklahoma road led him to give up eating animals. He had moved from Tennessee to Oklahoma for college, where he planned to study e-commerce. Driving at night, he came upon a cow in the middle of the road and could not stop in time. He says he looked the cow in the eyes, and she did not move, so neither of them was killed. He describes this as the moment he decided to stop killing and eating animals.

He says that becoming a vegetarian came first, and that about five years passed before he saw that dairy and egg production also end in slaughter. He claims that dairy cows are slaughtered after five to seven years of forced impregnation and separation from their calves, and that male chicks from egg hatcheries are killed on the day they are born. On that basis he says he went fully vegan, which he dates to about ten years before the talk. The talk offers no scripture, theology, or historical argument. Its case rests on personal experience and claims about animal agriculture. The spiritual and Bible framing in the YouTube description is not part of the spoken talk. The talk ends with the cow story and his thanks to the audience.

## Key Teachings

### The cow in the road as a turning point
Wescoe presents the encounter as the origin of his commitment. He reflects that he had "been killing and eating cows my whole life," and that the cow could have "get back at me" but instead "stood fearlessly and didn't move." He says this led him to resolve to spare animals' lives and their families' lives, and to stop treating them as things to "eat and exploit."

### From vegetarian to vegan
He says he first stopped eating cows and became a vegetarian, with little access to resources for veganism at the time. About five years later he came to see that the dairy industry is part of the meat industry. He argues that "being a vegetarian wasn't really much different than just eating animals," and that his earlier position had not gone far enough.

### Dairy and eggs as slaughter
He states that dairy cows are slaughtered after roughly five to seven years, following "enslavement forcible impregnation" and repeated removal of their babies. He says he learned that male chicks are "ground up alive the day he's born or suffocated in a garbage bag," and that every hen in the egg industry is slaughtered. From this he concludes, "if I'm paying for eggs if I'm paying for milk if I'm paying for cheese... I'm paying for slaughter."

### Animals as "someones," not objects
His closing claim is that animals are not objects for human use. He says they have families, friends, and their own lives, and that they are "not food." The talk gives no source for the industry figures. They rest on the speaker's own account.
