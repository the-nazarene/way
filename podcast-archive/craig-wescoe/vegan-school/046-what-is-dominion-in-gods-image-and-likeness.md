## Summary

This is the first session of the Vegan Christian Discipleship School, run by Craig Wescoe of Creation Care Church. The session takes up the question "What does it mean for humanity to have dominion in God's image and likeness?" and treats it as the main objection Christians raise against veganism. Wescoe, who describes his background as graduate study in religious studies and five years teaching New Testament, says the course is discussion-based and built around a study guide with a short answer and a longer, footnoted answer.

Wescoe's answer rests mainly on the Genesis creation account. He argues that Genesis 1:28 grants dominion, but that the verse immediately following (Genesis 1:29) assigns humans and animals a plant-based diet, so dominion cannot mean killing and eating animals. He then reads "dominion" as stewardship or responsibility, supported by the Hebrew verb *radah*, as quoted from Phelps, and by the "viceroy" and "steward" language he attributes to Clark and to Sarah Withrow King. He uses Ephesians 1:21-22, Mark 10:45 and 1 John 4:16 to argue that Christ's rule over the church is the pattern for human rule over animals, and he answers a hostile reading of Psalm 137:9 by explaining its context.

The session ends with participant discussion (cats and dogs on vegan diets, the Isaiah 11 peaceable kingdom, the "life more abundantly" verse, the gospel "to every creature"), a summary of the study guide's usefulness, and a closing prayer. Many of the participant responses are anecdotal and are not presented as evidence here.

## Key Teachings

### Genesis 1:28 is not a license to kill; Genesis 1:29 defines the diet
Wescoe's first move is to read the next verse after the dominion grant. He reads Genesis 1:28, then Genesis 1:29 ("I give you every seed bearing plant... they will be yours for food"), and argues that the very next divine instruction after dominion is a plant diet. He reads this as showing that "whatever Dominion consists of it definitely doesn't consist of killing and eat the animals." He points out that the text does not even list eggs or milk, so for him the diet is vegan, not merely vegetarian.

### Dominion as stewardship and responsibility
Participants offer "stewardship" and "responsibility," and Wescoe affirms them. He cites Clark's argument that Adam was "God's Viceroy here on Earth" and illustrates the idea with a house-sitter who is given specific instructions (mail, plants, dog) and is accountable to the owner. He adds that Genesis 2 gives explicit instructions to "tend and keep" the garden. He quotes King's *Vegangelical* (transcribed as "veg angelical"): being made in God's image "is not a license to conquer and kill it is a charge to keep and till." He also reads Phelps on *radah*: the verb is used for the authority of rulers over citizens, and rulers who abuse their subjects are condemned as unjust, so "there is no reason to judge our dominion over Animals by any other standard."

### Carol Adams and the "broken relationship" argument
Wescoe reads a passage from Carol Adams (in her essay on Genesis 1 in *A Faith Embracing All Creatures*): "the more the word dominion is broken away from its context of Genesis 1 the more likely it is that what one is defending is a broken relationship between humans and the other animals." On his reading, a tyrannical concept of dominion reflects "Fallen man" rather than the image of God, so the correction is to go back to what the Bible says about dominion.

### Christ as the pattern for dominion
Wescoe argues that Christ's dominion over the church (Ephesians 1:21-22) is the model for human dominion over animals: "if our Dominion doesn't look like Christ's dominion over us then it's not Dominion in God's image and likeness." He adds 1 John 4:16 ("God is love") and Mark 10:45 ("he did not come to be served but to serve") to argue that dominion in God's image is loving service, not tyranny.

### Answering objections: Psalm 137:9 and Isaiah 11
When a participant raises Psalm 137:9 as a supposed divine endorsement of violence, Wescoe says the speaker is a grieving parent wishing the same harm on the people who killed the speaker's child, so it is "not at all God endorsing that." On wild animals and the "fallen world," he says the Isaiah 11 peaceable kingdom and Revelation 21:4 describe God's original and final design, while the present interim requires humans first to "remove the plank from our own eye" before the question of animal violence can be addressed. He also cites Genesis 9:3 and Habakkuk 2:17 as evidence that animal fear is tied to human violence, and 1 John 4:18 ("perfect love casts out fear") to argue that love removes that fear.

### The Kingdom and the "life more abundantly" contrast
Wescoe argues that the gospel is good news for "all creation," citing Romans on creation "groaning" and Mark 16:15 (preach "to all creation"), and that animals share the eschatological hope of peace. He says that Jesus repeatedly calls followers to be prepared stewards now, not to wait for the Kingdom to fix things. He reads the contrast between the thief who "comes to steal and kill and destroy" and Jesus who gives life "more abundantly," and asks the group whether humans are stealing and destroying animal life or giving it.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Dominion means the right to kill and eat animals.** Wescoe argues the very next verse (Genesis 1:29) gives a plant diet, so dominion cannot include killing animals for food.
- **Psalm 137:9 shows God approves of violence, so God approves of killing animals.** Wescoe says the psalm records a grieving parent's wish for revenge and is not divine endorsement, and that this misreading cannot support animal killing.
- **Animal predation and "survival of the fittest" (or "circle of life") show that violence is natural and God's design.** Wescoe says the Bible presents predation as a consequence of the Fall, with the peaceable kingdom of Isaiah 11 as God's design and ending.
- **Cats are obligate carnivores, so vegan cat diets are abusive.** A participant reports this as a common attack. Wescoe says the claim "has been debunked" and that plant-based pet foods are often fortified with the same supplements (such as taurine and B12) as meat-based foods.
- **Christians can wait for the Kingdom to fix animal suffering, so present diet does not matter.** Wescoe says Jesus calls followers to be prepared stewards now.
- **"Life more abundantly" means prosperity and comfort.** Wescoe reads the verse as contrasted with the thief who steals, kills and destroys, and argues that Christian life includes rejection and persecution, citing Jesus' teaching.
