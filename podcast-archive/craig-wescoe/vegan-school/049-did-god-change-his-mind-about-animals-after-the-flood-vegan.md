## Summary

This is the third session of a Vegan Christian Discipleship School class, run by Craig (addressed as "Pastor Craig"), using a nine-page study guide with 23 footnotes. The question is whether God changed his mind about animals after the flood, so that Genesis 9:3 added animals to the human diet. Craig and the participants argue that he did not. Most of the two-hour session is group reading of the guide, with Craig answering questions. Craig's main line is that Genesis 9 mentions animals as covenant recipients (he says five times), that "given into your hand" describes human power rather than approval, that the Hebrew words for diet in Genesis 1 and Genesis 9 are different, and that Genesis 8:21 does not show God pleased by a sacrifice. The guide's preferred reading is that God was "warning" humanity, and that any permission was a concession to "hardness of heart," with consequences, on the model of Matthew 19:8.

In the last stretch Craig turns to what he calls the "historical critical" approach, which he presents as a second reading he is not required to adopt. He points to an anachronism (Noah knowing the clean/unclean distinction that Leviticus gives later), to the Elohist and Yahwist/Priestly sources, and to differing flood durations (150 days versus 40 days). He says this shows the text may be layered, while stressing that the apologetic reading remains open. A participant raises concern about "picking and choosing" Scripture, and Craig answers that the division follows divine names within the text. The session ends with a closing prayer, and the next meeting is set for sacrifice.

## Key Teachings

### Genesis 9 includes animals in the covenant
The guide argues that if God had given humans a license to eat animals, it would make no sense for him to name animals as recipients of the Everlasting Covenant, which it does in Genesis 9:16. Craig says participants have heard the claim that God mentions animals "five times" in the covenant passage, and he treats the repetition as a sign of importance. A participant notes that a friend answered by pointing out the phrase appears only once, but Craig keeps the repetition argument.

### "Given into your hand" describes power, not permission
The guide reads Genesis 9:2 as descriptive: the fear and dread of humans falling on animals describes "humanity's evil exercise of power." It cites Job 2:6, where God hands Job over to Satan, to show that being "in your hand" means being under someone's power without approval. Craig says the power was given for stewardship, and humans are using it for evil.

### Killing versus murder, and the blood prohibition
The guide reads Genesis 9:3 as permission to kill "every creature" only in the sense of power, because it cannot include human killing (murder is already forbidden, as Cain's punishment in Genesis 4 shows), it cannot include unclean animals, and Genesis 9:4 forbids eating flesh with its blood. A participant says the Hebrew word for "kill" and "murder" is the same in Exodus. Craig distinguishes intent and circumstances but treats the question as open.

### The diet of Genesis 1 and Genesis 9
Craig's main textual point is that Genesis 1:29-30 gives humans and animals two different diets, using different Hebrew words (the captions garble them). He argues that when God says "just as I gave you the green herb... I now give you everything" in Genesis 9:3, he is speaking to the animals, not to humans. On that reading, the reckoning in Genesis 9:5 ("for your lifeblood I will require a reckoning") is aimed at anyone who takes an animal's life, which he says reverses the usual reading. He presents this as a plausible reading, not the only one.

### Genesis 8:21 and the "pleasing aroma"
Some readers say God was pleased by Noah's burnt offering and so relented. The guide answers with Psalm 115:4-6 (idols have noses but cannot smell) and Ezekiel 6:13 (offerings of "pleasing aroma" to idols). Craig argues that the pleasant smell is attributed by the offerer, who follows "their own sense pleasures," as in Genesis 3 where the fruit was pleasing to the eye. He says a fuller treatment exists in a separate live talk.

### Temporary concession and the olive leaf
The guide considers the view that God allowed meat only until vegetation regrew after the flood. It answers with the dove returning with a freshly plucked olive leaf (Genesis 8:11), which shows that vegetation was already present. Craig adds that the ark had stored food and that the animals and humans were not described as running out. He also says the reply "God never makes covenants with plants" is part of the argument.

### Historical critical reading (appendix)
Craig presents a second reading as a closing appendix, with an explicit warning that it may not suit every listener. He cites Genesis 7:2-3 (seven clean and two unclean animals) against Leviticus 11:46-47, which defines clean and unclean later, as an anachronism. He then sets Genesis passages side by side in two columns, labelling one the Elohist and the other the Yahwist/Priestly material. The differing flood durations (150 days in one strand, 40 in the other) are also cited, and Craig says the Priestly strand is associated with animal sacrifice. He notes that Jeremiah's "lying pen of the scribe" will be discussed in the next meeting.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Genesis 9:3 changed God's mind and added animals to the menu.** The guide argues God never changed his mind; any permission is at best a concession to hardness of heart, with consequences.
- **"Given into your hand" means God approves.** Craig says the phrase describes power, not approval, citing Job 2:6.
- **Animals are not covenant recipients.** Craig says Genesis 9:9-17 explicitly names them.
- **Killing animals is not murder.** The guide argues that the distinction does not license killing in Genesis 9:3, since Genesis 9:4 forbids eating blood and Cain's story shows murder was already prohibited.
- **God was pleased by Noah's sacrifice.** Craig says the aroma is attributed by the offerer, citing Psalm 115 and Ezekiel 6:13.
- **Noah ate meat because vegetation was gone.** The guide cites the olive leaf and the stored food on the ark.
- **Humans and animals are different kinds of creature.** A participant's church claim that only humans have "spirit" is answered with Genesis's use of "ruach" and "nephesh" for both.
- **God never mentions animals in the Bible.** A participant states the average Christian believes this; Craig agrees.
