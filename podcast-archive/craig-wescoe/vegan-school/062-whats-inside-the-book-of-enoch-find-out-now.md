### Key Teachings

### 1. Dating Enoch against the New Testament

Wescoe's central claim is that the Dead Sea fragments reversed scholarly opinion. Before the discovery, he says, scholars thought Enoch depended on the New Testament; now "they have proof that the book of Enoch predates the New Testament." From that, he concludes that the New Testament's allusions to Enoch are the dependent side of the relationship, and that the text "causes everyone to take this text way more seriously." The talk does not show the fragments, their dates, or the scholarly argument, so this is presented as his reading of the evidence.

### 2. Section one: Watchers, giants, demons and the origin of sacrifice

Wescoe summarizes section one as following the storyline of Genesis 6. The *Benei Elohim* (sons of God) come down to take human women, producing Nephilim giants who fill the earth with violence. God rebukes the angels for marrying and procreating. He says Jesus's saying about the resurrected not marrying, being "like the angels in heaven," draws on Enoch 15, which he says also distinguishes demons from fallen angels. He further says section one describes the origin of animal sacrifice as beginning with demons possessing people.

### 3. Section two: the Son of Man

Wescoe summarizes section two as the coming Son of Man who "will come and save the faithful and restore his kingdom, the kingdom of God." He states that "the early church writers clearly identified the son of man as Jesus," a point that connects the text to later Christology, though he names no specific writers in this passage.

### 4. Calendar and transmission through Noah's line

Section three is Enoch's celestial calendar, which he says requires diagrams to handle "drift" and to reconcile it with the calendar in the Bible. Chapter 82, he says, is the passage Tertullian had in mind about Enoch's writings surviving the flood: Enoch passed instructions to Methuselah, who passed them to Lamech, who passed them to Noah.

### 5. The animal apocalypse, the two paths, and Noah's birth

Section four is the animal apocalypse, which Wescoe describes as cryptic and comparable to Daniel or Revelation, with symbols he says correspond to events in the Bible; his footnotes and an appendix decode them. Section five contains warnings and blessings, describing "the path of righteousness" and "the path of violence or wickedness," and ends with Noah's birth, which he says includes the claim that Noah was albino.

- **Belief that the Book of Enoch was dependent on the New Testament.** Wescoe says this was the predominant scholarly view before the Dead Sea discovery, and that the fragments show the reverse: the New Testament depends on Enoch.
