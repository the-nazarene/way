## Key Teachings

### Jeremy's opening claim is self-contradictory, and the verses are read in context
Steve says the title ("doesn't support") and the opening line ("opposed to ethical veganism") make different claims. Craig says the stronger claim would need stronger evidence, using an internet analogy. The speakers then argue that each of Jeremy's five passages is misread when taken out of its chapter or language.

### Genesis 9:3 is addressed to animals, not humans, and the covenant includes animals
Craig argues that Genesis 1:29-30 gives humans the plants and fruit and gives animals the green plants. In Genesis 9:3 ("Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you... as I gave you the green plants, I give you everything"), he reads the "you" as addressed to the animals, who are told they may now eat fruit as well. He cites Genesis 9:4 ("the flesh with the blood you shall not eat") as a prohibition that still stands. Steve and Craig also argue that Genesis 9 repeats the "everlasting covenant" with the birds, fish, and creatures on land, so God cannot have changed his mind about animals being food without contradicting the covenant text. Steve notes the Hebrew term Jeremy relies on for "creeping things" (*remes*), which he says covers bugs, swarming things, and possibly reptiles, and says he took this from a rabbi. Steve says he goes even further than Craig in allowing the Genesis 9:3 reading, and Craig says he is more sympathetic to a "blood is life" reading than to Jeremy's.

### "Milk and honey," the Exodus, and the quail story
Craig and Steve argue that "land flowing with milk and honey" refers to fruit and date syrup (the Middle Eastern *tubach* tradition), noting that the spies returned with fruit. Craig adds a textual point: the Hebrew *kalav* can mean either milk or fat, and he cites Maimonides ("Rambam") as reading it as a suckling baby's milk in the Abel story. Craig argues that a literal land of milk and honey would be "rancid stench and stickiness." On Deuteronomy 12, Craig says the "as your soul desires" phrasing comes from Numbers 11, where Israel lusts for quail meat and God sends a plague, and he links the plague to diet-related disease, citing the Physicians Committee (PCRM). Steve, reacting to the same passage, dismisses Jeremy's framing of "blessing" as a reading of complaining "whiny brats."

### 1 Corinthians 8 and 10 are about idol meat, and "meat market" is a mistranslation
Craig argues that Paul's "without raising any question on the ground of conscience" (1 Corinthians 10) is mistranslated. Craig says the Greek should be read as "without eating anything that's morally questionable," and that Paul names two morally questionable things, meat and wine, in 1 Corinthians 8:13 ("I will never eat meat again... cause my brother to stumble"). He argues Jeremy's reading is predominant only because of English translation choices. He also says the Greek word *sarx* ("flesh") is absent from the phrase often translated "meat market," and that translators would be inconsistent if they rendered it "meat market" here but not in the temple passage in the Gospels. Steve says that Paul's example in 1 Corinthians 11:1 ("follow my example as I follow Christ") points the same way.

### Luke 24 and the broiled fish: manuscript variants, Marcion, and the Gospel of Luke's sources
Craig argues the broiled fish of Luke 24 cannot ground a permanent diet. He makes four points:
- Two manuscript traditions differ, one with fish and honeycomb and one with fish alone. He says the honeycomb is itself a mistranslation of date paste, and the fish may have been a relish.
- Luke 24 is the only Gospel with this scene; Matthew, Mark, and John do not contain it, and the Gospel of the Hebrews has a different story with James.
- Luke 1:1-4 says the author is not an eyewitness and is compiling accounts. Craig says the source is Marcion's gospel, which predates the 180s, and that a reader who rejects Marcion cannot rely on this passage.
- The eating was to prove a bodily resurrection, not a menu. Steve and Craig deny that Jesus chose the meal, and Craig says Jesus ate what was there.

Craig also disputes the common claim that Jesus must have eaten lamb at Passover. He says John dates the crucifixion to the preparation day, that the Last Supper accounts list bread and wine, and that Essenes and Nazarenes did not eat Passover lamb. He says the Gospels contain no criticism of the Essenes, but a great deal against the Pharisees and Sadducees. Craig attributes "Luke as eyewitness" to Irenaeus (in *Against Heresies*), who derived it from the "we" passages in Acts; Craig says the author's own preface contradicts this.

### 1 Timothy 4 and the charge of demonic teaching
Craig says Jeremy's reading of 1 Timothy 4:1-5 would mean that calling vegan diets demonic is "blaspheming the Holy Spirit," which Jesus calls unforgivable in Matthew 12. Craig argues that the passage's two creation institutions, marriage and the plant-based diet of Genesis 1:29, are both called "very good," so anyone who forbids them is teaching something wrong. He notes that most scholars date 1 Timothy late (late second century), and he says that the believer's default assumption of Pauline authorship in the first century is what he himself uses, so he works within that frame. Steve also raises the view (which he attributes to others) that the passage targets Gnostics, Ebionites, or Nazarenes.

### Creation, image of God, and the vegan reading of Genesis 1
Craig argues that being made in God's image (Genesis 1) means eating the plants and fruit God gave as food, and that stewardship means mercy to animals, citing 1 John 4:8-16, Luke 6:36, and the prophets' "I desire mercy, not sacrifice." He says Jeremy's view that God gave animals to humans as food is "the opposite of what he gave us." Steve argues that Jeremy's reasoning, applied consistently, would make all of the Bible's food restrictions demonic, including Acts 15 and Revelation's warning against eating food sacrificed to idols.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Genesis 9:3 gives humans permission to eat animals.** Craig argues it is addressed to the animals, that the Genesis 9:4 prohibition on flesh with blood still stands, and that the everlasting covenant includes the animals.
- **"Land flowing with milk and honey" means animal milk and bee honey.** Craig and Steve argue it refers to fruit and date syrup, citing the spies' return with fruit and the Hebrew *kalav* ambiguity.
- **1 Corinthians 10 gives Christians permission to eat meat.** Craig says the passage concerns morally questionable food, and that "meat market" is a mistranslation of "marketplace" with no word for "meat" in the Greek.
- **Jesus ate lamb at Passover and ate fish after the resurrection, so meat is endorsed.** Craig argues the Passover reading is historically false, the Luke 24 fish is a disputed manuscript reading, and Jesus ate what was offered to prove he was not a ghost.
- **Calling vegan diets demonic is faithful to 1 Timothy 4.** Craig says that reading calls God and the Holy Spirit demonic, and that the passage's "very good" creation institutions cut against it.
