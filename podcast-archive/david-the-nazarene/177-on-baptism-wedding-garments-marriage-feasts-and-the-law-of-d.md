## Claims to Check

1. Dispensationalism came from séances in Darby's "haunted castle" and was financed by "high-level Jewish banking families." No source given; the repo's Darby file documents Powerscourt (1833), Tregelles and Scofield (1909) and nothing of the kind. The second clause is the sort of claim the repo should not carry forward in any form; `questions/CITATION-NOTES.md` already warns against the genetic framing of Darby and Scofield.
2. Luke 3:22 "this day have I begotten thee" is the original, changed after Nicaea. The reading is in Codex Bezae (5th century) and in Justin, Clement of Alexandria and Origen, all pre-Nicene; Sinaiticus and Vaticanus (4th century) read "in thee I am well pleased," as does the KJV (held). An early Western reading, not "the oldest manuscripts," and the change was not post-Nicene.
3. John 3:5 originally read "baptized of water." No Greek manuscript of John reads it; Homilies 11.26 and the Apostolic Constitutions quote freely, as fathers do. Sinaiticus's "kingdom of heaven" at 3:5 is a real variant and does not touch "born."
4. *Pisteuō* means obey and "lexicographers have known for 200 years." *Pisteuō eis* is "believe in, trust"; John 3:36 pairs it with *apeitheō* (disobey), which supports an obedience dimension, but "obey" as the meaning is his, not the lexica's. His 161 and 164 carry the argument.
5. "Dead carcasses" in Homilies 7.8 are relics. The word sits in a food list (strangled, blood), where it means carrion (Leviticus 17:15). His gloss is a stretch.
6. Paul's Christ was Satan or Simon Magus. Recognitions 4.34-35 (held) is the text behind "false apostles"; the identification is his conjecture, offered as one ("a new theory I have").
7. Paul "did not baptize." 1 Corinthians 1:14-16 names Crispus, Gaius and the household of Stephanas as his baptisms; Acts 16:15, 16:33, 18:8 and 19:5 record baptisms in his mission. Verse 17 is about his role, not a refusal.
8. 2 Corinthians 12:7 means Paul was possessed. The held KJV reads "the messenger of Satan"; the Greek is *angelos*; possession is a reading, also in his 157.
9. The thirty, sixty and a hundred commands are Matthew 13:8. Recognitions 4.36 is real and held; the link to the parable of the sower is his.
10. Homilies 11.26 and 9.19 use the triune formula. True in the held Greek text, which is a problem for his 178 claim that Rufinus inserted the formula into the Recognitions; note the tension.
11. Ehrman: meat was eaten only at sacrifice in the ancient world. A generalization about Greco-Roman urban practice; fish and non-sacrificial butchery existed. Not held.
12. The Nicodemus-as-Edomite claim, withdrawn here. Worth recording that he corrected it himself.
