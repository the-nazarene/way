## Summary

Tabor asks why Mary Magdalene appears and disappears so quickly in the sources. He says she is named in few independent texts, that she shows up at the cross and the burial among women who were intimate with Jesus's family, and that she is then the first witness of the resurrection. He calls the lecture a tribute to the late Jane Schaberg, who did much to "resurrect" Mary's reputation. Tabor credits the material to a co-presenter, "Tina," whose earlier lecture he refers to but who does not speak in this clip. He presents the talk as a presenter's teaching, with much of it built on his own reading of Mark, John, Luke and the Gospel of Peter.

His central argument is that the earliest resurrection account is not the Jerusalem material but a short, non-theological narrative in John 20:1-10, and that the original appearances took place in Galilee, as Mark 14:28 and 16:7 indicate. He treats Luke's and John's Jerusalem appearances as later developments and cites the Gospel of Peter's ending as consistent with a return to Galilee. He reads John 21 as the Galilean tradition, and he argues that Mary's presence at the tomb, and her intimacy with the family, make her a credible first witness. He admits Paul's list in 1 Corinthians 15 does not mention her, and offers two possibilities for that silence.

The talk ends with Tabor's own reflections on Mary's place in the Jerusalem movement, including her likely closeness to Jesus's mother and to James. He says he does not know whether Mary was married to Jesus or only a companion, and he closes by dedicating the lecture to Schaberg.

## Key Teachings

### Mary at the cross and the burial: an intimate family circle

Tabor counts only independent sources, not Matthew or Luke when they repeat Mark, and says there are few references to her. He argues her appearance at the crucifixion (Mark 15:40-41) is not accidental. He identifies the women as Mary Magdalene, "Mary the mother of James the younger and Joses," and Salome, and takes the "mother of James" to be Jesus's mother, citing Mark 6:3. He reads the name "Joses" as rare in inscriptional evidence. He argues that Mary's presence at the burial (Mark 15:47; 16:1) is intimate family work, washing and anointing a body, and that this makes her more than an incidental follower. He is explicit that this does not prove she was Jesus's wife.

### John 20:1-10 as the earliest resurrection account

Tabor calls these ten verses "the most precious bit of the new testament" on the resurrection. He notes what is absent: no angels, no earthquake, no stone rolled away, no one in the tomb. He reads the passage as a plain "newspaper account": Mary comes early, finds the stone moved, runs to Simon Peter and "the other disciple," who he thinks is James. The two men see the linen clothes, and the other disciple "saw and believed," though "as yet they did not know the scripture." He credits a scholar he calls "Shaw bird" (name as captioned) for the view that this is early and credible, and he refers to his own Hinnom Valley discovery of a shrouded first-century burial, with separate head and body cloths, as a parallel that he finds makes the account ring true.

### Galilee as the original site of the appearances

Tabor argues the disciples went home after the Passover week, and that Mark's "go before you to galilee" (Mark 14:28; 16:7) and the Gospel of Peter's ending, where the disciples return to their homes and Peter and Andrew go fishing, point to Galilee. He reads Matthew 28:16-17 (the eleven go to the mountain in Galilee, some doubt) as consistent with this. He treats John 21 (the Sea of Galilee appearance) as the Galilean tradition, and calls the Jerusalem appearances in Luke 24 and John 20 (after verse 10) later and "legendary." He notes that Luke 24's instruction to stay in the city until Pentecost does not fit the Galilean material. He treats Jerusalem as the site where the movement took root later at Pentecost, not where it began.

### Paul's list omits Mary

Tabor notes that 1 Corinthians 15 lists appearances to Kephas, the Twelve, "500 brethren," James, "all the apostles," and Paul himself, but not Mary Magdalene. He offers two possibilities: that the testimony of women was not respected in that setting, or that Paul "just doesn't know all the story," and that the list is more like formal testimony that does not say where things happened.

### Mary's origin in Migdal and the Galilean women's entourage

Tabor argues Mary is from Migdal (Magdala) in Galilee, not from Jerusalem, and he says he has been drawn to the idea that the sinner woman of Luke 7 or Mary of Bethany is Mary Magdalene but is "not convinced" by Jeffrey Butts's argument for it. He reads Luke 8:2-3 as describing a supported movement: Mary called Magdalene, Joanna (wife of Chuza, Herod's steward), Susanna, "and many other women" who provided from their means. He cites the Talmud's wordplay on the root for Migdal, and an ancient synagogue at Migdal excavated by the Israel Antiquities Authority, as supporting a Galilean setting for her community.

### Marginalization of Mary and the family

Tabor argues Luke systematically removes Mary Magdalene's name where Mark has it, and that Acts says nothing about her. He reads this as the deliberate marginalization of women close to the family, linked to a shift from a "fleshly" Jesus to a "spiritual Christ." He also says the beloved disciple at the cross (John 19:25-27) is James, not John son of Zebedee, and that the handing over of Jesus's mother to "John" is a legend developed to hide James.

### Mary as first apostle and witness

Tabor agrees with Tina that Mary is the first apostle, since an apostle is someone who has seen Jesus. He argues Mary saw Jesus first in Jerusalem, while the men saw him in Galilee. He cites the Gospel of Mary and the Gospel of Philip as presenting Mary as prominent. He reasons that Jesus's mother and brothers, including James, who lived on Mount Zion, would have consulted her as a leader of the movement. He says he does not know whether she was married to Jesus, how old she was, or whether she had children, but that she and the mother and sisters "were allowed great intimacy" with the family.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Mary Magdalene as the sinner woman of Luke 7 or Mary of Bethany:** Tabor says he is not convinced by the argument that she is either, and he favors her as a Galilean woman from Migdal.
- **John son of Zebedee as the beloved disciple:** Tabor argues it is James, and that John son of Zebedee had fled at the arrest.
- **Joseph of Arimathea's tomb as Jesus's permanent burial place:** Tabor argues the body was placed in a temporary tomb near the cross because of the approaching Passover, and then moved later to another tomb, possibly Joseph's family tomb.
- **Resurrection appearances originally in Jerusalem:** Tabor argues Galilee is the original location, and Jerusalem traditions developed later.
- **Easter Sunday as the expected time for the appearances:** Tabor says Jesus's words in Mark 14:28 send the disciples to Galilee and do not promise an appearance on Sunday morning.
- **Mary as Jesus's wife:** Tabor says he does not argue this, while acknowledging the image in popular films and his own view that she was a companion inside the family circle.
