## Key Teachings

### Pixner's Essene Gate and the Essene Quarter
Tabor reports that Pixner's identification of the Essene Gate is now repeated in the Oxford Archaeological Guide to the Holy Land and on Oxford Bible maps. He describes the Essenes' purity rules as the reason a gate would be needed. In his account, the Essene scrolls forbid sex and defecation within the holy city, so a community living on the lip of the city would go out through its own gate to relieve itself in the Hinnom Valley and then re-immerse before re-entering. Tabor says he does not think Jesus was himself an Essene, but he treats the question of Essene connections as open.

### Josephus's Walls Tour and the Bait-so
Tabor reads Jewish War 5 as Josephus describing a circuit of the walls. From the Hippicus Tower (which he links to the Tower of David near Jaffa Gate), the circuit goes east to the council house and temple, then south to a place called Bait-so ("house of filth," a latrine or drainage site), then to the Essene Gate, then east to the Pool of Siloam. Tabor says Pixner placed Bait-so and the gate near the southwest corner of Mount Zion, and Gibson placed them further north near the Tower of David.

### Mount Zion, the Upper Room, and the Nazarene Headquarters
Tabor describes Pixner's claim that the Upper Room (above) and the Tomb of David (below) preserve a first-century synagogue of James, which Pixner called the "synagogue of the apostles." He presents the building as Herodian ashlar reused after 70 CE and enclosed later by Crusader and Byzantine construction. He ties this to the Bordeaux pilgrim's account of a "little church of the apostles" on Mount Zion and to the Hagia Sion built under Theodosius. He also says Mary's death and burial are traditionally associated with Mount Zion (the "Dormition" name), and that James, Jude, and the brothers gathered there with the mother after Pentecost.

### Herodian Archaeology in Area E
Tabor describes his team's dig on Mount Zion since 2008–2009. Finds include stone vessels (which he links to ritual purity), murex shells (linked to purple dye for the temple), and the inscribed stone cup. He says a priestly house is likely, and that the team did not dig in 2010 because of funding. He argues that Mount Zion is the center of Herodian Jerusalem and that the Turkish wall obscures it.

### Gibson's Rival Gate and the Palace of Herod
Tabor presents Gibson's argument that Pixner's gate is late (Byzantine) and that the Essene Gate should be placed near the Tower of David. Tabor concedes that Gibson's gate and tunnel are a major discovery, but he argues that the stone steps and platform on the Armenian parking lot lead into Herod's palace. He cites John 18 (the disciple who knew the woman at the gate) and John 19 (Pilate going in and out, the crowd outside) as reasons to think John's account reflects an informed knowledge of the palace. He concludes that the platform was the praetorium where Jesus was tried, and he calls that place "the place of the trial of jesus."

### Pilate, Antipas and the Herodian Palace
Tabor argues that Pilate did not stay in the Antonia fortress (as the Via Dolorosa tradition holds) because the governor preferred Caesarea, and because Herod Antipas, when in Jerusalem for Passover, would have used the royal palace. He also questions whether the Matthew account of Pilate's wife implies the couple slept in the barracks, and he suggests that the palace is the more plausible location.

### Dating the Last Supper and Trial
Tabor argues that the Last Supper and trial took place on Thursday, not Friday, because the crowd would not enter the praetorium before Passover. He treats the Gospel of John's detail about the high priest's court and the praetorium as evidence of a source with real knowledge of Jerusalem's geography. He acknowledges that many scholars date John late and find it theologically heavy.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The Essene Gate's identity is settled.** Tabor says Pixner's identification is repeated in standard guides, but he argues that it has not been tested by excavation and that Gibson's excavations challenge it.
- **The Upper Room and the Tomb of David are the same building and the Upper Room is the Last Supper site.** Tabor presents this as Pixner's theory, notes that Jewish scholars do not think the lower chamber is David's tomb, and says the Upper Room's first-century origin is an inference.
- **Pilate stayed in the Antonia fortress.** Tabor argues that the governor preferred Caesarea and that the Herodian palace is the more likely residence in Jerusalem.
- **Jesus's trial and the Last Supper were on Friday and Thursday as the traditions have them.** Tabor argues for a Thursday trial, based on the crowd's Passover timing.
