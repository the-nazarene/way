## Summary

Tabor asks who Mary Magdalene was, why the gospels link her so closely to Jesus' mother and family, and why she is the first witness to the empty tomb. He argues that the Gospels mention her only briefly but place her with the inner family at the cross, the burial and the tomb. He says he is "not arguing that she's his wife," but that she was "considered part of the family on some intimate level." His main evidence is a close reading of Mark 15-16 and John 20:1-10, which he takes as the earliest intact resurrection account. He also uses the fragmentary Gospel of Peter and Paul's list in 1 Corinthians 15 to argue that the first appearances were in Galilee, not Jerusalem.

Tabor reads Mary as a Galilean woman from Magdala who supported the movement and was close to Jesus' mother and sisters. He treats her as the first witness of the resurrection, and he says he finds it "not so far-fetched" that she was prominent in the early movement, as the Gospel of Mary and the Gospel of Philip suggest. He ends by dedicating the lecture to Jane Schaberg, whose book he recommends, and by rejecting the Dan Brown picture of Mary. He says the point is that she is "the first witness of the resurrection," which he thinks is clear in the records.

The talk is almost entirely Tabor's lecture. Tina is referred to in the third person and is mainly a co-presenter whose earlier remarks Tabor responds to, so the speaker attributions above reflect that.

## Key Teachings

### Mary as an intimate family member, not necessarily a wife
Tabor counts the women at the cross and tomb in Mark 15:40-41, 15:47 and 16:1, and in Luke 8:2. He argues that "Mary the mother of james" is Jesus' mother, and that the names Mary, Joses and Salome form an intimate family group. He notes that Luke drops the names entirely and says only "the women from galilee." He treats the anointing of the body as a task "this is something that the family does." His conclusion is that she was "not just extraneous," though he explicitly declines to claim she was married to Jesus.

### The four women and the "family being played down"
Tabor reads John 19 as showing four women in two pairs: Jesus' mother, his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene. He says Jesus' mother is unnamed in John, and he calls the "family getting played down" a pattern. He cites "balcom and others" (transcribed "Balcom," probably Bauckham) for the two-pair reading. He notes that if Clopas is Joseph's brother, as "patristic records" say, then Clopas's wife is also an intimate. He identifies the beloved disciple as James, not John son of Zebedee, arguing that John son of Zebedee had "fled" and that the story of Jesus' mother going to John is a legend "to further hide james."

### The earliest resurrection account: John 20:1-10 and the Gospel of Peter
Tabor calls John 20:1-10 "the most precious bit of the new testament" on the resurrection and says it is "an intact account." He points to what is missing: no angels, no earthquake, no one in the tomb. Mary finds the stone moved, runs to Peter and the other disciple, and they inspect the linen clothes and the head napkin. He compares the napkin and cloth to his own find, the "tomb of the shroud," discovered in the Hinnom valley in 2000. He reads the unnamed disciple's belief as belief that the tomb was empty, "not that he's risen from the dead," since "they did not know the scripture" yet.

He then reads the Gospel of Peter, which he says was found in 1886 by a French archaeologist in upper Egypt. It has Mary finding the tomb empty and ends with the disciples going home after the feast of unleavened bread. He reads this as a return to Galilee and says the fragment "breaks off," so he has to guess that it "is going to pick up with what we get in john 21."

### Galilee as the original site of the appearances
Tabor argues that Mark, the earliest Gospel, has Jesus promise to go before the disciples to Galilee (Mark 14; the angel's words in Mark 16 repeat it). He notes that Matthew 28 has the eleven go to a mountain in Galilee. He treats John 21 as parallel evidence, and reads Luke 24 and the Jerusalem appearances as later developments that "contradict the galilee traditions." He also reads Paul's list in 1 Corinthians 15 (Kephas, the 500, James, all the apostles, then Paul) as silent on Mary, which he suggests may mean either that women's testimony was not respected or that Paul "just doesn't know all the story."

### Mary from Magdala and the Galilean women's movement
Tabor says he "really ha[s] wanted to believe" that the anointing woman in Luke 7 or the Mary of Bethany in John 12 is Mary Magdalene, and cites Jeffrey Butts's article as arguing it, but he is not convinced. He reads Luke 8:2-3 (Mary called Magdalene, Joanna the wife of Chuza, Susanna and "many other women") as a Galilean support group that "provided for them out of their treasury." He discusses the name "Magdala" as meaning "tower" and notes a Talmudic wordplay on the same root. He points to the Magdala synagogue excavated by the Israel Antiquities Authority as a likely place where Mary "attended" with the movement.

### Mary as first apostle and witness, and her erasure
Tabor agrees with Tina that Mary is "the first apostle," because "an apostle is someone who's seen jesus." He says she "got the extraordinary privilege of seeing jesus in jerusalem." He argues that Luke, by listing her only once (Luke 24:10), "deep sixth[s]" the women and marginalises those close to the family, and says it is strange that Acts never mentions her. He suggests that the mother of Jesus was "very prominent" and that James and the brothers would have consulted her. He concludes that Mary was "the most erased and forgotten character in the jesus story."

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Mary Magdalene as Jesus' wife:** Tabor says he is "not arguing that she's his wife," and treats the marriage idea as something he would not bring back, comparing it to *The Da Vinci Code*.
- **John son of Zebedee as the beloved disciple:** Tabor argues the beloved disciple is James, and that John son of Zebedee had fled and was not at the cross.
- **Jerusalem as the birthplace of the faith:** Tabor argues the faith "was born" in Galilee, with Jerusalem traditions developing later and conflicting with the Galilee ones.
- **Sunday morning as the appointed meeting time:** Tabor notes that Jesus said the disciples would see him in Galilee, not that he would meet them on Sunday morning, and says people forget this.
- **Mary as the anointing sinner or as Mary of Bethany:** Tabor says that he has "wanted to believe" the identification but is not convinced; he places Mary from Magdala and in the Galilean women's group.
- **Luke's account as the main guide to the resurrection:** Tabor treats Luke 24's Jerusalem material as later and legendary, and says Luke's naming of Mary is minimal.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **James and the Jerusalem community:** Tabor identifies the beloved disciple as James and argues that the Jerusalem group, including Jesus' mother and brothers, would have consulted Mary, "the queen mother of the movement."
- **Who Jesus' first followers were:** Tabor places Mary, Jesus' mother and sisters, and the Galilean women within an intimate family movement supported by wealthier women.
