## Summary

Tabor opens with a personal account of his first visit to Jerusalem as a teenager, around 1962 (his book's description gives age 16), and of the question that began his career: how to "find the historical Jesus." He recounts being disappointed that the sites shown to Christian pilgrims, including the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, the Mount of Olives, Gethsemane and the Upper Room, did not evoke the first century for him. Modern Jerusalem, he says, is largely Turkish, Crusader and Byzantine, and the modern street level sits "12 to 15 feet above that of roman times." His evidence here is his own observation, not a documented argument.

The most vivid episode is a midnight walk, Bible in hand, from his hotel on the Mount of Olives down to the Garden of Gethsemane. He says he could see bedrock cut or worn on both sides of the steep path, which he took to be an ancient road, and he imagined Jesus riding a donkey down it to the crowds. He also describes the steps leading up to Herod's Temple, which were 25 feet below the surface when he first visited and are now exposed. Tabor presents these moments as the start of a lifelong search, linking the spade of the archaeologist with the work of the historian.

The talk ends as a promotion for his book *The Jesus Dynasty*. Tabor describes it as "a new historical investigation of jesus his royal family and the birth of christianity," proposing "an original version of christianity" that he says "can be reliably traced back to the founder jesus himself." He warns that the book will upset some readers and asks listeners of any persuasion to "honestly weigh evidence and consider new possibilities." The talk itself presents none of that evidence, so its specific claims can only be assessed in the book.

## Key Teachings

### The disconnect between modern Jerusalem and the first century
Tabor's first argument is observational. Walking the Old City as a teenager, he could not connect "20th century jerusalem" with "the city and the time of jesus as described in the new testament." He says he saw mainly Turkish, Crusader and Byzantine remains and little from the first century AD, and that the modern street level lies 12 to 15 feet above Roman-period ground. The point sets up his broader claim that the past must be recovered by excavation rather than inferred from pilgrim sites.

### Bedrock on the road down from the Mount of Olives
At about midnight he walked from his hotel on the Mount of Olives toward Gethsemane. He reports that the steep path, now paved, showed bedrock "cut or worn" on both sides, "indicating this was the narrow road from ancient times." He pictures Jesus descending it on a donkey toward Jerusalem, "hailed by the crowds as the messiah a week before he was crucified." He offers this as a personal sense of connection with the past, not as a documented identification of the road.

### Herod's Temple steps buried beneath the surface
Tabor notes that the steps leading up to the Jewish temple "built in the time of herod the great" were 25 feet below the present surface when he first visited in 1962, and that excavation has since exposed them. He uses this to illustrate his claim that "the past is being exposed to the present by the spade of the archaeologist." No excavation report or date is cited in the talk.

### The thesis of *The Jesus Dynasty*
In the closing paragraph he states the book's claims. It is "a new historical investigation of jesus his royal family and the birth of christianity." It proposes "an original version of christianity long lost and forgotten" that he says can be "reliably traced back to the founder jesus himself." The talk does not explain the sources or method behind this claim, so it stays a stated position.
