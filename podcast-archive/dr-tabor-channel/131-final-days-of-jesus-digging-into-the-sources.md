## Key Teachings

### The Last Supper and the timing of Passover

Tabor says the Last Supper's relation to Passover "is controversial" because of a source difference. The Synoptic Gospels, with Mark as the main source and Matthew and Luke following, present the meal in a way that can be read as Passover. John, he says, "puts it before the Passover." He says he treats this in his posts and says Luke's version of the bread and cup words is, in his view, "a more... interesting take" on editing. He gives no verse-level argument in this video.

### A Thursday death rather than a Friday crucifixion

This is the thesis he flags at the start: he will "suggest an alternative for the crucifixion." A blog title in the list reads "Jesus died on a Thursday not a Friday." He frames this against the usual Palm Sunday through Good Friday and Easter schedule and says the results of his alternatives are "fascinating," without giving them.

### Golgotha and the praetorium

Tabor says a group he recently led to Israel stood on what he calls "pilate's judgment seat," which he says is "guaranteed authentic" and "not in the old city along the Via De La Rosa." He says historians and archaeologists know this even though tourists do not yet. On Golgotha he lists the Church of the Holy Sepulcher, the Garden Tomb north of Damascus Gate ("Gordon's calvary") and his own preferred candidate, the Mount of Olives. He says the Church of the Holy Sepulcher is held by "probably 98 of academics," a figure he states without citation.

### The endings of Mark and the Gospel of Peter

He calls the ending of Mark "strange" and says the "most important 10 verses of the gospels" are the ones to read on Easter morning. He also lists a post on "the surprise ending of the Lost Gospel of Peter." Neither is argued in the talk.

### Paul's resurrection appearances

Tabor cites Paul's "last of all he appeared to me" and asks what Paul claimed to have seen. He says "a spiritual Resurrection is the only sensible option," meaning a resurrection of the dead that is not "the immortal Soul sailing off to Heaven" and also not "the corruptable Flesh and Blood body." He says he argues this in one of his posts, and that faith in Jesus's resurrection "developed an old new hypothesis."

### The Talpiot tomb and the James ossuary

He describes the 1980 discovery of the "Jesus tomb" (Talpiot) and says "what we know 40 years later" has grown, including "new evidence on the James ossuary and its probable connection to the Talpiot tomb." He says scholars who doubt the family-tomb thesis have said that if the James ossuary is genuine, it would matter, and he says he does not think it is a fake. He disputes the idea that the Talpiot case has been refuted, pointing to his response to two colleagues whom he says he respects but disagrees with.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Traditional sites are reliable.** Tabor says the Church of the Holy Sepulcher, the Garden Tomb and the Mount of Olives are contested Golgotha candidates, and the description says traditional Jerusalem sites are "more often than not likely mistaken."
- **The Talpiot tomb has been refuted.** He says some claim the matter was settled years ago by academics, and he answers that the evidence "does not hold up" as proof but "shouldn't be dismissed."
- **Good Friday and Easter are settled dates.** He says the usual Friday crucifixion and Sunday resurrection schedule is questionable and that the Last Supper's relation to Passover is controversial.
