Tabor says the community behind the Dead Sea Scrolls, usually identified with the Essenes, expected the coming of three figures: a prophet like Moses, the Messiah of Aaron, and the Messiah of Israel. He identifies the Messiah of Israel as the Davidic king. The Messiah of Aaron, in his account, is a priestly figure also called a messiah. He presents this as the key that reorders various texts. ### "Messiah" as an anointing and Aaron as the first
He traces *moshiach* ("anointed one") to the Hebrew, and *Christos* to the Greek with the same meaning. He describes the anointing ritual, in which oil was poured on the head of a chosen person to confirm him as priest or king. He says "most people are surprised to learn that the very first messiah in the Bible was Aaron," anointed by his brother Moses and called a *moshiach* in Exodus 40.
