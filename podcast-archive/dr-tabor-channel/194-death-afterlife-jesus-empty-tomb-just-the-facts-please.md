## Summary

Tabor asks what can be known, and with what probability, about what happened after Jesus' crucifixion and burial. He argues that the usual harmonized "Easter story" should be set aside. He reads the sources in rough chronological order: Paul's letters (which he dates to the 50s CE), the Gospel of Mark, Matthew, Luke, John, the longer endings of Mark, the Gospel of Peter, and a reconstructed Sayings Gospel Q. He treats the traditional dates as uncertain and says he would put the Gospels later. His central claim is that Mark's original ending, with no appearances in Jerusalem, is the earliest Gospel witness and fits with Paul, and that the later Gospels add appearances and angels. He also argues that the first 10 verses of John 20 may preserve the earliest empty-tomb report, which in his reading describes a reburial rather than a resurrection.

His reconstruction runs as follows. Jesus was buried hastily near the place of crucifixion, perhaps in a temporary tomb, because the Sabbath and Passover intervened. A burial party, which Tabor thinks included Joseph of Arimathea, later moved the body into a permanent tomb. Mary Magdalene then found the first tomb empty. For Tabor, "resurrection" in Paul means glorification into a transformed heavenly body, not the revival of a corpse, and the visions were in Galilee. He presents this as the most probable historical reading, not as settled fact, and says that "each one of us has to make our own judgment."

The talk ends with Tabor pointing readers to two books (Dale Allison and John Granger Cook) and to a 2015 blog post on early Christian views of resurrection. He says the next installment will draw the eight-part series together. The talk is a solo lecture with slides, so Tabor carries all of it.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Paul is the earliest witness, and resurrection means a transformed body
Tabor calls Paul "the earliest record" and "the only eyewitness, first-hand account" (about 02:25), citing 1 Corinthians 15, where Paul lists appearances to Peter, the Twelve, "then 500 at once," and others. He says Paul describes "a new, transformed body that is no longer flesh and blood" (03:09), which he distinguishes both from resuscitation and from an immortal soul. He reads Paul's "if Christ is not risen" as the claim that without this glorification "the dead have perished" (58:17 to 59:17), and he presents this as the ancient Hebrew view of death.

### 2. Mark's ending is original, and the longer endings are later additions
Tabor reads Mark 16:1-8 closely. The women are told that Jesus "is not here" and will be seen in Galilee, and they "said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid." He argues that Mark's readers were content with this ending, since "we have strong textual evidence that the first generation of Jesus' followers were perfectly fine with the gospel account that recounted no appearances" (15:54). He reads the longer ending (Mark 16:9-20) as a patchwork of Matthew, Luke, and Acts, and the two further endings as clearly forged. He claims that Mark's tradition has the disciples return to Galilee after the eight-day Passover festival, and he finds support for this in the Gospel of Peter, which he dates to the early second century and calls "very, very dependent on Mark" (19:37 to 22:21). He also uses the Galilee-appearance theme to link Mark to Paul.

### 3. Q has no resurrection narrative, but a "vindication" saying
Tabor says Q contains nothing about the empty tomb. He identifies one relevant saying in Luke 13:34-35 ("you will not see me until the time comes when you say, blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord"), which he links to Psalm 118:26. He follows Daniel Smith's book on Q, which reads the saying as a postmortem vindication: Jesus disappears and will be seen again "in the clouds of glory," the same pattern Tabor sees in Mark's "I will go before you to Galilee" (24:08 to 27:30). He compares this to ascent narratives, drawing on his own dissertation, *Paul's Ascent to Paradise*.

### 4. John 20:1-10 may preserve the earliest empty-tomb report
Tabor calls John 20:1-10 a possible "piece of the most original report of the empty tomb" (28:28 to 29:18). He credits Jane Schaberg with the argument. In this unit Mary comes alone, finds the stone removed, and runs to Peter and "the other disciple." The two men see the linen clothes, and the other disciple "saw and believed," though Tabor argues he believed only that the body had been taken, not that Jesus was risen. He reads the reference to "the other disciple" as James the brother of Jesus, not the Beloved Disciple as traditionally understood (31:47). He notes that the unit contains no angels, no young man, and no appearance, and he argues that the disciples "went back to their homes" in Galilee (32:31).

### 5. The reconstruction: hasty burial, reburial, and a "temporary emergency burial"
Tabor argues that John 19:38-42 shows Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus burying Jesus quickly in "a new tomb where no one had ever been laid" near the crucifixion, because the Sabbath and Passover made proper burial impossible. He says "predators will eat it, literally" if the body is left out (34:41). On his account, the body was moved Saturday night to a permanent tomb, which is why the first tomb was empty. He compares the timing to Jewish burial practice and adds that Joseph's wealth and family ties make a family tomb plausible, though he calls the link to Joseph of Arimathea "a later tradition" (52:29 to 54:03). He lists his reconstruction in steps at 53:17 to 54:03, and he concedes that whether there was a tomb at all, and whether it was empty, is a live question among colleagues (55:46).

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Popular belief:** Mark's Gospel is incomplete because it has no resurrection appearances. **Correction:** Tabor argues that Mark "did not consider his account deficient," that the appearances are later additions, and that Mark is "the original faith" (15:54; 16:43).
- **Popular belief:** Harmonizing the four Gospels gives one true sequence of Easter events. **Correction:** Tabor says the accounts differ in who goes, who or what is in the tomb, and where Jesus appears, and that merging them obscures their separate emphases (39:04 to 39:45).
- **Popular belief:** Resurrection means a physical revival of the corpse, eating and showing wounds. **Correction:** Tabor reads Paul and Revelation as describing a transformed heavenly body, and he treats the bodily proofs of Luke and John as later apologetics (1:04:0 to 1:06:35).
- **Popular belief:** Joseph of Arimathea's tomb was his own private tomb near Jerusalem where Jesus was laid. **Correction:** Tabor argues the burial was temporary and emergency-driven, with the body moved to a permanent place (33:19 to 34:41).
- **Popular belief:** The Beloved Disciple of John 20 is John the son of Zebedee. **Correction:** Tabor suggests it is James the brother of Jesus (31:47).

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** Tabor argues that Mark's Galilee-centred ending and Paul's account of visions of the glorified Christ agree, which bears on whether Paul's resurrection doctrine is a development from Jesus' teaching or a continuation of it. He does not address Jesus' own resurrection teaching beyond the Last Supper saying and Q's vindication saying.
- **James and the Jerusalem community:** Tabor's identification of the "other disciple" with James the brother of Jesus (31:47) and his mention of James among those who had visionary experiences (1:06:35) are direct points of contact. He does not develop the Jerusalem community as such.
- **Resurrection, afterlife and hell:** This is the talk's main subject. Tabor argues that the earliest view was glorification at God's right hand, that the dead "perish" without resurrection, and that the corpse-revival view arose after 70 CE.
- **Gospels and canon:** The talk offers a detailed account of how the endings of Mark were expanded (Mark 16:9-20, the forged endings, and the Gospel of Peter). Tabor's course title, *Why Mark's Gospel was Forgotten*, frames this as a question of marginalization.
- **Who Jesus thought he was:** Mostly out of scope, though Tabor quotes Jesus' answer to Pilate ("you will see the Son of Man... coming in the clouds of heaven") in the Q discussion (27:30).
