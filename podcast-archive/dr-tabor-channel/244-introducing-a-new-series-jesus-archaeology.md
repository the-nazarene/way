## Key Teachings

### Archaeology is material evidence, and it is not treasure hunting
Tabor defines archaeology as "the material evidence of the human past" and says it "should not have an ideological motivation behind it." He contrasts the discipline with the Indiana Jones image and describes most of it as "moving dirt very carefully and recording every single thing that's there." He says the aim is to record everything found, which he calls "reversing the past slowly inch by inch."

### Archaeology is not apologetics
Tabor says archaeology is not meant to "prove the Bible or prove Jesus or verify something that's in the gospels." He says he has lectured for 30 years on the Bible, archaeology and Jesus, and that audiences often respond by saying discoveries are "proving the Bible." He rejects that framing and says the researcher should "let the chips fall where they may."

### Site selection should be strategic, and the direction of inquiry matters
Tabor says excavation should not seek a predetermined result, so the choice of where to dig must be strategic. He says his own work at Sepphoris was not an attempt to find Jesus. On method, he stresses the direction of study: "reading ancient texts in the light of the archaeological record," not "reading the archaeological record in the light of ancient text." He says the second approach "would be getting it backwards." He also says archaeology sheds light on texts "in some degree," but that researchers must be careful.

### Recommended reading
- *The Jesus Dynasty* (Tabor's own book): he calls it "a historian's book" and says the last 20 of his 30 years of research relied heavily on archaeological material.
- *Beneath the Stones, Behind the Text* (John Dominic Crossan and Jonathan Reed): Tabor says he disagrees with many of its conclusions but praises the friendly scholarly exchange.
- *Excavating the Land of Jesus* (James Strange): he presents it as a practical handbook on how archaeologists study "the people of the gospels," and notes Strange's work at Shikhin, which the transcript renders as "shikim," a Galilean village north of the Sea of Galilee.

### Participating in a dig
Tabor describes *Biblical Archaeology Review*'s January "Dig issue," which lists active excavations in Israel with dates and costs, some offering college credit and some not. He says he has taken up to a thousand students to Israel over the years and calls digs "like nothing you've ever done."
