## Key Teachings

### The heel-bone nail (Yehohanan)
Tabor's main evidence is the skeletal remains of Yehohanan, found in a 1968 tomb north of the Old City off Nablus Road. Tabor says a crucifixion nail was found through the heel bone, not through the front of the foot as artists had long depicted. He notes Yehohanan is otherwise unknown and that his name is fairly common. Tabor shows models of the foot bone and the nail, saying the models reproduce the actual find.

### Why the heel rather than the front of the foot
Tabor reasons that a nail through the front of the feet would cause heavy bleeding. He says the heel has no major blood vessels, so a nail there would not cause the victim to bleed to death within minutes. Tabor argues this matters because the purpose of crucifixion was to prolong suffering over several days. He describes the arrangement as something like nailed stirrups, while calling it a rough idea to picture.

### The arms: tied, not nailed through the wrists
Tabor says his graduate-school teaching was that the arm nails went through the wrists between the radius and ulna to hold the body's weight, and he states that this is not correct. His current view is that the arms were tied, probably with ropes higher up, to carry the weight. He acknowledges that his drawing of this arrangement may not be exactly accurate.

### The hands: tacked, using the Antigonus remains
For the hands, Tabor says they were placed on the back of the crossbar and fixed with small nails. He cites the remains of Antigonus (Matthias), whom he identifies as the last Hasmonean king, executed by Mark Antony after Herod defeated him. Tabor says DNA was taken from the jaw, and that a hand bone shows a nail still held in place. He reads this as a nail meant to keep the hand from moving back and forth, so the hands stayed on the crossbar.

### Cause of death and application to Jesus
Tabor applies the findings to Jesus, saying the crucifixion would have involved dehydration, fatigue, shock and blood loss, especially after scourging, beating, whipping and being up all night under torture. He says it is no surprise that Jesus would have died rather quickly. This is Tabor's inference from the archaeology and the gospel narrative, not something the finds directly establish.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Nails through the front of the feet.** Tabor says this is the traditional picture, shaped by artists imagining both feet nailed together, and that the Yehohanan find shows the nail went through the heel.
- **Nails through the wrists to hold the body's weight.** Tabor says he was taught this in graduate school, but he now holds that it is not correct and that the arms were tied to carry the weight.
