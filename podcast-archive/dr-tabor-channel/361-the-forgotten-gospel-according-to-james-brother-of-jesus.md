The reading makes its strongest claim here: "so Faith by itself if it has no works is dead," and "you see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone." The letter cites Abraham offering Isaac on the altar and Rahab welcoming the messengers as examples. The description's framing of James as a pre-Pauline voice makes this passage a natural point of comparison with Paul's language of justification, but the talk does not name Paul or draw the comparison.

### Doing the word and the royal law (James 1:22-25; 2:8-13)
The letter says to be "doers of the word and not merely hearers," and calls the "perfect law" "the law of Liberty." It also calls the commandment to "love your neighbor as yourself" the "Royal law" and says that breaking one point makes one "accountable for all of it." The talk does not explain how these relate to the Torah as a whole.

### Care for the poor and against partiality (James 2:1-9; 5:1-6)
The letter condemns showing favoritism to the rich over the poor in the assembly and says God "has chosen the poor in the world to be rich in faith." Later it warns the rich of coming miseries and accuses them of defrauding laborers.

### Speech and the tongue (James 3:1-12; 4:11-12)
The reading develops the image of the tongue as a small rudder and a fire, "a Restless evil full of deadly poison," and warns against judging one's neighbor, since this amounts to judging the law.

### Prayer, anointing, and confession (James 5:13-18)
The letter says "the prayer of faith will save the sick," instructs the sick to call the elders and be anointed "in the name of the Lord," and uses Elijah's prayer for drought as an example. The talk offers no comment on how this passage has been used.
