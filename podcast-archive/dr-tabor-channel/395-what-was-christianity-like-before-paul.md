## Summary

This 15-minute clip, an excerpt from a longer interview with hosts Aaron Abke and Kameron Waters, asks what "Christianity" looked like before Paul. Tabor opens by conceding that "Christianity" is anachronistic and that a fully formed religion arguably appears only in the second through fourth centuries. He says he kept the title on purpose, to make readers ask what distinguishes Paul from Jesus, John the Baptizer, and James, the brother of Jesus, who led the movement after Jesus. He says the period from about 30 CE into the 50s is hard to reconstruct, because our main sources are Paul's letters and later texts that post-date it.

Tabor's method for the pre-Pauline material is to expand the Q document by taking what Matthew and Luke share and that is not from Mark. He illustrates with Jesus' love-your-neighbor teaching and with Luke's parable of the Pharisee and the tax collector, which he reads as Jesus' own account of justification. He then argues that Paul, though he diverges from Jesus, came out of the Essene/Nazarene milieu. Tabor cites Paul's "children of light" language and his reference to going into the "Arava" after his vision, which he treats as the Dead Sea Rift region, not Arabia. He also points to Paul's apocalyptic urgency about marriage and practice, and to Romans 14 on diet and days.

Tabor draws his firmest line at justification by grace through faith and the substitutionary blood atonement that he sees built on it. He contrasts this with Romans 2 ("doers" of Torah), Habakkuk 2:4, and Genesis 15:6, which he reads through a rabbi's eyes. He concludes that "other things that Paul said they do reflect the Ebianite way," but he presents this as his reading, not settled fact. The clip ends there, with music, and without further argument.

### Key Teachings

### Paul as a product of the Essene/Nazarene milieu, not an outsider to it
Tabor says he is "not really a Paul Basher," for two reasons. The first is that Paul "comes from the Essen Nazarene," which the transcript garbles and which the context identifies as Essene/Nazarene. He notes Paul was a Pharisee who became a "follower of the way." As evidence he cites 1 Thessalonians 5 ("children of light") and Paul's statement that after his vision he went into "Arabia," which Tabor says was translated as "the Arava." Tabor insists the Arava is the Dead Sea Rift Valley where Qumran lies, and that Isaiah 40's "prepare the way in the Arava" is the same region the movement occupied. He says Jesus' own wilderness period fits this pattern, and that Paul "was influenced by this group."

### Apocalyptic urgency shaping Paul's practical rulings
Tabor argues that Paul "is so convinced that the end is coming," and reads 1 Corinthians 7 ("the appointed time has grown very short") and Daniel 12 ("time of distress") as the source of Paul's advice against marriage. He sets this against Jesus' saying about woe to those nursing, and against the practical instructions he says Paul gave, such as women asking questions at home. His point is that these were situational rulings for a short interval, and that later Christians "universalized it and made ironclad laws for all time." He ties the same logic to Romans 14, where one person eats meat and another does not, and one observes a day and another does not, and Paul says "let each be persuaded in his own mind."

### Justification by grace through faith as the line Tabor will not cross
This is where Tabor says he must "draw the line." He treats justification by grace through faith, dependent on the shed blood of Jesus as a sacrificial atonement, as the feature he cannot accept, and he argues that substitutionary blood atonement "based upon the temple system" is an "inroad" and a "heresy" to the groups he studies, who trace a tradition "from the prophets" back to Moses. He notes that the suffering servant of Isaiah 53 may have been applied to Jesus, but says there are four suffering servant songs, and that Jesus' call was to become a suffering servant as well. He quotes Dietrich Bonhoeffer's line that when Jesus calls a man, "he calls him to die."

### Romans 1-3 read through Torah and a rabbi's objections
Tabor says that Romans 1 and 2 are "totally fine," and that Romans 2 ("those who hear and do the Torah will be justified... the doers as well") matches the Essene emphasis on doing. Romans 3's "none is righteous" is the turn, in his reading, toward needing the blood. He describes a classroom exercise asking what a rabbi would say to Paul after Romans 3 and 4. His rabbi would say Paul misquotes Habakkuk, whose "just person will live by faith" is not about a sacrificial death, and would read Genesis 15:6 as Abraham putting his faith in God, trusting God's word about a child. Tabor cites the New Oxford Annotated Bible notes for a similar translation.

### Jesus' teaching on justification, through the expanded Q and Luke's parable
Tabor explains how he expands Q: he takes Matthew and Luke's shared material not found in Mark, removes each evangelist's own material, and keeps what remains as a core. He gives the love-your-neighbor saying followed by the "who is my neighbor" story as an example. He then reads Luke's parable of two men at the temple: the Pharisee who thanks God he is not like others and the tax collector who will not look to heaven and says "God be merciful to me a sinner." Tabor says "that's not the way of salvation" as the Pharisee tells it, and that Jesus' verdict was that the tax collector went home justified. He presents this as Jesus' teaching that Paul's later formulation departs from.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **That "Christianity" existed as a full religion before Paul:** Tabor says the term is anachronistic and that a distinct new religion called Christianity arguably only takes shape in the second through fourth centuries. He uses the phrase "Christianity before Paul" deliberately, to prompt the question of what differs between Paul and those before him.
- **That Paul's "Arabia" means Saudi Arabia:** Tabor says Paul's reference is to the Arava, the Dead Sea Rift region, and that "it doesn't mean Saudi Arabia." He says it is the region Isaiah 40 names, and that Jesus' wilderness period was there too.
- **That Romans 3-4 teaches justification by the blood of Jesus as a substitutionary atonement:** Tabor says a rabbi would call this a misquotation of Habakkuk, and that Genesis 15:6 means Abraham put his faith in God rather than in any sacrifice. He says "this is not what the texts say."
- **That Paul's rulings on marriage and practice are timeless law:** He argues they were responses to an expected imminent end, and that later Christians "universalized" them into "ironclad laws for all time."
