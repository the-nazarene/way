## Key Teachings

### The significance lies in the early scrolls, not the total count
Tabor says the reason he shows the growing editions is "just that we have more scrolls," and then rejects that as the point ("Nope"). He argues that the central material for his comparison comes from the earliest published scrolls, which contain the "basic ideas and seeds of the movement." Later texts are useful and he wants them, but the movement was already established in the 1947–1950 material.

### The movement has been accessible for about seventy-five years
Tabor's last point is that the group's ideas have not been hidden or recently discovered. In his words, the movement has been around "since the 40s really of the previous century." This sets up his later argument, not developed in this clip, that the group can be used as a reference point for reading Jesus.
