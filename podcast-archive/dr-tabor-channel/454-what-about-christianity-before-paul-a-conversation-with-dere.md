## Summary

Derek Lambert opens by asking how the course title "Christianity Before Paul" squares with the common view that Paul invented Christianity. Tabor answers that the label is a compromise, since "it isn't really Christianity, but it is." He proposes that the movement was a Jewish, messianic and apocalyptic group within late Second Temple Judaism, with John the Baptist, Jesus, James the brother of Jesus, Mary (the mother of Jesus) and Mary Magdalene as participants. He suggests "Nazarene" or "Messianist" as names for it and notes that the Dead Sea Scroll community also used "the way" for itself. He argues that Paul came later and is best read as a derivative figure who, in Tabor's reading, had to defend his own revelation against the earlier leaders, citing Paul's curse on "the so-called pillars of the church" who disagree with him.

Tabor's main textual evidence is the claim that Jesus was a disciple of John the Baptist. He points to Jesus calling John "more than a prophet," which he reads through Deuteronomy 18:15-18 as a "prophet like Moses." He also cites a saying from a Hebrew text of Matthew (not the canonical Greek Matthew) that "among human beings, there's none greater than John." He argues that Paul's account in Acts of later baptism by Apollos and the Ephesian disciples shows a John-based baptism that predates Christian baptism. Tabor treats the Gospels' baptism scenes as evidence of a developing portrait in which Jesus becomes the central figure, moving from Mark's version to Matthew's. He says the Hebrew Matthew material and other sources form a "matrix" that he hopes to show is not a translation of Greek texts.

The talk is a promotional conversation for Tabor's course. Tabor ends by describing a planned additional lecture on archaeological evidence from his excavations, and then asks listeners to sign up. Lambert's comments praise the course as "evergreen" and compare Tabor's approach to Albert Schweitzer's. The transcript does not label speakers, so some closing lines may be Lambert's rather than Tabor's.

### Key Teachings

### Naming the movement
Tabor says no single label works. He rejects "Jesus movement before Paul" because the movement starts with John the Baptist, and "John the Baptist movement" because it includes James. He offers "Nazarene," a later name for the group, and "Messianist," meaning people who believe in the Messiah, linking it to modern "Messianic Jews." He describes the movement as "apocalyptic, messianic, and even eschatological," and says it resembles the Dead Sea Scroll community but was distinct. He explains that Josephus's term "Essene" was not used by the scroll community itself. In the scrolls the group calls itself "children of light," "new covenanters," "the poor ones," and "people of the way," which he says this movement also used.

### Paul as derivative and in conflict with the earlier leaders
Tabor argues that Paul is derivative of this earlier movement and that Paul's own letters show tension. He says Paul went to Jerusalem to meet "the original founders," claimed his gospel came by his own revelation, and then cursed those who disagreed, including "the so-called pillars of the church." Tabor reads this as Paul being "kind of upset" and trying to establish his authority. He contrasts Paul's position with the claims of James and Peter, who in Tabor's reading say that Paul never met Jesus, was not baptized by John, and never knew Mary, while they did. He notes that Mary Magdalene was at the cross and the burial, and says Paul's relation to these figures is different. Tabor attributes the idea that Paul and the earlier leaders were in opposition to a 19th-century scholar, F.C. Baur, and says "fundamentalist harmonizers" would deny the conflict.

### Jesus as a disciple of John the Baptist
Tabor's central historical claim is that Jesus was part of John's movement. He says Jesus was baptized by John, and that this is evidence for Jesus's place as a follower. He also points to Jesus's statement that John is "more than a prophet," which Tabor says means "a new Moses," and he cites Deuteronomy 18:15-18 about a prophet like Moses. He reports that John answers that he is "the voice preparing the way for the Lord," and says Jesus joins "the preparation movement for the way" because the group expected God to return.

### The baptism scenes as a developing picture
Tabor reads the four Gospels' baptism accounts as showing change over time. In Mark, Jesus goes to John without objection. Later accounts add objections that Jesus has no sins and should not need baptism. Tabor also points to later sayings, such as Jesus's "I have to decrease, you have to increase," which he reads as Jesus being made the central figure. He argues that if Jesus were greater than John, he would contradict the saying "none greater than John."

### The Hebrew Matthew saying
Tabor cites a saying from a Hebrew version of Matthew: "among those born of women, meaning human beings, not angels... there's none greater than John." He says the canonical Matthew does not contain it, but church fathers knew it was there. He argues this Hebrew text is not a translation of the Greek and that it was "interpolated and passed on," but that the material it keeps "comport[s] and fit[s] with other texts." He describes his method as a "matrix" of sources that he compares against one another.

### Baptism in Acts and John's message
Tabor argues that the baptism in Acts shows the movement's earlier practice. He points to Apollos, whom he calls a preacher of "the Christianity before Paul," and to disciples of John in Ephesus whom Paul then instructs. He asks who baptized the twelve apostles and says they could not have had a "Christian baptism," because John's message did not mention the death or blood of Christ. He says John preached "repentance toward God" and "baptism for the remission of sins," as described by Josephus. Tabor acknowledges that Acts may not be historical on this point.

### Archaeology (promised, not presented)
Tabor says he spent over 30 years excavating in Israel, including Jerusalem, the Galilee, the Transjordan, Masada and Qumran. He says there will be an additional lecture on archaeological evidence for the movement before Paul, and says the answer will be "big." ## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Paul invented Christianity.** Lambert raises this at the start; Tabor says he has not changed his view that Paul is later and derivative, and that the earlier movement existed before him.
- **Paul and the earlier leaders agreed.** Tabor says Paul's own language and the reported response of James and Peter show tension, and that harmonizing readings are a mistake.
- **Jesus was superior to John.** Tabor argues Jesus called John the greatest born of women and was a disciple of John, not his superior.
- **Essenes is the name the Dead Sea group used.** Tabor says "Essene" comes from Josephus and the scroll community did not use it for itself.
- **The twelve apostles had a Christian baptism.** Tabor says John's baptism, which lacked reference to Christ's death, would have been their baptism.
