Tabor's argument rests on several sources read against each other. ## Key Teachings

### Jesus had a brother, and that point anchors the historical case
Tabor calls the brother of Jesus "probably the key thing in the course" (03:41). He says he is not doing mythicism. He notes that "brother" might be read as cousin, friend, or even a cosmic brother, but argues that the texts he surveys treat James as a real family member. Paul is cited as saying he met James (04:24), though the talk does not give a verse for this. Tabor's line of reasoning: if Paul's letters are historical, and Paul met James, then James is historical, and so is the figure of Jesus behind him. He frames this as a chain: "Now, if Paul's a myth, all his letters are myth, James is myth, Jesus is myth, then you got me."

### Eusebius reluctantly confirms James and labels the Ebionites
Tabor reads Eusebius, "the father of church history," as a hostile witness. Eusebius "can't stand the Ebionites" and tags them with a name that Tabor says mocks them ("the poor ones") because of their "poor" views: that Jesus is not God, not begotten by the Holy Spirit, and not born of a virgin (05:50). Tabor says the Ebionite name goes back to the Dead Sea Scrolls, with the "poor ones" and the "teacher of righteousness" (05:07 to 05:50). In Tabor's reading, Eusebius "had to tell you" about James in earlier chapters but seems reluctant to do so, and he states that the church fathers "do not want to admit James is anything" (04:24). Eusebius's account has Peter and John choosing James to lead, and Tabor says they "weren't jealous" (13:18).

### Gospel of Thomas saying 12 and the Q overlap
Tabor cites Gospel of Thomas saying 12 as a tradition that predates its Gnostic layers, and says about a third of it "sounds a lot like the Q source" (06:41). In the saying, the disciples ask where they should go after Jesus leaves them, and Jesus answers "Go to James the Just, for whom heaven and earth came to be" (07:13). Tabor declines to explain the final phrase in the talk, saying it is a Jewish code that can be deciphered from other sources and that he covers it in the course (07:34).

### Acts 15 and Acts 21 show James as the decision-maker
Tabor reads Acts 15 as an assembly in which Peter and Paul speak, and then "an unidentified guy" named James issues the decision (10:36 to 11:20). He notes that Acts does not identify James here in the way Eusebius does. In Acts 21, Tabor says James questions Paul, reporting that Paul is teaching Jews to abandon the Torah, and asks him to pay for men undergoing a Nazirite vow to show that the charge is false (12:16 to 12:40). Tabor says he does not know whether this happened, and that the story's point is the authority James holds. He calls the setting "so rabbinic," with James as "the rebbe" and "chief rabbi of the Nazarene movement" (10:36). He also says Paul is a ring leader, but in the "top tier of three, four, five," with James above him.

### James as "bishop of the bishops" in the Ebionite texts
Tabor says that other texts describe James as bishop of Jerusalem, and that "the Ebionite texts say, 'He's the bishop of the bishops'" (14:07). He glosses this as "the first Pope," noting that "Pope" just means papa, father, and "bishop" means shepherd or overseer (14:16). He then asks who would have tried to elevate James, and answers that no text in his survey is trying to put James in charge, except "the internal texts of the Ebionites" (14:39). In his view, the hostile sources want to remove James, and the sources that love him keep him in place.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Jesus is a myth (mythicism).** Lambert says he once held this view, then moved toward a historical Jesus because some texts still preserve historical details. Tabor says he is "not going to do mythicism" and that the sources he cites presuppose a real James.
- **Jesus was an insignificant nobody from Galilee.** Lambert says he used to ask why a "nobody from Galilee" would be deified, and now sees Jesus as "clearly an important figure" with popular support.
- **"Brother" must mean cousin or friend.** Tabor acknowledges these readings but says the consistent presentation in the sources is of a biological brother, which Eusebius admits.
- **James, the son of Zebedee, is the James of the early church.** Tabor warns that Mark's James and John are a different pair from the brother of Jesus, and that students confuse them.
