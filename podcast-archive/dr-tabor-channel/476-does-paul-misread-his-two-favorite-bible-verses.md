## Key Teachings

### Genesis 15:6 does not describe a sinner forgiven by faith
Tabor doubts that the Hebrew text "even says or implies that Abraham's a great sinner and wicked, but God forgives him because he believes in God." He says there is "nothing in the context that indicates that." His preferred rendering is that "Abraham trusted God and counted him as righteous." He does not cite manuscripts or other translations in this clip. His argument rests on his reading of the Hebrew and its context.

### The context is God's faithfulness to his promise
Tabor says the reason Abraham trusts God is that "God's righteous" and "he's going to fulfill his promise." He points to the surrounding details: a son promised to a man who is 99 and a wife past menopause. On his reading, the faith in view is trust in a promise-keeping God under apparent impossibility.

### Habakkuk 2:4 is parallel and concerns the righteous
Tabor says "You see how similar that would be to Habakkuk?" He reads Habakkuk 2:4 ("he who through faith is righteous will live") as a description of "a righteous person trusting God and having faith and not giving up even though it would seem that there would be a lot of doubt." He says nothing in the context of Habakkuk is about "a wicked person getting saved by faith."

### Paul uses the verses as doctrinal pillars rather than in context
Tabor says "Paul isn't interested in the context" and that Paul is "making these points" with "these two pillars of the faith." In Tabor's words, Habakkuk 2:4 teaches that "not by your own deeds" is how one lives, and that trusting God is counted "as your righteousness." He concludes that "the whole gospel depends on these two principles." Tabor does not say here what Paul's letters say or where in them these verses appear.
