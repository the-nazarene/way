## Summary

Tabor asks how Daniel 9's "70 weeks" prophecy should be read, and why it has been used so often to calculate the time of the end. He states that the prophecy is "by far" the most influential in the Bible for such calculations. He argues that the most natural reading is historical. Daniel is meditating on Jeremiah's 70 years of exile, and the prophecy concerns the restoration and rebuilding of Jerusalem after 587 BCE. He reads the 490 years as running to the desecration under Antiochus IV Epiphanes around 164 BCE, with the 1 Maccabees account as the best evidence. Tabor says "most Scholars" would take this reading.

His main claim is that each later attempt to extend the 490-year clock is a reinterpretation made after the predicted end failed to arrive. He traces this through the Dead Sea Scrolls community, the Jesus movement, Paul, Josephus, William Miller and the Adventists, the Jehovah's Witnesses, a "70 jubilees" scheme, and finally John Nelson Darby's dispensational gap, which he says was popularized by the Scofield Bible. He presents these as a history of ideas, not as a verdict on which is correct, though he is openly skeptical of date-setting.

The talk ends with a preview of the next episode, which will cover Daniel 11 and 12 and how Tabor thinks those chapters were extended. Tabor carries the entire talk; there is no guest.

## Key Teachings

### The 490-year structure and the historical reading
Tabor explains that Daniel 9 speaks of "70 heptads," which most interpreters take as 70 weeks of years, or 490 years. He divides it into three parts: seven weeks (49 years), then 62 weeks (434 years), then a final week. The first part ends with "an anointed Prince" tied to the rebuilding of the temple and walls around 520-515 BCE, which he connects to Zechariah 3 and the high priest Joshua (whom he calls "yahushua"). The 62-week period ends with an anointed one being "cut off." Tabor reads this as the murder of the high priest Onias III, a descendant of Joshua, by Antiochus IV. The "desolator" then sets up the "desolating sacrilege," which Tabor identifies with an altar to Zeus and a pig sacrificed on the altar, and he points to 1 Maccabees 1 for the account. He says the clock therefore "ends" with the desecration and the Maccabean period.

### Reading the "anointed one" in context
Tabor argues that "just reading it in context" the anointed figures are priests in the Second Temple setting, not a figure to be identified with Jesus. He says Christians "later try to make this Jesus," and that he will cover that interpretation later in the series. He also corrects a common assumption about the building in the video: he notes that the Dome of the Rock is what many people mistake for "the mosque of Omar," and that the Temple Mount is where Herod's Temple stood.

### The Dead Sea Scrolls community's reapplication
Tabor cites the Damascus Document, column 1, and the Qumran Cave 11 text he calls "melis cedc" (Melchizedek). The community expected something "in the first week of the 10th Jubilee," which he computes as seven years into the final 49-year period, so that "you're getting very close to the end." He reads the Damascus Document's totals (390 years of wrath, then 20 years of wandering, then a teacher whose rule lasted about 40 years, followed by about 40 more years) as a way of fitting the 490 years to their own time. When the end did not come, he says, they "said that all the prophecies have been fulfilled we were right but God has extended the time," and he links this to 2 Peter.

### The Jesus movement, Paul, and Josephus
Tabor argues that Jesus, as recorded in Mark, proclaimed "the time is fulfilled," and that he expected the sign of the end to be the desolating sacrilege. He suggests Jesus knew the Maccabean story (he cites the Hanukkah reference in John 10) but thought the prophecy could "be applied to a coming one yet in the future." Paul, in 1 Corinthians 7 ("the appointed time has grown very near") and 2 Thessalonians 2 (the "lawless one" in the temple), is said to be "moving it Forward," and Tabor suggests Paul had the emperor Caligula's 41 CE attempt to set up a statue in mind. Josephus, in Tabor's reading, identified the Jewish War's ambiguous "Oracle" as Daniel 9 and applied it to Vespasian, who "desolated the temple" and whose Jewish-Roman war ended with the destruction of Jerusalem. Tabor says Hadrian later rebuilt the city as Aelia Capitolina, which he takes as further evidence that the prophecy's restoration never happened.

### Later extensions: Miller, the Adventists, and the Jehovah's Witnesses
Tabor describes William Miller's combination of the 2300 days (covered in an earlier episode) with the 70 weeks, starting from Ezra's 457 BCE and placing the Messiah's death in the midpoint of the final week. He says Miller's scheme led to an 1844 end date, which Ellen White, the Adventist "prophetess," reinterpreted. He also describes a Jehovah's Witnesses calculation that places the 70 weeks in the 30s CE. Both, he says, do not fit the text well. He also describes a "70 jubilees" scheme (3430 years from the Exodus, which he dates to 1446 BCE) that he notes was suggested by a friend and that had been extended to the year of recording.

### Darby's gap and the dispensational reading
Tabor says that in the 1830s John Nelson Darby argued for seven dispensations and an indefinite gap. The 69 weeks end with the Messiah being "cut off," and "prophetic time stopped," so that the final seven-year week waits for the church age to end. He credits the Scofield Reference Bible (1909, still published in 1917 printing as he notes) with popularizing the idea, and says a book by Tim LaHaye (his transcript spells the name garbled) sold widely and was adapted into films. He concludes that this view "made it infinitely elastic," so that the Rapture is the next event on the calendar. Tabor criticizes the dispensational model as having no way to be disproven, and says that the historical reading (fulfilled under Antiochus) is the one that makes sense, while acknowledging that the end "didn't come" and so later readers keep adding.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **The Temple Mount building is the "mosque of Omar."** Tabor says many people misunderstand this; the building on the Temple Mount is the Dome of the Rock.
- **The "anointed one" cut off in Daniel 9 is Jesus.** Tabor says Christians later read it this way, but in context he reads the figures as Second Temple priests and the cut-off figure as Onias III.
- **Daniel 9 is an unfinished prophecy awaiting a far-future Rapture and Tribulation.** Tabor argues the dispensational gap was introduced by Darby in the 1830s, and that it makes the prophecy "infinitely elastic" with no way to disprove it.
- **The 490-year clock was never "wrong," just extended.** Tabor presents the extensions (Dead Sea Scrolls group, Paul, Adventists, Jehovah's Witnesses, Darby) as later reinterpretations made after the predicted end failed to arrive.
