## Summary

Tabor continues a list of parallels between the Dead Sea Scroll community and the Jesus movement, following an earlier talk in the series. He describes this set as "top four" while saying there may be eight. The items are: the "Children of Light," a self-description he says has no parallel elsewhere in Jewish literature except in the Gospels and in Paul's letter to the Thessalonians; initiation by water immersion; a teacher called the Teacher of Righteousness, in whose teaching one is saved; a teacher expected to be "a prophet like Moses"; and the Holy Spirit as a present reality in the community.

## Key Teachings

### 1. Children of Light
Tabor says the community calls its members "Children of Light," with the contrast "Children of Darkness," and that no other known Jewish group uses this phrasing. He says the Gospels and Paul (1 Thessalonians) use the same contrast, and he calls this a major parallel. He sets it beside the Pharisees and Sadducees, whom he says never used it.

### 2. Immersion as initiation
Tabor says the Community Rule describes repentance followed by immersion in water as the way into the group, unlike the routine mikvah used in Judaism. He says Qumran has several immersion pools, some for water collection and some with steps, and that the Hebrew word for this rite fits John the Baptist's "immerser" role.

### 3. The Teacher of Righteousness
Tabor says the group followed a teacher called the Teacher of Righteousness, and that the phrase is better read as "the true teacher." He quotes the group's claim that members will be "saved... by your faith in the teacher of righteousness," and says Jesus's sayings about being "the way and the truth" use the same kind of language.

### 4. "A prophet like Moses"
Tabor says the Scrolls describe their teacher as "a prophet like Moses," which he links to Deuteronomy 15 and to Stephen's speech in Acts 7 about a prophet like Moses.

### 5. The Holy Spirit in the community
Tabor says the group believed the Holy Spirit (the "Spirit of holiness") dwelt among them in their gatherings and assembly hall. He links this to Paul's language about the Spirit being present where believers meet.

### Yoke of the Torah
In passing, Tabor compares the group's strict Torah interpretation with Jesus's invitation to take on "my yoke," which he reads as a teacher's interpretation of the Torah.

- **Misconception:** Terms like "saved by faith" and "the Teacher" are purely Christian salvation language. **Tabor's correction:** He says these are Jewish usages from the Scrolls, which Christian readers have read in later terms.
- **Misconception:** Josephus's account of the Essenes is a full description of the group. **Tabor's correction:** He says Josephus leaves out the children of light, the teacher, and the prophet-like-Moses language.
