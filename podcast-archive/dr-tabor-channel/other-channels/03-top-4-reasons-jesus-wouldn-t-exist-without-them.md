## Key Teachings

### 1. "New Covenant"
Tabor says the Scroll group calls itself the people of the New Covenant, which he links to Jeremiah 31:31 and to the "cup of the New Covenant" in Mark. He says no other Jewish group he knows of from the Exile to 70 CE uses this self-designation, and that the group's self-description dates to about 150 BCE.

### 2. "Preparing the way in the wilderness"
Tabor says the Community Rule opens with a statement of purpose to prepare the way in the wilderness, drawn from Isaiah 40:3, and that the group moved to the area by the Dead Sea (the Arava). He ties this to John the Baptist's self-description in Mark 1.

### 3. Apocalyptic expectation
Tabor says the group, like many in the period, read Daniel's prophecies (the 490-year period in Daniel 9 and the vision in Daniel 7) as pointing to the end. He quotes Paul's "the appointed time... has grown very short" (1 Corinthians 7) and Mark 13's "this generation will not pass" as the same expectation. He notes that the expected victory over Rome did not come.

### 4. Teacher and two Messiahs
Tabor says the group followed a "Teacher" (the true teacher, not simply "teacher of righteousness") and expected two other Messiahs, a priest and a king, "the Messiahs of Aaron and Israel." He links this to the sons of Zebedee's request in Mark 10 to sit at Jesus's right and left.

### "People of the way"
Tabor says the group called itself "people of the way," which he compares with the Acts phrase "the Way" as a name for the Jesus movement.
