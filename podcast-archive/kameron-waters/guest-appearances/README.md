# guest-appearances

Long-form interviews and debates where **Kam is the guest on someone else's channel**, as opposed to `../`, which holds the uploads from his own channel.

These matter for a different reason than his own videos do. On his channel he sets the frame; as a guest he gets asked things he didn't choose, by hosts and opponents with their own agenda, and he answers in real time. That makes these the best available record of **how the argument actually goes when it's contested** — which objections keep coming back, which answers hold, and which ones he'd want back.

Requested by Matt (2026-10-16) with a specific purpose: so that when Kam opens this repo, it already knows what he has said in public. His own prior arguments should come back to him as retrievable material, the same way Aaron's 66 podcast episodes do.

**Twelve appearances, ~22 hours, ~257,000 words.** All timestamped.

### The four worth reading first

- **01** is the full Voice of Reason debate. **02 is a ten-minute clip of one exchange from the same matchup**, uploaded separately five weeks later — don't mistake the short for the whole thing. The short is where the death-before-the-fall objection appears, which `questions/MINING-2026-10-debates.md` flags as a question the repo has no card for.
- **08** is a joint appearance with Dr. James Tabor on the Ebionites — the closest thing here to a primary-source conversation. Standing rule applies: Tabor's positions get cited by paraphrase from his published books, **never quoted from an auto-caption like this one**.
- **09** is a theologian cross-examining him on Genesis 9, the Hebrew Gospel of Matthew, the fish miracles, and his use of the church fathers. The host later posted "Did I misrepresent Kameron Waters?", so there was a dispute afterward. Best single source here for objections.
- **11** is directly on the Hebrew-Gospel question, where the repo's own numbers currently trace to a book it does not hold.

## Caveats

Same as the rest of `podcast-archive/`: **auto-generated YouTube captions.** No speaker labels (`>>` appears only where YouTube marks a change of speaker), so in a two- or three-way conversation you have to infer who is talking from content. Names, Hebrew and Greek transliterations, and technical terms are frequently wrong — the mining report documents several places where a caption garbled a scholar's name badly enough that it couldn't be identified.

**These are pointers, not proof.** Cite a moment as `kam-guest-01 @ 41:12` and check it against the audio — the YouTube link plus `&t=<seconds>` goes straight there. Nothing in this folder is a source for a claim about what Jesus taught; for that, see the source hierarchy in `CLAUDE.md` and the file list in `VERIFIED-SOURCES.md`.
