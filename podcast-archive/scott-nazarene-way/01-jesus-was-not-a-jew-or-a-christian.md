## Key Teachings

### 1. Jesus as a Nazarene: Matthew 2:23 and the *netzer*

Sheeper says the first evidence for Jesus being a Nazarene comes from Matthew 2:23. He reports that scholars have searched the Hebrew Bible (which he calls the Tanakh) for a prophecy that the Messiah would be called a Nazarene and have found none. What they found instead is Isaiah 11:1, which speaks of a *netzer* (branch) that he says forms the root of "Nazareth." He reads this as the branch of David. He then points to the genealogy in Matthew 1, which traces Jesus through David and ends with Joseph. Sheeper says this is odd if Jesus was not Joseph's biological son. He also notes that Mark has no genealogy in its first two chapters and that Mark opens with John the Baptist. Sheeper says the Nazarenes were not Virgin-birth believers and that they held Jesus was born naturally to Joseph, who belonged to the *netzer*/branch of David. He also says that Jesus "became the son of man" at his baptism (Mark 1), which he ties to Mark being "the earliest written gospel."

### 2. "People of the Way" and the name Nazarene

Sheeper argues that the followers of Jesus called themselves "people of the Way," a phrase he says he found in Acts. He says outsiders called them Nazarenes, while insiders used "the Way." He credits James Tabor with the point that the followers of John the Baptist and Jesus used "the Way" before "Nazarene" or "Christian" came into use. He traces the phrase to Hebrew Bible prophecy about a messenger who will "pave the way," and he presents a two-Messiah model in which John prepared the way for Jesus as king. He says Acts should be read without what he calls a "Pauline lens," which he claims smooths over the Jewish character of the early movement.

### 3. Epiphanius and Jerome on the Nazarenes

Sheeper presents Epiphanius as a bishop and "patristic church father" who wrote the *Panarion*, cataloguing about 80 heresies. He says one of these was the religion of Jesus and his original followers, the Nazarenes, and that this is one reason we know about them. He reads Epiphanius as saying that the Nazarenes were fluent in Hebrew, read the law, prophets and writings in Hebrew, and "are different from the Jews and different from the Christians." Sheeper says Epiphanius criticized them for being "fettered" (chained) to the law: circumcision, the Sabbath and the rest. Sheeper reverses this, arguing that Jesus was himself "fettered" to the law, and that the charge is an accidental endorsement. He then cites Jerome, whom he says wrote that the Nazarenes "will be both Jews and Christians" but are "neither Jews nor Christians." Sheeper notes that some scholars have found Jerome stretches the truth and that Jerome's sources need caution. He presents the patristic writers as sincere but "swayed" by what he calls Paul's religion.

### 4. Persecution from Jews and Christians

Sheeper claims the Nazarenes were banned from synagogues in Jerusalem soon after Jesus's death, which he says is why they met in houses. He describes a daily Jewish curse, which he attributes to Samuel the younger in the second century, that called for "no hope for the apostates the nazarenes" and for "all the sectaries" to perish. He acknowledges that later Jewish communities suffered severe persecution at Christian hands, and he warns against using the curse to blame Jews alone. He argues that after the fall of the Temple in 70 CE and the Bar Kokhba revolt, Romans and Christians lumped Jews and Nazarenes together. He says the Christians survived because they allied themselves with the Roman government. He cites Hugh Schonfield (transcribed as "Shanfield") for the claim that by the end of the fourth century the Nazarenes were "wholly discarded by the Catholic church" and reserved for heresy.

### 5. Three core Nazarene beliefs

Sheeper gives three beliefs he says the Nazarenes held (he says there were about 25 in total, to be taken up in later sermons):

- **Observance of the law:** The Nazarenes kept the Sabbath on Saturday, observed the Ten Commandments and the Hebrew Bible, and wore zizit. He calls Sunday observance "a pagan Roman idea" and says the Hebrew Bible is "The Testament of Jesus."
- **Two greatest commandments:** Sheeper reads Matthew 22 (love God with all your heart, soul and mind; love your neighbor as yourself) as the two highest priorities, with "all the others fall[ing] under them." He says Jesus also said he came "to fulfill" the law (Matthew 5). He describes the Temple sellers and money changers as profiting from animal sacrifice, which he presents as one reason Jesus was angry.
- **Action over faith, with focus on the poor:** Sheeper says James the Just taught that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone, and that the Nazarenes prioritized the *ebionim* ("the poor ones"). He identifies the Ebionites with the Nazarenes' emphasis on the poor.

### 6. James versus Paul

Sheeper presents James the Just as Jesus's brother and the leader of the Jerusalem community, alongside Peter and John, the "three pillars." He quotes James's teaching on works and faith (James 2) and contrasts it with what he calls Paul's "faith alone" message. He says Paul had not known Jesus "in the flesh," and that Paul's message undermined the importance of Jesus's earthly teaching. He says Paul was summoned back to Jerusalem to answer to James, and that James allowed uncircumcised Gentiles to be accepted if they still followed the Torah. He also says James told Paul to prioritize the Ebionites. Sheeper dismisses the Catholic "first pope" reading of Matthew 16 and says Peter had a wife who travelled with him.

- **Jesus was a Jew:** Sheeper says Jesus was neither a Jew nor a Christian, and that the Nazarenes were a distinct group from both.
- **Christianity as Jesus's religion:** He argues that the Christianity of the Catholic Church and of later branches descends from Paul's teaching, not from Jesus's own practice.
- **Faith alone as Jesus's message:** He argues that James's "works, not faith alone" reflects Jesus's message, and that Paul's emphasis on faith alone departed from it.
- **Peter as the first pope:** He says Matthew 16 is taken "out of context" and that Peter was married and reported to James.
- **Sunday as the Sabbath:** He argues the Sabbath was kept on Saturday by the Nazarenes and that Sunday observance is "a pagan Roman idea."
- **Jesus abolished the law:** He says Jesus came "to fulfill" the law and that the Catholic Bishops did not observe it.
