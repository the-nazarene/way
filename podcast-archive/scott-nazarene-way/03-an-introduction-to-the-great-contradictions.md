## Summary

Scott, presenting as the Nazarene Way Church, asks how a reader should choose between pairs of New Testament verses that he says contradict each other. He sets out three "great contradictions": faith versus works, the status of the Torah, and whether salvation is for all or only for the lost sheep of the House of Israel. In each pair, the verse on the left is attributed to Paul and the verse on the right to Jesus. He concludes that "we choose the path on the right."

His evidence is textual: he reads paired verses aloud, most without naming the book, and reasons from them. He also appeals to the Didache's "two ways" and to the teachings of James the brother of Jesus, whom he presents as favouring the poor. He does not cite manuscripts, dates of composition or scholarship beyond brief remarks on the word "Christian" and the Nicene Creed. He says he will "elucidate" the contradictions in later sermons, so this is an introduction.

The talk ends with his "Nazarene three": love of God with heart, soul and mind; love of neighbour as oneself (which he links to the Torah's 613 commandments and the tzitzit); and active service to the poor, rather than faith alone or attending church. It opens with a Christmas segment in which he argues that Christmas is pagan and that the Bethlehem story is a myth.

## Key Teachings

### Faith versus works
Scott sets "we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law" and "by Grace you have been saved through faith... not a result of works" against "if you would enter life... keep the Commandments" and "not everyone who says to me Lord Lord will enter the kingdom of heaven but the one who does the will of my father." He argues these cannot both be right, and that the one who follows God's will enters the kingdom.

### The Torah: abolished or fulfilled?
He sets "for Christ is the end of the Torah" against "do not think that I have come to abolish the Torah or the prophets," noting that the Hebrew Bible has three parts: Torah, Prophets and Writings. He reads the second as an instruction to keep following the Torah. He also says the term "Old Testament" is a Catholic label and that the proper term is Tanakh.

### Salvation for all or only the lost sheep
He pairs "there is neither Jew nor Greek... for you are all one in Christ Jesus" with "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the House of Israel." He says that Christians, through Paul's teachings and later Catholic developments, are now the lost sheep who need to be restored to Jesus's teaching.

### Resolving the contradictions: Paul versus Jesus
Scott's resolution is that in every pair the left-hand verse is Paul's and the right-hand verse is Jesus's. He calls Paul "the father and inventor" of Christianity, says Christianity was "essentially invented after the death of Jesus," and says "Paul never knew Jesus." He cites the Didache as describing "two ways," one of which is the way of Jesus. He calls this group the "people of the way."

### The Nazarene three
He gives three principles. The first, from Matthew, is the greatest commandment: love God with all your heart, soul and mind, and love your neighbour as yourself. He says "on these two Commandments hang all the law and Prophets" shows Jesus did not abolish the law. He counts the 613 commandments as represented by the tzitzit, with four tassels standing for the four elements of the greatest commandment. He admits that "no one's perfect" and that no one can keep all 613.

### James the just and the poor
Scott identifies James as Jesus's brother, head of the Jerusalem church, and says Peter ("sephus AKA Simon AKA Peter," which I have corrected to Cephas in prose) and John were his lieutenants. He argues that the Catholic Church has tried to recast Jesus's brothers as cousins or half-brothers. He reads James's line about God choosing "the poor ones in the world," and says the Hebrew term is *ebionites*, which he links to the later Nazarenes and Ebionites. He says that Jesus and James always prioritised the poor, and that "not by faith alone but through acts" one experiences Jesus's true nature.

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Scripture is internally consistent.** Scott argues that if the Bible is the inerrant word of God, it cannot contain contradictions, so readers must choose. He says apologists "hand wave" the conflicts by appealing to context.
- **Christianity is the teaching of Jesus.** He argues Christianity was "essentially invented after the death of Jesus" by Paul, who "never knew Jesus."
- **Jesus's brothers were cousins or half-brothers.** He says the Catholic Church has tried to recast James and the other "brothers" in Mark this way, and that the evidence supports real brothers.
- **The Hebrew Bible is the "Old Testament."** He says the term is a Catholic label and that the proper name is the Tanakh.
- **Christmas is a Christian holiday.** In the opening segment he says it has pagan origins in Saturnalia, that Santa Claus and the Christmas tree date only to the 1800s, and that the Bethlehem story is a myth.

## Connection to the Repo's Questions

- **What Jesus taught vs. what Paul taught:** The talk's central thesis is that each contradiction pairs a Pauline verse with a Jesus verse, and that readers should follow Jesus. Scott supplies no textual analysis to show that the verses come from different authors.
- **James and the Jerusalem community:** He presents James the just as Jesus's brother and head of the Jerusalem church, with Cephas and John as his lieutenants, and as the voice for the poor.
- **Nazarenes and Ebionites:** He links the Hebrew word for "poor ones" to the Ebionites, and says the Nazarenes later "branched into" that group. He gives no sources for this.
- **Torah and diet/sacrifice/nonviolence:** The Torah section and the greatest commandment bear on Torah observance. He says the 613 commandments are represented by the tzitzit, but he does not argue for their present-day observance beyond "we respect them and strive for them."
- **The Kingdom:** He ties entry into the kingdom of heaven to doing God's will rather than to faith alone, and to serving the poor.
- **How the gospels and canon were formed:** Only glancingly, through his claims about *christianos*, the Nicene Creed and the Catholic Church's role.
