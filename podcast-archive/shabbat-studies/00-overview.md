## The Series in Brief

Shabbat Studies is a run of Saturday-night livestreams recorded at the close of Shabbat in Charlotte, North Carolina, in the autumn and early winter of 2023 (the hosts' son is a newborn throughout, and the recap is dated December 23). Jen, who came to faith in 2013 from a non-Christian home in Sweden, interviews and reads alongside Chris, who grew up in a Christian school and church, worked in the Los Angeles entertainment and worship-music world, and has been researching the questions in the series since about 2015. Both keep the Sabbath, the feasts, and the food laws, have been vegetarian since 2018, and describe their method as nine years of prayer, translation comparison, interlinears, and reading the first-century texts the Roman church set aside. The format is mostly Chris working from notes for two to four hours while Jen handles the baby, reads the chat, and adds her own readings (the Odes of Solomon, Sirach, Jeremiah 2:13); the last hour of most sessions is audience Q&A.

The series has one through-line, stated in the recap and restated at the opening of the Paul chapter: Jesus's own salvation teaching (keep the commandments, repent, the kingdom is at hand now) stands on its own, while Paul's gospel (confess, believe, saved by grace through faith, Jesus's death as a blood atonement) stands or falls with the Levitical sacrificial system, and the hosts hold that system was never ordained by God. The chapters are ordered to build that case: who Jesus said he was (Messiah, Prophet of Truth, son of God as the righteous are, not God); how the Bible was assembled and why it should be read as inspired but not infallible; the prophets against the priests on sacrifice, with a logistics argument from the Torah's own numbers; four sessions on Paul as a false apostle placed in the canon, in their view, as a Deuteronomy 13 test; and a recap with Q&A on practice (which laws to keep, circumcision, tithing, the calendar).

The hosts frame the whole thing as an audit rather than a demolition. They say the Bible is the most important book on earth, that the Holy Spirit rather than the manuscripts preserves the truth, that most of Paul's sentences are true, and that nobody should take their word for any of it ("be Bereans"). They read each author separately rather than letting one book interpret another, and they describe themselves as following the first-century Nazarene/Ebionite community, whom every establishment of the day called heretics. Where this repo and the hosts share ground, which is most of the way, the chapter notes record it; where they differ (Christ consciousness, mysticism, the Edomite-priesthood frame, Eisenman's dating, several specific citations) the notes and the combined list below say so.

## The Main Arguments

### Who Jesus is

- By his own words, Jesus is not God. The hosts' texts are John 7:16 and 14:24 ("my doctrine is not mine but his that sent me"), John 14:12 ("greater works than these"), Gethsemane and the cry from the cross (not self-addressed), and John 15:1 and the parable of the tenants (Jesus the vine and the son, the Father the vinedresser and owner). The proof texts for deity are either insertions (1 John 5:7) or misread (John 1:1, where Chris reads *logos* as the Creator's spoken intent or mission, which people and angels are meant to carry forward). [Ch1, Ch2, Recap]
- The titles they affirm: Messiah (an anointed office, not a demigod status), the *Navi Emet* or Prophet of Truth that Moses promised (Deuteronomy 18), Son of Man (far more common than Son of God), the Standing One, the one the Samaritan woman expected. "Son of God" in the sense that all the righteous are sons and daughters. Calling Jesus a prophet is not a demotion. [Ch1, 3A, Recap]
- Jesus was a righteous man raised by righteous parents inside an existing Torah-faithful subculture (the Nazarenes, who predated him and included John the Baptist's family), and many of the apostles were his relatives. The canon underplays the family: the unnamed mother at the tomb, "James" for Ya'akov, Salome, and (in Chris's reading) Joseph of Arimathea as Jesus's father. The virgin birth, in their view, is a later motif patterned on demigod births that breaks Davidic descent through Joseph; the Ebionites held Joseph to be the father. [Ch1, Ch2, 4C]
- His purpose: to get Israel back to the beginning, to the Father's instructions, clearing away "traditions of men"; to model a life that leads to the Father ("no one comes to the Father except through me" read as "through the model"); and to announce a kingdom at hand now. Asked directly how to gain eternal life (Matthew 19), he lists commandments and says follow me. He forgave sins before the cross, so forgiveness did not depend on his death. The sign of Jonah means Nineveh listened and repented; it did not believe in Jonah's blood. [Ch1, 3C, 4B, Recap]
- "Believe" in John 3:16 should be read as obey or be faithful; the Torah was a lifestyle code, never burdensome ("my yoke is easy"). Matthew 7:21-23's "workers of *anomia*" means people who used his name without keeping his Father's Torah. [Ch1, Ch2, 4C]
- Why they led with this: the hosts see a "false light" movement (Christ consciousness, Sananda, theosophy) as a coming deception that the "Jesus equals God" habit prepares people for, and worship music addressed to Jesus rather than the Father as part of the drift. [Ch1, Recap]

### Scripture and canon

- Inspired, yes; inerrant or infallible, no. The three words mean different things, the book never claims the latter two, and the record shows errors (Kings vs. Chronicles, Mark 16, 1 John 5:7, the Matthew 6:13 doxology across translations). The "infallible or worthless" binary is false; the hosts hold a third position: a treasure map with tears and white-out, under the Spirit's oversight above the Roman oversight. [Ch2, Recap]
- About thirteen living canons exist, so no one list can be infallible; there was no "Bible" until the late fourth century (Hippo, Rome, Carthage), and Nicaea fixed Christology and Easter, not the canon. The 1611 KJV carried the Apocrypha; Enoch, Jubilees, Sirach, and Jasher are "hidden books" worth reading. [Ch1, Ch2, Recap]
- The text spent most of its life under hostile management: captivity under Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Persia; a Hasmonean, Herodian, and Roman-appointed priesthood; then Rome. Jeremiah 8:8 ("the lying pen of the scribes") and 2:8, and Josiah's lost book, are in-canon evidence that the text was never pristine. God is capable of preserving his word but, as far as the hosts can tell, did not; the Spirit (*ruach*) preserves the truth. [Ch1, Ch2, 3A, 4A, Recap]
- "English is the trick": the Name replaced by "Lord" (which Chris links to Baal), "James" for Jacob, the italicized "is" of 2 Timothy 3:16 (which they read as "every God-breathed writing is profitable," with "scripture" naming the Writings, the third tier below Torah and Prophets), the pictographic alphabet, Yeshua vs. Jesus/Joshua. Consult every translation; put no version on a pedestal. [Ch2, Recap]
- Method: read each author for himself (Isaiah as Isaiah, Paul as Paul) instead of gluing the canon into one voice; test everything the way the Bereans did; "picking and choosing" is what Jesus and every warning about false prophets command. The book contains both God's path and the alternative so that free will means something (Deuteronomy 13). [3A, 4A, Recap]
- Read the first-century texts the Roman church rejected: the Clementine Homilies and Recognitions (which they call Peter's account or the Nazarene Acts of the Apostles), the Gospel of the Ebionites, the Dead Sea Scrolls (a witness for the text and a window on the first-century conflict), Enoch and Jubilees. [Ch1, Ch2, 4A-4D]

### Sacrifice

- God never asked for animal sacrifice. The sacrifices happened, and the prophets confirm they happened; what the hosts deny is that God commanded them. Their ten passages: Isaiah 1, Psalm 51, Psalm 40, 1 Samuel 15, Hosea 6, Micah 6, Isaiah 66, Amos 5, Hosea 4, Jeremiah 7. The pattern they ask listeners to watch for: sacrifice condemned alongside idolatry and "harlotry," blood always the problem and water the solution, the rhetorical questions ("who asked this of you?"), and the standard rebuttal (God wanted sacrifice with the right heart) never stated in any text. [3A, 3B, Recap]
- Team priest vs. team prophet. Prophets always side with the Most High; priests capitulate to the state. The sacrificial legislation was inserted "under Canaanite influence" (Abba Hillel Silver) during periods of captivity, by a priesthood with an interest in blood; from the Hasmonean period on, the hosts hold, that priesthood was Edomite/Herodian rather than Levitical and managed the text. Friedman's documentary hypothesis is their answer to "who added it." [3A, 3B, Ch2]
- The logistics: on the Torah's own figures (603,550 fighting men, two to three million people, three officiating priests, a camp the size of Mecklenburg County, the sin-offering bull carried outside it), the Levitical system could not have been performed in the wilderness, which is what Jeremiah 7:22 and Amos 5:25 / Acts 7:42 say. [3C, Recap]
- Blood/death vs. water/life: everything the Father, the prophets, Jesus, and the apostles emphasize is water (baptism, *mikveh*, *teshuvah*, living water); blood cleans nothing. The crowd's "his blood be on us" (Matthew 27:25) set against "wash me in the blood." [3A, 3C, Recap]
- The parent analogy: no father needs the death of something innocent to reconcile with his children; "do this and we're square" models are exploitable; "if a child isn't okay with it, maybe that's something to consider" (Jen). Atonement as at-one-ment. [3A, 3C]
- Jesus and the system: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (Matthew 12, Hosea 6:6); the temple action as an attack on the animal trade (John names the sheep and oxen); the Gospel of the Ebionites saying "unless you cease from sacrificing, the wrath will not cease from you." A human male was never a Torah category of offering; the "ultimate sacrifice" is a later invention; sacrifices continued forty years after his death; a lamb that walks again three days later does not count. Gethsemane: Jesus allowed what he foreknew so that people would do what Nineveh did. [Ch1, 3A, 3B, 3C]
- Two readings the hosts reject: "the Old Testament God is evil" (God is the definition of love; the sacrificial layer is the problem) and "harsh Old Testament law vs. gracious New Testament Jesus" (grace is in the Torah: Exodus 20:5-6; the Father is the same). [3C, 4B]

### Paul

- The Bible contains the truth to life and a test, and the test is a false prophet whose words Jesus and the original apostles would have identified as such: the Apostle Paul. The frame is Deuteronomy 13:1-5 (God allows a prophet whose signs come true, to know whether the people love him). Most of Paul's sentences are true; a deception works because it is mostly truth, and the disagreements fall exactly on salvation, justification, works, and the law. [4A, 4D, Recap]
- Two salvation models: Romans 10:9-10 and Ephesians 2:8-9 (confess, believe, grace through faith, not works, Jesus's death as atonement) against Matthew 19:16-22, Revelation 20, and the kingdom-at-hand sayings (obedience, works worthy of repentance, a kingdom present now). Remove Paul and Jesus's model stands; remove the gospels and the Christian salvation formula is unchanged. "Christianity is a religion of Paul with Jesus as the mascot"; ask why anyone does not keep Torah and the answer is a Paul verse. [4B, Recap]
- "Paul anomalies": the only figure chosen while on his way to murder; the only person Jesus blinds; three conversion accounts that differ (Acts 9, 22, 26); "kick against the goads" from Euripides; a light-from-heaven commissioning like the founders of later religions; Matthew 24's warning not to believe "he is in the wilderness"; no second witness; Acts 1's criteria; Paul never quoting Jesus; the messenger of Satan his Jesus declines to remove; on first-name terms with the chief priests. [4A, Recap]
- Peter's side: the Clementines' Peter asks how an apparition can authorize anyone to teach, calls Paul "the man who is my enemy," and does nothing but quote the master; Antioch (Galatians 2) retold from Peter's side; Eisenman's reading that Acts rewrites Peter and the Clementines preserve the same source more faithfully; Acts 10 was about men, not food; Mark 7:19's parenthesis is an editorial note. [4A, 4C]
- Who Paul was: Herodian and Edomite (Romans 16:11, Eisenman's family tree), Roman-protected, more Sadducee than Pharisee, quoting the Septuagint, preaching in synagogues while claiming the Gentile mission; two names (Sha'ul/Sheol, Paulus/least) paired with Matthew 5:19. [4A, 4C, 4D]
- Texts: Jones's list of contradictory statements (law, commandments, mercy, forgiveness, justification, cost, approval of men, teachers, how many saved, depravity, confession, sacrifice, payment); Revelation 2 (Ephesus tested false apostles while all Asia turned from Paul; the teaching of Balaam against 1 Corinthians 8; the Nicolaitans); 2 Peter 3:15-16 (brother, not apostle; *dysnoētos*); Galatians 3 against Habakkuk 2:4, Genesis 15:6, Deuteronomy 27-28, and the collective "seed"; James 2 against Galatians; Marcion's existence as proof of a Paul/twelve split; the Ebionite testimony collected by Vujicic; the Scrolls' Spouter of Lies. [4B, 4C, 4D]
- The rapture is Paul's and inverts Jesus's harvest imagery, where the wicked are taken and the righteous remain. Working hunches, flagged as such: Paul fits the description of antichrist (against, in place of, in front of), and much of Matthew 24 and Revelation was fulfilled in 68-73. [4C, 4D, Ch1]
- Call to action: tally Paul citations against Jesus citations in any salvation sermon (ten to one, in Chris's experience). [4A, 4D]

### Practice

- What the hosts keep: the Sabbath (where they started in 2015), the *moedim* (Passover, Unleavened Bread, First Fruits, Shavuot, Yom Teruah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot), the food laws, lending without interest, the seven-year release. Not legalism but learning the spirit of the instructions; their full list fit on one sheet of paper. With no temple, the sacrificial procedures do not apply either way. [Recap, 4D]
- Vegetarian since 2018: Genesis 1:29, clean and unclean appearing only after the Watchers, the Nazarene view that the original model is the model of the eternal kingdom; "consuming life leads to life, consuming death leads to death." They will not tell anyone what to do. [4B, Recap]
- Circumcision reversed after two or three years of prayer (their son is not circumcised): circumcision of the heart makes sense, it was an Egyptian practice, no Christian thinks it saves, Genesis 17 sits in the Priestly source. No condemnation of parents who circumcised. [Recap]
- Tithing: in the law, so churches that say the law is nailed to the cross keep the one line that pays them; the original tithe was food for landless Levi and for orphans and widows; give directly to people in need. [Recap, 4C]
- The Enochic/Jubilees calendar (364 days, year beginning in spring on the fourth day; a jubilee of 49 years); separate episodes promised on the calendar and feasts. Shabbat dinner on Friday; holidays replaced with the *moedim*; no 501(c)(3) church, but meeting with "called-out" people on Shabbat. [Ch1, Recap, 4D]
- Method and counsel: pray for truth and mean it; ask the Father whether what you heard is true; several translations, interlinears, and concordances; a chapter of Proverbs a day; Jen's Sirach readings; purge the house of what carries darkness (a crucifix, under the second commandment); start with the Ten Commandments and the Sabbath and go easy on yourself; don't trust us blindly. [Ch1, 4D, Recap]

### A. Who Jesus is

1. **"Christos" was the chief title of Mithras; Greek had no word for Messiah and borrowed it** (Ch1 #11; 3C #18; 4A #10; 4B #6). *Christos* is from *chriō*, "to anoint," and is the Septuagint's ordinary rendering of *mashiach* (Leviticus 4:5, 1 Samuel 2:10, Psalm 2:2, Daniel 9:26), translated by Jews centuries before the Roman Mithras cult spread; "Christos Mithras" is not an attested title. The notes say do not repeat as stated. The accurate neighboring point is item 16 (Joshua/Yeshua).
2. **"James" was Jacob until King James renamed him** (Ch1 #15; Ch2 #7; 3C #16; 4C #30). "James" comes from Late Latin *Iacomus* and is in Wycliffe (1380s), Tyndale (1526), Geneva (1560), and the Bishops' Bible; the KJV inherited it. That the name is Ya'akov stands; drop the king.
3. **Son of Man vs. Son of God counts** (Ch1 #1: 84-87 vs. about 15; Recap #1: 80-plus vs. fewer than 10). Son of Man is roughly 80 in the gospels; "Son of God" applied to Jesus is well over 10 (about two dozen in the gospels, more across the New Testament, depending on what is counted). The two chapters disagree with each other; count before quoting either.
4. **Jesus never claims equality with the Father in red text** (Ch1 #4). The standard counter-texts (John 10:30, 8:58, 20:28; Mark 14:62) are not addressed; `questions/trinity-deity-of-jesus.md` has prepared answers for each.
5. **"My doctrine is not mine but his that sent me"; "the *logos* is not mine"** (Ch1 #2). John 7:16 has *didachē*; the *logos* wording is 14:24 (and 17:14). Cite both; accurate and safe.
6. ***Pisteuō* (*eis*) means obey; "believeth" in John 3:16 means obeys** (Ch1 #7; Ch2 #15; 4C #5). No lexicon glosses it that way; it means believe, trust; "obey" is *hypakouō* or *peithomai*. The support is contextual (John 3:36 pairs believing with disobeying; 14:15; Hebrew *emunah*; James 2; Bates on *pistis* as allegiance). State it as an argument about semantic range, not as what the Greek "really says."
7. **The Trinity is "a Roman installed pagan concept"; *Elohim* encompasses elders, seraphim, and hosts** (3A #2). The repo's fourth-century position is in `christianity/trinity-fourth-century-construction.md`; the *Elohim* gloss is the hosts' own, and mainstream grammar reads the plural with singular verbs as a plural of majesty. Interpretation.
8. **Trinity, virgin birth, and "immaculate conception" were foreign for 100-200 years and took hold in the 300s; "immaculate conception" used for the virgin birth** (Ch1 #26; Ch2 #31; 4C #28). Co-equal Trinitarian dogma, yes (325, 381). The virgin birth is in Matthew, Luke, and Ignatius (c. 110), so "foreign" needs the Ebionite qualification in item 9. The Immaculate Conception (defined 1854) is about Mary's own conception without original sin, a different doctrine.
9. **The virgin birth breaks Davidic descent through Joseph; a feminine *Ruach* makes the conception story read like a Nephilim story, a later insertion; John and Mark omit it** (Ch1 #14; Ch2 #31; 4C). *Ruach* is grammatically feminine: accurate. Matthew 1 and Luke 3 run through Joseph, and the standard answer is legal paternity. Mark and John have no birth narrative; John's silence is not denial. The stronger card the hosts do not play: the Ebionites denied the virgin birth and used a Matthew without the birth narrative (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.2; Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.2-3, 30.13-14; in `christianity/Incoming/`).
10. **The "other Mary" at the tomb is Jesus's mother; Mark's "Mary the mother of James and Salome" are his mother and sister** (Ch1 #5). Matthew 27:56 and Mark 15:40 call her the mother of James and Joses; Mark 6:3 lists James and Joses among Jesus's brothers, so the identification is arguable and some scholars hold it, but the gospels do not make it. Inference.
11. **Salome was Jesus's sister** (Ch1 #5, #25; 4C #29). Epiphanius (*Panarion* 78.8-9) names Joseph's daughters Mary and Salome; Mark 15:40 and 16:1 name a Salome among the women. Plausible tradition, not established fact.
12. **Joseph of Arimathea is Jesus's father; "Arimathea" is not a place but a patronymic** (Ch1 #6). The standard identification is Ramathaim (1 Samuel 1:1; LXX *Armathaim*; Eusebius's Onomasticon); the patronymic reading is a popular etymology without scholarly support. Chris says he has the paperwork; ask for it before repeating. The attached claim that the Last Supper was not a Passover seder is the real John-vs-Synoptics chronology problem (John 18:28, 19:14 vs. Mark 14:12); see item 134.
13. **Simeon bar Clopas succeeded James; John Mark was a brother of Jesus** (Ch1 #24). The first is Hegesippus via Eusebius, *Church History* 3.11 and 4.22 (a cousin, son of Joseph's brother Clopas); accurate. The second has no ancient witness; Chris sources it to "something I was watching."
14. **The Odes of Solomon are psalms by Jesus's sister Salome** (Ch1 #25). Syriac/Greek hymns usually dated late first or second century, author unknown; the attribution is Jackson Snyder's. Jen's cup / milk / breast image is Ode 19 almost verbatim and worth citing on its own.
15. **Jonah died in the fish and was resurrected** (Ch1 #9; 3A #20). Some readers take Jonah 2:2 ("from the belly of Sheol") and 2:6 literally; the text does not say so. Label it a reading.
16. **Joshua and Yeshua share the Hebrew spelling and meaning, yet one is "Joshua" and the other "Jesus"** (Ch1 #13). Substantially right: Nehemiah 8:17 spells Joshua as Yeshua; LXX and New Testament render both *Iēsous*; the KJV has "Jesus" for Joshua at Acts 7:45 and Hebrews 4:8. A good, citable point. The related claim that **the names of God and Yeshua were removed from Bibles in the 1500s** (Ch1 #17) is covered under item 35.
17. **Mary was of the house of Zadok via Elizabeth; Zechariah was a Zadokite; Jesus's family was priestly** (4C #27; Ch2 #18 and Key Teachings). Luke 1:5 makes Elizabeth "of the daughters of Aaron" (Aaronide, not specifically Zadokite) and 1:36 Mary's kinswoman. Ezekiel 44:15-16 on the sons of Zadok is accurate; the Zadok etymology for "Sadducee" is the usual one and debated. The Melchizedek etymology (*melek* + *tsedeq*) is right. Tabor's Levi/Matthat argument in `christianity/james-the-just-key-to-understanding-jesus.md` is the scholarly cousin.
18. **Jesus was not a Pharisee or Pharisee-sympathetic; "honest scholars" say Hillel quoted Jesus** (Recap #31; 3B #33). Matthew 5:20 and 23 are accurate; some scholars (Maccoby, Falk, Flusser in part) place Jesus closest to the Pharisees, with the sharpest conflicts against the Sadducean temple leadership. Hillel died c. 10 CE, so the direction can only run one way or reflect common tradition. The hosts and the repo both lean on the priest/prophet axis, a different cut from Pharisee/Sadducee.
19. **"Jesus was not Jewish by what we understand jewishness today"** (Ch1 #33). Qualified on air (culturally yes; rabbinic Judaism postdates him); without the qualifier it will be misheard.
20. **Lucifer was the minister of music** (Ch1 #27). A popular reading of Ezekiel 28:13 (KJV "tabrets and pipes") and Isaiah 14:11; the Hebrew is obscure and most modern translations drop the instruments. Tradition, not text.
21. **Jesus "allowed what he foreknew so the prophecies could be fulfilled"** (3C #22). The hosts' theological reconstruction, offered as such; compatible with Matthew 26:54 and Luke 24:26-27. A reading, not a quotation.
22. **Checked and holds (cite and move on):** John 14:12 "greater works" (Ch1 #3); the Torah never includes a human male as an acceptable offering (Ch1 #8; the usual counter is Hebrews 9-10 and Isaiah 53, handled in `questions/blood-atonement-and-salvation.md`); Matthew 7:23 *anomia* = lawlessness (3A #12; Ch1; 4C); Matthew 15:13 "plant," not "tree" (3A #11); sins forgiven before the cross (Ch1, Ch2, 4D); Sabbath as the sign, Exodus 31:13, Ezekiel 20:12 (Recap #22); Jen's "do not call Abraham your father, God can raise up stones" is John the Baptist (Matthew 3:9), as she flags herself, and Matthew 21:13 as Isaiah 56:7 plus Jeremiah 7:11 is correct (Ch2 #13); Jeremiah 2:13, John 4:10-14, Micah 6:8 as read (3C #24).

### B. Scripture, canon, transmission, translation

23. **"Thirteen official variations" of the canon; canonized about 350 years after Jesus** (Recap #6; Ch2 #2; 3A #1; 3C #23). The thirteen match the columns of Wikipedia's "Biblical canon" table and depend on how traditions are split (Lutheran, Anglican, and generic Protestant counted separately); say "about a dozen living canon traditions" and name them. The markers are Athanasius's festal letter (367), Hippo (393), Carthage (397). The number 13 as a formal figure needs a source.
24. **Canon and council history** (Ch1 #10; Ch2 #27, #29; 4C #3). Right and useful: Nicaea (325) addressed Christology and the Easter date, not the canon; Rome 382, Hippo 393, Carthage 397 and 419 for the Western canon; Jerome's Vulgate c. 382-405. Needs fixing: the first Latin Bible was Jerome's, not "Justin's"; Trent's canon decree is 1546; Luther's 1522 preface set four books apart as lesser but removed nothing, and his 1534 Bible moved the Apocrypha to a separate section; Arius was a presbyter of Alexandria, not a pope; Nicaea's sanction was anathema and exile, not death (Constantine's edict threatened death for concealing Arius's writings, the nearest thing); two bishops refused to sign; "smaller councils before Nicaea established a bi-deity" needs a source; "hundreds of canons" between 70 and 397 is loose (many lists: Marcion, Muratorian, Origen, Eusebius, Cyril, Laodicea).

Whether the differences show dishonesty or Luke's shaping for three audiences is the interpretive question; `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` takes the second view.
84. **"Kick against the goads" is Dionysus's line from Euripides' *Bacchae*, in meter** (Recap #15; Ch2 #30; 4A #5). *Bacchae* 794-795, Dionysus to Pentheus, but also Aeschylus (*Agamemnon* 1624) and Pindar (*Pythian* 2.94-96); a Greek proverb. Tragedy is iambic trimeter, not pentameter, and Acts' three words match the proverb rather than a full line; the captions have Chris calling Dionysus "the son of Apollo" (son of Zeus). "Quotes a Greek proverb best known from the *Bacchae*" is accurate; "quotes Dionysus" overstates.
85. **Acts 26:18 is the first time "faith in me" language is heard from Jesus** (4A #6). "Sanctified by faith in me" is unique to Acts 26:18, but belief directed at Jesus is not absent from the gospels (John 14:1; 3:16-18; 6:29; Mark 9:42). State the narrower claim.
86. **God and angels only ever appear to a waking man as men, never as a light** (4A #7). The burning bush, the pillar of fire, and the glory on Sinai are counterexamples; the stronger point (no commissioning appearance in the terms Matthew 24 warns against) stands without the generalization.
87. **Peter says apparitions cannot authorize anyone to teach; angels always appear as men** (4A #28). Clementine Homilies 17.13-19 for the first; the "always as men" rule is not a formulation found there (17.14-16 argues that visions come to the impious from demons and the righteous see God face to face). Check before attributing the rule to Peter.
88. **Ananias of Acts 9 belongs to the high-priestly family that killed Jesus and James** (4A #9). The Ananias of Acts 9 is a disciple in Damascus; the high priest Ananias son of Nedebaios appears at Acts 23-24; Jesus's hearing was under Caiaphas (son-in-law of Annas); James was killed under Ananus ben Ananus (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200); Simeon son of Clopas died under Trajan. Chris himself says "maybe, maybe not"; keep it there.
89. **Paul disappeared into Arabia for fourteen years, then a week and a half with Peter** (4A #20; 4D #16). Galatians 1:17-18: Arabia, back to Damascus, then after three years fifteen days with Peter; the fourteen years (2:1) run from that visit to the next. "Arabia as the Edomite stronghold" is geographically fair (Nabataean territory overlapped old Edom). The genuine discrepancy, which the source Chris reads in 4D states correctly, is that Acts 9:19-30 has no Arabia and puts Paul in Jerusalem with the apostles soon after Damascus.
90. **The office of apostle closed at twelve (Acts 1:21-26); Matthias is not a thirteenth as Paul is** (4A #24; 4B; 4C). The repo has retired this argument (`questions/CITATION-NOTES.md`; `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` `### 2`; `FIX-LIST.md` flags the public page still carrying it): Acts 1 is the criterion for filling Judas's seat, and the New Testament calls Barnabas (Acts 14:14), James (Galatians 1:19), and Andronicus and Junia (Romans 16:7) apostles. The hosts' point survives as "Paul was not one of the Twelve and not an eyewitness."
91. **No one but Paul and the one man he trained ever calls Paul an apostle** (4A #23). Luke calls Paul and Barnabas apostles (Acts 14:4, 14), presumably the man meant; 2 Peter 3:15 says "brother." Accurate with Luke named.
92. **Paul never quotes Jesus (once, maybe twice); 2 Corinthians 12:9 is his only quotation; all his companions have Greek names; he always quotes the Septuagint while the real apostles used the Hebrew** (4A; 4A #25; 4D #12). `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md` counts about two sayings (1 Corinthians 7:10-11; 9:14) plus the Supper words (11:23-25); 12:9 is a vision-word, not a saying of the earthly Jesus; Romans 16 names Jewish co-workers (Aquila, Priscilla, Mary, Andronicus, Junia, Jason, Sosipater). Septuagint dependence is broadly accurate, but most Old Testament quotations in the gospels and in Peter's speeches as Acts reports them also follow the Septuagint, the common text of Greek-speaking Jews; the contrast is weaker than stated. Tighten "never" and "all."
93. **Rabbis who trace their lineage to Gamaliel laugh at Paul's Pharisee claim; Paul was most likely a Sadducee** (4A #22; 4D #13). The scholarly version is Hyam Maccoby, *The Mythmaker* (1986); mainstream scholarship (Sanders, Dunn, Tabor) accepts the claim; the Sadducee hypothesis stands against Philippians 3:5, Acts 22:3, 23:6, 26:5 (Eisenman suggests Herodian/Sadducean leanings). Cite Maccoby rather than unnamed rabbis and label the hypothesis as the hosts'.
94. **Peter was the apostle to the Gentiles according to Jesus; Paul only ever spoke to Jews / preached to Jews in every city** (4A #26; 4B #16; 4D #17). Acts 10 and 15:7 have Peter open the Gentile mission; Galatians 2:7-9 assigns Peter to the circumcised; no saying of Jesus assigns the Gentiles to Peter (Matthew 28:19 sends all the Twelve, the repo's argument). Paul's synagogue-first pattern is real (Acts 13:5, 13:14, 13:46, 14:1, 16:3, 17:1-2, 17:10, 17:17, 18:4, 18:19, 19:8) and Acts 9:15 and Romans 1:16 frame it as both-and; but he also addresses Gentile crowds (Lystra, Acts 14:8-18; Athens, 17:16-34) and founds largely Gentile congregations (1 Corinthians 12:2; 1 Thessalonians 1:9). "Only Jews" is not accurate.
95. **Theophilus was Nero's pagan magistrate; Acts was never written for Christians but to exonerate Paul; Luke then wrote his gospel to set the record straight and it is the most anti-Pauline gospel; "Luke was hired by Herod's rich relative"** (4A #8; 4C #10, #12). Theophilus is unidentified (proposals range from a Roman patron to the high priest Theophilus ben Ananus). John W. Mauck, *Paul on Trial* (2001), does argue Luke-Acts as a legal defense for a Roman official, but Mauck is a Christian lawyer defending Acts' reliability; the extensions are the hosts' own with no scholarly sponsor named. Luke as Paul's companion rests on Colossians 4:14, Philemon 24, 2 Timothy 4:11, and the "we" passages.
96. **The "python priestess" who vouches for Paul in Acts; Corinthian tongues linked to the spirit of python** (4C #11; 4D #25). Acts 16:16-18 describes a slave girl with a spirit of python who follows Paul proclaiming his party servants of God and whom Paul exorcises; the tripod, paid consultations, and Delphi are details of the Pythia; the girl does not speak in tongues. The structural observation (a pagan spirit testifying for Paul) is in the text; the Acts 2 vs. 1 Corinthians 14 contrast is itself a real debate.
97. **"This day I have begotten thee" stood in Matthew and Luke before Nicaea and was removed** (4A #12). A Western-text variant of Luke 3:22 (Codex Bezae, Old Latin, Justin, Clement of Alexandria) and the Gospel of the Ebionites' baptism account (*Panarion* 30.13.7, in the repo); not attested for Matthew; critical editions prefer the majority reading on manuscript grounds. A real variant; "removed after Nicaea" is the inference.
98. **Paul has more words than anyone in the Bible, including Jesus** (4A #14). Luke-Acts is longer than the thirteen letters combined; Moses's five books dwarf both. Paul is the largest single voice among the letter writers, roughly comparable to Jesus's recorded words.
99. **Ten to fifteen first- and second-century writers say the apostles and Paul did not get along** (4A #19). The early witnesses are fewer: Galatians 2 itself, the Epistle of Peter to James and the Clementines (second to fourth century), Epiphanius's report of the Ebionites, and Irenaeus's and Origen's notes that the Ebionites rejected Paul. Name them rather than count them.
100. **The Greek says stake (*stauros*), not cross; the cross motif is Roman** (4A #11). *Stauros* does mean an upright stake; Roman crucifixion used several configurations and the shape in Jesus's case is not recoverable from the word. The Jehovah's Witnesses make the same argument; linguistically fair, historically open.
101. **Paul sought letters of recommendation from the Jerusalem assembly and did not get them** (Recap #19). Not in the text: Acts 9:1-2 has letters from the high priest before his conversion; 2 Corinthians 3:1 has Paul saying he needs none; Galatians 2:9 has the "right hand of fellowship." Ask what the hosts had in mind.
102. **Luther wanted James out as anti-Paul and later saw that as a problem; "in 1545 said the hell with those three"** (4A #16; Ch2 #29). The 1522 preface called James "an epistle of straw," a remark dropped from editions after 1534; he never removed the letter; the 1534 Bible moved the Apocrypha to a separate section; he died in 1546. Direction roughly right; the details need the dates.
103. ***Dysnoētos* means "nonsense," or "something a lot stronger than hard to understand"** (Recap #35; 4A #17; 4C #32; 4D #39). Standard lexica gloss δυσνόητος as "hard to understand"; the on-air Google Translate synonym list is not evidence of first-century range. The stronger sense is the hosts' inference; the point that a warning about letters being twisted to destruction is not an endorsement does not depend on it.
104. **A non-canonical Petrine text accuses the church of making "a religion of blood"** (4A #18). Not located in the Clementine Homilies, Recognitions, the Epistle of Peter to James, or the Preaching of Peter fragments; Recognitions 1.36-39 (sacrifice permitted for a time; baptism in place of sacrifices) is the nearest. Ask the hosts which text they had in mind.
105. **Checked and holds:** Deuteronomy 13:1-5 read accurately, and the hosts' summary is the plain sense of verse 3 (4A #1; 4D); Sirach 1:22 as Jen reads it (4A #30); 2 Corinthians 12:7 as "a messenger of Satan" is in the repo, with "walk-in" the hosts' own vocabulary (4A); Deuteronomy 25:4 and 1 Corinthians 9:9-10 / 1 Timothy 5:18 are accurately cited on both sides, and Paul says the ox is not the point (4C #15; `OPEN-RESEARCH.md` §2); Acts 10 is about men, per Acts 10:28 and 11:12 in Peter's own interpretation, though 10:14 is still the verse opponents press (4C #17); Paul's model requires a human in place of a lamb, the Torah has no such provision, and the temple sacrifices continued after Jesus's death (4B #22).

### F. Paul: the texts and the doctrine

106. **Sha'ul means "hell / the grave"; Paulus is a (double) diminutive meaning "the least"; Greek scholars acknowledge 1 Corinthians 15:9 as a nod to the name** (Recap #10; 4B #3, #4; 4D #39). Sha'ul (שָׁאוּל) means "asked for" (1 Samuel 1:20's wordplay; King Saul's name); Sheol (שְׁאוֹל) shares consonants but is a different word with different vowels. *Paulus* is an ordinary Latin adjective, "small, little" (the diminutive is *paullulus*); "least" is a gloss, and the Spanish *-itito* analogy overstates it. The Matthew 5:19 wordplay works in Greek on *elachistos*, not through Latin. Some commentators note a possible pun in 15:9; it is a suggestion, not a consensus, so name one. The notes say this argument should not be repeated as stated.
107. **Remove the gospels and nothing about Christianity would change** (4B #2). Rhetorical, and the hosts know it; the defensible form they also give is that the salvation formula of mainstream Christianity comes from Paul, not from anything Jesus is recorded saying.
108. **Tertullian, against Marcion, says Paul is not an apostle and does not fit the criteria; "slams Paul"** (4B #5; 4D #3). *Against Marcion* 5.1 challenges Marcion to prove Paul's apostleship from his own canon and remarks that a man cannot be both claimant and witness; 3.5 calls him "the apostle of the heretics." A dialectical move; Tertullian accepted Paul and quotes him constantly. Cite the passage, not the conclusion. The repo uses the same principle in `christianity/paul-false-prophet-deuteronomy-18-test.md`.
109. **Marcion, c. 144, built the first canon of "only Paul"; a bishop and a Gnostic; the Roman canon came 397; "Colossians is the most Gnostic book in the Bible"** (4C #3; 4D #2). Marcion's canon was an edited Luke plus ten Pauline letters, which Chris says correctly in 4D; c. 85-160, from Sinope, a shipowner (son of a bishop by some accounts), active in Rome c. 140, expelled 144; usually distinguished from Gnosticism proper (no aeons, no secret gnosis) while sharing the two-God structure. The Colossians remark is an opinion some scholars voice about the letter's opponents, not a consensus.
110. **Paul's language in Philippians 2:6-8 and Romans 8:3 denies real flesh (docetism), which is how Marcion used it, so Paul fits 1 and 2 John's definition of antichrist** (4D #4; 4D antichrist section). Marcion did lean on this language and *homoiōma* ("likeness") is what docetists used, but Paul also writes "born of a woman" (Galatians 4:4), "descended from David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3), and "the man Christ Jesus" (Romans 5:15; 1 Corinthians 15:21), which docetists had to excise. Present it as "language Marcion could use," not "Paul denied the flesh." Tabor reads Philippians 2 as an Adam-Christology, the opposite of docetism (`podcast-archive/dr-tabor/08-...`).
111. **"Anti" can mean anterior, positioned in front of; vowels are interchangeable; James's "O empty man" (2:20) mocks Paul's "emptied himself" (Philippians 2:7)** (4D #31, #32). Greek *anti* means against or in place of (both legitimate for *antichristos*); Latin *ante* (anterior) is a different word in a different language, so drop the analogy. *Kenos* / *kenoō* share a root, but the word is common and the relative dating of James and Philippians is uncertain. Hypothesis only.
112. **Romans 3:7 is Paul admitting to lying, softened by later translators' "some might say"** (4D #19). The Greek has no "some might say"; translations that add it (NIV) are interpreting, not following a variant. But Romans 3:5-8 is a diatribe in which Paul voices an objection and 3:8 calls it a slander against him; the context argues against the plain reading. Not a manuscript issue either way.
113. **The Galatians 3 readings** (4D #20-#24; the repo's `OPEN-RESEARCH.md` §2 and `podcast-archive/dr-tabor/02-`, `34-`, `38-`). Habakkuk 2:4 *emunah* = faithfulness: accurate, and the Qumran pesher (1QpHab 8) applies it to those who keep the law; Paul follows the Septuagint's *pistis* (Tabor's argument). Genesis 15:6 as Abraham reckoning God's promise: the Hebrew subject is grammatically ambiguous and the reading has been argued (Tabor; Ramban as one option), but Nehemiah 9:8, Psalm 106:31, 1 Maccabees 2:52, Jubilees 14:6, 4QMMT, and James 2:23 all take God as the one reckoning; present it as a live alternative, not the obvious meaning. Galatians 3:10 vs. Deuteronomy 27:26: Paul's "all things" follows the Septuagint (the Hebrew has "the words of this law"); the point that Deuteronomy 27-30 is a blessings-and-curses structure with repentance built in (30:1-10), not a one-strike rule, is sound. "Seed" singular (3:16): *zera* is collective in Genesis 12:7, 13:15, 17:7-8, 22:17-18, and Paul's reading is a midrashic move he qualifies himself in 3:29. The law "through angels by an intermediary" (3:19-20): the intermediary is Moses, the angels come from the Septuagint of Deuteronomy 33:2 and a tradition also in Acts 7:53 and Hebrews 2:2, and Paul's point in 3:20 is that a mediator implies two parties; Chris's reading misses this.
114. **The James-against-Galatians pairs** (4D #26). All verses check out as cited. A fair presentation should include the repo's own concession that Paul can sound like James (Romans 2:6-13; `questions/pauls-legitimacy.md`).
115. **The Jones list** (4C #4, #7, #8, #9). The source is Edgar Jones's "Paul versus Jesus: A List of Contradictory and Compatible Statements" on his Voice of Jesus website; cite it as a web source and check each pairing. Three slips: Romans 3:10-12's "as it is written," whose source Chris could not place, is Psalm 14:1-3 (= 53:1-3), and the psalm itself distinguishes the fool from "the righteous" (14:4-5), which is the out-of-context point he suspected; Matthew 23:35's Zechariah son of Barachiah is the murdered prophet of 2 Chronicles 24:20-22 or the postexilic prophet, not John the Baptist's father (Luke 1:6 does call that Zechariah righteous, so the point survives with the right verse); "saved by my gospel" runs 1 Corinthians 10:33 ("that they may be saved") together with Romans 2:16 and 16:25.
116. **"God suffers that no man die but that everyone come to repentance" is in the Tanakh** (4C #6). The wording is 2 Peter 3:9; the Tanakh form is Ezekiel 18:23, 32 and 33:11. Cite Ezekiel.
117. **Revelation 2:14 vs. 1 Corinthians 8 / Romans 14 on idol meat; Revelation 2:2 (Ephesus tested false apostles) vs. 2 Timothy 1:15 (all in Asia turned from Paul)** (Recap #12, #13; 4B; 4C). The verses are accurate. 1 Corinthians 10:14-22 has Paul forbidding participation in idol feasts, so his position is more layered than "indifferent"; Revelation 2:2 names no one, so the link to 2 Timothy 1:15 is inference. The repo's strongest form of the idol-meat argument is one the hosts do not use: James's decree in Acts 15:19-20, reversed in writing at 1 Corinthians 10:25 (`christianity/acts-15-james-four-decrees.md`).
118. **"Nicolaitans" is an Aramaic nickname for "those who eat whatever"** (4B #12). The name is Greek in form; the ancient sources (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.3; Epiphanius, *Panarion* 25, in the repo) derive it from a man named Nicolaus, and the popular modern etymology is *nikaō* + *laos*. No Aramaic derivation is attested; get the book's title and author before repeating.
119. **Paul affirms the law only when on trial, one or two times against twenty to one (or "thousands") of denials** (4B #17; 4C #33). Acts 21:20-26 and 24:14-18 (also 25:8, 28:17) are trial-context affirmations; Romans 3:31, 7:12, 7:22, 13:8-10, and 1 Corinthians 7:19 are affirmations in letters with no trial in view. The tension is real and the repo documents it; the count is rhetoric. Count before quoting.
120. **50,000 denominations come from interpreting Paul** (4B #15). The common figure from the World Christian Encyclopedia's count of denominational bodies (around 45,000 in 2019), most differing on polity and ethnicity rather than soteriology. Use with that caveat.
121. **Jesus only ever cursed a fig tree; James says do not curse; Paul curses dissenters** (4B #24; 4D). Matthew 21:19 and Mark 11:14; James 3:9-10; Galatians 1:8-9. Jesus also pronounces woes (Matthew 11:21; 23:13-36), not curses in the technical sense but closer than the sentence allows.
122. **The rapture appears only in Paul; in Jesus's imagery the ones taken are the wicked** (4C #1; 4D). Strong, and the repo's position (Matthew 24:37-41 with the flood; Luke 17:34-37 with the vultures). John 14:3 ("I will take you to myself") is the verse defenders reach for outside Paul; have an answer ready. Note that Paul uses "death as sleep" as freely as anyone (4C #35).
123. **Jeremiah was the only true prophet of his day** (4C #2). Uriah son of Shemaiah (Jeremiah 26:20-23), Ezekiel, Habakkuk, and Zephaniah were contemporaries; the point that most recognized prophets were false (Jeremiah 23) stands.
124. **Jesus was killed under Nero** (4C #14). Jesus died under Tiberius, with Pilate as prefect (c. 30-33); Nero (54-68) is the emperor of Paul's trial and the first persecution. The Nero anecdotes are from Suetonius and Dio.
125. **Jesus and women vs. Paul on women; "Paul never supports women"** (4D #1). The "woman at the well" (John 4) and the woman caught in adultery (John 7:53-8:11, textually suspect) are run together. 1 Corinthians 14:34-35 is widely argued to be an interpolation and 1 Timothy is disputed Pauline, so the silence passages may not be Paul's; the undisputed letters name Phoebe a deacon, Junia an apostle, Priscilla a co-worker (Romans 16:1-7), and assume women praying and prophesying (1 Corinthians 11:5). "The head-covering and silence texts are Pauline-stream opinion, not Jesus" is the defensible form (`questions/the-gospel-of-thomas.md` applies the same layer-dating).
126. **There are no lost works of Paul, but plenty of lost sayings of Jesus** (4D #18). 1 Corinthians 5:9, Colossians 4:16, and 2 Corinthians 2:4 refer to letters now lost. The asymmetry in surviving material is real; "zero" is not.
127. **Checked and holds:** Romans 10:9-10, Ephesians 2:8-9, Matthew 19:16-22, 1 Corinthians 15:1-11, Luke 4:43, Luke 19:8-9, Luke 23:39-43, Exodus 20:5-6, Matthew 5:23-24, Revelation 2:1-2, 2 Timothy 1:15, Acts 19:8-10, 1 Corinthians 8:4-8, Romans 10:4, Ephesians 2:15, Galatians 3:24-25, Matthew 5:17-18 all read accurately from the RSV or close to it; the interpretive load is in the pairings (4B #1); Matthew 5:19, 1 Corinthians 15:9, Ephesians 3:8 (Recap #11); Sirach 35:1-7 as Jen reads it, Vulgate/KJV numbering (4B #25; Recap #29); the hosts' "the law is not hard" has Deuteronomy 30:11-14 as its text, worth adding (4B #23); Saturday carries the Sabbath in many languages, and Sweden and ISO begin the week on Monday (4D #37).

### G. The early church: Nazarenes, Ebionites, Clementines, Eisenman

128. **The "unless you cease from sacrificing" saying is from the pseudo-Clementines / the Clementine Homilies** (3A #17; 4C #13; Ch2, paraphrased without naming Epiphanius; 3B cites it correctly). It is the Gospel of the Ebionites as quoted by Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.16.5 (verbatim in `christianity/Incoming/epiphanius-panarion-book-1-full-text.md`); the Clementines do not contain the saying, only the related doctrine (Recognitions 1.36-39). Cite Epiphanius.
129. **The writers who describe the Nazarenes are Epiphanius, Josephus, Irenaeus, and Eusebius** (3A #15). Josephus never mentions Nazarenes or Ebionites. The witnesses are Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Origen, Eusebius (and Hegesippus through him), Epiphanius, and Jerome, collected in `christianity/Incoming/witnesses-on-flesh-and-sacrifice-selected-texts.md`.
130. **Epiphanius says the Nazarenes repudiate certain apostles and do not acknowledge the sacrificial system; the Nazarenes took the Nazirite vow** (3A #16; Ch2 #24). Those are the Ebionites (*Panarion* 30); *Panarion* 29 on the Nazoraeans says they keep the law and that Epiphanius cannot say what they believe about Christ, and says nothing about sacrifice. *Nazōraios* and *nazir* are different words; James's Nazirite-like practice comes from Hegesippus, but the sect's name is not from the vow. `questions/nazarene-sect-sacrifice-and-diet.md` (`## Do not say these`) flags this conflation.
131. **"Essene" derives from a Hebrew word for "the doers"** (Ch1 #21; Ch2 #24). One proposal among several ('*osim*, "doers of the law," favored by Eisenman and Goranson), alongside Aramaic *hasya* ("pious") and *asya* ("healer"). Unsettled; say so. "Ebionim" as "the poor" is standard.
132. **The Nazarenes fled to Pella, Qumran, and Beirut** (Ch2 #23). Pella: Eusebius, *Church History* 3.5.3; Epiphanius, *Panarion* 29.7. Qumran was destroyed by the Romans in 68 CE and was not a refuge. "Beirut" is probably Beroea in Coele-Syria (Aleppo), where Epiphanius places Nazarenes.
133. **The first-century Nazarenes/Ebionites rejected the death penalty specifically** (Recap #18). The Homilies' "false pericopes" (2.38, 3.42-57) reject passages about sacrifice and about God being warlike or deceiving; Epiphanius says the Ebionites rejected parts of the Pentateuch. Whether any source singles out capital punishment needs checking in the held texts.
134. **The Last Supper was not a Passover seder; Jesus kept the older priestly calendar's Passover** (Ch1 #6; 4D #36). The Synoptic/Johannine discrepancy is real; the proposal that Jesus kept an Essene solar-calendar Passover is Annie Jaubert's (1957), with supporters and critics. Cite it as a hypothesis.
135. **The Nazarenes were excommunicated; the daily prayer's curse on the *minim* was aimed at them** (4D #7). Standard scholarship (the Birkat ha-Minim, usually dated to the late first or early second century; some Genizah versions name the *notzrim*); the dating and target are debated.
136. **Orthodoxy was imposed under penalty of death; thousands butchered under Constantine; Catholic councils in the first and second century argued whether Jesus suffered** (4D #6, #33). Constantine exiled Arius and burned his books but did not execute dissenters en masse; the first recorded execution for heresy is Priscillian (385); large-scale killing is medieval. There were no councils in the first two centuries; docetism was fought in letters and treatises (Ignatius, Irenaeus, Tertullian). The point about orthodoxy as the winning side's view stands without the inflated history.
137. **The Clementines are "Peter's journals," the "Nazarene Acts of the Apostles"; Clement hears Matthias in Rome; "matmonim" is Hebrew for the Apocrypha** (Ch2 #43; 4B #18; 4A; 4C). The Recognitions and Homilies are third- to fourth-century compositions attributed to Clement that scholars believe preserve earlier Jewish-Christian sources; "Peter's journals" overstates, and *The Nazarene Acts of the Apostles* is the title of a modern repackaging of the Recognitions (`christianity/clementine-literature-nazarene-writings.md`; `VERIFIED-SOURCES.md`). In the Recognitions (1.6-13) Clement hears Barnabas in Rome, in the Homilies (1.9-14) in Alexandria; James's commission to write is in the Epistle of Clement to James and Recognitions 3.74-75; the gist is right, the names need fixing. The usual Hebrew term for the Apocrypha is *sefarim hitzonim*; "matmonim" may be a mis-hearing.
138. **Peter broke table fellowship at Antioch to protect his authority over demons** (4C #18). The Homilies (7.3-4, 7.8; 13.4) do give table-of-demons and purity reasons for Peter's refusal to eat with Gentiles who are unbaptized or eat idol meat; they do not narrate Antioch. The application to Galatians 2 is the hosts' synthesis; label it.
139. **Mark 7:19 "thus he declared all foods clean" is a scribal insertion found in no Greek manuscript** (4C #16; 4D; Recap). Incorrect as stated: the words *katharizōn panta ta brōmata* are in the Greek manuscripts. The dispute is grammatical: most modern editors read the masculine participle as Mark's editorial comment about Jesus (hence the parenthesis), while the KJV construed it with the digestive process ("purging all meats"). Jen's version (a parenthetical editorial note that translations stopped bracketing) is the accurate one. `questions/jesus-and-the-torah.md`.
140. **Simon Magus is Paul because Rufinus swapped the names; "all scholars think so"** (4D #34). Baur's Simon-as-Paul reading is a literary identification accepted by many for parts of the Homilies. Rufinus's Latin Recognitions (c. 400) admits omissions in its preface, but the Homilies survive in Greek, independently of Rufinus, and also feature Simon. The name Simon is in the Greek tradition too.
141. **Stephen did not exist; the story is about James; "most scholars" / "everyone who has looked critically" agrees** (3C #15; 4C #19). Robert Eisenman's thesis (*James the Brother of Jesus*, 1997), built on Recognitions 1.70-71 and Hegesippus's account of James's death; a minority position. The repo elsewhere uses Stephen's speech (Acts 7:42-43) as evidence in its own right, which sits awkwardly with treating Stephen as a fiction; cite the thesis as Eisenman's.
142. **The Dead Sea Scrolls identify Paul as the Spouter of Lies; James is the Zaddik; the Wicked Priest killed James; the scrolls "do nothing but speak to the witness" of James** (4B #13; 4D #27; Ch1 #23; Ch2 #22). The Liar / Spouter of Lies (CD 1, 4, 8, 20; 1QpHab 2, 5, 10) is dated by nearly all scholars to the second century BCE; Eisenman alone redates the sectarian scrolls to the first century CE and identifies the Liar with Paul and the Teacher with James. Hegesippus (Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23.18) does connect James's death with the siege, which gives the last point an ancient witness. Cite as Eisenman's minority view; the repo's DSS files take the mainstream date.
143. **James's death triggered the Jewish-Roman war** (4D #38). Josephus dates James's death to 62 (*Antiquities* 20.200); the war began in 66; Hegesippus says Vespasian besieged the city "immediately" after. Ancient linkage exists; causation is interpretation.
144. **"Herodion my kinsman" puts Paul in the Herodian family tree; Paul was "Herod's uncle"; Agrippa his cousin and step-brother; descended from an incestuous union** (4A #21; 4C #24; 4D #11). Romans 16:11 is the whole textual basis; *syngenēs* in Romans 9:3 and 16:7, 21 means fellow Jew, and the name Herodion suggests a connection to the Herodian household (perhaps a freedman), which is the most the text yields. The rest is Eisenman's reconstruction (via Josephus's "Saulus," kinsman of Agrippa, *Antiquities* 20.214; *War* 2.418) plus a video the hosts found the night before; "uncle" and "step-brother" are in no source. Keep the verse and Eisenman separate from the rest; `podcast-archive/the-jesus-way/023-` makes the same argument.
145. **Paul courted the high priest's daughter; "some scholars say"** (4A #27; 4C #26). Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.16.8-9 (and 30.25.1), reporting what the Ebionites said: a Greek who came to Jerusalem, wanted to marry the priest's daughter, became a proselyte, and when refused wrote against circumcision, the Sabbath, and the law. A hostile ancient tradition, not scholarship; it is in the repo's held text and unused on any card.
146. **The Paul-Seneca correspondence; the Sonnini manuscript (Paul in Gaul and Britain with the Druids)** (4C #22, #23; 4D #14). The fourteen letters are a fourth-century Latin composition regarded as spurious by essentially all scholars (Jerome mentions them; Erasmus rejected them); the "Long Lost Chapter of the Acts of the Apostles" published by Sonnini in 1801 is regarded as a modern forgery. Do not use either as evidence of Paul's connections.
147. **470 soldiers; the appeal to Caesar and two years in Herod's praetorium imply family connections** (4C #34; 4D #15). Acts 23:23 gives 200 soldiers, 70 horsemen, 200 spearmen (470); the appeal (Acts 25:11) was a Roman citizen's right (*provocatio*), and Acts 22:28 says Paul had citizenship by birth; the praetorium at Caesarea was the governor's residence (Acts 23:35, 24:27). Neither requires patronage.
148. **The Vujicic quotations and the book's details** (4D #5; 3C #2; 4B #27). Each secondary quotation needs a page reference before reuse: the "demoniacal hallucination" article (likely the 1909 *Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Ebionites"); *Encyclopaedia Judaica* (article and edition); Philip Hughes, *A History of the Church*, vol. 1, p. 57; James Dunn (probably *Unity and Diversity in the New Testament*; note that Dunn is also the author of the New Perspective objection, so quoting him for "denigrated Paul" should not imply he shares the hosts' conclusion); Boettner, *Roman Catholicism* (1962); Lockyer, *All the Apostles of the Bible* (1972). The patristic core (Irenaeus 1.26.2; Origen, *Contra Celsum* 5.65; Eusebius, *HE* 3.27; Epiphanius 30.16, 30.25) is accurate and in the repo. Verify the book's exact subtitle, publisher, and year (it appears to be self-published, mid-2010s); the author's "irrefutable evidence" is his rhetoric.
149. **Eisenman's chapter title "The Anti-Semitic Peter"; "Paul in work or purse"** (4C #21). Verify the title and pagination in the 2006 edition before citing; the passage read on air is consistent with Eisenman's argument in both books.
150. **Who Is Esau-Edom** (4D #10). Charles A. Weisman's 1991 book comes out of the Christian Identity movement and identifies modern Jewry with Edom. The hosts explicitly disclaim antisemitism and use it for the Herodian/Idumean point; readers should know the book's provenance, and the Idumean ancestry of the Herods can be cited from Josephus (*Antiquities* 14.8-9) without it.
151. **Gad is the Igbo tribe; two Shabbat candles for the two houses; "the lost sheep of the house of Israel" are millions today who do not know they are Israel** (4D #8, #9; 3C #21; Recap). The Igbo claim belongs to Igbo-Jewish identity movements, not established history, mentioned in passing. The traditional reasons for two candles are *zakhor* and *shamor* (Exodus 20:8; Deuteronomy 5:12); the two-houses reading is a Messianic/Hebrew Roots interpretation. Matthew 15:24 is accurate; the "greater Israel" application is a modern framework the hosts say they will develop elsewhere, and the repo holds no two-house framework.
152. **The Panarion survived intact because Rome did not burn it** (3A #18). Plausible as a generalization about orthodox vs. heterodox texts, but a guess; it survives in several medieval manuscripts like other patristic works. Not load-bearing.

### H. Eschatology, Revelation, the calendar

154. **Revelation was written before 70 and before the Gospel of John; "scholarship now dates it before John"; most of chapters 1-20 were fulfilled in 68-73; John was released from Patmos** (Ch1 #29; Ch2 #42; 4D). The early (Neronian) date is a minority view (Robinson, Gentry); the majority dates Revelation to Domitian's reign, c. 95, as `christianity/2026-03-23-revelation-deep-dive.md` does; John's return to Ephesus is tradition (Eusebius 3.20, 3.23). Chris flags his conclusion as provisional; keep it that way. "This generation is forty years" (Matthew 24:34, 33 plus 40 to 73; the war ended 73/74 with Masada) is standard and the repo's own argument for the Olivet Discourse (Ch1; Ch2; 4D #28).
155. **The thousand years have already elapsed and Satan was released about a generation ago** (4D #30). Speculation Chris labels as such; it departs from the repo's partial preterism and would need its own case.
156. **Why does the whole world date time from the first century; a cataclysm, not a birth, would be needed** (4D #29). The Anno Domini count was devised by Dionysius Exiguus in 525 for Easter tables, popularized by Bede, adopted across Western Europe over centuries, and spread globally through European expansion; "BC" counting is later still; no first-century person called it the first century, and Jewish, Islamic, Persian, and Chinese calendars never adopted it. The puzzle has a conventional solution.
157. **Three books of Enoch; "supposed to be 364 books"** (Recap #32). 1 Enoch (Ethiopic), 2 Enoch (Slavonic), 3 Enoch (Hebrew, late) are real; 2 Enoch 23:6 and 68:2 say Enoch wrote 366 books; "364" in the captions may be a mis-hearing or a conflation with the 364-day year.
158. **Yeshua rose on "one of the Sabbaths" (*sabbaton* twice in the Greek); the first day of Unleavened Bread always falls on a Wednesday** (Ch1 #18). Matthew 28:1 has *sabbatōn* twice and Mark 16:1-2 *sabbatou* then *sabbatōn*, but *mia sabbatōn* is the ordinary idiom for the first day of the week (Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2), because days were counted from the Sabbath; "one of the Sabbaths" is a minority reading. Two Sabbaths in Passover week is standard (Leviticus 23:7; John 19:31). Nisan 15 on a Wednesday holds for the 364-day Qumran/Jubilees calendar, not for the lunar calendar in use at the temple.
159. **Checked and holds:** the Enochic/Jubilees year begins in spring on the fourth day of the week, matching the scholarly reconstruction of the 364-day calendar, and Jubilees 4:1 as read is verbatim in the repo's Charles translation (Recap #33); 613 commandments is a rabbinic enumeration (Makkot 23b; Maimonides), not a biblical count (Recap #21).

### I. Practice, Genesis, diet, and the rest

160. **The woman caught in adultery as evidence of Jesus refining Torah** (Recap #17; 4D #1). John 7:53-8:11 is absent from the earliest manuscripts and bracketed in most critical editions; it can still be early tradition, but a transcript that leans on textual criticism elsewhere should flag it. The same point is made in `christianity/old-testament-through-jesus-lens.md`.
161. **Clean and unclean appear only a thousand years after Genesis 1, after the Watchers; the Watchers taught flesh-eating; Jubilees says God made twenty-two kinds** (4B #20, #21; Recap #27; 3A). Genesis 7:2 is the first use of clean and unclean; on the Masoretic chronology the flood is 1,656 years after creation; the Watchers' descent is in the days of Jared (Jubilees 4:15; 1 Enoch 6:6, both verifiable in `christianity/ethiopian-bible/unique-ethiopian-texts.md`); 1 Enoch 7:3-5 has the giants devouring people and drinking blood, and "taught mankind to eat dead flesh" is an interpretation. Jubilees 2:15-23's twenty-two are works of creation matched to the generations to Jacob and the letters, not animal categories.
162. **Cain and Abel may be inverted** (Recap #28). The hosts present it as their own reading. John 8:44 ("murderer from the beginning") is about the devil; 1 John 3:12 says Cain was "of the evil one"; Hebrews 11:4 praises Abel's sacrifice. No manuscript or ancient witness is cited; keep it labeled hypothesis.
163. **Jubilees: Abram serves dinner to his father's idols** (Ch2 #40). Jubilees 11-12 has Abram reject idolatry and burn the house of idols (Haran dies in the fire); the feeding scene is from Genesis Rabbah 38:13 and the Apocalypse of Abraham.
164. **Circumcision: an Egyptian practice; Genesis 17 is P; vitamin K peaks on day eight; complication and mortality statistics** (Recap #23). Egyptian practice attested (Herodotus 2.104; the Saqqara relief); Genesis 17 as P is the standard source-critical assignment; the vitamin-K claim circulates in apologetics on old prothrombin studies and is contested; the mortality statistics Chris says he would have to look up, so do not repeat without the article. Thomas 53 is the repo's one direct text.
165. **"Jesus was a Nazarene, so he did not eat meat"; Jen's "Jesus ate fish"** (Recap #14; 3A). The repo's case rests on patristic reports about James, the Ebionites, and the Gospel of the Ebionites, with the Luke 24 fish passages needing an answer (`christianity/biblical-case-for-vegetarian-jesus.md`, `## V`). Treat as a position with evidence, not a settled fact.
166. **The Talmud says Jesus is punished in boiling excrement** (Recap #30). Gittin 57a in uncensored editions, where the figure is named in some manuscripts; whether the passage refers to Jesus of Nazareth is disputed (Schäfer, *Jesus in the Talmud*, argues yes; others no). Cite the tractate and the dispute.
167. **Christmas falls on Mithras's birthday; Mithras born December 25** (4B #8; Ch1 #12). December 25 was the Roman *Natalis Solis Invicti* (attested in the 354 calendar; the festival of Sol Invictus promoted by Aurelian in 274); the specific link to Mithras rests on the Sol-Mithras association and Persian sources do not supply it. Say Sol Invictus.
168. **The Blavatsky/Bailey "doctrine of Lucifer" quote; Sananda Jesus Immanuel** (Recap #34; 4A #29; Ch1 #31). Versions of the quote circulate attributed to Blavatsky or Alice Bailey with uncertain provenance; do not attribute without a page reference. Sananda is a figure from twentieth-century channeled material (Sister Thedra, the Ashtar Command literature); the hosts are right that it has no first-century connection; the repo has no coverage.
169. **A tax levy of up to 100 percent under Rome, Herod, and the temple** (Ch1 #30). Hyperbole Chris attributes to hearsay; scholarly estimates vary widely and run far below that. Do not repeat the figure.
170. **The 500-year gap between Malachi and Matthew** (Ch1 #32; Ch2). The intertestamental period is roughly 400 years (Malachi c. 430 BCE); the repo's Dead Sea Scrolls and Ethiopian canon files supply the context Chris says nobody teaches.
171. **Recording dates** (Ch1 #34; Ch2 #1; 3A #26; 3C #26; 4A #31; 4C #36; Recap #36).
