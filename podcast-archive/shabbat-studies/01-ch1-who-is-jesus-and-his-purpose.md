## Claims to Check

Specific statements a careful reader should verify against primary texts before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation.

1. **Son of Man 84-87 times, Son of God about 15, five or six to one** [2:50:35]. Son of Man is about 80-85 in the gospels. "Son of God" applied to Jesus runs to roughly two dozen in the gospels by most counts and well beyond that across the New Testament; the ratio depends on what is counted ("the Son," "his Son," "Son of the Blessed"). Note the recap's version of the same claim is "80-plus versus fewer than 10" [recap 18:53]; the two chapters disagree, so count before quoting either.
2. **"My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me"** [22:03] [40:47]. John 7:16; also John 14:24 and 12:49. Accurate and safe; the Greek in 7:16 is *didachē*, not *logos*, so "the *logos* is not mine" blends 7:16 with 14:24 (*logos*) and 17:14. Cite 7:16 and 14:24 together.
3. **"Greater works than these will you do"** [42:18] [1:10:26]. John 14:12. Accurate. Already in the repo.
4. **Jesus never claims equality with the Father in red text** [22:03]. The standard counter-texts are John 10:30, 8:58, 20:28, and Mark 14:62; `questions/trinity-deity-of-jesus.md` has prepared answers for each. Chris does not address them here.
5. **The "other Mary" at the tomb is Jesus's mother; Mark's "Mary the mother of James and Salome" are his mother and sister** [48:32] [50:06]. Matthew 27:56 and Mark 15:40 identify her as the mother of James (the less) and Joses; Mark 6:3 lists James and Joses among Jesus's brothers, so the identification is arguable and some scholars hold it, but the gospels themselves do not make it. Salome as Jesus's sister comes from later tradition (Epiphanius, Panarion 78.8-9, names the sisters Mary and Salome; Gospel of Philip and Protevangelium traditions vary). Present as inference, not text.
6. **Joseph of Arimathea is Jesus's father; Arimathea is not a place** [50:06] [51:41]. The standard identification is Ramathaim (1 Samuel 1:1; LXX *Armathaim*), which Eusebius's Onomasticon also makes. The patronymic reading is a popular etymology without scholarly support; Chris says he has sources, so ask him for them before repeating. **Last Supper not a Passover seder** [50:06]: this is the real John-versus-Synoptics chronology problem (John 18:28, 19:14 put the crucifixion before the Passover meal; Mark 14:12 puts the meal on Passover). Defensible, and a genuine debate; cite it as such.
7. ***Pisteuō* with *eis* means obey** [1:04:13] [1:55:56]. No lexicon glosses it that way. The inference has company: *pisteuō eis* is a distinctively Johannine idiom, John 3:36 pairs believing with disobeying (*apeithōn*), and scholars such as Matthew Bates argue *pistis* means allegiance. State it as an argument about the semantic range, not as what the Greek "really says."
8. **The Torah never includes a human male as an acceptable offering** [1:15:13]. Accurate; Leviticus 18:21, 20:2-5, Deuteronomy 12:31 and 18:10 forbid it. The usual counter is Hebrews 9-10's typology and Isaiah 53, both handled in `questions/blood-atonement-and-salvation.md`.
9. **Jonah died in the fish** [1:37:15]. A reading some hold on the strength of Jonah 2:2 ("from the belly of Sheol") and 2:6; the text does not say so outright. Label it a reading.
10. **Nicaea did not form the canon; Carthage (397) did; the first Latin Bible came in 405 "by Justin"** [1:41:55] [2:47:29]. Nicaea addressed Christology; accurate. The 397 Carthage canon list is one marker among several (Athanasius 367, Hippo 393); the Vulgate is Jerome's, completed around 405 (Chris corrects himself mid-sentence). Replace "Justin" with Jerome.
11. **Christ was the title of Mithras; Greek had no word for Messiah** [2:56:44]. The Septuagint, translated by Jews two centuries before Jesus, renders *mashiach* as *christos* throughout (1 Samuel 2:10, Psalm 2:2, Daniel 9:26), so Greek did have the word and it was the Jewish choice. "Christos Mithras" is not an attested title; Roman Mithraism's epithets are *Sol Invictus* and *deus*, and the cult only became widespread in the empire in the second century. Do not repeat as stated. The accurate neighboring point is the one about Joshua (item 13).
12. **Nimrod, Semiramis, and Tammuz as the pagan trinity behind the virgin-born son of God** [2:10:01] [2:44:23]. This triad is Alexander Hislop's nineteenth-century construction (*The Two Babylons*); the historical Semiramis (Shammuramat) was a ninth-century BCE Assyrian queen, and Tammuz/Dumuzi is not her son in any ancient source. The broader point (dying-and-rising sons of divine mothers across the ancient Near East) can be made without Hislop. Mithras's December 25 birth is attested only via the Roman *Natalis Invicti* (354 CE calendar), not Persian sources.
13. **Joshua and Yeshua share the Hebrew spelling and meaning, yet one is transliterated Joshua and the other Jesus** [1:59:01]. Substantially right: Nehemiah 8:17 spells Joshua son of Nun as Yeshua; the LXX and New Testament render both as *Iēsous*; and the KJV even has "Jesus" for Joshua at Acts 7:45 and Hebrews 4:8. The divergence is an artifact of translating the Old Testament from Hebrew and the New from Greek via Latin. Good, citable point.
