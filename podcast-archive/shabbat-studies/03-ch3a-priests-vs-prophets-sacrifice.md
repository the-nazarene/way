## Summary

Chapter 3 is the third of the four planned installments and the hinge of the series: the hosts say they need the sacrificial system on the table before they can talk about Paul, because Paul's gospel depends on it and, in their reading, Jesus's does not. The chapter runs about 5.75 hours across three videos (3A, 3B, 3C) that YouTube split from one Saturday-night livestream; internal references put it in late 2023 (a seven-week-old baby is in the room, and the Sweden story is dated "five years" earlier to December 2018). Chris carries nearly all of it. Jen is off camera feeding the baby for most of 3A and surfaces a handful of times: to clarify the Pilate story, to ask "what about not touching blood or a dead carcass," and to ask Chris to repeat what sign Jesus said he would give.

This first part (2:08) is setup. Chris recaps chapters 1 and 2 and explains the order; explains the sacrificial system "for noobs," first as he understands the Jewish framing (ox, sheep, or goat; the priest kills, sprinkles blood, burns and eats; no temple, so no sacrifices today) and then the Christian replacement model (Jesus as the perfect lamb); names what he calls the "Christian premise," the altar-call assumption that the death of something good covers someone bad; states the hosts' actual position (they hold what they understand to be the first-century Nazarene/Ebionite view, that the sacrificial legislation was a later insertion and Jesus came to abolish it); and sets up the chapter's two running motifs, team priest vs. team prophet and blood/death vs. water/life. The long middle is the origin story: Sweden, winter 2018, reading Exodus and Leviticus with the HalleluYah Scriptures and a conversion table, doing the tabernacle arithmetic, praying, and finding a video that had sat unwatched on his desktop for nine months, which led him to Abba Hillel Silver's *Moses and the Original Torah*. Then the friend in Los Angeles who had spent three years in a ritual animal-sacrifice group and described it to them in February 2019. The last half hour touches Friedman and the documentary hypothesis, who the Nazarenes/Ebionites were and which ancient writers describe them, Jesus as Messiah and *Navi Emet* (Prophet of Truth), the sign of Jonah, Matthew 19, and a first statement of the hosts' thesis that the Jerusalem priesthood from the Hasmonean period onward was Edomite rather than Levite. It closes by listing the ten prophetic passages that fill 3B.

Chris is explicit about the shape of the claim several times: the sacrifices happened, the prophets confirm they happened, and the hosts are not saying the text is "a lie"; they are saying the practice was never ordained, and that a priesthood with an interest in it managed the text. He also says more than once that he is appealing to logic and to parenthood rather than to scholarship, and that nothing he is describing is his own idea. The textual case is in 3B; the arithmetic is in 3C.

### The sacrifice system explained: Jewish and Christian perspectives

- Jewish framing as Chris presents it: for a sin offering, a blemish-free ox, sheep, or goat (he notes the Hebrew words for goat and ox are similar); bought at the temple if you have none; the priest kills it in the prescribed way, removes the entrails, takes them outside the camp, burns the animal, sprinkles blood on the altar; the blood "cleanses" ritually; the whole animal must be eaten or burned, nothing wasted; only Aaron's line officiates. The backstory is the covenant stipulation that death follows violation ("the wages of sin is death"). [28:15] [29:49] [31:23] [32:57]
- Why there are no sacrifices today: no temple since 70 CE, and the priestly position that sacrifice can only happen there. Most Jews, he says, read the Torah "astutely": it says bull, lamb, or goat, not man, so Jesus does not qualify. [32:57] [34:30]
- Christian framing: the system was for a season, with a redemption plan always intended; Jesus as the perfect lamb, the one exception to the species list; sin "transmuted" onto the victim (otherwise, he says, it is no different from hitting a deer on the way to synagogue); and Jesus as triune, which raises the Gethsemane problem (who was he asking?).
