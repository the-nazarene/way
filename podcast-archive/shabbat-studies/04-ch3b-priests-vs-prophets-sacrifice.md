## Summary

This is the textual core of chapter 3 (2:25). Chris reads ten passages, mostly whole chapters or long stretches, from the NIV and NKJV with the KJV and Douay-Rheims as backups, and comments as he goes: Isaiah 1, Psalm 51, Psalm 40, 1 Samuel 15, Hosea 6, Micah 6, Isaiah 66, Amos 5, Hosea 4, and Jeremiah 7, in that order, which he says runs from the softer passages to the clearest. He then reads Jesus: Matthew 12:1-8 (the grain-field dispute and the Hosea 6:6 citation), Matthew 21:12-13 and John 2:13-22 (the temple action, with the detail that only John names the sheep and oxen), and the Gospel of the Ebionites saying about ceasing from sacrifice, with Eisenman's *New Testament Code* held up near the end. The last minutes set up the logistics chapter in 3C. Jen is off camera for nearly all of it; the one on-air exchange is Chris asking her to screenshot questions. Three chat questions are answered along the way: why the Catholic Church "drinks Jesus's blood," a critic's "rightly dividing" comment, and a blog owner's rebuttal that Jeremiah 7:22 only denies a command "in that day."

The reading method is the same for every passage. Chris asks the audience to watch for a pattern he says all ten share: sacrifice is condemned alongside idolatry and "prostitution" (spiritual unfaithfulness); blood is always the problem and water the solution; the questions ("who asked this of you?", "did you offer me sacrifices in the wilderness?", "shall I come with burnt offerings?") are rhetorical; and the standard pastoral rebuttal, that God wanted sacrifice but with the right heart, is never stated in any of the texts and has to be inferred to keep the book consistent. He also argues three times that comparative wording ("more than" in Hosea 6:6, "as great" in 1 Samuel 15:22, and Psalm 51:18-19 as a whole) is a translator's or scribe's softening of a flat denial. Running underneath is the thesis introduced in 3A, that the priesthood from the Hasmonean period on was Edomite/Herodian rather than Levitical and managed the text.

Compared with the recap, this part gives the full walk through the ten, adds two passages the repo does not use (1 Samuel 15 and Hosea 4), develops the Psalm 51:18-19 interpolation argument, connects Ahaz's Assyrian-style altar to Isaiah 1, and gives the hosts' reading of Matthew 12:7 ("the guiltless" are the animals) and Jeremiah 7:6 ("innocent blood" is animal blood). The Saturn/Remphan material from Amos 5:26 and Acts 7:43 also appears here, including a claim about the hexagram that needs care (see Claims to Check).

## Key Teachings

### How to read the ten: the pattern Chris asks listeners to watch for

- The ten are presented as "prophets saying Israel, stop sacrificing; this was never something you were supposed to do." He concedes he has not checked every prophet, but the pattern holds in every one he has read. [00:00] [01:32]
- Three recurring moves: the passages pair sacrifice with idolatry and "harlotry" (Israel giving itself to other gods and practices); blood is always bad and water is always the fix; obedience, meekness, and a contrite heart are what God asks for. [01:32]
- He reads whole chapters deliberately, so that nobody can say he pulled a verse out of context, and says he rereads them before each talk for the same reason. [48:21] [49:54] [1:32:02]
- The standing rebuttal he addresses in nearly every passage: "God rejected *their* sacrifices because they were in rebellion, not sacrifice as such." His answers: the text never says that; the sin offering is precisely the prescribed remedy for being in a state of sin, so rejecting it in that state makes the system incoherent; and the prophets never say "I wanted it, just with a better attitude," which is an inference readers add to keep the book infallible. [07:46] [09:20] [1:25:51] [1:39:46]
- Kings vs. Chronicles genealogical discrepancies are given as a quick example that no book, sacred or secular, is error-free. [00:00]

## Claims to Check

Specific statements to verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need the citation.

1. **Ahaz saw the altar in Assyria** [03:05]. 2 Kings 16:10: he saw it in Damascus, where he had gone to meet Tiglath-Pileser of Assyria. The link to Isaiah 1:1 is accurate.
2. **Isaiah 1:11-17 as read** [06:12]. Accurate NIV. Note that the very next verse, 1:18 ("though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow"), is where the hymn language comes from, and in Isaiah the whitening follows repentance, not blood. It supports the hosts' water/blood point and they do not use it.
3. **"Your new moons, your sabbaths" vs. "my feasts"** [15:34]. Accurate: Isaiah 1:13-14 has the second-person possessive (חָדְשֵׁיכֶם, מוֹעֲדֵיכֶם), Leviticus 23:2 has "my appointed times" (מוֹעֲדָי). Amos 5:21 likewise has "your feasts." Fine.
4. **Isaiah 1:21 "murderers" means killers of animals** [13:59]. The hosts' reading. The surrounding verses (1:15-17, 1:23) are about injustice, bribes, and the oppressed; mainstream readers take the murderers as human bloodshed. Label as interpretation.
5. **"The oldest copy of the Masoretic text is from about 1050 AD"** [17:10]. The Leningrad Codex is 1008 CE; the Aleppo Codex is c. 930. But the Dead Sea Scrolls (3rd century BCE to 1st century CE) contain Isaiah almost complete and much else, and their consonantal text is largely the Masoretic one, so "a thousand years of unchecked copying" is not the situation. The scrolls are also the place to test the hosts' Psalm 51 claim (see 7).
6. **Psalm 51:11 has *ruach ha-kodesh* ("your holy spirit")** [20:21]. Accurate (רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ). "David 800 BC" is about two centuries late for David; Pentecost is roughly a thousand years after him, not 840.
7. **Psalm 51:18-19 are a later addition** [23:26]. Widely held by commentators (the shift to Zion's walls and a restored cult reads as exilic or post-exilic). But the verses are in the Septuagint and every Hebrew manuscript, so if added, they were added before the 3rd-2nd century BCE, not by a medieval or Edomite-era scribe. "Scribes add at the end of a scroll" is a general observation, but Psalm 51 is not the end of a scroll. The repo's wording ("long recognized as a post-exilic addition") is the safe form.
8. **Mark 16:9-20 is a later addition** [23:26]. Standard; absent from Sinaiticus and Vaticanus. Already in `questions/method-and-criteria.md`.
9. **Psalm 40:6-8 as read** [31:12]. Accurate. Note the counter-use: Hebrews 10:5-7 quotes the Septuagint form ("a body you have prepared for me") and uses this exact passage to argue that Christ's body replaces the sacrifices. Anyone citing Psalm 40 against sacrifice should know Hebrews cited it for substitution first.
10. **Exodus 20:6 "showing mercy to thousands" puts *chesed* inside the Decalogue** [32:46]. Accurate.
11. **1 Samuel 15:22: "as great" is italicized and therefore the comparison is a scribal insertion** [43:42] [45:14]. Half right. The italics (KJV, NKJV, NASB; not the NIV he names) mark words the *translators* supplied, not words ancient scribes inserted. And the Hebrew itself is comparative: הַחֵפֶץ לַיהוָה בְּעֹלוֹת... כִּשְׁמֹעַ ("is the LORD's delight in burnt offerings... *as* in obeying?"), followed by "to obey is *better than* sacrifice" (טוֹב מִזֶּבַח). The translators supplied "as great" to render a comparison that is there in the text. The verse still says what the hosts want (obedience ranks above sacrifice); it does not say God never delighted in sacrifice. Same shape as the Hosea 6:6 correction.
12. **Hosea 6:6: "mercy" footnoted as faithfulness/loyalty** [48:21] [56:03]. Accurate; *chesed*. **"More than" in 6:6b is a thin patch** [51:27]: the Hebrew has the comparative *min* (מֵעֹלוֹת). See the overlap section; the repo withdrew the equivalent claim. Use the subordination framing instead.
13. **Hosea 6:8-9 as read** [51:27] [52:59]. Accurate NKJV.
14. **Revelation 2's Balaam warning** [59:10]. Pergamum, 2:14 (and Thyatira, 2:20, for idol meat). Micah 6:5's Balaam reference is about Balaam's answer to Balak, not the idol-meat teaching; the two are connected only by the name.
15. **Micah 6:7 puts animal and child sacrifice in one list** [1:02:20]. Accurate, and the repo reads it the same way. "Israel never had an official child-sacrifice system" is accurate as a statement about the law; 2 Kings 16:3 and 21:6 and Jeremiah 7:31 record kings doing it.
16. **Isaiah 66:3 "whoever sacrifices a bull is like one who kills a person"** [1:07:03]. Accurate NIV, and the common rendering. The Hebrew is a string of participles without "like," so a minority of translators (JPS 1985) read it as the same people doing both: "who slaughter oxen and slay humans, who sacrifice sheep and immolate dogs." Know the alternative; it does not help the opposing side much, but it exists.
17. **Amos 5:25 is rhetorical; 5:26 is Sikkuth and Chiun in the Masoretic text, Moloch and Remphan in the Septuagint; Acts 7:43 follows the Septuagint** [1:19:36]. Accurate. Chiun/Kaiwan as Saturn is well supported in Assyriology and is in the repo with references.
18. **The star of Remphan is the hexagram / "Star of David"** [1:21:10] [1:22:43]. No ancient source describes Remphan's star, and the hexagram did not become a Jewish emblem until the medieval and early modern period (Prague, 14th to 17th century). The identification is a modern one that circulates in polemical and antisemitic literature, which is why apologetics sites answer it the way Chris describes. The hosts say on air that they are not attacking a people group; given where this claim comes from, it is the one in the chapter most likely to be read otherwise, and it does nothing for the sacrifice argument that Amos 5:26 does not already do. `christianity/blood-magic-origins-saturn-sacrifice.md` stops at Kiyyun = Saturn, and should.
19. **"The priesthood establishment was carrying Saturn worship"** [1:21:10]. Amos is addressing the northern kingdom in the 8th century; the verse does not name priests. Keep it as Amos's charge against Israel's cult, which is enough.
20. **Hosea 4:1 "brings a charge" = legal complaint** [1:32:02]. Accurate; the Hebrew *rîv* is a lawsuit term. The admiralty-law reading is the hosts' lens, not the text.
21. **Hosea 4:2 "bloodshed touching bloodshed"** [1:38:12]. Accurate literal rendering (דָּמִים בְּדָמִים נָגָעוּ).
22. **Multiple sanctioned sacrificial sites** [1:39:46] [1:44:32]. Shiloh (1 Samuel 1-4), Gilgal, Gerizim/Ebal (Deuteronomy 27; Joshua 8:30-35), Bethel, and others are all attested; Deuteronomy 12 centralizes to one place. Source critics read this as a real historical layering (D's centralization vs. earlier practice). The hosts' instinct here matches mainstream scholarship.
23. **Jeremiah 7:6 "innocent blood in this place" means animal blood** [1:49:11]. The hosts' reading. Jeremiah uses "innocent blood" elsewhere for human victims (2:34, 19:4, 22:3, 22:17, 26:15), and 7:9 lists murder among the sins. Label as interpretation; the argument does not need it.
24. **Queen of Heaven = Lilith / Semiramis / Isis / Ishtar / "Easter" / Inanna / Athena** [1:46:05]. Jeremiah's Queen of Heaven is usually identified with Ishtar/Astarte (or Asherah); Inanna and Ishtar are the same figure. Lilith is unrelated. The Semiramis identification comes from Alexander Hislop's *The Two Babylons* (1853) and is not accepted by historians. "Easter from Ishtar" is a folk etymology; Bede derives the English word from Eostre, and most European languages use a Pascha derivative. The Marian link is the hosts' view.
25. **Jeremiah 7:22 and the "in that day" rebuttal** [1:52:20]. Both sides have a point. The verse does carry the time marker; the repo's prepared answer uses it as the strength of the claim (origin, not permission), and that version also survives Jeremiah 33:18, which the hosts do not address. Do not state Jeremiah 7:22 as "God never permitted sacrifice."
26. **Silver: the very names of the sacrifices were borrowed from Canaan** [1:56:59]. Ugaritic texts do share sacrificial vocabulary with Hebrew (*šlmm* / *shelamim*, *dbḥ* / *zebach*). Shared vocabulary is established; direction of borrowing is interpretation.
27. **"160 years" of Edomite additions to the Torah; Hasmonean/Idumean priesthood; Herod bought his position; Sadducees "Greek-ish" and Edomite-infiltrated** [2:01:37] [2:03:09] [2:04:42]. See 3A claim 22. Herod was Idumean and was made king by Rome (Antony and Octavian, 40 BCE), not by purchase from the Judeans; the Sadducees were the priestly aristocracy, named for Zadok. The "Edomite scribes puffed up the Torah" claim has no textual basis; the Hebrew Bible's text was fixed well before the Herodian period, as the scrolls show.
28. **Matthew 12:7 "the guiltless" are the animals** [2:06:20]. The Greek (τοὺς ἀναιτίους, plural) most naturally refers to the disciples the Pharisees have just condemned. The repo reads it as a general link between sacrificial logic and condemning the innocent. Both are readings.
29. **"The synoptics don't mention the animals; only John does"** [2:14:05]. Matthew 21:12 and Mark 11:15 both mention "those who sold doves," and Mark 11:16 has him stop anyone carrying anything through the temple. John alone names sheep and oxen and the whip. "Only John names the large animals" is the accurate form.
30. **Mark is earliest; shorter texts are older** [2:12:31]. Markan priority is the majority view. "Shorter is older" is a rule of thumb with exceptions, not a law.
31. **John "was the only one who was there"** [2:12:31]. Traditional authorship; most critical scholars date John c. 90-110 and do not treat it as eyewitness. The hosts use Markan priority a minute earlier, which sits uneasily with this.
32. **Passover animals are lambs or goats, so John's oxen were for other offerings** [2:20:19]. Exodus 12:5 says lamb or goat; Deuteronomy 16:2 says "of the flock and the herd," and 2 Chronicles 35:7-9 has bulls provided for Passover. Oxen in the temple court at Passover could be for Passover-season peace offerings as well as sin offerings. The point that the trade existed stands.
33. **"Jesus was a Pharisee" / "Jesus quoted Hillel"; "honest scholars say Hillel quoted Jesus"** [2:15:38] [2:17:14]. Hillel died c. 10 CE, before Jesus's ministry, so the direction can only run one way or reflect common tradition. See recap claim 31 for the Pharisee question.
34. **Eisenman, *The New Testament Code* (2006)** [2:18:47]. Real title and subtitle as Chris gives them; about 1,100 pages. Eisenman's Stephen/James and Jesus-family theses are minority positions in the field; cite as his.
35. **The Catholic mass as "a white magic ritualized version" of an underground blood practice** [10:54]. The hosts' view, stated as such and deferred to a future episode. Not a checkable claim and not one the repo makes.
36. **"The day of the Lord" verses describe the present** [1:28:56]. Application, not exegesis; the hosts present it that way.
