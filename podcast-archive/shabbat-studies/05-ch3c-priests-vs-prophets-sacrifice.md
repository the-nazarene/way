## Summary

The closing part of chapter 3 (1:13) is the arithmetic and the wrap-up. Chris reads aloud, nearly in full, chapter 10 of John Vujicic's *The Ebionites* ("Sacrifice in the Desert"), which argues from the Torah's own numbers that the Levitical sacrificial system could not have been carried out in the wilderness: 603,550 fighting men (confirmed three ways, including the half-shekel silver count), a total population of two to three million, 300,000 Passover lambs a year, a camp of tens of square kilometers with the altar at its center, the sin-offering bull to be carried outside that camp, roughly 300 childbirth purification offerings a day on top of everything else, latrines outside the camp, and exactly three men (Aaron and two surviving sons) permitted to officiate. Chris then brings it home: 1,500 square kilometers is about the size of Mecklenburg County, where he lives, and he invites listeners to run the comparison on their own county. He ties the result to Jeremiah 7:22, Amos 5:25, and Stephen's speech in Acts 7:42-43, adding Eisenman's suggestion that the Stephen story is really about James.

The second half is the "tell you what I told you" close. Chris puts the question to the listener directly (do you require bloodletting from something innocent to forgive your own children?), reframes Gethsemane (Jesus was not negotiating with himself; he allowed what he foreknew so that prophecy would be fulfilled and people might listen, as Nineveh listened to Jonah), and spends time on how *not* to take the chapter: not as "the Old Testament God is evil" and not as "harsh Old Testament law vs. gracious New Testament Jesus," both of which he rejects. He answers a Hebrews 9 rebuttal from the week before (a testament takes effect only at the testator's death, so the verse "calls God dead"), says a lamb that gets up three days later does not count as a sacrifice, and argues that 1 Enoch's lamb imagery was lifted out of context onto Jesus. He restates that Jesus is Messiah and the one to follow, and that the Messiah came "to correct the record and the actions of Israel." Next week's chapter is announced: a second New Testament voice whose salvation doctrine diverges from Jesus's. Jen closes with the child's perspective (would a child be okay with killing a rabbit? her own memory of learning where ham came from) and reads Jeremiah 2:13, the fountain of living waters against broken cisterns, which Chris ties to Jesus and the Samaritan woman. The benediction is Micah 6:8, the song his childhood church sang to end every service.

New in this part relative to the recap: the full logistics argument with its numbers and sources, the Mecklenburg comparison, the Stephen/James suggestion, the Hebrews 9:16-17 rebuttal, and the "two readings we reject" section, which is the clearest statement in the chapter of how the hosts want their position couched.

## Claims to Check

Specific statements to verify before repeating. The arithmetic here is checkable in ten minutes against the KJV in `christianity/Incoming/kjv/`.

1. **The tablets held ten commandments; the rest did not fit on stone** [00:00]. Deuteronomy 4:13 and 10:4 say the tablets held "the ten words"; Deuteronomy 31:9, 24 says the rest of the law was written in a book. Rhetorical point, and consistent with the text.
2. **The book's title and author** [01:33]. John Vujicic, *The Ebionites*; verify the exact subtitle, publisher, and year (it appears to be self-published, mid-2010s) before citing. The author's "irrefutable evidence and absolute proof" is his rhetoric; the argument is a reductio from the Torah's own numbers.
3. **Leviticus 4:11-12: every sin-offering bull was carried outside the camp** [07:49]. That rule applies to the bull for the anointed priest or the whole congregation (Leviticus 4:3-21). An ordinary Israelite's sin offering (4:27-35) was a female goat or lamb whose flesh the priests ate in the sanctuary (6:26). The outside-the-camp burden is real but smaller than the chapter implies.
4. **603,550 confirmed three ways; 22,000 Levites; two to three million total** [10:58] [12:32] [14:05]. Numbers 1:46, Exodus 38:26 (the arithmetic is exact), Numbers 3:39: all accurate. The population inference is the standard one. Note where it leads: most historians and many biblical scholars conclude from exactly this arithmetic that the census figures are not historical (symbolic, or *eleph* meaning "clan" or "unit" rather than "thousand"). The hosts' argument and the critical consensus agree that the wilderness narrative as written is not literal history; they differ on what to do with that.
5. **300,000 Passover lambs a year; a flock of three to four million** [14:05] [17:10]. Follows from the assumptions (ten per lamb; one-year-old males only). Exodus 12:4 lets small households share a lamb, so ten is a reasonable working number.
6. **Tent math: 300,000 tents, 7.5 million square meters** [17:10]. Arithmetic correct (7.5 km²). The captions then give "10,000 km²" for the congregation area and "1,500 km²" as the total, which do not reconcile with each other; check the figures in the book before repeating any of them. The Mecklenburg comparison depends on 1,500 km².
7. **1,500 km² is about 580 square miles; Mecklenburg County is about 520 to 550** [28:06] [29:42]. 1,500 km² = 579 sq mi. Mecklenburg County is 546 sq mi total, 524 land. Accurate.
8. **About two million births over forty years, roughly 300 a day** [20:16]. Two million over 14,600 days is about 137 a day, not 300; 300 a day implies about 4.4 million births. The author's or the caption's figure needs checking. Even at 137 the point stands.
