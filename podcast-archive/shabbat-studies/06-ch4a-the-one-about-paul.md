## Summary

This is the fourth livestream of the series and the first half of the Paul chapter, which the hosts call the most important puzzle piece of the whole project. Chris says at the outset that he has never spoken about Paul publicly before, only privately, and that the position comes from roughly nine years of research; Chris places the recording at the tail end of 2023, with a two-month-old baby below the frame. The first hour is setup: how the hosts came to the position, why they think God permits a false prophet inside the canon, and which New Testament books are Paul's. The second hour is the first of several evidence sections, which Chris calls "Paul anomalies": things about Paul's story that have no parallel elsewhere in the Bible. The direct Jesus-versus-Paul verse comparisons are announced here and begin in 4B.

The stated thesis [17:11]: the Bible contains the truth to life, but it also contains a test, and the test is a false prophet whose words Jesus and the original apostles would have identified as such, known to most people as the Apostle Paul. The theological frame for that is Deuteronomy 13:1-5, read aloud at [49:56]: God says he allows prophets whose signs come true in order to test whether the people love him and keep his commandments. Chris pairs this with the temptation of Jesus, Balaam, and Simon Magus as other cases of God allowing a tester, and with Jeremiah 8:8 and the leaven of the Pharisees as evidence that God did not shield the text itself from corruption. The hosts are careful to say that most of Paul's sentences are true and that a deception works precisely because it is mostly truth; the disagreements, in their view, fall exactly on salvation, justification, works, and the law.

The origin story matters for reading the rest of the chapter. Chris traces it to 2015-2016, when he and Jen were investigating the entertainment industry they worked in and came across a Blavatsky or Bailey quote about using the Masonic fraternity and the Christian church to bring in the doctrine of Lucifer [21:50]. An unnamed woman's podcast then said Paul was a false apostle and offered two patterns: Paul is the only figure God chooses while he is on his way to commit murder, and the Damascus road is the only time Jesus blinds anyone [24:59] [26:33]. Chris says he deleted the podcast, could not shake it for two months, prayed for witnesses, and found Douglas Del Tondo's *Jesus' Words Only*, which he worked through for five or six months [31:15]. The hosts' method statement here is that one person's opinion is not enough and they needed a second, third, and twentieth witness; the rest of the chapter is their attempt to supply them.

The anomalies section [1:11:44] onward covers: the three conversion accounts in Acts 9, 22, and 26 and their differences (read aloud and charted); Jesus blinding Paul and Paul's Jesus later blinding Elymas; the "kick against the goads" line and its appearance in Euripides' *Bacchae*; the light-from-heaven motif as the origin story of several later religions; the two-witness rule and Paul's lack of any witness to his conversion or apostleship; Matthew 24:23-27 as a warning against exactly a wilderness appearance; the Acts 1 criteria for apostleship; what "apostle" means (emissary, with a long Swedish-consulate analogy); Paul almost never quoting Jesus; and 2 Corinthians 12:7-9, the messenger of Satan Paul's Jesus declines to remove. Jen contributes her own conversion story as a contrast case (a year of seeking, then a real person reaching out), a reading of Sirach 1:22, and the question of how a man on his way to murder could be transformed and commissioned on the spot. The hosts lean on the Clementine literature (which they call Peter's writing or the Nazarene Acts) for Peter's argument that an apparition cannot authorize anyone to teach. The stream ends with the sermon-counting exercise (tally Paul citations against Jesus citations in any sermon) and the line that opens 4B: Christianity is a religion of Paul with Jesus as the mascot.

## Claims to Check

Specific statements a careful reader should verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation.

1. **Deuteronomy 13:1-5** [49:56]. Read accurately. The hosts' summary (God allows false prophets in order to test) is the plain sense of v. 3.
2. **Paul is the only person God chose while on his way to commit murder; Cyrus and Nebuchadnezzar are the only exceptions** [26:33] [28:07]. Moses had killed an Egyptian before the burning bush (Exodus 2:12), and David was chosen before, not after, his crimes. The pattern is defensible as stated about *intent at the moment of calling*, but "every single person was in a state of righteousness" needs Moses addressed.
3. **The Damascus road is the only time Jesus blinds anyone; God heals blindness, never causes it** [26:33] [1:19:33] [1:44:54]. True of the gospels. In the wider Bible, the angels at Sodom strike men blind (Genesis 19:11), Elisha's prayer blinds the Aramean army (2 Kings 6:18), and blindness is among the covenant curses (Deuteronomy 28:28). Acts 13:11 (Elymas) is accurately cited as the only other New Testament case.
4. **Acts 9, 22, 26 differ on who saw, who heard, who fell, and what Paul was told** [1:21:11] [1:29:02]. Accurate on all four points (9:7 vs. 22:9; 26:14 "we had all fallen"; 26:16-18 vs. 9:6 and 22:10). Already in the repo. Whether the differences show dishonesty or Luke's rhetorical shaping for three audiences is the interpretive question, and the repo has taken the second view.
5. **"Kick against the goads" is Dionysus's line from the Bacchae** [1:22:47]. The phrase is at *Bacchae* 795, spoken by Dionysus, but it is also in Aeschylus (*Agamemnon* 1624) and Pindar (*Pythian* 2.94-96); it was a Greek proverb. Also, the captions have Chris calling Dionysus "the son of Apollo"; Dionysus is the son of Zeus. Recap notes claim 15 covers the rest.
6. **Acts 26:18 is the first time "faith in me" language is heard from Jesus** [1:24:18]. "Sanctified by faith in me" is unique to Acts 26:18, but belief directed at Jesus is not absent from the gospels (John 14:1; 3:16-18; 6:29; Mark 9:42 "who believe in me"). State the narrower claim.
7. **In the Bible God and angels only ever appear to a waking man as men, never as a light** [1:27:28]. The burning bush (Exodus 3:2), the pillar of fire, and the glory on Sinai are counterexamples to "never a light"; the hosts' stronger point (no commissioning appearance to a non-seeker in the gospels' own warning terms) stands on Matthew 24 rather than on this generalization.
8. **Theophilus was the pagan magistrate of Nero** [51:29]. Unknown. Theophilus is unidentified; proposals range from a Roman patron to the high priest Theophilus ben Ananus (37-41 CE). No source names him a Neronian magistrate.
9. **Ananias in Acts 9 belongs to the high-priestly family that killed Jesus and James** [1:17:59]. The Ananias of Acts 9 is a disciple in Damascus. The high priest Ananias son of Nedebaios appears at Acts 23-24, a different man. Jesus's trial was under Caiaphas (son-in-law of Annas); James was killed under Ananus ben Ananus (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200); Simeon son of Clopas died under Trajan (Eusebius, *Church History* 3.32), not at the hands of Ananus's son. Chris himself says "maybe, maybe not"; keep it there.
10. **Christos was the chief title of Mithras and Greek borrowed it because it had no word for messiah** [1:33:45]. Not so. *Christos* is from *chriō*, "to anoint," and the Septuagint uses it to translate *mashiach* centuries before the Roman Mithras cult (e.g., 1 Samuel 2:10 LXX). Do not repeat.
11. **The Greek says stake (stauros), not cross** [1:00:51]. *Stauros* does mean an upright stake or pole; Roman crucifixion used several configurations, and the shape in Jesus's case is not recoverable from the word. The Jehovah's Witnesses make the same argument; it is linguistically fair and historically open.
12. **"This day I have begotten thee" stood in Matthew and Luke before Nicaea and was removed** [1:08:39]. The reading is a Western-text variant of Luke 3:22 (Codex Bezae, several Old Latin witnesses, quoted by Justin and Clement of Alexandria) and the Gospel of the Ebionites' version (*Panarion* 30.13.7). It is not attested for Matthew, and critical editions prefer the majority reading on manuscript grounds, not conciliar ones. A real variant; the "removed after Nicaea" mechanism is the inference.
13. **Oldest complete codex about 400 years after the events** [1:03:58]. Sinaiticus and Vaticanus are mid-fourth century, roughly 300 years after; papyri fragments are earlier. Adjust the number.
14. **Paul has more words than anyone in the Bible, including Jesus** [1:05:31] [1:13:17]. In the New Testament, Luke-Acts (one author) is longer than the thirteen Pauline letters combined; in the whole Bible, Moses's five books dwarf both. The accurate form is: Paul is the largest single voice among the *letter* writers, and his corpus is roughly comparable in size to Jesus's recorded words.
15. **Hebrews was only recently attributed to Barnabas** [1:07:05]. Tertullian attributed it to Barnabas around 200 CE (*De pudicitia* 20). The attribution is ancient, not recent.
16. **Luther wanted James out and later changed his mind** [34:22]. Luther's 1522 preface called James "an epistle of straw" compared to John, Romans, and 1 Peter; the remark was dropped from editions after 1534, though he never removed the letter. The direction of the host's claim is roughly right; the detail needs the dates.
17. **Dysnoētos means "nonsense" in Greek** [43:42]. Standard lexica give "hard to understand." Recap notes claim 35.
18. **A non-canonical Petrine text accuses the church of making a religion of blood** [59:19]. Not located in the Clementine Homilies, Recognitions, or the Epistle of Peter to James in the repo's full texts, nor in the Preaching of Peter fragments. The Recognitions (1.36-39) do say Moses permitted sacrifice temporarily and the Prophet would abolish it. Ask the hosts which text they had in mind.
19. **Ten to fifteen first- and second-century writers say the apostles and Paul did not get along** [43:42]. The actual early witnesses are fewer: Galatians 2 itself, the Epistle of Peter to James and the Clementines (second to fourth century), Epiphanius's report of the Ebionites, and Irenaeus's and Origen's notes that the Ebionites rejected Paul. Name them rather than count them.
20. **Paul disappeared into Arabia for fourteen years, then spent a week and a half with Peter** [1:40:12] [1:57:25]. Galatians 1:17-18: Arabia, back to Damascus, then after three years fifteen days with Peter in Jerusalem. The fourteen years (Galatians 2:1) run from that visit to the next. "Arabia as the Edomite stronghold" is geographically fair (Nabataean territory overlapped old Edom), but the two numbers are being merged.
21. **Herodion my kinsman; Paul in the Herodian family tree; Paul "Herod's uncle"; Agrippa as his cousin and step-brother** [1:41:44] [1:43:21]. Romans 16:11 says "Herodion my kinsman," which is the whole textual basis. The rest is Eisenman's reconstruction (via Josephus's "Saulus," kinsman of Agrippa, *Antiquities* 20.214; *War* 2.418) and the hosts' overnight video; "uncle" and "step-brother" are not in any source. Keep the verse and Eisenman separate from the rest.
22. **Rabbis who trace their lineage to Gamaliel laugh at Paul's Pharisee claim** [1:10:11]. The scholarly version is Hyam Maccoby, *The Mythmaker* (1986), who argued Paul was not a Pharisee on the grounds of his exegetical method and Septuagint usage. Mainstream scholarship (Sanders, Dunn, Tabor) accepts the claim. Cite Maccoby rather than unnamed rabbis.
23. **No one but Paul and the one man he trained ever calls Paul an apostle** [1:58:58]. Luke calls Paul and Barnabas apostles (Acts 14:4, 14), which is presumably the man meant. 2 Peter 3:15 calls him "brother," not apostle (the recap covers this). Accurate as stated, with Luke named.
24. **The office of apostle closed at twelve** [1:08:39] [1:58:58]. See the repo's retired-argument note above; Galatians 1:19, Romans 16:7, and Acts 14:14 use "apostle" for people outside the Twelve. The hosts' point survives in the form "Paul was not one of the Twelve and not an eyewitness."
25. **Jesus's actual apostles used the Hebrew scriptures; Paul quotes the Greek** [1:11:44]. Paul's citations do mostly follow the Septuagint. So do most Old Testament quotations in the gospels and in Peter's speeches as Acts reports them; the Septuagint was the common text of Greek-speaking Jews. The contrast is weaker than stated.
26. **Peter was the apostle to the Gentiles according to Jesus** [1:47:59]. Acts 10 and 15:7 have Peter opening the Gentile mission (Cornelius); Galatians 2:7-9 has the Jerusalem agreement assigning Peter to the circumcised. No saying of Jesus assigns the Gentiles to Peter specifically; Matthew 28:19 sends all the Twelve to all nations, which is the argument the repo's Deuteronomy 18 file makes.
27. **Some scholars say Paul wanted the high priest's daughter** [1:51:08]. It is Epiphanius's report of Ebionite tradition (*Panarion* 30.16.9), in the repo. Not a scholarly reconstruction.
28. **Peter says apparitions cannot authorize anyone to teach; angels always appear as men** [1:54:15] [1:55:49]. Clementine Homilies 17.13-19 for the first; the second is not a formulation found there (Homily 17.14-16 argues that visions come to the impious from evil demons and that the righteous see God face to face as a friend). Check the chapters before attributing the "always as men" rule to Peter.

30. **Sirach 1:22** [1:44:54]. Jen's reading matches Sirach 1:22 (NRSV: "Unjust anger cannot be justified, for anger tips the scale to one's ruin"). Accurate.
