## Summary

Despite the word "redo" in the title, this is not a re-recording of 4A. It is the second stream of the same evening: it opens on the sentence that closed 4A (Christianity is a religion of Paul with Jesus as the mascot) and ends when the platform's time limit hits, with Chris promising to re-read his first comparison item at the start of the next part. Jen is on and off camera with the baby; most of the hour and fifty minutes is Chris working from notes and reading two outside articles aloud.

The stream has three movements. The first [00:00] to about [17:13] is Chris's elevator pitch of the two salvation models. Paul's, which he says is the bedrock of Christianity, is Romans 10:9-10 plus Ephesians 2:8-9: confess, believe that God raised Jesus, saved by grace through faith and not works, with Jesus's death as a blood atonement under the Levitical model. Jesus's, which he takes from Matthew 19:16-22, Revelation 20, and the kingdom-at-hand sayings, is obedience to the Father's instructions, works worthy of repentance, and a kingdom already present. His test for the difference: remove Paul's letters and Jesus's salvation model is intact; remove the gospels and Christianity would not change.

The second movement [17:13] to [54:37] is a read-through of Douglas Del Tondo's article "Paul's Gospel vs. the Gospel of Jesus," with running commentary. The article treats 1 Corinthians 15:1-11 as a creed of two facts (Jesus died for sins; Jesus was raised) whose acceptance Paul equates with salvation, and sets against it Luke 4:43 (Jesus already preaching the gospel of the kingdom), Zacchaeus (salvation declared before the crucifixion, after a work of restitution prescribed in Torah), and the thief on the cross (saved by acknowledging Jesus as king, not by the two facts). The line the hosts seize on is that in Jesus's gospel you do not save yourself; obedience leads to a worthiness that God's favor then saves. Along the way Chris gives the Sha'ul/Sheol and Paulus/least name argument in detail, Jen reads Sirach 35 from the HalleluYah Scriptures, and Chris reads Exodus 20:5-6 to argue that mercy and grace are a Torah concept, not a Pauline discovery.

The third movement [54:37] to the end collects the hosts' Revelation material and the start of a second article. Chris describes Christianity as Paul's doctrine with Jesus as the mascot and lists the practices he thinks prove it (Sunday, Christmas, the Eucharist). He pairs Matthew 19:16-22 with Ephesians 2:8-9, reads Acts 1:21-26 for the apostleship criteria, and then builds his Revelation 2 case: John 5:43 (one who comes in his own name), 1 Corinthians 8:4-8 on idol meat against Revelation 2:14 and the teaching of Balaam, the Nicolaitans (which he says an Aramaic scholar reads as a nickname meaning those who eat whatever), and Revelation 2:1-2 (Ephesus tested false apostles) against 2 Timothy 1:15 and Acts 19:8-10. An audience question about abstaining from meat draws out the hosts' own vegetarianism and the Genesis/Watchers reasoning behind it, which Chris says he had cut from his notes to keep the night from being more provocative than necessary. He shows Robert Eisenman's *The New Testament Code* for its Herodian family tree, then begins Edgar Jones's list of twenty-five contradictory statements between Paul and Jesus; item one (Romans 10:4, Ephesians 2:15, and Galatians 3:24-25 against Matthew 5:17-18) is read before the stream cuts off.

### Jesus's salvation model

## Claims to Check

Specific statements a careful reader should verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation.

1. **Romans 10:9-10, Ephesians 2:8-9, Matthew 19:16-22, 1 Corinthians 15:1-11, Luke 4:43, Luke 19:8-9, Luke 23:39-43, Exodus 20:5-6, Matthew 5:23-24, Acts 1:21-26, Revelation 2:1-2, 2 Timothy 1:15, Acts 19:8-10, 1 Corinthians 8:4-8, Revelation 2:14, Romans 10:4, Ephesians 2:15, Galatians 3:24-25, Matthew 5:17-18.** All read or paraphrased accurately from the RSV or close to it. The interpretive load is in the pairings, not the texts.
2. **Remove the gospels and nothing about Christianity would change** [06:16]. Rhetorical, and the hosts know it; the creeds, the liturgical year, and the sacraments all draw on the gospels. The defensible form is the one they also give: the *salvation formula* of mainstream Christianity comes from Paul, not from anything Jesus is recorded saying.
3. **Paulus is a double diminutive meaning the least** [31:12]. *Paulus* is an ordinary Latin adjective, "small, little"; the diminutive is *paullulus*. "Least" is a gloss, not the word's meaning, and the Spanish -itito analogy overstates it. The Matthew 5:19 wordplay works in English on "least" and in Greek on *elachistos* (both verses), not through Latin. The recap's claim 10 covers Sha'ul: same consonants as Sheol, different word ("asked for"), and the name of Israel's first king.
4. **Greek scholars acknowledge that 1 Corinthians 15:9 is a nod to Paul's name** [29:38]. Some commentators note the possible pun (Paul uses *elachistos* and *mikros*-type language of himself elsewhere); it is a suggestion, not a consensus. Name a commentator.
5. **Tertullian, against Marcion, says Paul is not an apostle and does not fit the criteria** [09:28]. Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 5.1, challenges Marcion to prove Paul's apostleship from Marcion's own canon and remarks that a man cannot be both claimant and witness in his own case. It is a dialectical move against Marcion; Tertullian himself accepted Paul and quotes him constantly. Cite the passage, not the conclusion.
6. **Christos was the title of Mithras and the hosts' Christo-Mithras bull imagery** [12:35]. Addressed under 4A's Claims to Check (item 10): *Christos* translates *mashiach* in the Septuagint and has nothing to do with Mithras.
7. **Constantine, via the Edict of Milan and Nicaea, made Sunday canon law punishable by death** [56:10]. The Edict of Milan (313) granted toleration; Constantine's Sunday rest law is 321 CE (*Codex Justinianus* 3.12.2); Nicaea (325) dealt with the Easter date and the Arian question, not the Sabbath. The anti-Sabbath canon is Laodicea (c. 363, canon 29). Capital penalties for Sabbath-keeping are not in any of these. Rebuild the sentence from those dates.
8. **Christmas falls on Mithras's birthday** [57:43]. December 25 was the Roman *Natalis Solis Invicti* (attested 354 CE; the festival of Sol Invictus was promoted by Aurelian in 274). The specific link to Mithras rests on the Sol-Mithras association and is weaker than the Sol Invictus link. Say Sol Invictus.
9. **Sirach was in every Bible until the Church of England removed it** [51:32]. Sirach was in the Septuagint, the Vulgate, Luther's Bible (in a separate Apocrypha section, 1534), and the 1611 King James (in its Apocrypha). The Westminster Confession (1647) declared the Apocrypha non-canonical; British and Foreign Bible Society editions dropped the section in the nineteenth century. Jen's attribution to the English church is roughly right in direction; the mechanism was Protestant, gradual, and not specifically Anglican.
10. **Revelation 2:14 is addressed to Thyatira** [1:11:42]. The Balaam charge is to Pergamum (2:12-17); Thyatira's letter (2:18-29) has the parallel charge about Jezebel teaching the eating of idol food. Two churches, both correct for the hosts' point; the name was wrong in the moment.
11. **Two to four of the seven churches are warned about idol meat** [1:08:34]. Two: Pergamum (2:14) and Thyatira (2:20). Ephesus (2:6) and Pergamum (2:15) are the two with Nicolaitans.
12. **Nicolaitans is an Aramaic nickname for those who eat whatever** [1:22:36]. The name is Greek in form; the ancient sources (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.3; Epiphanius, *Panarion* 25, in the repo) derive it from a Nicolaus, and the popular modern etymology is *nikaō* + *laos*, "conquer the people." No Aramaic derivation is attested. Get the book's title and author before repeating; it is otherwise an unsourced etymology.
13. **The Dead Sea Scrolls community identifies Paul as the Spouter of Lies** [54:37]. The Liar / Spouter of Lies (CD 1, 4, 8, 20; 1QpHab 2, 5, 10) is dated by nearly all scholars to the second century BCE. Robert Eisenman alone redates the sectarian scrolls to the first century CE and identifies the Liar with Paul. Cite Eisenman and say it is his minority view; the repo's own DSS files take the mainstream date.
14. **Eisenman is the reason anyone has the Dead Sea Scrolls** [1:36:36]. Eisenman was prominent in the 1989-1991 campaign that ended the editorial monopoly on the unpublished Cave 4 fragments (via *Biblical Archaeology Review* and the Huntington Library photographs). The scrolls themselves were found in 1947 and largely published before that. "Central to breaking the publication monopoly" is the accurate form.
15. **Paul says both things; he has a forked tongue; 50,000 denominations come from interpreting him** [34:21]. That Paul's statements on law and works pull in two directions is conceded in the repo (`questions/pauls-legitimacy.md`, Romans 2:6-13 beside Romans 3:28). "50,000 denominations" is the common figure from the World Christian Encyclopedia's count of denominational bodies (around 45,000 in 2019), most of which differ on polity and ethnicity rather than soteriology. Use the number with that caveat.
16. **Peter was the apostle to the Gentiles according to Jesus; Paul only ever spoke to Jews** [1:31:54] [1:33:27]. See 4A's item 26 for Peter. Acts has Paul begin in synagogues but also address Gentile crowds (Lystra, Acts 14:8-18; Athens, Acts 17:16-34) and found largely Gentile congregations (1 Corinthians 12:2; 1 Thessalonians 1:9). The synagogue-first pattern is real; "only Jews" is not.
17. **Acts 24: Paul affirms the law only when on trial, one or two times against thousands of denials** [1:30:20]. Acts 21:20-26 and 24:14-18 are the trial-context affirmations; Romans 3:31, 7:12, and 13:8-10 are affirmations in letters with no trial in view. The tension is real and the repo documents it; the count is rhetoric.
18. **The Clementine frame story: Clement hears Matthias in Rome and James commissions his journals** [11:00]. In the Recognitions (1.6-13) Clement hears Barnabas in Rome; in the Homilies (1.9-14) he hears Barnabas in Alexandria. Matthias is a caption mishearing or slip. James's commission to write is in the Epistle of Clement to James (prefixed to the Homilies) and Recognitions 3.74-75, where Peter orders the books sent to James. 19. **Peter only ever ate herbs and bread; James was an avid vegetarian** [1:19:29]. Homilies 12.6 (bread, olives, seldom pot-herbs) and Hegesippus in Eusebius, *Church History* 2.23.5 (no wine, no flesh). Both accurate; both in the repo's full texts and cards.
20. **Clean and unclean appear only a thousand years after Genesis 1, after the Watchers** [1:17:55]. Genesis 7:2 (clean animals at the flood) is the first use; on the Masoretic chronology the flood is 1,656 years after creation. The Watchers' descent in the days of Jared is Jubilees 4:15 and 1 Enoch 6:6 (recap claim 27). The "thousand years" is loose; the sequence is as stated.
21. **Jubilees says God made twenty-two kinds** [1:17:55]. Jubilees 2:15-23: twenty-two kinds of work in creation, matched to the twenty-two generations to Jacob and the twenty-two letters. It is about works of creation, not animal categories; the hosts' category reading is an interpretation.

The second and third points are accurate (Hebrews 10 itself concedes the system was still operating; Acts 21 has Paul funding offerings). 24. **Jesus only ever cursed a fig tree; James says do not curse; Paul curses dissenters** [1:44:30]. Matthew 21:19 and Mark 11:14, James 3:9-10, Galatians 1:8-9.
