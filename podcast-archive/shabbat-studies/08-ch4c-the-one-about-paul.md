## Key Teachings

### Opening frame: the rapture and the direction of taking away

- Chris's claim: every rapture-like passage in the New Testament comes from Paul; when Jesus speaks of a taking away, the wicked are removed and the righteous remain to inherit the earth, with a renewed heaven and earth coming down to a damaged earth rather than the faithful leaving it. Paul inverts the direction. He says he has not zeroed this discrepancy in and is going for the lowest-hanging fruit. [00:00] [01:35]
- To a viewer raised to think the rapture is a deception: the word rapture is not in the text; *harpazō* (a catching up) is, in Paul. The *Left Behind* version is a modern construction. Jesus's agricultural images (threshing floor, weeds) show the evil leaving and the righteous staying. [03:12]
- The Jeremiah precedent: in Jeremiah's day a whole region of God's people were following figures they took for prophets while, in the hosts' telling, only Jeremiah was speaking for God; so a situation in which almost everyone follows a false prophet has happened before. [01:35]

### Marcionism at one remove

- Chris's account: Marcion is credited with the first canon, around 144, consisting of Paul; the Roman canon came about 150 years later (397). Marcion held Paul to be the only apostle and the twelve irrelevant. The church condemned him, but in practice modern Christianity quotes Paul, rarely the twelve, and barely Jesus, which Chris calls Marcionism once removed. His analogy is an institution publicly disowning a program while continuing it. [06:19] [07:52]
- The underlying point: readers were told by the Roman church that the book is infallible and that Paul must be validating Jesus, so they never hold Paul's words up against Jesus's. [06:19]

## Claims to Check

Specific statements a careful reader should verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation.

1. **The rapture appears only in Paul; Jesus's "taken" are the wicked** [00:00] [03:12]. The direction argument is strong (Matthew 24:37-41 with the flood; Luke 17:34-37 with the vultures) and is the repo's position. Note that John 14:3 ("I will take you to myself") is the verse defenders reach for outside Paul; have an answer ready.
2. **Jeremiah was the only true prophet of his day** [01:35]. Overstated: Uriah son of Shemaiah (Jeremiah 26:20-23), Ezekiel, Habakkuk, and Zephaniah were contemporaries. The point that most of the recognized prophets were false (Jeremiah 23) stands.
3. **Marcion, c. 144, first canon, "only Paul"; Roman canon 397** [06:19]. Marcion's canon was an edited Luke plus ten Pauline letters, not Paul alone (Chris says this correctly in Part D). 144 is the traditional date of his break with the Roman church. Carthage 397 is one of several canon markers (see the recap's Claims to Check 6).
4. **Edgar Jones's list** [03:12]. The source is Edgar Jones's "Paul versus Jesus: A List of Contradictory and Compatible Statements," published on his Voice of Jesus website. Cite it as a web source, and check each pairing against the texts; Jones's and Chris's verse references are mostly accurate (see below for the exceptions).
5. **"Believeth" in John 3:16 is Greek for "obeys"** [14:04]. *Pisteuō* means believe or trust. The nearest textual support is John 3:36, where "believes" is paralleled with "does not obey" (*apeitheō*), and the general Johannine linkage of belief and keeping commandments (14:15). State it as a semantic argument from context, not a translation fact.
6. **"God suffers that no man die but that everyone come to repentance" is in the Tanakh** [15:36] [17:11]. The wording is 2 Peter 3:9. The Tanakh form is Ezekiel 18:23, 32 and 33:11 (God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked). Cite Ezekiel.
7. **Romans 3:10-12, "as it is written," source unknown** [41:59]. Paul is quoting Psalm 14:1-3 (= Psalm 53:1-3), with Ecclesiastes 7:20 and Psalm 5:9, 140:3, 10:7, 36:1, Isaiah 59:7-8 in the following verses. The Psalm itself distinguishes the fool from "my people" and "the righteous" (14:4-5), which is the out-of-context point Chris suspected.
8. **Zechariah in Matthew 23:35 is the righteous priest of the gospels** [43:33]. Matthew 23:35's Zechariah son of Barachiah is the murdered prophet of 2 Chronicles 24:20-22 (son of Jehoiada) or the postexilic prophet, not John the Baptist's father (Luke 1:5-6), whom Chris seems to have in mind. Luke 1:6 does call that Zechariah righteous, so the broader point survives with the right verse.
9. **1 Corinthians 10:33, "saved by my gospel"** [28:01]. The verse says "that they may be saved"; "my gospel" is Romans 2:16 and 16:25 (and 2 Timothy 2:8). Two verses run together.
10. **Mauck's thesis** [31:08]. John W. Mauck, *Paul on Trial* (2001), does argue Luke-Acts was composed as a legal defense of Paul for a Roman official named Theophilus. Mauck is a Christian lawyer defending Acts' reliability; the extensions (Acts never meant for Christians; Luke wrote his gospel afterward to correct the record; Luke's gospel the most anti-Pauline) are the hosts' own and have no scholarly sponsor named on air.
11. **The "python priestess" who vouches for Paul in Acts** [32:42]. Acts 16:16-18 describes a slave girl with a spirit of python who follows Paul proclaiming his party servants of God, whom Paul then exorcises. The twenty-foot tripod, paid consultations, and Delphi are details of the Pythia at Delphi, not of this episode. The structural observation (a pagan spirit testifying for Paul) is in the text; the rest is conflation.
12. **"Luke was hired by Herod's rich relative"** [34:14]. No source. Luke as Paul's companion rests on Colossians 4:14, Philemon 24, 2 Timothy 4:11 and the "we" passages of Acts.
13. **The Gospel of the Ebionites line, "if you do not cease sacrificing, wrath will not cease from you," is in the Clementine Homilies** [51:21] [56:05]. It is the Gospel of the Ebionites fragment preserved by Epiphanius (*Panarion* 30.16.5), not the Homilies. The Recognitions (1.37, 1.39) make a related argument. The repo has both texts; cite Epiphanius.
14. **Jesus killed under Nero** [51:21]. The sentence runs together Nero's cruelties with "who killed God." Jesus died under Tiberius, with Pilate as prefect (c. 30-33). Nero (54-68) is the emperor of Paul's trial and the first persecution. The Nero anecdotes (the pregnant Poppaea kicked to death, the marriage to Sporus) are from Suetonius and Dio and are standard, if lurid.
15. **Deuteronomy 25:4 and 1 Corinthians 9:9-10 / 1 Timothy 5:18** [56:05]. Accurate on both sides; Paul explicitly says the ox is not the point (1 Corinthians 9:9-10). `OPEN-RESEARCH.md` §2 already lists this among Paul's four misappropriated Hebrew texts (Tabor).
16. **Mark 7:19 "declared all foods clean" is found in no Greek manuscript** [1:32:03]. Incorrect as stated. The words *katharizōn panta ta brōmata* are in the Greek manuscripts. The dispute is grammatical: the masculine participle is read by most modern editors as Mark's editorial comment about Jesus (hence the parenthesis), while the KJV construed it with the digestive process ("purging all meats"). Jen's version (a parenthetical editorial note that translations stopped bracketing) is the accurate one. The repo's answer is in `questions/jesus-and-the-torah.md`.
17. **Acts 10 is about men, not food** [1:35:10] [1:38:17]. Supported by Acts 10:28 and 11:12 in Peter's own interpretation. Acts 10:14 ("Not so, Lord") is still the text opponents press (see `questions/MINING-2026-10-debates.md`).
18. **Peter broke table fellowship at Antioch to protect his authority over demons** [1:35:10] [1:36:43]. The Clementine Homilies (7.3-4, 7.8; 13.4) do give table-of-demons and purity reasons for Peter's refusal to eat with Gentiles who have not been baptized or who eat idol meat. They do not narrate Antioch. The application to Galatians 2 is the hosts' synthesis; label it as such.
19. **Everyone who has looked critically agrees Stephen is James** [1:16:30]. This is Eisenman's thesis (*James the Brother of Jesus*, drawing on Recognitions 1.70-71 and Hegesippus's account of James's death). It is a minority position; most scholars treat Stephen as a distinct figure. The repo elsewhere uses Stephen's speech as evidence (see Overlap).
20. **The Dead Sea Scrolls were held under lock and key for fifty years by the "Rockefeller Institute," all but one of the editors Catholic** [1:19:37]. Partly right. The Cave 4 fragments were controlled by a small editorial team based at the Palestine Archaeological Museum (the Rockefeller Museum, East Jerusalem) and most remained unpublished until 1991, roughly forty years. The team included Catholic priests (de Vaux, Milik, Starcky, Benoit, Skehan) and Protestants (Allegro, Cross, Strugnell). Eisenman, with Hershel Shanks's *Biblical Archaeology Review*, the Huntington Library, and the Eisenman-Robinson facsimile edition (1991), was central to breaking the monopoly. Eisenman was not a lone campaigner, but he was a principal one.
21. **Eisenman's chapter title "The Anti-Semitic Peter"** [1:21:11]. Verify the title and pagination in the 2006 edition before citing; the passage read on air (Acts 10, "in work or purse," Galatians 2:13) is consistent with Eisenman's argument in both *James the Brother of Jesus* and *The New Testament Code*.
22. **The Paul-Seneca correspondence** [1:10:15]. The fourteen letters between Paul and Seneca are a fourth-century Latin composition, regarded as spurious by essentially all scholars (Jerome mentions them; Erasmus rejected them). Do not use as evidence of Paul's connections.
23. **The Sonnini manuscript / Paul in Gaul and Britain with the Druids** [1:10:15]. The "Long Lost Chapter of the Acts of the Apostles" (Acts 29), published by C. S. Sonnini in 1801, is regarded as a modern forgery. Do not use.
24. **Paul in the Herodian dynasty** [1:10:15] [1:46:09]. Romans 16:11 (Herodion, "my kinsman") is the textual hook; *syngenēs* in Romans 9:3 and 16:7, 21 means fellow Jew. The Herodian-Paul thesis is Eisenman's and is not mainstream. The-jesus-way 023 uses the same argument. Label as Eisenman.
25. **The Sadducees were Edomite through and through; the priesthood was pay-to-play** [1:47:48]. Herod was Idumean and appointed high priests from favored families (Boethus, Ananus), and Josephus describes the office being bought under the procurators. Calling the Sadducean priesthood Edomite by descent goes beyond the sources.
