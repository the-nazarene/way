## Claims to Check

Specific statements a careful reader should verify before repeating. "Check" does not mean wrong; several are right and only need a citation.

1. **Jesus's women and Paul's women** [01:33] [03:07]. The "woman at the well" (John 4) and the woman caught in adultery (John 8) are run together; the stoning episode is John 7:53-8:11, which is textually suspect (recap's Claims to Check 17). On Paul's side: 1 Corinthians 14:34-35 is widely argued to be an interpolation and 1 Timothy is disputed Pauline, so the silence passages may not be Paul's; and the undisputed letters name Phoebe a deacon, Junia an apostle, and Priscilla a co-worker (Romans 16:1-7), and assume women praying and prophesying (1 Corinthians 11:5). "Paul never supports women" needs qualification; "the head-covering and silence texts are Pauline-stream opinion, not Jesus" is defensible.
2. **Marcion: early second-century bishop; Paul the only apostle; Gnostic; canon of Paul and Luke** [06:19] [09:31] [11:06]. Marcion (c. 85-160, from Sinope) was a shipowner and the son of a bishop by some accounts, active in Rome c. 140, expelled 144; his canon was an edited Luke plus ten Paulines; he is usually distinguished from Gnosticism proper (no aeons, no secret gnosis) though he shared the two-God structure. "Colossians the most Gnostic book" is an opinion some scholars voice about Colossians' opponents, not a consensus.
3. **Tertullian "slams Paul" and denies his apostolic authority** [14:12]. Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 5.1, does demand proof of Paul's apostleship and calls him the apostle of the heretics (3.5), but he accepts Paul and argues Marcion misreads him. Use the rhetorical challenge, not the conclusion. The repo cites the same passage.
4. **Paul's docetism from Philippians 2:6-8 and Romans 8:3** [14:12] [1:58:51] [2:00:25]. Marcion did lean on this language, and *homoiōma* (likeness) is what docetists used. But Paul also writes "born of a woman" (Galatians 4:4), "descended from David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3), and "the man Christ Jesus" (Romans 5:15; 1 Corinthians 15:21), which docetists had to excise. Present it as "language Marcion could use," not "Paul denied the flesh."
5. **The Vujicic quotations** [20:27] to [37:32]. Each needs a page reference before reuse: the "demoniacal hallucination" article (likely the 1909 *Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Ebionites"); *Encyclopaedia Judaica* (article and edition); Hughes, *A History of the Church*, vol. 1, p. 57; Dunn (probably *Unity and Diversity in the New Testament*); Boettner, *Roman Catholicism* (1962); Lockyer, *All the Apostles of the Bible* (1972). The patristic core (Irenaeus 1.26.2; Origen, *Contra Celsum* 5.65; Eusebius, *HE* 3.27; Epiphanius 30.16, 30.25) is accurate and in the repo.
6. **Orthodoxy imposed under penalty of death; thousands butchered; Constantine** [29:42] [31:15]. Constantine exiled Arius and burned his books but did not execute dissenters en masse; the first recorded execution for heresy is Priscillian (385). Large-scale killing of heretics is medieval. The point about orthodoxy meaning the winning side's view stands without the inflated history.
7. **Nazarenes excommunicated; the daily prayer's curse on the minim** [35:59]. Standard scholarship (the Birkat ha-Minim, usually dated to the late first or early second century; some Genizah versions name the *notzrim*). Cite as the hosts state it, with the caveat that the dating and target are debated.
8. **Two Shabbat candles for the two houses** [42:13]. The traditional rabbinic reasons are *zakhor* and *shamor* (Exodus 20:8; Deuteronomy 5:12). The two-houses reading is a Messianic/Hebrew Roots interpretation.
9. **Gad is the Igbo tribe** [42:13]. A claim within Igbo-Jewish identity movements, not established history. Chris mentions it in passing; do not repeat as fact.
10. **Who Is Esau-Edom** [45:21] [2:48:51]. Charles A. Weisman's 1991 book comes out of the Christian Identity movement and identifies modern Jewry with Edom. The hosts explicitly disclaim antisemitism and use the book for the Herodian/Idumean point; readers should know the book's provenance before citing it, and the Idumean ancestry of the Herods can be cited from Josephus (*Antiquities* 14.8-9) without it.
11. **Romans 16:11, Herodion "my kinsman," as a Herodian family greeting** [46:54]. *Syngenēs* in Romans 9:3 and 16:7, 21 means fellow Jew. The name Herodion suggests a connection to the Herodian household (perhaps a freedman), which is the most that can be said from the text. The Herodian-Paul thesis is Eisenman's.
12. **Paul never quotes Jesus; all his companions have Greek names; he always uses the Septuagint** [50:00]. Paul cites Jesus's teaching in 1 Corinthians 7:10-11, 9:14, and 11:23-25 (the repo says "about twice"); Romans 16 names Jewish co-workers (Aquila, Priscilla, Mary, Andronicus, Junia, Herodion, Jason, Sosipater). Septuagint dependence is broadly accurate. Tighten "never" and "all."
