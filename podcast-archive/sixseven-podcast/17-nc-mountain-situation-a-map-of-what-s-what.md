## Summary

Recorded Thursday, October 3, 2024, a week after Hurricane Helene, and released two days later. Chris opens with a recording of his first call with his friend in the Burnsville area, who reached him from a fire department where a private helicopter had dropped off a Starlink: no communication or way out of the valley, roads gone, landslides, houses lost, people evacuated, no power or running water, and appliances lodged thirty feet up in trees by the rising water. Chris then walks through a 3D satellite map of western North Carolina (Asheville, Black Mountain, Montreat, Swannanoa, Spruce Pine, Burnsville, Chimney Rock, Lake Lure, Waynesville) to explain why some places were ruined and others were fine. His explanation is topography: each valley is a "cereal bowl" that filled with rain and spilled downhill, so damage depended on where a house sat relative to the flow. He relays his friend's report of 106 mph winds at the top of Mount Mitchell and 30 inches of rain, and the friend's view that it was not natural, together with the observation that the community is coming together. He places the Kings Mountain lithium mine near Charlotte, outside the mountains and largely unaffected, to answer online talk that ties the lithium mine to the flooding, and he describes a viral rescue post from Black Mountain whose author he called, who told him everyone was rescued. He relays an unconfirmed figure of 900 unaccounted-for people at one hospital in North Asheville, against an official toll then reported at two or three hundred, and closes with thanks that the people in the viral post got out.

## Claims to Check

1. **30 inches of rain in about 10 hours, or in a night** (01:29, 09:26, 12:50). National Weather Service totals for Helene in western North Carolina peaked around 30 inches over roughly three days (September 25-27, 2024), most of it on the last day. Same as episode 16.
2. **106 mph winds at the top of Mount Mitchell** (12:50). A gust of about that speed was widely reported; the official station record should be checked.
3. **900 unaccounted for at one hospital in North Asheville, against a toll of two or three hundred** (20:11). Something he was told; no source. North Carolina's official Helene death count rose past 100 in the following weeks; national counts passed 200.
4. **Montreat underwater and gone; Swannanoa gone** (08:35, 10:27). Reports he relays; Swannanoa and parts of Black Mountain were among the hardest hit.
5. **Asheville's population 94,589 in 2020** (17:34). Matches the 2020 census.
6. **I-40 as "the largest highway in the country"** (14:15). I-40 is the third-longest Interstate; I-90 is the longest.
7. **Kings Mountain as a Charlotte suburb with the lithium mine, about 70 miles from Black Mountain** (18:29, 30:21). The Kings Mountain mine (Albemarle) is about 30 miles west of Charlotte; the distance from there to Black Mountain should be measured.
8. **"That's not natural. That's not an act of God"** (12:50). The friend's view, also in episode 16; no mechanism is proposed here.
9. **The viral Black Mountain rescue post** (27:07). He says he called its author, who said everyone was rescued.
