## 2. New holes — things the cards cannot currently answer

Ordered by how much damage they do.

### HOLE 5 — Isaiah 25:6, the meat banquet on the same mountain

> *"And in this mountain shall the LORD of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things… of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined."* — Isaiah 25:6, verified at `christianity/Incoming/kjv/23-isaiah.md:559`

Jones raised it in one interrupted sentence (5 @ 120:22), was cut off with "whoa whoa friend," and the host moved on. **He took the point anyway.**

This is the most damaging text in ten hours of debate. **Same mountain as Isaiah 11:9** (`:286`, "they shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain") **and Isaiah 56:7** (`:1288`, "my holy mountain… house of prayer"). The whole peaceable-kingdom/holy-mountain architecture has an eschatological meat-and-wine feast sitting fourteen chapters later, in the same prophet. **It appears nowhere in this repo in any form.**

### "Death before humanity" — the objection that is upstream of everything

**Correction, logged 2026-10-16 after this section was written.** Recording 1 is a **ten-minute clip**, uploaded 2025-08-23. The same two people have a **2h39m full debate** — Digital Social Hour #1448, 2025-07-15 — which was not read for this report. Both are now in the repo at `podcast-archive/kameron-waters/guest-appearances/` as 02 and 01 respectively.

What that changes: the *gap* finding stands on its own, because the repo demonstrably has nothing on death before the fall regardless of what any recording contains. What it softens is the characterization of how Kam handled it — the judgment below is drawn from ten minutes and should not be taken as his full answer until someone reads recording 01. **Read the long one before anyone repeats "he had no answer."**

The clip itself: the critic sets a trap, Kam walks in, and the last six minutes are an uninterrupted monologue with Kam reduced to "Mhm" and "Right."

The hinge (1 @ 03:58): *"Was there death in the time of Adam and Eve before the fall?"* Kam: *"Reading the text plainly, death was not a part of the picture"* — a plain-reading literalism he had declined two sentences earlier ("both literal and metaphorical," @ 03:43). The critic then spends five minutes making plain-reading look like a 14-year-old's bookshelf, using **Origen, Augustine and Aquinas**, who all held there *was* death before the fall, plus dinosaurs, plus the classical move that natural evil came from the fall of the angels.

Why this is urgent out of proportion to its runtime: Isaiah 11 is named in recording 3 @ 43:01 as *"the crux of our entire thesis,"* and the *netser*→Nazarene etymology rests on Isaiah 11:1. **If Eden was never bloodless, the Eden→Kingdom frame loses its anchor.** The attack also comes from the Catholic/Orthodox direction, not the evangelical one — it grants deep time and historical criticism and still keeps death in Eden. The bank is built against Protestant apologists.

Cheap to fix, and Kam's own first answer was already most of the way there before he abandoned it: concede pre-human animal death entirely (it costs nothing), refuse the inference, and name the asymmetry — Isaiah 11 and Genesis 1:29 are **prescriptive about the kingdom, not descriptive of the Jurassic.**

### "There is no death-free meal"

> *"Unless the only things you eat are from your specifically grown garden, I don't want to hear nothing about death-free meals… I'm guilty of much more death than you are. Sure, you're better than me, but does that really matter?"* — Ray Hicks, 4 @ 148:51

The card leans on the non-harm framing three separate times and never defends it against unavoidability. The attack doesn't dispute that killing is harm; it denies that any threshold is *principled*, converting an ethic into a preference.

**The answer is already in our own primary texts and nobody used it.** *Hom.* 3.45 — displeased with the **slaughtering** of animals, "not wishing them to be slain" — makes the objection intentional killing, not all death. *Hom.* 8.15 — "that they might not **through want of food** turn to the eating of animals" — makes necessity the hinge. The second-century Jewish-Christian sources draw the intention/necessity line the modern debate flails at.

## 5. Assets we already hold that nobody used

This is the most frustrating section. In a three-hour debate the opponent said *"I never hear you quote the Didache"* and then **got the Didache backwards** — he claimed it permits meat offered to idols. Our own Tier 1 file, `christianity/Incoming/didache-full-text.md:93`, reads: *"Now about food: undertake what you can. But keep strictly away from what is offered to idols, for that implies worshiping dead gods."* **The biggest unexploited rebuttal in ten hours, sitting in a file we already hold.**

Also unused:

- ***Hom.* 12.6** — Peter: *"I use only bread and olives, and rarely pot-herbs"* — held verbatim at `clementine-homilies-full-text.md:7208`. Kam said *"Peter clearly ate only plants and I can give you multiple instances"* (5 @ 37:04) and then gave none. **The Clementines are never mentioned once in ten hours.**
- ***Rec.* 1.39**, titled *"Baptism Instituted in Place of Sacrifices"* — "purified not by the blood of beasts." Kam argued exactly this point at 5 @ 107:50 from nothing.
- **Romans 14:2** — "another, who is weak, eateth herbs." Paul, c. 57 CE, against his own interest, in a debate about whether early Christian vegetarians existed. `kjv/45-romans.md:408`.
- **Thomas 12** (James succession) — the answer to *"the only thing we know about the Ebionites is that they* claim *to follow James."* **The Gospel of Thomas is never mentioned once in 41,000 words, by either side** — source #2 in our hierarchy, absent from the biggest debate on the question.
- **Eusebius, *Demonstratio* 1.10**, read on air by Abke (2 @ 102:37): *"he did not enjoin sacrifices or require them… nor did he lay down any other of the various precepts of Moses except the spiritual laws."* The repo holds *DE* **3.5** only, and **1.10 is stronger for the sacrifice card.**

## 8. The uncomfortable finding

Recording 4 is the one to sit with. Ray Hicks, the carnivore, **concedes every premise and keeps the conclusion.** He grants that humans aren't carnivores (@ 74:28), that meat isn't necessary (@ 31:27), that Eden was plant-prescribed (@ 43:47), that Eden is the ideal (@ 54:53), that this makes him *"a hypocrite"* (@ 59:26), that might-makes-right is a fallacy (@ 27:28), and *"I definitely have moral inconsistencies"* (@ 138:18).

He does not budge. The load is carried by hierarchy, permission, and the fact that the diet stopped him wanting to die.

Two and a half hours of the strongest available case, delivered by a prepared advocate, produced exactly one millimetre of movement: *"maybe it's not worth eating at some franchise where there's 500 cows in one patty"* (@ 148:10).

For a repo whose entire method is textual, that is worth writing down. **Winning the exegesis did not move him.** It belongs as a pastoral datum on `what-we-actually-offer.md` — which is already the card that says the room is won or lost on the pastoral question, and now has evidence.
