# Objections Bank — what critics have actually said, and where we answer it

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Type:** Index (objections → answers)
**Contributed by:** Matt Fracek, 2026-10-08 — built from the response episodes in `podcast-archive/` and Kam Waters' two conversations with Dr. Pete

The topic cards each carry a "Questions People Raise" section written from the strongest traditional case. This file is different: it lists objections **a named critic actually raised**, in the words they used, with a pointer to the moment, and says whether the collection answers it. The cards are what we would say. This is what we have been asked.

**How to read a pointer.** `kam-04 · "did Jesus eat lamb on Pasca"` means the file for Kam's video 04 in `podcast-archive/kameron-waters/`, at the line containing that exact phrase. `grep -F` the phrase and you are there. Episode numbers alone (`026`) are Jesus Way episodes. Once the timestamped re-pull of the archive lands (in progress, 2026-10-08), a pointer can also be `026 @ 41:12`.

**Status.** ANSWERED: a card has a prepared response, named by heading. THIN: a card touches it but would not hold up as written. OPEN: nothing prepared. Each OPEN has a brief at the bottom; the work is listed in `OPEN-RESEARCH.md`.

**Standing rule, stated again because it applies here most of all:** the transcripts are the hosts *restating* a critic. Until the critic's own words are archived (`OPEN-RESEARCH.md` item 6), every objection below is one step removed from its source.

## Open — briefs for the ones we cannot yet answer

**A. "Christ came for people, not animals."** (row 3) The honest answer concedes the priority and then shows that the texts never separate the two: Hosea 6:6 and its two uses by Jesus are about *mercy* as the thing God wants instead of sacrifice; Isaiah 11:6-9 and Genesis 1:29 frame the kingdom as the end of killing; Matthew 12:11-12 and Luke 13:15 argue from the animal to the person; the Clementine Homilies 3.45 put the displeasure with slaughter in Jesus's own mouth (Tier 1, held). The claim to make is narrow: the gospel is for people, and its content includes how people treat what is in their power. What a good answer needs: one paragraph on `sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md` under "Questions People Raise," with those five references checked in the held texts.

**B. The monastic gradient.** (row 4) Dr. Pete's position: the church kept the abstinence for the monks and the fasts, which proves the tradition and also proves it was never meant for everyone. A good answer does three things. It grants the history (the monastic diet *is* the preserved Nazarene practice, and that is evidence for us). It shows that the gradient is Orthodoxy's later arrangement, not the first community's: Hegesippus on James, the Ebionites per Epiphanius, the Didache's Two Ways, none of which has a lay exemption. And it says plainly what we actually ask of a layperson, which is the pastoral question `what-we-actually-offer.md` already handles for salvation and has not been asked for diet. Needs: a heading on `sacrifice-culture-vegetarian-jesus.md`, and probably a short section on `christianity/eastern-orthodox-theosis-closest-to-original.md`, which is the file that already treats Orthodoxy as the nearest surviving relative.

**C. Virtue-signaling, grounding, and the culture of food.** (rows 5, 6) Not a source question. The answer is a way of speaking: never contempt for the table someone grew up at, the concession that the loudest advocates have often been the least grounded, and the plain fact that the people making this case here have families. Needs: two paragraphs at the end of `questions/LIVE-SETUP.md` or `what-we-actually-offer.md` on tone when diet comes up, written by someone who has had the conversation at a family dinner.

**D. "The Theosophists invented it."** (row 8) **Answered 2026-10-10** on the sacrifice card. What it needed was done: the Eusebius, Josephus, Philo, Porphyry and Jerome passages are held (`christianity/Incoming/witnesses-on-flesh-and-sacrifice-selected-texts.md`, thirteen excerpts sliced from public-domain editions), and the card has the heading. Two findings from the pass, both in the file: **Porphyry never says the Essenes abstained from animal food** (the line quoted as his is not in his text, and it was in three of our files and on air in 026 at 1:29:10), and the only ancient text that says it, Jerome, credits Josephus, who doesn't. The Nazarene movement's abstinence is well attested; the Essenes' is not.

**E. Theodicy of the many damned.** (row 18) `hell-afterlife.md` needs one heading that joins the character argument to the texts: Luke 15 (the father runs), Matthew 18:14 ("not the will of your Father that one of these little ones should perish"), 1 Timothy 2:4 if we want to quote Paul against Paulinism, Thomas 113, and the plain reading of *apollumi* as destroy. The hosts' "no loving parent" line is fine as a closer, not as an opener. Needs: half a session on the card.

An objection goes in when a real person raised it: name them, name the file and the phrase (or the timestamp), and say honestly whether a card answers it. If it does, name the heading. If it does not, write a brief and add the job to `OPEN-RESEARCH.md`. If the answer is "that is not a question about the texts," say so and mark it N/A rather than inventing a rebuttal.
