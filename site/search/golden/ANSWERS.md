> — [Mark 10:24-27 (KJV)](https://github.com/the-nazarene/way/blob/main/christianity/Incoming/kjv/41-mark.md#L441-L444)

**The usual reading:** Most Christians read these sayings as a warning against trusting in riches, and many take the call to sell everything as aimed at one man's particular obstacle rather than a rule for all.

**What the library finds:** The library holds that the passages show Jesus answering a question about life with behavior: keep the commandments, then give to the poor and follow him [2][7]. Mark pictures the difficulty as trusting in riches, yet adds that with God all things are possible [5]. The rich man and Lazarus parable is about neglecting a starving man at the gate, not about wrong belief [1]. Early readers heard the same note: Irenaeus took the story as a warning against luxury and forgetting God [9], and Jerome cited Zacchaeus to show a rich man can be saved [10].

**The strongest verse the other way:**

> And Jesus looking upon them saith, With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible.  
> — [Mark 10:27 (KJV)](https://github.com/the-nazarene/way/blob/main/christianity/Incoming/kjv/41-mark.md#L444)

Jesus does not say riches shut a person out for good: with God even a rich man can be saved, so the teaching is not a flat condemnation of the wealthy.

**Not settled:** The passages do not settle whether the command to sell everything binds every disciple or was addressed to one man. Jerome saw it as an invitation to perfection [4].
